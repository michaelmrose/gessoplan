* Status, purpose, and authority
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: status
:END:

This is an *exploratory planning note*, not an amendment to the canonical
Gesso R4 design, a claim of implementation progress, or an approved public API.
It records a possible route from today's application-preflight assurance to
mechanically established properties of downstream authoritative operations.
Any change to the canonical plan or implementation order requires an explicit
design decision.

The organizing conjecture is:

#+begin_quote
Many application mutations are instances of a small family of authoritative
*resolution templates*.  If Gesso establishes the correctness of the reusable
resolution mechanism and checks the domain-specific obligations of each
instantiation, it may provide strong application guarantees without proving
arbitrary Clojure programs from scratch.
#+end_quote

This approach should retain Gesso's existing fundamentals:

- downstream developers write ordinary, inspectable Clojure constructions;
- application policy belongs to the application, not to Gesso;
- Gesso performs normalization, checks, proof bookkeeping, and diagnostics;
- Choreo coordinates admitted authoritative operations, not private FX stages;
- the browser cannot confer authority on itself;
- an unchecked model implementation is never declared proved by naming it;
- unsupported or missing evidence remains explicit under the selected
  assurance profile; and
- *admission correspondence* and *execution refinement* are distinct obligations.

This note is intentionally concerned with *new classes of guarantees*, not
additional assurance-report fields in isolation.

* Why resolution templates may change the economics
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: motivation
:END:

A typical model operation combines two quite different kinds of complexity.

1. *Resolution mechanics*: admit a command, bind identity and authority, check
   preconditions at a valid commit boundary, settle contention, record a durable
   outcome, replay compatible retries, reconcile unknown delivery, and retain
   after-commit work.
2. *Domain meaning*: decide which request states, principals, memberships,
   organizations, quantities, or temporal conditions permit the transition,
   and what domain invariants the resulting state must satisfy.

Resolution mechanics recur across many domains.  It is wasteful and dangerous
for every application to reinvent them, and equally wasteful for verification
to prove the entire retry/contention/recovery protocol afresh for every
operation.

The intended division of work is:

| Responsibility                  | Primarily owned by                     |
|---------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| Resolution protocol             | Gesso template                         |
| Command identity and retry      | Gesso template                         |
| Database-independent semantics  | Gesso template plus admitted instance  |
| Domain guards and effects       | Application's admitted construction    |
| Domain invariants               | Application's admitted construction    |
| Template mechanism theorem      | Gesso verification effort              |
| Instance-specific proof         | Gesso checker, from domain declarations|
| Backend lowering/refinement     | Gesso implementation and evidence      |
| External host implementations   | Trusted or independently checked       |
| Browser optimism and recovery   | Choreo and existing Gesso runtime      |

The important distinction: a correct template does not automatically imply a
correct business rule, and a correct symbolic rule does not automatically imply
that production executed the rule correctly.

* Proposed family of resolution templates
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: template-family
:END:

The following is a *candidate taxonomy*, not evidence that exactly six distinct
primitives are necessary.  The goal is the smallest useful set with explicit,
composable guarantees.

** Guarded atomic transition

Examples: claim, cancel, approve, complete, reject, transfer ownership.

Contract: guard the decision-relevant authoritative facts and atomically
incorporate the specified domain effects and bound command outcome.  On
rejection, do not partially incorporate the proposed transition.  Preserve
unknown delivery when the outcome cannot yet be determined.

This should probably be the *first template* and perhaps the base from which
several other templates are derived.

** Conditional creation

Examples: create Request, register Membership, open case, enroll participant.

Contract: atomic creation subject to identity/uniqueness conditions, durable
command association, and retry semantics.  Define what happens when another
command creates an equivalent or conflicting entity first.

It may be a guarded atomic transition with an absent-to-present state change,
rather than a truly separate mechanism.

** Guarded replacement or versioned mutation

Examples: edit fields, change preferences, modify a budget, reassign owner.

Contract: compare the relevant expected version or current predicates at
commit; reject/re-evaluate stale decisions; prevent lost updates according to
the declared conflict policy.

A blind version comparison may be insufficient when correctness depends on
other entities (for example active Membership or Organization policy).

** Monotonic accumulation

Examples: append a durable event, record an acknowledgement, add a fact to a
set, collect approvals.

Contract: declare an additive/monotone transition and duplicate policy.
Convergence or commutativity must be established under the actual merge/order
semantics, not inferred from the label =monotonic=.

** Reservation or lease

Examples: reserve capacity, temporarily assign a resource, hold a slot.

Contract: guarded exclusive allocation, a well-defined expiration clock and
boundary, renewal/release rules, and fencing or equivalent protection against
stale holders.  Expiration is a domain transition, not just a browser timer.

This adds temporal proof obligations and may compose with guarded atomic
transition rather than requiring an independent commit engine.

** Durable multistep process

Examples: external payment, provisioning, delivery, third-party approval.

Contract: each externally observable intermediate state is a valid domain
state; progress, unknown outcomes, retry/compensation, and irreversible effects
are explicit.  A single atomic local transaction does not make a remote side
effect atomic.

This is likely a larger protocol built out of smaller templates and must not
inherit the guarantees of one atomic transaction across system boundaries.

** Candidate operation classification exercise

Before freezing the taxonomy, classify the actual HumanHelp production model
operations.  Useful examples include Request creation, claim, cancel,
reassignment, completion, expiration, Membership activation/deactivation, and
Organization policy changes.  For each, record its natural template, involved
authoritative entities, unusual failure semantics, and any escape hatch that
would be needed.

If almost every operation requires substantial bespoke handling, the taxonomy
has not captured the real commonality.

* Resolution as a product of semantic dimensions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: dimensions
:END:

An alternative hypothesis is that several apparent templates are compositions
of a small number of independent *resolution dimensions*:

| Dimension              | Candidate policies                                      |
|------------------------+---------------------------------------------------------|
| Commit discipline      | guarded atomic, additive, lease, staged process         |
| Conflict policy        | reject, re-evaluate, compare-version, serialize         |
| Identity and retry     | bound durable command, replay, duplicate suppression    |
| Outcome knowledge      | known success, known rejection, unknown/reconcile       |
| Temporal discipline    | immediate, deadline, lease, scheduled expiry            |
| External effects       | durable debt, retryable delivery, compensation          |
| Browser realization    | authoritative-only, provisional, advisory refresh      |

Do not assume all combinations are meaningful or safe.  A type-like
compatibility checker could accept approved compositions and reject impossible
ones, while the public API exposes a few understandable defaults.

In particular, *browser realization is not a transaction commit policy*.
Choreo may select an optimistic presentation without changing authoritative
claim eligibility, command identity, or the commit protocol.

A promising internal design is one dominant default resolution protocol:

#+begin_example
bound-command
  + authoritative-guarded-atomic-transition
  + durable-command-outcome
  + durable-after-commit-debt
  + unknown-outcome-reconciliation
#+end_example

Conditional creation and versioned mutation may then be specializations of
this protocol.  This is a hypothesis to test against actual operations.

* The generic guarded-transition contract
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: core-contract
:END:

** Symbols and intended meaning

Let:

- =S= be admitted authoritative state;
- =C= be a command with stable identity, operation, arguments, and bound principal;
- =G(S,C)= be the admitted domain guard;
- =E(S,C)= be the admitted effect producing proposed authoritative state;
- =I(S)= be a domain invariant;
- =R= be a durable command-result record; and
- =D= be durable debt for required after-commit delivery.

The core template should establish, conditional on its backend assumptions:

1. A fresh successful command incorporated only when =G= held at the authoritative
   commit boundary under an appropriate transaction snapshot/isolation contract.
2. The authoritative domain write, bound command-result record, and applicable
   durable delivery debt are incorporated atomically.
3. The command identity cannot be reused with incompatible operation,
   arguments, or principal; permitted compatible replay returns the recorded
   outcome without repeating the effect.
4. Competing commands cannot each successfully incorporate a mutually
   exclusive transition if the declared guards and effects really enforce that
   exclusivity under the transaction substrate.
5. A confirmed rejection cannot leave a partial transition from the rejected
   attempt.  It does not assert that other concurrent operations made no
   changes.
6. A lost response after submission does not turn an unknown outcome into
   known rejection.  Reconciliation consults authoritative outcome evidence.
7. Failure to publish, notify, or render *after* incorporation does not undo
   the incorporated result or reclassify it as failure; delivery debt remains
   separately resolvable.
8. Any replayed result is disclosed only when the caller is permitted to learn
   it.  Matching command bytes are not themselves authentication.

The words =at the commit boundary= are substantial.  A prepare-phase read is
not a guard; a precheck is not a guard; a browser-observed basis is not a guard;
and a later unconditional write cannot discharge the guard obligation.

** Application-specific invariant obligation

Even with all generic template guarantees established, an individual operation
must show something like:

#+begin_example
I(S) AND G(S,C)  =>  I(E(S,C))
#+end_example

This is an application-specific proof obligation.  Other admitted operations
must also preserve =I=, including operations that change a referenced
Membership or Organization policy.  Otherwise the invariant is not global.

For a temporal/history-sensitive property (for example eligibility *at claim
time*), the state or proof subject must contain appropriate claim-time evidence.
Current eligibility after an independent policy change is a different property.

** Initiality and environment assumptions

The preservation implication is not enough by itself.  A state invariant
requires an admissible initial state and closure under *every* authoritative
transition in the declared universe, including background jobs and
administrative operations.

A conditional theorem should state transaction atomicity/isolation,
authentication reliability, backend correctness, and any timing/fairness
assumptions as assumptions until stronger evidence discharges them.

** Liveness differs from safety

A proof that no competing Claim can assign two owners is a safety result.
A proof that a Request is serviced, cancelled, or timed out within N minutes
also needs assumptions about clock progression, scheduling, worker availability,
and deadline execution.  Template selection cannot silently turn an enabled
timeout into a guaranteed timely execution.

* What an application author would supply
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: authored-instance
:END:

The developer-facing form should remain ordinary Clojure and should not
require author-written TLA+, Quint, Recife, or a pervasive macro DSL.

One *illustrative* construction, not a finalized API:

#+begin_src clojure
(gesso.operation/construct
 {:operation :request/claim
  :resolution :guarded-atomic-command

  ;; These are named, typed semantic rules, not arbitrary predicates
  ;; whose internal behavior has magically been verified.
  :requires
  [[:= [:request :request/status] :open]
   [:relationship :membership/for-request-organization]
   [:rule :organization/permits-claim]
   [:authorized? :request/claim]]

  :transition
  {:request/status :claimed
   :request/helper-id [:principal :user/id]}

  :preserves
  #{:request/at-most-one-current-owner}

  :outcomes
  #{:incorporated :guard-rejected :unknown-needs-reconciliation}})
#+end_src

Requirements for a useful real design:

- Each rule resolves to a declared, typed, source-owned semantic definition or
  an explicitly trusted predicate with a conformance obligation.
- Field/path references have nominal types, optionality, provenance, and
  authority/knowledge rules as appropriate.
- The transition has known writes, including required command/outcome data.
- Decision-relevant reads can be identified and protected by the selected
  lowering; queries, phantoms, and cross-entity predicates are not hand-waved.
- The command carries correlation, never browser-selected authority.
- The model public API remains the semantic boundary that Choreo invokes.
- Source-derived rule evidence is not maintained in a second manual registry.
- Errors explain *which proof obligation cannot be discharged* and why.

An ordinary helper function may still compute presentation data or diagnostics.
If an opaque helper determines whether an authoritative mutation occurs, the
strong-profile checker must either require additional conformance evidence or
reject that claimed guarantee.  Merely naming the helper must not prove it.

* HumanHelp Claim: the first vertical slice
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: claim-slice
:END:

** Admitted command and authority

A Claim command includes the target Request and stable command identity.
The authenticated helper principal is supplied by a trusted server boundary,
not derived from a browser-provided actor ID.

Before permitted replay, an existing command record must be checked for the
correct operation, target Request, arguments, bound principal, and permission
to disclose the previous outcome.

A new command's candidacy depends at least on:

- current Request status permits Claim;
- current Membership is active and belongs to the relevant Organization;
- Membership/Request/Organization associations are consistent;
- Organization policy permits this helper to claim this Request;
- the authenticated principal is authorized for this operation; and
- command binding is valid and nonconflicting.

** Transition and atomicity

A successful Claim writes Request ownership and the durable command result in
one authoritative transaction, together with required delivery debt.  The
Request, Membership, Organization, association, and command facts whose change
would invalidate the decision must be protected by effective commit guards.

The exact XTDB lowering must be validated against the transaction and isolation
semantics actually in use.  A read/write list alone is not proof of protection.

** Candidate invariant(s)

Start with a precise property, not the vague phrase =Claim is correct=:

#+begin_example
For any admitted Request state, at most one current owner is recorded.
#+end_example

A second, more demanding property could be:

#+begin_example
Every incorporated Claim has durable evidence that the bound principal
passed the required eligibility and authorization decisions at the time
of incorporation.
#+end_example

The second property needs a clear evidence/retention design and does not mean
membership can never be revoked afterward.

** Adversarial traces to cover

- Two helpers attempt Claim concurrently.
- Membership becomes inactive after preparation but before commit.
- Organization policy changes after preparation but before commit.
- A command succeeds; its HTTP response is lost.
- The same command is retried after the Request is no longer open.
- The same ID is reused with different arguments or principal.
- The command commits, but publication or SSE delivery fails.
- A different operation subsequently reassigns or cancels the Request.
- The browser provides a forged actor or stale observed basis.
- Existing result disclosure is attempted by a different principal.

For each trace, distinguish *template behavior* from *domain-invariant
obligations* and from *trusted execution assumptions*.

* How proof reuse would work
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: proof-reuse
:END:

** Prove the generic mechanism, check each instance

A template proof is a theorem about a parameterized machine.  It should not
be replaced by checking one representative concrete instance.

For a guarded atomic template, expected generic judgments include:

- successful incorporation implies effective guard satisfaction;
- the durable effect/result/debt are atomic within the defined scope;
- compatible retries do not duplicate authoritative effects;
- conflicting bindings cannot share a successful command identity;
- unknown outcome delivery does not imply rejection; and
- recorded outcomes and after-commit effects maintain their distinct meanings.

A particular operation then supplies the typed guard/effect pair and proves or
checks the invariant-preservation, interference, disclosure, and
relationship-specific obligations relevant to it.

This is an instance of *conditional compositional certification*:

#+begin_example
verified template contract
        +
well-formed typed instantiation
        +
proved operation-specific invariant obligations
        +
checked execution refinement / declared trusted premises
        |
        v
operation certificate with precise scope and assumptions
#+end_example

** Proof DAG and semantic identity

The certificate should bind the normalized instance, template version,
relevant rule/schema/relationship identities, chosen backend lowering,
authoritative implementation evidence, and exact propositions.

Changes to a relied-upon rule, policy, operation, template, compiler, or
implementation should invalidate affected downstream evidence.  Equivalent
structure alone should not impersonate a certified artifact.  The proof DAG
should allow unaffected operations to retain their evidence.

** Cross-operation interference

Separately certified transitions do not automatically compose.

If an Organization policy update or Membership revocation affects Claim's
invariant, Gesso must establish the shared preservation/interference
obligations.  The eventual checker needs a complete admitted set of operations
that may modify relied-upon facts, or an explicit, limited-world assumption.

Live-effect overlap is helpful for UI impact analysis but is *not* a substitute
for authoritative-state dependency or transaction-conflict analysis.

** Backend correspondence is a separate theorem/obligation

Proving symbolic Claim and then executing an unrelated Clojure function proves
nothing about the latter's behavior.

Two possible implementation modes should be made explicit:

- *Generated/controlled realization*: Gesso lowers admitted semantics to a
  narrowly constrained commit plan and checks its lowering contract.  This is
  the stronger long-term path for supported operations.
- *Opaque implementation*: the application supplies a host function and a
  named conformance premise, or independently checked refinement evidence.
  The report must label it accordingly.

A generated plan still depends on the backend's actual transaction semantics,
correct lowering, and correct runtime execution.  Shared source of truth is
valuable, but not itself a proof of compiler correctness.

* The role of model checkers and the proof kernel
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: checking-tools
:END:

** Gesso remains the semantic owner

Developers should not author separate Quint/TLA+/Recife specifications.
Gesso should derive its verification subjects from admitted constructions and
make any counterexample understandable in terms of real operations and rules.

Potential mechanisms:

- *Direct Clojure semantic interpreter*: one reference meaning for the
  admitted transition/rule subset, suitable for differential tests.
- *Finite-state exploration*: find counterexample interleavings among a small
  number of Requests, helpers, commands, and policy changes.
- *Symbolic/bounded checking*: explore larger or parameterized bounded
  obligations with an external solver/model checker where supported.
- *Inductive proof obligations*: establish invariant preservation for all
  states of a supported theory, rather than only tested finite cases.
- *Small proof kernel*: validate derivations/certificates without trusting
  every heuristic elaboration step.

Quint and Recife remain candidates, not yet an architectural decision.
The first milestone should compare them against *the same Gesso-derived Claim
transition system*, including the ability to report understandable failing
traces.  Choose tools by the properties they can actually establish and the
cost of keeping them in correspondence with the production semantic core.

** Assurance classification

Do not flatten these different results into =proved=:

| Evidence                                  | Meaning                               |
|-------------------------------------------+---------------------------------------|
| Shape/type checked                        | Admitted construction well formed     |
| Assembly verified                         | Required declared runtime edges close |
| Finite model checked                      | No counterexample in stated universe  |
| Bounded symbolic checked                  | No counterexample within given bound  |
| Inductively/kernel proved                 | Stated theorem under listed premises  |
| Runtime enforced                          | Trusted execution guard is operational|
| Conformance tested                        | Empirical behavior under tested cases |
| Trusted assumption                        | Not discharged by present evidence    |
| Unknown or unsupported                    | No claimed guarantee                  |

These are conceptual descriptions; use R4's canonical assurance taxonomy and
certificate vocabulary when implementing them.

** What model checking will not resolve

- A bounded model is not an unbounded proof.
- A proof of template semantics is not a proof of arbitrary host code.
- Fair scheduling and timely external service require stated assumptions.
- A correctly enforced but foolish business policy is still possible.
- A forged or stale certificate must not become an authority token.

* Relation to the existing Gesso R4 design
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: canonical-crosswalk
:END:

This note is a proposed *reuse strategy inside the existing R4 architecture*.
It should not create a rival semantics or a separately curated registry of
business-policy truth.

| Canonical R4 part | Relevant existing responsibility                     |
|-------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| Part II           | Semantic model, authority, knowledge, domain meaning |
| Part IV           | Atomic authoritative operations and progression     |
| Part III/V/VI     | Choreo, optimism, browser realization and recovery   |
| Part VII          | Whole-application construction and preflight        |
| Part VIII         | HumanHelp proving application, Claim witness        |
| Part IX           | Compositional certification and proof DAG           |
| Part X            | Production/formal correspondence; trust boundaries  |
| Part XII          | Implementation order, especially Stages 9–12        |

The present =semantic_audit= module reports established source-derived facts,
missing model-policy evidence, and trusted boundaries.  That is useful and
should remain honest.  A template checker would *establish new obligations and
certificates upstream*; the audit can then present those results.  Expanding the
audit alone does not deliver this architecture.

Current implementation evidence through v780 includes decomposed application
preflight, direct and integration tests for semantic auditing, and the repaired
projection of per-operation execution capabilities.  Those do *not* establish
that a protected HumanHelp model transition has yet been proved.

Template work should be reconciled with the canonical stages rather than
implicitly jumping over Stage 6 gates or silently redefining Stage 10's typed
rule language.

* Proposed focused experiment and acceptance gates
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: plan
:END:

** Phase A — Inventory and hypothesis test

Inspect real HumanHelp public model operations and candidate generic examples.
For each operation, record:

- authoritative guard, effect, command identity, replay, and after-commit needs;
- transaction participants and relevant cross-entity dependencies;
- likely template and required semantic dimensions;
- opaque decision points and unsupported constructs; and
- whether the template avoids duplicating existing model policy.

Deliverable: a small classification table and a concrete choice of *one* first
resolution template.  Revisit the taxonomy if it requires too many exceptions.

Acceptance: Claim, Create, and Cancel can be represented meaningfully without
pretending that private host-code behavior has been analyzed.

** Phase B — One admitted template and tiny rule subset

Build the minimal semantic subject for guarded atomic command resolution.
Reuse R4 typing, proposition, and identity concepts.  Include only rule forms
needed to express one serious Claim property: equality, state membership,
entity relationships, principal binding, and a typed named policy premise.

Provide a reference interpreter and explicit error diagnostics.

Acceptance: malformed rules are rejected; typed but substantively wrong rules
remain representable and reviewable; opaque policy use leaves a clear premise.

** Phase C — One authoritative executable realization

Derive or check the Claim commit plan against the admitted subject.  The plan
must incorporate Request ownership, durable command result, and required
publication debt atomically and protect *all* decision-relevant facts at commit.
Use the backend's documented transaction semantics, not assumptions inferred
from an API name.

Acceptance: forged principal, stale Membership, changed Organization policy,
competing Claim, mismatched command replay, and lost acknowledgement have
precisely defined outcomes, tested against the real transaction substrate.

** Phase D — One actual safety result

Establish at least one Claim invariant under explicitly listed premises.
Start with one-owner preservation; extend to durable claim-time eligibility
evidence only after specifying what history must be retained.

Acceptance: a proof/certificate states the exact proposition, domain,
assumptions, and implementation correspondence evidence.  Deliberately broken
guards or transitions invalidate the proof or produce a reproducible
counterexample.

** Phase E — Composition and second instantiation

Add Cancel (or another operation sharing an important invariant) under the same
semantic template.  Check interference and stale-certificate invalidation.

Acceptance: most resolution machinery is reused; only domain-specific
obligations are new.  If the second operation needs substantial duplicated
machinery, revisit the template boundary before expanding the catalog.

** Phase F — Integration with Choreo and root assurance

Feed established operation contracts into the existing Choreo construction,
application preflight, and proof-DAG vocabulary.  Preserve the public Request
model operation as Choreo's authoritative boundary.

Acceptance: root assurance distinguishes checked theorem, runtime enforcement,
external assumptions, and unproved model behavior.  A stale template/rule/
implementation identity causes a meaningful failure in a stronger profile,
not a silent downgrade.

* What to measure as progress
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: measures
:END:

Prefer measures that indicate new mechanically established properties:

- number of *distinct verified propositions* and their assumption scopes;
- number of real operations instantiated without bespoke resolution plumbing;
- ability to reject missing commit-time guards *before deployment*;
- ability to generate counterexamples for deliberately broken invariants;
- ability to detect stale or forged evidence across the entire dependency DAG;
- ratio of required opaque model premises to checked semantic dependencies;
- proof/certificate reuse when adding a second and third operation; and
- real XTDB/Chromium fault coverage for execution and recovery.

Source lines, assertion totals, report size, and revision counts are useful
maintenance signals but not evidence that downstream correctness has advanced.

* Open design questions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: open-questions
:END:

1. Can guarded atomic command resolution cover most ordinary mutations, or
   should creation, versioned replacement, and leases be first-class templates?
2. Are resolution dimensions genuinely composable, and which combinations are
   invalid or require extra obligations?
3. What is the *smallest* closed typed rule vocabulary that expresses Claim's
   meaningful guards without hiding business logic in an opaque escape hatch?
4. Should a named typed policy predicate remain trusted initially, or can
   source-owned semantic definitions be normalized and checked immediately?
5. Which predicate dependencies can be statically extracted, and when must
   a conservative whole-relation/database predicate be protected at commit?
6. How is absence/uniqueness checked safely under concurrent creation and
   phantom-producing queries in the specific XTDB transaction substrate?
7. What is the right theorem boundary between template semantics and backend
   lowering, and what evidence would genuinely discharge the latter?
8. Can we establish generic template theorems parametrically, or would an
   initially smaller, kernel-checked operational calculus be more economical?
9. Where should a finite model checker be used for bug discovery, and where
   does the desired guarantee demand an inductive or refinement proof?
10. How much of the rule/operation representation can be reused directly from
    canonical Parts II, IV, IX, and X instead of introducing another layer?
11. What exactly must the selected certification profile forbid when only a
    trusted host implementation is available?
12. Can the second real operation reuse most of the first operation's proof
    machinery?  This is the decisive abstraction-quality test.

* Recommended next discussion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: next-discussion
:END:

Do *not* begin by implementing all candidate templates or a large general
proof language.

Begin with an authoritative-operation inventory and one sharp question:

#+begin_quote
Can Gesso represent HumanHelp Claim as a typed instance of a reusable guarded
atomic command template, and mechanically establish one real domain invariant
while preserving the distinction between admitted semantics and production
execution?
#+end_quote

If yes, use the second operation to demonstrate reuse and composition.  If no,
let the concrete failed obligations show which semantic primitive is missing.

The goal is to make the right construction easy, an apparently working but
incorrect construction difficult, and the remaining assumptions impossible to
mistake for proofs.

* Part III — Choreography and projection
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-03-choreography-and-projection
:PART_REVISION: 03-20261107
:END:

This part defines choreography as a global semantic specification, the operation
contracts it exposes, and the obligations for deriving role-local behavior. It
uses the evidence classifications of Part I and the identity, admission,
knowledge, and authority model of Part II. Concrete model/FX integration follows
in Part IV; browser realization and whole-application construction follow in
Parts VI and VII.

The intended architecture supports ordinary full-language Clojure under the
[[#req-full-language-construction][checked construction contract]]. A small
portable choreography vocabulary is an interface for distributed coordination,
not a replacement for the application's implementation language. Opaque code
retains its named contracts and accepted premises through verification,
projection, assembly, and execution.

Examples use illustrative declaration and API names. Their normative
content is the specified semantic contract rather than the literal spelling
of an example's helper names. Any public construction surface realizing the
example must establish the corresponding checked bindings. Generated plan
excerpts and state tables are inspection views, not a second handwritten
workflow. Fixture symbols such as R, C, and E denote identities or
values bound by the surrounding example.

** What a choreography is

A choreography is a global description of a distributed state machine involving
N roles.

The global choreography is projected into N role-local machines. At runtime,
concrete actors instantiate those roles and execute the local machines
independently. The entry/participation contract binds each role instance to its
actual actor and principal or authority. A role name is not a credential, and
two tabs for one principal do not share knowledge merely because that principal
is the same.

The application may have many concurrent executions of the same choreography.
Admission states which instances may participate, how their messages and
resources are separated, and whether a logical role can have multiple members.
An aggregate role is not silently treated as one omniscient actor. Projection
of one execution also does not prove isolation from other operations; model
invariants and atomic guards govern their shared authoritative effects.

There does not need to be a second centralized global machine executing beside
them. The global choreography is the specification from which local machines
are verified and projected.

Under the declared entry, runtime, and environmental premises, the interaction
of admitted projected machines must refine behavior allowed by the global
choreography. The refinement claim must identify its supported language subset,
observable behaviors, and treatment of internal or physical steps.

The refinement relation is between admitted global semantic executions and
admitted distributed executions of the independently projected machines under
the specified environment, message-delivery, and fault contracts. A global
=:communicate= occurrence is a semantic communication boundary; emission of a
packet by a projected sender is not, by itself, proof that the receiver admitted
the matching message or acquired its declared knowledge. Permitted hidden local
steps and transport activity require an explicit trace-observation relation.

A successful structural projection check, role-local reachability result, or
replay of one concrete schedule establishes only its stated checking property.
It is not an all-schedules refinement theorem or a fairness/liveness proof.
A stronger claim must quantify over the admitted schedules and retain its
reordering, delivery, terminal-outcome, and environment premises. In
particular, a projected role finishing does not mean it knows the authored
global terminal result unless the choreography establishes that knowledge.

This claim about cooperating projected machines does not assume that an
adversarial browser follows its projection. Trusted-side admission and model
enforcement must still preserve the protected authority boundary for arbitrary
client inputs, as specified in [[#req-trust-boundary][the trust model]].

A choreography may describe:

- local computation;
- communication;
- authoritative establishment and observation requirements;
- environmental events;
- branching;
- authoritative operations;
- provisional projections;
- resource acquisition and release;
- failure paths;
- timeout paths;
- settlement and reconciliation;
- convergence requirements.

A choreography does not imply that all participants change state atomically.
Distributed actors may temporarily hold different knowledge. Model guards
protect authoritative effects at their own qualified transition boundaries;
projection neither serializes every concurrent execution nor transfers one
participant's unobserved authoritative result to another. Missing messages,
indeterminate outcomes, and stalled environments remain explicit recovery
or progress obligations rather than being erased by successful construction.

The important distinction is between:

#+begin_example
authoritative state
provisional state
actor-local knowledge
#+end_example

and not a false claim that the entire distributed system changes simultaneously.

A choreography also does not imply one persistent runtime machine per connected
client or one persistent server process per conceptual workflow.

The global description may be realized by short-lived projections that interact
through durable authoritative facts.


*** A concrete coordination vocabulary

The canonical global representation is a versioned graph of typed semantic
states. States have stable source identities for explanation, successors, and
the contracts their transitions use. Semantic keys and options are closed for
the selected representation version: an unknown opcode, misspelled required
field, unsupported option, or unresolved contract reference produces a specific
construction failure. An explicit nonsemantic metadata/extension area may be
retained, but its contents cannot affect execution or strengthen a claim without
a declared interpretation. The closure guarantee belongs to the *admitted semantic interface* after
checked elaboration, not to the mere existence of a Clojure map or successful
low-level structural normalization. A low-level normalizer may retain fields
it does not interpret. The supported construction profile must distinguish
meaningful semantic fields from inert, declared metadata and reject unknown
or misspelled executable fields before claiming a closed contract. Silently
discarding such a field could weaken an operation without a visible failure.

A supported baseline has six node kinds:

| Global node | Meaning | Required contract |
|-------------+---------+-------------------|
| =:local= | Work at one role | Named implementation, placement, inputs, outputs, effects, failure and completion behavior |
| =:authoritative= | Result of a public authoritative operation at its trusted owner | Operation interface, caller/authority binding, result alternatives, and realization premises |
| =:branch= | Selection using an already established local value | Owning role, typed selector, exhaustive supported alternatives or an explicit unmatched-value failure |
| =:communicate= | One semantic transfer between roles | Sender, receiver, payload, correlation, disclosure, and channel contract |
| =:await= | A role admits an event from its environment | Registered source, event alternatives, owner, payload, and provenance obligations |
| =:return= | Declared global terminal disposition | Meaning of completion, remaining obligations, and any permitted handoff |

The corresponding role-local vocabulary includes =:send= and =:receive= for
the two sides of communication, alongside local work, authoritative work,
branching, environment waits, and local return. A participant message cannot
satisfy an environment wait merely because its event keyword matches. An
environment event likewise cannot impersonate a participant receive.

Selectors distinguish missing bindings, present nil, and false according to the
declared type. If nil is a supported member of that selector type, it needs an
explicit alternative; it must not be conflated with an absent value. A target
that cannot represent such a selector rejects the unsupported contract during
construction rather than interpreting the value through generic truthiness.

A local node is not permission to conceal a database write, outbound message,
protected disclosure, or required resource transition. Its interface exposes
effects that matter to the claimed behavior. A computation may be hidden in a
particular distributed trace only when the trace abstraction permits it; it
remains subject to local safety, resource, and implementation obligations.

An authoritative result is not synonymous with a committed write. It may report
rejection, a disclosed recorded outcome, or uncertainty under the operation's
contract. The result's category and payload determine what the next state may
conclude. Merely naming the state =:authoritative= establishes no runtime trust.

An acquisition can be realized as a declared effect followed by an environment
completion carrying an admitted authoritative observation. It need not be a new
portable observation opcode, and it must not be mistaken for a new model
mutation. The observation contract binds the authority, subject, query,
projection, principal context, basis, and temporal meaning required by Part II.

Higher-level constructs such as an operation invocation, authoritative
acquisition, retry, resource scope, or a source-language conditional elaborate
to these transitions or to an explicitly versioned extension with defined
semantics. An invocation at a browser role does not relocate the authoritative
operation into the browser: elaboration includes the declared request,
trusted-side operation, and outcome-delivery boundary. A macro or helper must
expose this expansion for inspection and checking.

For an invocation whose caller role and authority role are distinct,
the elaborated program must account for three *different* propositions:
the caller has issued a command under an admitted binding; the trusted
authority has produced an admitted command-specific outcome; and the caller
has actually acquired that outcome under its own knowledge, source, and
correlation contract. The semantic =:authoritative= boundary belongs to
the trusted authority, not the initiating browser or manager. A network
request, HTTP response, or effect callback is not by itself a global
=:authoritative= result or an admitted knowledge transfer.

A declared participant communication may carry the bound request or its
result, producing the corresponding global =:communicate= occurrence and
projected =:send= / =:receive=. Alternatively, an explicitly authored
environment acquisition may admit a trusted outcome observation at the
waiting role through =:await= and its registered source contract.
Different admissible realizations need not add a peer message merely to
model transport. They *must* preserve whatever communication or
observation the authored protocol actually requires, and establish
the result's command, principal, authority, subject, outcome, and
causal relationship before a dependent branch. No compiler insertion of a
synthetic environment event may make an otherwise unsupported global
history appear to have occurred.

The final =:return= describes a global terminal judgment under the
authored protocol. Individual projected roles may become locally
=:completed= earlier; that fact neither supplies the global return
outcome nor discharges unfinished required communication, external
effects, recoverable delivery, or command uncertainty. Any terminal
refinement claim relates the full admitted distributed execution to
an actual global terminal trace under its named progress and
environment assumptions.

For example, this small graph classifies an already acquired review input. It
does not itself perform an approval:

#+begin_src clojure
(def classify-review-graph
  {:name :expense/classify-review
   :initial :classify
   :states
   {:classify
    {:op :local
     :role :manager
     :action :expense/classify-route
     :requires #{:review/inputs}
     :outputs #{:review/path}
     :next :choose}
    :choose
    {:op :branch
     :role :manager
     :on :review/path
     :cases {:finance :finance-required
             :manager :manager-route}}
    :finance-required
    {:op :return :outcome :review/finance-required}
    :manager-route
    {:op :return :outcome :review/manager-route}}})
#+end_src

The operation entry binds =:review/inputs= to the manager's admitted observation.
The action interface promises an output of type =[:enum :finance :manager]=
under its typed policy or retained opaque contract. The branch consumes that
output; it does not compute the policy or invent the selector. If the action
can fail or yield a third alternative, its actual interface and graph must
expose that path or the admitted outer failure contract must handle it. The
constructor cannot accept a partial success-only graph as a totality proof.

The displayed map is an illustrative semantic graph, not a verified
standalone typed program. Its =:review/inputs= entry binding and the
=:review/path= enum are supplied by external admitted entry/action contracts;
neither is established merely by the keys in this map. Structural
verification establishes the well-formed state, value-key and branch
successor contracts; role-local definite-knowledge analysis establishes
whether the selector is available at the required program point. Those checks do not by themselves prove that an opaque
Clojure action always returns one of the two enum alternatives or that its
implementation respects the intended policy. A checked nominal enum and
complete cases require a separately admitted type/operation contract and
correspondence evidence, or an explicit unmatched-value failure. The baseline branch-case contract does not admit =nil= as a case value.
An explicitly admitted extension for *present nil* distinguishes it from
an absent value and from =false=, and checks all corresponding case and
failure continuations.

*** Vocabulary admission counterexamples

| Apparent input | Required classification |
|----------------+-------------------------|
| A local state uses =:output= instead of =:outputs= | Reject an unknown executable field at the checked construction boundary; do not normalize it to an empty output contract |
| A two-case route action returns =:deferred= | Reject the unmatched selector or use an admitted third case; never silently choose a successor |
| An =:await= event matches a participant message keyword | Enforce the environment-source boundary; it cannot satisfy projected =:receive= |
| An open message contains an extra map key | The extension does not automatically become known semantic input or authority |
| A =:local= action hides a protected write or disclosure | The effect still requires an exposed admitted contract and trusted enforcement |
| One projected role completes while another still has work | Do not claim that the global outcome occurred or that every role knows it |

These are requirements for selected construction and realization profiles.
They are not assertions that the current raw graph normalizer, static verifier,
or browser implementation already proves the whole closed, typed,
provenance-qualified and physically enforced contract.

The sample spells out input/output sets to show the semantic representation.
Authoring helpers derive them from inspectable expressions and operation
interfaces where possible. An opaque action's declared boundary remains an
explicit commitment. Source states such as =:classify= may become compact
runtime locators; that encoding does not change these semantics.

*** A worked expense review

Consider a manager reviewing a previously submitted Expense. This short
choreography starts with a submitted Expense selected from an authorized queue.
The employee's earlier submission and Finance's possible later approval are
separate short executions connected by durable state. This keeps an unused
Finance role from waiting forever in a protocol that never needed its action.

The application owns the domain schemas and public operations:

#+begin_src clojure
(def Expense
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:expense/id uuid?]
   [:expense/employee-id uuid?]
   [:expense/department keyword?]
   [:expense/amount Money]
   [:expense/status
    [:enum :draft :submitted :awaiting-finance :approved :rejected]]])

(def ApprovalPolicy
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:policy/department keyword?]
   [:policy/finance-required-at Money]])

(def finance-route-rule
  {:rule/id :expense/requires-finance
   :inputs {:expense Expense :policy ApprovalPolicy}
   :preconditions
   [[:= [:expense/department :expense] [:policy/department :policy]]
    [:same-currency [:expense/amount :expense]
                    [:policy/finance-required-at :policy]]]
   :returns :boolean
   :expression
   [:money/>= [:expense/amount :expense]
               [:policy/finance-required-at :policy]]})
#+end_src

=Money= and its operations use the explicit money profile from Part I. The rule
does not compare unspecified host decimals or mix currencies. Its policy is
deliberately simple: amounts at or above the threshold require Finance; smaller
amounts may take the manager path, subject to all other model guards. A real
application may supply a richer opaque Clojure policy under an explicit contract.

The review acquisition returns Expense and Policy under one compatible read
context. It binds the Expense to the selected id and the Policy to that Expense's
department. Knowing an id does not establish any of these facts. The manager
principal and permission to read come from trusted acquisition context.
Compatibility of independently acquired values must be established by the
read/authority contract; merely putting them in the same return map does not
prove they were read from one coherent basis.

A domain may also impose a manager approval ceiling. This simplified
Finance-threshold example does not silently assert that a manager has
unlimited approval authority: if the real domain applies a manager ceiling,
that rule belongs to the admitted public model operation and its
transaction-current guards (and, where used for routing, to the declared
review policy). It must not survive merely as an unused schema field or
an informal browser-side assumption.

The operation invocation contracts are:

| Operation | New-incorporation requirement | Successful recorded effect |
|-----------+--------------------------------+----------------------------|
| =:expense/approve-as-manager= | Current manager permission, submitted Expense, applicable Policy, and manager-route eligibility | Expense becomes approved |
| =:expense/mark-awaiting-finance= | Current manager permission, submitted Expense, applicable Policy, and Finance-route eligibility | Expense becomes awaiting Finance |

Each operation atomically protects its current dependencies, domain changes,
and command-result record under Part IV. The preparation read determines which
operation to try; it does not reserve eligibility or authorize incorporation.
A compatible replay follows the recorded-outcome and current disclosure rules
without reapplying a new-incorporation status guard. The authoritative
incorporation's notification contract is chosen independently of the
subsequent caller-visible send. If the business operation requires a
*durable review notification*, C2 incorporation also establishes the
relevant stable publication/work intent at that atomic boundary, or the
operation supplies an equivalently qualified durable discovery path.
A manager browser's later result-bearing send may prompt that work but
cannot be the only recoverable record when the browser may disappear
between commit and send. If the notice is deliberately best effort,
the operation does not manufacture mandatory delivery debt; its
accepted command and terminal claims explicitly retain that weaker
notification guarantee.

Here is the choreography's control structure. The invocation and acquisition
rows are authoring constructs whose elaboration exposes the boundaries described
above; the table does not introduce additional hidden primitive semantics.

| State | Owner and action | Successors |
|-------+------------------+------------|
| Acquire review inputs | Manager requests the admitted Expense/Policy observation | Success: classify; denied/not-found: close the session without notice; temporary failure: declared retry or close without notice |
| Classify | Manager applies the typed Finance-route rule to the bound observation | Finance required: invoke escalation; otherwise: invoke manager approval |
| Invoke escalation | Trusted authority processes the bound escalation command | Settle the command-specific model result |
| Invoke manager approval | Trusted authority processes the bound approval command | Settle the command-specific model result |
| Settle | Manager admits the disclosed result for the actual command | Accepted: publish review notice; rejected: handle rejection and close without notice; unknown: reconcile or hand off recovery, then close this session without notice if needed |
| Publish review notice | Manager emits the permitted historical review result toward the notifier | Manager's sending boundary may finish under its declared handoff; semantic receiver consumption, physical notification and global terminal remain separate |
| Close without notice | Manager emits the declared no-notification disposition | Notifier must admit that closure message or follow the declared missing-delivery/retirement path; command recovery remains with its owner |
| Notify or close | Notifier receives the correlated disposition | Review notice: perform the declared effect; no-notification: return without that effect; delivery failure follows the session recovery contract |

**** Admitted elaboration of the review protocol

The table is an authoring view. It does not justify the manager learning a
value merely because the authoritative operation appears earlier in
the *global* graph. Checked elaboration retains the following boundaries
and source-to-plan evidence:

| Authored step | Admitted semantic/role-local obligation |
|---------------+----------------------------------------|
| Manager acquires Expense and Policy | The manager's declared acquisition effect and admitted =:await= observation establish scoped, coherent input facts and a suitable authority basis, or the declared denied/retry/close path; a readable id or HTTP success does not establish those facts |
| Manager classifies the route | A =:local= action or supported typed expression establishes the manager-known selector; a =:branch= checks both routes and any declared failure/unmatched path without exposing the selector to the notifier |
| Manager submits C2 to Expense authority | The bound command request reaches the declared trusted operation interface under the chosen transport/participant contract; if the source declares a participant message, its actual =:communicate= occurrence is retained, not replaced with a fabricated =:local= step |
| Expense authority settles C2 | The `=:authoritative=` semantic operation occurs at the trusted authority role with authenticated caller, immutable command binding, current guards, and an admitted committed/rejected/unknown-or-reconciliation result; a browser's completion or transport acceptance is not that occurrence |
| Manager acquires C2's result | A declared authority-to-manager =:communicate= with admitted =:receive=, or a genuinely authored trusted =:await= outcome observation, establishes the manager's command-specific knowledge; copying the authority's global result into manager context is forbidden |
| Manager selects notification or closure | A role-local =:branch= uses the actually admitted outcome. Recorded success uses the result-bearing notifier contract; rejection/unavailable cases use the declared no-notification/recovery paths, without treating unknown incorporation as definite failure |
| Notifier receives the selected disposition | The chosen admitted =:communicate= establishes the notifier's permitted fields at actual receiver consumption; unmatched or overlapping variants, false correlation, and absent delivery are not accepted as another branch |
| Notifier performs the declared notification effect | The effect has a declared semantic footprint and a separately evidenced physical issue/outcome, with stable work identity and allowed retry/duplicate handling. A hidden =:local= calculation cannot conceal a notification whose completion the contract claims |
| The review session finishes | Local =:return= / completion may occur at different times for manager and notifier; the claimed global =:return= requires the actual semantic receiver occurrence, declared notification-effect disposition or a separately admitted durable handoff/retirement under the selected terminal contract |

This is an *obligation mapping*, not a claim that the rows are literally
nine global opcodes or that every deployment must use direct
manager-to-authority messages. Different transport and observation
choices may be valid only under their exact authored contracts and
a checked refinement relation. The compact executable plans and
their source/proof mappings must preserve causal order, role
knowledge, observable operation identity, failure alternatives,
and all terminal obligations. The notation does not assume that a
physical provider callback is an authoritative database decision.

The model outcome and the presentation settlement are distinct:

| Model evidence | Permitted continuation |
|----------------+------------------------|
| =:committed= with recorded successful approval/escalation | Accepted continuation for that exact command |
| =:already-incorporated= with a permitted recorded success | The same accepted continuation, without another incorporation |
| =:rejected= with an authoritative command rejection | Rejection handling; no accepted-success notice |
| =:commit-unknown= or indeterminate result delivery | Unknown handling until command-specific authoritative evidence resolves it |

If a model retains other terminal outcomes, the disclosed recorded disposition
has its explicit mapping. A transport failure after a successful commit does
not turn that commit into a rejection. An unrelated Expense now being approved
does not establish that this command approved it.

This worked review chooses a *result-bearing notifier protocol*: the
recorded message deliberately discloses the permitted result of the bound
manager command to the notifier. This is a different disclosure contract from
the *neutral notifier protocol* in the later behavior-sensitive example.
The outward notification text can be identical under both manager outcomes,
but an internal recipient of =:review/result= still learns which result
was reported. Matching text on screen is not a proof that the notifier
cannot distinguish the branches.

The message contains only the declared, disclosure-permitted review
information. It does not contain the manager's private threshold or entire
observation. The issuer or admitting trusted boundary justifies the command
outcome before that result is disclosed. A small inspection view of
this *result-bearing* contract is:

#+begin_src clojure
{:message/id :expense/review-disposition
 :from :manager
 :to :notifier
 :required #{:review/session-id :review/disposition}
 :correlation #{:review/session-id}
 :variants
 {:recorded
  {:required #{:expense/id :gesso/command-id :review/result}
   :result-type [:enum :approved :awaiting-finance]
   :source-evidence :admitted-command-outcome}
  :no-notification
  {:required #{}}}
 :delivery-contract :expense/review-notification}
#+end_src

The =:variants= entry in this map is *higher-level contract inspection data*,
not a self-executing global =:communicate= operation or a second independent
message language. Checked elaboration turns the alternatives into actual
admissible communications and disjoint projected receive alternatives,
for example with separately identified events or an admitted discriminating
closed payload contract. Each resulting communication retains its required,
optional, correlation, sender/receiver, and disclosure contracts. The
constructor must reject unresolved overlaps rather than letting message
arrival or an optional field arbitrarily choose a continuation.
Role-local knowledge-flow checks alone are not proof of the
application-level tagged result's meaning or trusted source.

The admitted review session supplies its correlation identity at entry.
The declared =:review/disposition= discriminator must be checked as
=:recorded= or =:no-notification=, with the recorded variant's
=:review/result= bound to its closed approved/awaiting-Finance
alternative and exact command-result evidence. Checked elaboration
makes the common and variant-specific obligations explicit:

| Admitted variant | Closed semantic fields and discriminator | Required justification |
|------------------+------------------------------------------+------------------------|
| =:recorded= | Common =:review/session-id= and literal =:review/disposition :recorded=, plus =:expense/id=, =:gesso/command-id= and =:review/result= | Trusted command-specific successful incorporation or permitted recorded replay; source, subject, command and result must agree |
| =:no-notification= | Common =:review/session-id= and literal =:review/disposition :no-notification=; no command result or failed-read private subject | Admitted session and source with the declared closure/recovery reason; no result, non-incorporation or domain absence is inferred |

The variants' schemas, source/recipient admission, correlation and
permitted disclosure are independent obligations; the =:variants=
inspection map alone does not prove them. They may elaborate into
distinct identified communication events or a common event with an
admitted disjoint tagged-payload contract. A receiver may not select
a variant by absent optional fields, message order, or a convenient
fallback match. The receiver's actual consumed variant alone
establishes its declared knowledge. Only the recorded variant
carries a command outcome. The no-notification variant
terminates participation without claiming the Expense is absent, the command
failed, or no review was ever recorded. It does not carry a failed read's
private subject. Both variants have closed payload contracts and declared
recipient permission; timing and participation metadata also follow the
declared disclosure policy. A required participant is not left waiting merely because
an earlier acquisition or operation failed; unavailable delivery follows the
session's explicit timeout/retirement or recovery contract.

This is contract inspection data, not a credential. The trusted notification
boundary must validate the origin and permitted command outcome if an
adversarial manager browser transports the notice. A manager-role label or
plausible result string cannot establish that a review occurred. If the service
instead derives the notice from an authoritative outbox, that is the declared
realization and causal path; projection must not invent it silently.

The delivery contract also decides what failure means. The following
milestones have *different* completion evidence:

| Review/notification milestone | What it establishes, and what remains open |
|-------------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| C2 incorporated | The bound Expense operation and command outcome are authoritative; if required, a protected durable notification intent also exists, but delivery does not follow |
| Publication work recorded | The model owns a stable scoped work/intent identity and recovery obligation; neither a particular notifier's receive nor a provider-side effect has occurred by implication |
| Manager local =:send= completed | The manager's declared local emission succeeded under the send contract; the notifier may still know nothing |
| Notifier admitted =:receive= | The particular global semantic =:communicate= occurrence is realized and declared communicated facts are learned; no outside notification is proved |
| Physical notification issued or accepted | The registered notifier/provider completed the *named* physical stage under its correlation and failure contract; an accepted dispatch is not proof a human saw the notice |
| Notification effect fulfilled, failed or handed off | Only the separately declared completion, failure, retry, retirement or durable continuation evidence discharges the corresponding effect obligation; a terminal claim names the actual milestone it asserts |

**** Notification realization profiles and recovery ownership

The worked table above depicts a *manager-mediated* review session. It
does not silently define the physical topology of every durable notice.
The application selects and admits its actual realization, including
the producer of each semantic message, recovery owner, and terminal claim:

| Authored notification profile | Permitted behavior after C2; required proof boundary |
|-------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| Manager-mediated, best effort | After an admitted result, the manager may send the correlated disposition. Loss of that send is permitted only under the authored attempt, timeout or retirement contract. A required waiting notifier still needs its declared closure or recovery; neither global receive nor actual delivery is inferred |
| Model-owned durable publication | C2 creates or qualifies stable discoverable work. A separately admitted worker/notifier execution acquires that work under trusted origin, scope and disclosure rules and produces its *own* declared message/effect steps; the vanished manager's projected =:send= is not manufactured |
| Recoverable manager-mediated delivery | If the authored global protocol requires the manager-to-notifier occurrence, durable state alone cannot substitute for it. An admitted continuation, delegated sender with a checked role/issuer transfer, or explicit protocol alternative must preserve or replace that dependency under a stated refinement relation |

The chosen profile is a *global-plan choice*, not a mere
transport choice for an unchanged manager-to-notifier graph:

| Authored graph | Relevant terminal obligation |
|----------------+------------------------------|
| Manager-mediated communication | The projected manager actually sends, the notifier admits the correlated receive, and the global terminal accounts for the named effect or authored retirement/recovery. Local send completion alone does not establish recipient completion |
| Separate durable publication | C2's guarded success and retained work may complete the review execution under a work-recorded terminal. A new worker execution starts from admitted durable state, with its own origin, observation, permission and physical effect contract; no vanished manager send or old notifier receive is fabricated |
| Checked continuation or delegation | A required original communication persists through an admitted owner/role transfer or semantic alternative, with the changed source, pending occurrence, fencing and refinement relation checked before the successor acts |

The graphs may have the same business-level *work-recorded*
postcondition while admitting different participant traces. The
checker must validate the *selected* global specification, projected
source and receiver roles, knowledge paths, and property-specific
terminal evidence. A later worker effect cannot retrofit a missing
manager message into the old graph. Equally, a work-recorded review
terminal does not wait for future worker fulfillment when that
stronger effect was not its postcondition; the worker's unfinished
obligation remains live under its separately admitted owner.

A worker may continue the *business notification obligation* after the
manager's browser retires without continuing the manager's old runtime
execution. A model-derived outbox trigger is not by itself a peer
=:communicate=, a consumed =:receive=, or the old manager's actuation.
If the worker's protocol is a new short execution, its entry knowledge,
current authority, consumed work identity and effect ownership must be
established there. If a checked alternative replaces the manager send,
the specification and projected plans expose that alternative rather
than silently rewriting a manager-origin message as worker-origin.

The result-bearing disclosure contract is retained under each profile:
an authority- or worker-origin notice may disclose an admitted command
outcome only through its own trusted command binding and current
recipient policy. The best-effort profile cannot interpret a failed or
omitted post-commit send as an authoritative =:no-notification= result
unless that exact closure path is authored and admitted. In particular,
silence does not turn accepted C2 into a rejected C2.

A durable worker and an optional browser hint may coexist, but the
contract must relate their publication identities, independently
admitted occurrences and deduplication effects. A repeated hint is not
a second business command, and an outbox retry is not proof the provider
failed the earlier attempt. A global terminal for the manager session
can promise C2's incorporation and recorded work independently of
the later notification execution. A terminal promising actual external
fulfillment still needs that later effect's qualified evidence and
assumptions; a promise of eventual fulfillment additionally names
the permitted discovery, availability, provider and fairness premises.

The stable delivery/work identity is not interchangeable with C2's
semantic command identity, a transport attempt, or the root identity of
one participant message. A compatible command replay does not create
new required work. A retried delivery may create a new physical attempt
without authorizing an additional externally significant notification;
the provider and receiver contracts govern deduplication, reconciliation,
and any legitimately repeated effect. An exactly-once physical effect
claim requires evidence strong enough for the chosen failure model;
a durable outbox by itself does not establish it.

An accepted
=:send= completion establishes that the sender discharged its *local sending*
boundary, not that the global =:communicate= occurred at the recipient or
that the notification effect executed. The declared semantic communication
is realized at admitted receiver consumption; if that cannot happen
before local retirement, the global terminal path requires a distinct,
explicitly admitted handoff or failure/timeout continuation with its
own business meaning. Neither a missing receive nor a sender's local
=:return= silently chooses =:no-notification=. For a required durable
notification, the model transaction or another justified handoff must retain
recoverable notification work. A direct browser send that can disappear after
commit cannot establish eventual notification. For a best-effort notice, loss
may be allowed but the assurance claim must say so. The normal global
terminal for such a protocol may promise an *attempt* or admitted
retirement, not guaranteed delivery. A durable-handoff terminal may
promise that recoverable work exists, not that the future effect
already happened; that stronger future-delivery claim remains subject
to explicit scheduler, provider, and fairness premises. If the declared
terminal promises actual physical fulfillment, the proof must also
include the qualified effect-completion evidence and the physical
observation/refinement contract, not only a coarse distributed trace.
Replaying an accepted command result must not resend an externally
significant notification unless its own deduplication or repeat
policy permits it.

*** Worked-review counterexamples and correspondence duties

| Case | Required response |
|------+-------------------|
| Manager's Policy/Expense observation is inconsistent or no longer eligible by incorporation | Reject or reacquire under the declared command path; do not publish an uncommitted success notice |
| A manager approval ceiling applies but is absent from the chosen operation's guarded policy | The operation contract is incomplete; do not count browser routing as enforcement |
| A recorded and no-notification disposition use the same event, and the receiver selects by whether optional result fields happen to be present | Reject ambiguous or overlapping variants unless the required literal discriminator and schema are checked at admission |
| Manager completes a transport send of the no-notification variant and the compiler records that the notifier returned without admitting it | Reject premature receiver/global completion; a sent closure message is not a received closure event |
| A retry discloses a permitted recorded successful command outcome | Do not incorporate again; notification replay still follows its *own* deduplication and disclosure rules |
| A browser fabricates =:recorded= with a plausible Expense id or command id | Trusted-side notifier admission must establish the actual bound outcome or use an authoritative outbox |
| A result-bearing message discloses =:review/result= to the notifier while a proof calls the notifier route-ignorant because its displayed notice is neutral | Reject the confidentiality claim or use a separately authored neutral message contract with justified coarse outcome evidence |
| The global graph contains the authority's C2 result and the manager settles on it without a receive, trusted observation or other admitted knowledge path | Reject the manager's result-dependent continuation; a globally recorded authority output does not establish manager knowledge |
| A manager-side HTTP 200 response is lowered into =:authoritative= at the manager rather than at Expense authority | Reject the trust/owner substitution; transport success is not the trusted operation or its verified command outcome |
| The manager finishes locally after sending a notifier message, and the compiler reports global success while the required notifier receive/effect remains unresolved | Distinguish local completion from global terminal correspondence; preserve required delivery/effect or an expressly admitted recovery handoff |
| Two result variants can match the same sender/event/channel and payload | Reject ambiguous projection or require a checked discriminator, not arrival-order selection |
| An accepted model commit is followed by notification delivery failure | Retain delivery/recovery debt if notification is durable; do not retroactively reject the command |
| C2 commits and a required notification is lost because the browser closes before sending, with no durable intent or discovery | Reject the claimed required-delivery construction; the authoritative incorporation must establish qualified recoverable work independently of the browser |
| A notifier admits the review message and the global terminal is reported as a delivered user notification before the provider effect completes | Reject the stronger effect claim; semantic receipt is not physical fulfillment |
| A notification worker retries after an unknown provider response using a new unbound work identity | Preserve/reconcile original delivery intent and its duplicate policy; a second attempt is not permission to double-notify |
| The manager chooses an approval path without notifying Finance | Finance's later participation requires its own admitted queue observation and guards, not an absent earlier message |

These are example-level acceptance and correspondence obligations, not
by themselves a certification of compiler, application, or notifier
realization. An all-participant choreography remains permissible *when*
its declared delivery and branch-knowledge obligations can be met;
this short review intentionally uses a later Finance execution
rather than requiring an idle Finance participant to wait indefinitely.

Projection produces complementary plans, not separately authored workflows:

| Projection | Required behavior and knowledge |
|------------+---------------------------------|
| Manager | Knows selected Expense id; acquires scoped inputs; derives the route; invokes the selected operation; interprets its command result; transfers a permitted notice or closes participation without notice |
| Expense authority | Admits each actual invocation, authenticates the caller, applies replay or atomic new-incorporation semantics, and discloses the permitted result |
| Notifier | Receives the declared disposition; performs the notification effect with its own outcome handling or returns without that effect |

Under this result-bearing contract the notifier acquires the disclosed
=:review/result= when the recorded variant is admitted, whether or not
its physical notification text varies with that result. This contract
cannot be used to prove that the notifier is ignorant of the manager's
route. It does not require disclosure of the private threshold itself.
An application requiring a route-neutral notifier instead authors the
separate neutral contract discussed under behavior-sensitive choice;
the projector cannot erase a required result-bearing message merely
because the visible notification would look the same.

Finance's later queue observation establishes entry knowledge for a new
execution; no unused role in this short choreography has to infer that
a missing message means it may terminate.

A role's local return means that role has no remaining work under its contract.
It is not proof that every other role completed, every message was delivered,
or the whole business workflow finished. The global completion judgment and
any durable recovery handoff retain those distinctions.

*** Choreography is specification, not a hidden coordinator

The global choreography is the semantic specification of allowed distributed
behavior.  It is not a requirement that Gesso run one omniscient global state
machine alongside every projected role.

The implementation must therefore preserve two complementary truths:

- the global description is authoritative for what behaviors are admitted;
- projected actors execute locally and independently using only the knowledge,
  resources, and authority available to them.

A runtime architecture that requires every local step to consult a central
choreography interpreter solely to rediscover projected control flow would
defeat an important purpose of projection. This does not prohibit a declared
authoritative operation from consulting its centralized model authority or
checking a trusted runtime premise. The prohibition concerns an unnecessary
global control interpreter, not model-owned coordination whose semantics the
application actually requires.

Conversely, independently executing local machines do not become semantically
independent programs.  Their combined traces remain constrained by the global
description.

The executable global semantics may still run as a reference oracle in a
checker, simulator, or deterministic test harness. Such a harness may schedule
several projected roles and compare a supplied execution with the global
machine. This is not a second production coordinator, nor is one successful
witness a proof that every admitted distributed schedule refines the global
specification. Actual production participants must obtain their needed local
knowledge and satisfy their own message, owner, environment, and authoritative
operation contracts without consulting a hidden global control state.

The intended relationship is:

| Stage | Product and obligation |
|-------+------------------------|
| Global specification | Declares the allowed distributed behavior |
| Verification and projection | Derives admitted role-local plans with explicit premises |
| Independent local execution | Produces concrete interactions under the realization contracts |
| Refinement judgment | Relates those interactions to the admitted global behavior and stated observation alphabet |

The absence of a central runtime machine is therefore not a weakening of the
global specification.  It is the point of a successful projection.

The *observation alphabet matters* when the choreography carries
externally significant effects. A coarse distributed semantic trace
may omit ordinary =:local= steps; this does not authorize hiding a
notifier's real external delivery, a charge, a protected disclosure,
or an actual DOM installation from a claim whose postcondition
includes it. A checked local action exposes its effect contract and
the relevant issued/fulfilled/failed boundaries to its realization.
An assurance claim about the physical effect uses a corresponding
physical observation and owner/effect identity relation, even when a
different semantic trace intentionally abstracts that action away.

The return condition is property-specific: "command incorporated",
"required publication intent persisted", "notifier consumed the
message", and "recipient was actually notified" are distinct
postconditions. A global terminal outcome cannot establish a
stronger physical postcondition merely because a weaker semantic
trace reached =:return=. Durable handoff can discharge a
*work-recorded* obligation if the authored terminal permits it,
while retaining a separate future fulfillment obligation and any
named environmental progress premises.

The *claim subject* matters as much as the observation alphabet.
An assurance result over one reviewed global plan does not directly
certify a second worker execution, its installed effects, or the
composed business workflow. Gesso derives each required judgment
from the authored application and selected assurance policy, then
joins only evidence whose subjects, identities, premises and scopes
actually correspond:

| Claimed property | Required evidence subject and boundary | Not established by that evidence alone |
|------------------+----------------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| Review choreography terminates | Selected global plan, exact terminal alternative, and its admitted participant/role-local trace or justified general relation | A different notification graph, an unobserved receiver, or any undeclared physical effect |
| C2 incorporated | Bound command, qualified authoritative operation, protected state/command record and actual incorporated outcome under the Part IV boundary | That the manager received the result, a publication was delivered, or a browser adopted it |
| Required work was durably recorded | C2's protected stable work identity and authoritative atomic/discovery contract, with an admitted recoverable owner | Worker startup, recipient consumption, provider acceptance, or eventual fulfillment |
| Worker can undertake the work | Its separately admitted plan and entry knowledge, trusted acquisition/authorization, active work ownership, installed execution/effect bindings and compatible deployment | That it has already executed or that its provider effect succeeded |
| Physical effect was issued, accepted or fulfilled | Stage-specific source-bound effect/attempt/work identity, registered provider semantics and corresponding actual physical observation | A stronger provider stage, human receipt, or general exactly-once delivery |
| Outstanding work eventually resolves | Appropriate safety/correspondence premises plus complete recovery, permitted discovery, scheduling, provider availability, retry policy and fairness assumptions | Unconditional liveness from a finite successful schedule or a stored outbox row |

The composed application claim preserves the *dependency edges*
between those subjects: C2's publication record, worker acquisition,
issued effect, and reported completion need trustworthy correlation
and source-to-installed-realization correspondence. A certificate
about the authored choreography may be sound while the deployed
worker, route, scheduler, codec or provider binding is absent or
substituted. Part VII requires those actual paths in the assembly;
Parts IX–X own evidence admission and property-specific refinement;
Part XI decides which claims and remaining premises a release
profile requires. The semantic plan does not create an installed
worker, transport route, physical effect or acceptance result by
declaring the same identifier in multiple maps.

An admissible *work-recorded* terminal may be used by a caller
without waiting for a worker that has not yet started, as long as
the separate work and recovery obligation remains accounted for.
If the requested release claim additionally promises physical
fulfillment or conditional eventual delivery, it must include
the relevant installed paths and stronger evidence rather than
silently promoting the review's weaker terminal. Independent
local and worker executions may compose into a justified
business-level property without becoming one fictitious
long-lived Choreo machine.

*** Semantic behavior versus physical realization

A choreography describes semantic behavior.  Physical realization is permitted
to vary when that variation does not change the semantic trace.

For example, the semantic statement:

#+begin_example
:request-authority establishes the result of :request/claim
#+end_example

does not require:

#+begin_example
one fixed Aleph process
one fixed HTTP connection
one fixed DOM node
one fixed browser tab
one fixed database connection
#+end_example

The authority may be realized through different physical resources at different
times.  A retry may reach another host.  A fragment may be replaced by a new DOM
node.  A browser may reconnect.  None of these physical substitutions creates
a new semantic authority or silently changes the meaning of the operation.
Neither does their mere occurrence establish that continuation is safe:
admission must preserve or re-establish the relevant command binding, qualified
authority/incarnation and basis, role/principal rights, execution or physical
resource ownership, and permitted artifact/projection correspondence.

A host restart or changed transport attempt cannot manufacture command
nonincorporation or authorize retry after an unknown outcome. A new DOM
resource cannot inherit the old resource's pending effect permissions merely
because its logical target name is equal. A database with the same authority
label but a different unqualified incarnation cannot be treated as the same
authoritative history. The exact checks are owner- and operation-relative,
not a demand to attach every identity to every semantic event.

This end-state design therefore requires every layer that crosses a
replaceable physical boundary to preserve the logical identities needed by the
semantic layer rather than reconstruct them from physical coincidence.

Detailed multi-node routing belongs to its separately scoped realization
contract.  The semantic requirement established here is only the stronger
and more durable one:

#+begin_quote
A host may realize a role or authority for an execution, but physical hosting
does not create semantic authority.
#+end_quote

*** Physical realization correspondence cases

| Proposed realization | Required evidence or prohibited inference |
|----------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| A deterministic test harness advances all roles together | Useful bounded witness; it does not require a production lockstep coordinator or prove all schedules |
| A command is retried through a different HTTP host after a lost response | Preserve command identity and unresolved incorporation; host replacement does not authorize a duplicate write |
| An HTMX target is replaced before a delayed effect completes | The retired resource loses actuation rights; matching logical target IDs alone do not admit the stale effect |
| A replacement authority reports the same semantic name with different database lineage | Re-establish qualified authority continuity and admissible observations before relying on the old history |
| A projected executable plan is repackaged or deployed elsewhere | Check the admitted artifact/assembly compatibility, principal context and actual execution correspondence; equal plan labels do not suffice |
| A coarse semantic trace hides the notifier's effectful local action, yet an assurance result claims successful external delivery | Require qualified physical issue/fulfillment evidence and an observation alphabet that preserves the asserted effect; global terminal alone does not prove delivery |

These cases define realization and assurance obligations. Listing the
cases is not a proof that physical transport and browser effects refine
the abstract Choreo machine; that stronger judgment requires its own
property, premises, and correspondence evidence.

*** Choreography may be partial with respect to the whole application

One choreography need not describe an application's entire lifetime.

A choreography may describe one distributed semantic operation or one coherent
short-lived protocol.  Durable model facts may connect many such protocols over
hours, days, deployments, or browser lifetimes.

This means that the following are both legitimate:

A short Claim choreography invokes the operation, interprets the authoritative
outcome or preserves uncertainty, settles its local obligations, and terminates
or hands off recovery under the declared contract.

and:

| Execution | Authoritative role in the workflow |
|-----------+------------------------------------|
| E1 | Create the approval request, establish pending state, and settle or hand off its command |
| E2, later | Acquire current approval premises, act on the request, and settle its own command |

The durable relationship between E1 and E2 lives in authoritative model state
unless the application explicitly adopts a durable workflow facility.
The later execution must acquire its own current premises and authority,
not inherit an expired browser execution's knowledge, resource rights,
pending callbacks, or command identity. E1's command may still need
separately owned reconciliation after E1's presentation retires.
The model's durable relation supplies continuity of business state,
not an implicit suspended local program or permission to replay E1.

This separation prevents Gesso from smuggling a universal workflow engine into
ordinary Choreo merely because some business processes span human time.

*** Choreography validity and application realizability are distinct questions

A choreography can be internally valid while the surrounding application fails
to realize a required use of it. An intentionally unexposed operation is not
automatically an assembly defect: required exposure follows the declared
realization scope and independent product acceptance.

For example, a verified operation may still be unusable because:

- no view binds it;
- no projected browser plan is packaged;
- the packaged artifact is stale;
- no transport binding reaches the trusted server;
- the server has no corresponding execution capability;
- no authoritative acquisition path can observe the successor state.

Missing required realization edges are whole-application assembly failures.
Omitting an intended user capability can instead leave the remaining assembly
closed while failing behavioral completeness. Those distinctions follow the
[[#req-application-assurance-claims][three application assurance claims]] in Part I.
Neither failure changes the meaning of choreography validity itself.

An assembly claim is therefore relative to an *admitted required-use scope*,
not every operation merely present in the model. For a declared use, closure
requires the specified correspondence edges across the projection, artifact,
route, execution capability, model operation, settlement, and observation or
recovery path. A route whose name happens to match does not prove the actual
deployed handler or physical outcome; that stronger claim needs separately
qualified evidence. An omitted optional affordance may be acceptable for
assembly closure and nevertheless fail the product's independent capability
acceptance. The checker must report which question it answered.

The distinction is important:

#+begin_example
valid choreography
    !=
complete application realization
#+end_example

and equally:

#+begin_example
HTTP route exists
    !=
valid choreography
#+end_example

The unified design therefore preserves Choreo as a strong semantic layer while
also requiring whole-application checks of the joins between semantic and
physical/application layers, production enforcement of those checks, and
independent capability acceptance. Closure results retain their actual assurance
classification rather than automatically becoming semantic theorems.

*** Construction must fail when the contradiction is already knowable

Choreography construction and verification must reject known contradictions at
the earliest supported boundary possessing enough information to establish them.
Unsupported constructs must be identified explicitly; they must not receive a
successful result by omission from the checker.

Definition-time failures include, for example:

- unknown roles;
- malformed transitions;
- impossible branch references;
- invalid message declarations;
- inconsistent operation metadata;
- use of an undeclared authority;
- a local behavior that requires a fact that the role can never know.

These failures should not be deferred to browser execution merely because a
runtime implementation could eventually notice them.

This does not mean Gesso should pretend to know dynamic domain facts in
advance.  A Request becoming claimed by another user after rendering remains a
runtime/domain event.  Static strictness applies only where the contradiction is
already present in the declared semantic structure. Static admission must
also require declared handling or recovery for dynamic conditions that can
strand a required operation. It can check that an unknown-result path exists
without predicting whether the next network delivery will be indeterminate.

A structural contradiction, unsupported proof obligation, and accepted opaque
contract are different results. Full-language code is admitted through its
declared boundary and assurance policy; a missing role, unresolved operation
binding, or impossible continuation is not waived by calling it an assertion.

A declared recovery branch is not sufficient on its own when an application
requires that branch to be usable: its owner, required knowledge, temporal
and authority scope, executable plan, and necessary physical route or
acquisition must also be checked at the layer that can inspect them. If a
required edge is known missing, report an assembly failure rather than
calling the branch 'conditionally proven'. If the edge exists only under
an uninspected trusted-host premise, retain that premise explicitly rather
than calling it a proven deployment guarantee. Runtime uncertainty remains
possible even after a sound static check of the available paths.

*** Distinct diagnostic and acceptance cases

| Situation | Correct judgment |
|-----------+------------------|
| An operation is well-formed but intentionally unpublished and not a required application use | Not an assembly closure defect; product completeness is judged separately |
| A declared Claim affordance references a missing browser plan or command transport route | Missing required realization edge; application preflight must reject the required assembly |
| A graph branch reads a value never definitely available to its owning role | Static choreography knowledge/use failure under the admitted entry contract |
| A correctly declared operation races with another actor's Claim | Dynamic authoritative outcome and declared recovery; static validation must not invent rejection or success |
| A =:commit-unknown= route names reconciliation but has no usable registered recovery capability | Reject required-path assembly when checkable; otherwise retain an explicit open binding premise |
| A preflight report agrees with supplied route declarations but no deployed-handler adoption evidence exists | Report bounded source/declaration closure, not a certified installed-server correspondence |
| E2 begins after E1 completed, using a current authorization read | Valid new execution with its own admission; E1's resource/callback rights do not transfer |

The checks can fail at definition, projection, application assembly,
startup or authoritative runtime depending on which trusted facts are
available there. An accepted opaque contract does not override a
known contradiction. A finite local check must not be promoted to a
global proof, and a structurally complete application must not be
called behaviorally complete without its independent acceptance evidence.

*** Canonical authoring principle

Application authors should state semantic intent once whenever the system can
derive the rest.

A choreography should not require synchronized copies of the same semantic fact
in:

- operation metadata;
- view metadata;
- browser-plan names;
- transport declarations;
- route strings;
- server registries;
- bootstrap registries.

Where an independent physical choice genuinely exists, it may be declared
separately and checked for correspondence.  Otherwise the preferred direction
is:

One semantic declaration supplies the derived local plan, binding metadata,
transport requirements, and diagnostics. Independently chosen realization
parameters are checked against that declaration.

This principle does not collapse all layers into one macro.  It reduces
duplicated sources of semantic truth while preserving explicit architectural
boundaries.

"Declare once" is relative to the *fact being declared*, not a demand for
one universal application map. The public model owns business invariants,
authorization dependencies and the actual effect of its operations. The
choreography owns role-relative coordination and admitted continuations.
The view owns the authored presentation choice; the application realization
owns genuinely independent physical routes, resource bindings and deployment
choices. A generated plan, handler summary or action descriptor is a derived
view of these sources, not a rival authority for any of them.

For each generated edge, the system must retain its source and applicable
version/identity, distinguish a mechanically derived fact from an imported
trusted contract, and report unknown or unsupported derivation separately
from a failed check. Opaque Clojure business code cannot become a proven policy
merely because its operation name was imported. A hand-copied declaration
with matching strings is not an acceptable substitute for an established
source-to-assembly correspondence.


HumanHelp's Claim action gives a useful target for that authoring principle
because the same semantic intent must eventually appear in a view, browser plan,
transport, trusted handler, model operation, settlement, and authoritative
reacquisition path.

A declaration-first surface separates the helper's inputs from authoritative
obligations and explicitly maps model outcomes into presentation behavior:

#+begin_src clojure
(def request-claim
  {:operation/id :request/claim
   :roles
   {:helper {:principal :user :actor :client}
    :request-authority {:authority :request}}
   :input [:map {:closed true} [:request/id uuid?]]
   :entry
   {:helper {:values #{:request/id}
             :observation-for-optimism :request/card}}
   :invocation
   {:authority-role :request-authority
    :model-operation :request/claim
    :arguments {:request-id [:input :request/id]}
    :principal-source :trusted-authentication
    :command-protocol :stable-bound-command}
   :provisional
   {:role :helper
    :projection :request/claiming
    :owner-contract :current-execution-target-and-resource}
   :settlement-contract :request/claim-settlement})
#+end_src

The model-operation reference imports its authoritative interface. Its current
Request, Membership, Organization policy, and Request/Organization association
are protected by the new-incorporation contract; those are not facts the browser
is expected to establish. In particular, current Membership, Request,
Organization, and Claim authority premises remain actual model-owned
dependencies; keeping them behind an imported public operation does
not remove their transaction-current guard obligations. The checked import must
identify the source-owned operation and applicable contract revision, including
which rules are inspected or still opaque. An authorized observed card supports
only the declared optimistic presentation. A non-optimistic invocation can have
a different entry contract without claiming that same provisional behavior.

The admitted command boundary allocates a stable =:gesso/command-id= before the
first possible send and binds it to the semantic operation, arguments, principal,
and authority scope. It preserves that identity through retries. A browser may
carry the bytes, but command authority comes from the trusted binding and model
protocol, not from choosing a UUID.

The settlement contract is inspectable as follows:

| Admitted result | Presentation settlement | Required next path |
|-----------------+-------------------------+--------------------|
| Successful =:committed= | =:accepted= | Acquire the permitted authoritative successor |
| Permitted =:already-incorporated= with recorded success | =:accepted= | Same successor path, with no new incorporation |
| Authoritative =:rejected= | =:rejected= | Settle provisional ownership and permit authoritative card reacquisition |
| =:commit-unknown= or indeterminate delivery | =:unknown= | Preserve command identity and reconcile against command-specific outcome evidence |

Denied disclosure, unavailable acquisition, and an expired or incompatible
recovery context also have declared paths. They do not become fabricated
success or rejection. A reconciliation response may resolve a command while a
later read is still needed to determine the current card state.

The view refers to the operation and supplies only its domain arguments:

#+begin_src clojure
(defn request-card
  [{:request/keys [id title status]}]
  [:article.request-card
   {:data-request-id id}
   [:h3 title]
   [:p.status (name status)]
   (when (= status :open)
     (gesso/action
       {:operation :request/claim
        :args {:request/id id}}
       "Claim"))])
#+end_src

The rendering boundary supplies the admitted target and observation context;
the raw domain map and the conditional button are not authorization. The action
constructor resolves the operation's admitted binding and derives the necessary
plan, transport, and runtime descriptors. User-visible absence of the button
does not enforce the model guard.

A generated helper plan has these obligations:

| Concern | Derived or imported requirement |
|---------+---------------------------------|
| Entry | Selected Request id and the observation required for this optimistic mode |
| Command | Stable command reference with the admitted operation/argument binding |
| Provisional display | Current execution, target, principal context, and concrete resource ownership |
| Result intake | Expected operation and command, admitted source, permitted result, current owner, and settlement mapping |
| Recovery | Unknown remains unknown until reconciliation; reacquisition observes the declared scope and progression |

Command identity alone is not enough to own a provisional DOM effect. A later
execution may reconcile the same command while the earlier execution's callback
has lost all rights to mutate the current target.

Only independent physical choices need separate authoring:

#+begin_src clojure
(assembly/realize-operation
  humanhelp-assembly
  {:operation :request/claim
   :transport {:kind :http/post
               :route :route/request-claim}
   :model-binding request-model/claim-operation})
#+end_src

The assembly derives and checks the view/action, browser-plan, server-entry,
decoder, model, and settlement joins from the declaration and these bindings.
It must not require the author to copy the generated plan name back into a
separate bootstrap registry. Repeated metadata may be an inspection view or
an explicit correspondence constraint, but is not an independent source of truth.

These code blocks specify *authoring and realization contracts*. The names
=gesso/action=, =assembly/realize-operation= and
=assembly/require-dispatched-binding!= are illustrative; their spelling
is not a separate normative requirement. A plan registry or preflight
check that proves a narrow structural join cannot simply be renamed into
an end-to-end source-derived executable binder. The admitted authoring
surface either derives the promised edge from checked source material or
exposes the independently authored binding and its explicit open
correspondence premise.

A schematic trusted handler illustrates the authority handoff:

#+begin_src clojure
(defn request-claim-handler [ctx request]
  (let [binding (assembly/require-dispatched-binding!
                  ctx :route/request-claim)
        principal (auth/current-principal! (:session ctx))
        trusted-ctx (assoc ctx :principal principal)
        command (server/decode-bound-command!
                  binding trusted-ctx request)]
    (server/invoke-bound-model-operation!
      binding trusted-ctx command)))
#+end_src

The dispatched binding comes from the admitted route, not a route name supplied
by the browser. Authentication precedes any binding that claims an authenticated
principal. The decoder checks the envelope and command reference under the
operation contract; the public model boundary independently validates the
authoritative command binding and current permission to disclose a replay.
For new incorporation it protects all current guard dependencies and effects
atomically with the command record. Separate handler pre-reads cannot supply
that atomicity or reject a compatible replay merely because Claim succeeded
earlier and the Request is now claimed.

The handler also uses the admitted response and recovery mapping. A response
rendering failure after incorporation leaves the recorded command result intact
and may leave the browser uncertain. Part IV owns the concrete transaction;
Parts VI and VII own presentation and application construction.

The application author should not have to synchronize:

#+begin_src clojure
;; Avoid this style of duplicated semantic naming.

{:button-operation "claim"
 :button-plan "claim-v9"
 :hx-post "/requests/claim"
 :wire-command "__gesso_live_optimistic_command"
 :handler-var 'humanhelp.routes/claim
 :server-capability "claim-handler"
 :model-op "request/claim"
 :settlement-fragment "request-card"}
#+end_src

when those values are all consequences of one declared semantic operation.
Where the physical route or capability name genuinely remains independently
authored, assembly construction should check its correspondence rather than
hoping the strings stay synchronized.

This example also makes choreography validity and whole-application realization
visibly different. The choreography elaborated from =request-claim= can be valid while the
=:request/card= view omits the affordance, while the current browser artifact
omits =:plan/request-claim=, or while the route is not bound into the current
assembly. Where those edges are required by the declared realization scope,
those are application-realization failures. If the operation is intentionally not exposed,
its absence is not automatically a closure defect. Whether the intended product
capability is present remains an independent acceptance question, not a reason
to weaken the operation contract.

*** Authoring and assembly acceptance cases

| Apparent construction | Required treatment |
|-----------------------+--------------------|
| A view uses =:request/claim= but the corresponding required browser plan is absent or stale | Reject the required realization edge at the appropriate build/startup boundary; no silent unverified HTMX fallback |
| A server route string matches the declared operation but the actual deployed handler is uninspected | Preserve installed-handler correspondence as an explicit premise, not a certificate based on naming |
| A copied operation summary omits Membership or Organization claim guards | Reject an incompatible checked interface where inspectable; otherwise retain the original model-owned policy obligation |
| A browser selects a command id, actor role or claimed principal | Carry the bytes as inputs only; admitted trusted binding and current model guards establish authority |
| A rendered card omits the optional Claim affordance | Do not invent an assembly defect unless required by its admitted realization scope; product capability acceptance remains independent |
| An optimistic callback completes after its DOM owner retires, while reconciliation still recognizes the command | Historical command identity can remain valid; the retired callback acquires no new presentation rights |
| A static surface snapshot passes but another path returns unvalidated raw HTML | Report the actual preflight coverage and open canonical-handler/deployed-render premises, not universal affordance completeness |

These are target design obligations. Static semantic construction, an
assembled PlanRegistry, validation of supplied rendered surfaces, physically
installed Biff handlers, and real command/DOM effects have distinct
evidence boundaries. The authoring system must not hide a missing boundary
by inventing a derived registry, accepting unchecked metadata as proof,
or silently downgrading the operation.

** Choreographic operation types
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-choreographic-operation-contract
:END:

A choreographic operation type is richer than an ordinary function type.

An ordinary function type is roughly:

#+begin_example
A -> B
#+end_example

A choreographic operation type describes an entry into a distributed protocol
and the valid continuations from that entry.

It may include:

- ordinary value schemas;
- evolving values and their basis;
- roles participating in the operation;
- principal and authority requirements;
- facts known by each role on entry;
- provenance justifying those facts;
- facts on which roles agree;
- resources required by operations;
- facts and values produced;
- possible outcomes;
- provisional execution permissions;
- authority transitions;
- continuation requirements;
- compatibility/version requirements where a suspended projection may outlive a
  deployment.

The operation type should *not* duplicate the complete choreography by manually
listing every branch already represented by the choreography.

The choreography is the source of truth for control flow.

The type declaration supplies external semantic commitments that cannot or
should not be inferred, while analysis derives as much as possible from the
actual choreography.

An admitted operation type therefore joins several different judgments,
rather than representing one large schema:

- *value and presence*: input/output shapes, required versus optional
  values, and the distinction between absence, nil, and a present false;
- *knowledge and provenance*: which role can justify using each fact at
  entry and at each use, not merely which keys appear in the global store;
- *authority and resources*: the bound principal and public model-operation
  interface, plus the resource and ownership permissions needed for effects;
- *control and settlement*: permitted semantic results, role-local
  continuations, and required recovery when an outcome remains uncertain;
- *compatibility*: which source contract, projection, artifact, and version
  conditions allow an already-started execution to continue.

The choreography body supplies derivable branches and value flow. The public
model operation owns its dynamic guard and authoritative effects. An external
operation type may import the model's exposed contract, but may not fabricate
proof of source Clojure predicates, transaction-current enforcement, or
deployed handler correspondence from a matching operation keyword. A checked
judgment identifies the exact supported language and which obligations were
proved, runtime-enforced, asserted, or remain open.

For example, the declaration may state:

#+begin_src clojure
{::choreo/roles
 {:helper
  {:principal :user
   :actor :client}

  :request-authority
  {:authority :request}}

 ::choreo/inputs
 {:request-id
  {:schema :uuid
   :known-by #{:helper}
   :provenance :input}

  :observed-basis
  {:schema ObservedBasisClaim
   :known-by #{:helper}
   :provenance :input}}

 ::choreo/authority
 {:request-authority #{:request/claim}}

 ::choreo/optimism
 {:role :helper
  :projection :request/claim}}
#+end_src

This example declares role requirements, input knowledge, and the intended
optimistic realization. It does not grant claim authority to whoever sends a
matching role label. Trusted server admission must establish the concrete
principal, subject, and operation premises. The helper's input knowledge of an
=ObservedBasisClaim= is knowledge of the claimed token, not evidence that the
helper acquired the observation it names. A schema check on that token can
establish its shape, not its issuer, observation coverage, progression, or
permission to use it as a trusted basis. If a later boundary needs an acquired
=:request/card= observation for optimism, a browser-submitted token alone
cannot satisfy that acquisition premise.

The names =ObservedBasisClaim= and =AuthoritativeBasis= are conceptual semantic
categories.  They do not require two unrelated physical token formats, nor do
they require a particular Clojure nominal-type implementation.  The browser may
carry the same underlying XTDB token bytes that trusted code later compares or
uses to construct a consistency requirement, while the semantic status of those
values remains different.

XTDB-specific representation belongs in the XTDB/Live consistency adapter rather
than being permanently baked into the generic Choreo type vocabulary.

The verifier derives knowledge changes, branch requirements, and continuation
structure from the choreography body. Where a claim depends on an external
model guard, opaque derivation, registered runtime producer, or physical
effect, the checker must retain the corresponding obligation rather than
inventing a derivation from control-flow structure.

*** Operation type admission counterexamples

| Apparent well-typed input | Required classification |
|---------------------------+-------------------------|
| The browser supplies a valid UUID-shaped principal or command identifier | Shape is not authenticated principal or admitted stable command binding |
| The browser supplies a well-formed observed-basis token | An input claim is not a qualified authoritative observation or fresh progression witness |
| A role branches on a declared fact available only on another role's path | Reject unsupported role-local use, even if the global value schema accepts the fact |
| A projected outcome is =:accepted= while authoritative incorporation remains =:commit-unknown= | Preserve the unknown command-specific continuation; presentation cannot manufacture commit evidence |
| A valid operation schema refers to an absent resource or retired DOM owner | A type-shape check does not authorize physical actuation or restore resource rights |
| A suspended execution resumes against a changed contract with the same operation keyword | Require admitted contract/artifact/version compatibility; same name does not validate continuation |
| A typed model operation declares a business invariant but its executable guard is opaque or uninspected | Retain an implementation/policy premise; static schema typing does not prove current model correctness |

A report should identify the failing boundary or open premise: malformed
value, missing role knowledge, unauthenticated provenance, unauthorized
operation, unsupported continuation, or unqualified physical realization.
These categories must not collapse into one successful "typed" verdict.
Finite projection or typed-rule checks may discharge their named properties
without being promoted into an end-to-end physical certification.

The exact Clojure syntax remains tentative. These semantics are the intended
contract.

*** Operation identity is semantic, not transport identity

A choreographic operation has a stable semantic identity that is distinct from
the mechanism used to invoke it.

For example:

#+begin_example
:request/claim
#+end_example

is not synonymous with:

#+begin_example
POST /requests/123/claim
#+end_example

nor with:

#+begin_example
button id="claim-request"
#+end_example

nor with:

#+begin_example
browser plan index 17
#+end_example

The transport, view, and executable-plan coordinates are realizations of the
semantic operation.  They may change across refactors or deployments without
changing the operation's meaning, provided compatibility rules permit the
change.

This distinction is required so that application closure can later verify
correspondence instead of mistaking physical equality for semantic identity.

A stable operation name is also distinct from the normalized semantic identity
of its contract. Keeping =:request/claim= unchanged while changing a relied-upon
authority rule, input requirement, outcome, or postcondition does not preserve
dependent validity merely because the name stayed the same. Conversely, a
transport refactor need not change the semantic contract if its checked
realization and compatibility obligations remain satisfied.

These identities have different continuity rules. The semantic operation name
identifies the intended capability; the source-owned contract and its applicable
version/fingerprint bind the dependencies on which a particular use relies.
A =CommandId= denotes one bound intention across compatible retries, whereas
an =ExecutionId= denotes one execution attempt, and a role, principal, actor,
authority, and host each answer different questions. An executable-plan digest
identifies exact artifact content, not the model rule it invokes or the
deployment that actually installed it. The compatibility checker must compare
the relevant *source contract, admitted realization and required continuation*,
not merely equality of any one of these names or hashes.

A fingerprint or digest can support exact content comparison only within its
specified canonicalization and source-binding contract. It does not establish
semantic correctness, issuer authenticity, model authorization, or physical
deployment adoption by itself. Keeping a command identifier stable while
changing its principal, operation, arguments, authority scope or incompatible
contract is not a compatible replay just because the raw identifier matches.

*** Semantic categories at operation boundaries

Operation APIs must preserve distinctions whose accidental substitution would
violate a semantic invariant even when the underlying Clojure representations
are identical or structurally compatible.

Representative distinctions include:

- =ObservedBasisClaim= versus trusted =AuthoritativeBasis=;
- semantic =CommandId= versus runtime =ExecutionId=;
- provisional versus authoritative observations;
- raw declarations versus checker-admitted declarations;
- raw certificate data versus checker-accepted certificate evidence.

The requirement is semantic non-interchangeability through the supported API.
The design does not mandate that every such category be implemented as a
=defrecord=, Java class, wrapper object, or other nominal runtime type.  Permitted
realizations include:

- nominal or opaque host-language values;
- smart constructors with inaccessible or conventionally private construction;
- checker-produced admitted values;
- explicit tagged data whose use is guarded by the appropriate admission path;
- another admitted representation that prevents accidental
  semantic-category substitution.

The choice should be the smallest mechanism that makes the wrong ordinary call
difficult and the resulting diagnostic precise.  A representation technique is
not valuable merely because it looks more typed.

Ordinary downstream application code should normally author domain values and
semantic declarations, not manually wrap every UUID, basis token, command, or
projection in framework ceremony.  Gesso should perform category establishment
at elaboration, checking, authoritative acquisition, model, or runtime
boundaries wherever it can derive the distinction itself.

For example, a useful public failure is conceptually:

#+begin_example
boundary:                trusted claim preparation requiring an acquired basis
requires:                AuthoritativeBasis for the declared scope
received:                unadmitted ObservedBasisClaim
missing step:            trusted authoritative acquisition / admission
#+end_example

rather than accepting the same-shaped value and relying on a comment to explain
why the caller should not have done so. The diagnostic applies at the boundary
that requires the acquired basis. A command intake may legitimately accept an
=ObservedBasisClaim= as weaker context; it must not demand that the browser
manufacture server-trusted authority before submitting a command.

The category and admission rules are defined in
[[#req-semantic-admission][Part II]]. When the supplied category cannot be
established, diagnostics report an unknown or unadmitted value rather than
trusting a client-supplied category label.

A nominal host-language value also does not retain trusted status merely because
it was serialized and reconstructed.  Crossing an adversarial wire boundary
returns the value to an untrusted representation until the relevant verifier,
authenticator, decoder, or trusted acquisition path re-establishes the stronger
semantic category.

Category establishment is scoped to the evidence issuer, subject, operation,
time/context, and consumer boundary that rely on it. For example, a cooperating
JVM may hold a checker-issued, identity-bound structural admission handle; a
copied description or a deserialized lookalike is neither that live handle nor
proof of a theorem. Even the live structural handle attests only its named
structural check, not production authorization. Likewise, a shape-valid tagged
principal returned from an untrusted wire must be authenticated again, and an
apparently valid authoritative progression witness requires the trusted
authority's actual ordering judgment. API ergonomics should hide this work
from ordinary application authors where Gesso can perform it automatically,
without turning a raw or stale value into a trusted one.

*** Identity and admission counterexamples

| Apparent correspondence | Required treatment |
|-------------------------+--------------------|
| Two calls use the same =:request/claim= keyword after the model policy changed | Compare the relied-upon semantic contract and compatibility obligations; a name alone is insufficient |
| A retry keeps its =CommandId= but changes arguments or the bound principal | Reject incompatible binding; the identifier does not grant permission to incorporate again |
| An =ExecutionId= matches a command's raw UUID, or a host has the same name as an authority | Keep the categories distinct; neither coincidence changes semantic identity or authority |
| A browser sends a tagged =Principal= or an =AuthoritativeBasis= looking map | Validate shape separately from trusted authentication or scoped observation acquisition |
| A serialized copy of a structural checker handle or report carries the expected fingerprint | Do not grant live admission, theorem truth, certificate status, or application permission |
| Two executable plans share an admitted content digest but one host runs an unverified handler | Exact artifact content is not installed handler/physical execution correspondence |
| A once-qualified resource or basis witness is reused for another owner, scope, or authority incarnation | Require requalification under the receiving boundary; stale category evidence does not transfer rights |

These cases distinguish structural mismatch, incompatible contract, missing
trusted origin, stale scope, and unproved physical implementation. The end-state
authoring API should report the missing establishment step and owning boundary,
not force developers to fabricate wrapper values or allow an untrusted label
to satisfy the requirement.

This is primarily an accidental-misuse boundary inside cooperating application
code.  It does not claim that deliberately hostile trusted host code is unable
to bypass Gesso, import lower-level implementation namespaces, or directly use a
database capability it already possesses.

*** Operation contracts export a semantic interface

An operation type should expose a normalized semantic interface containing only
facts that downstream reasoning is allowed to rely on.

Depending on the operation, that interface may include:

- input value types;
- required entry knowledge;
- required provenance;
- role participation;
- principal/authority constraints;
- model operation identity;
- possible semantic outcomes;
- values/facts produced;
- authority changes;
- resource requirements;
- provisional permissions;
- settlement requirements;
- continuation compatibility;
- named assumptions and guarantees.

It should not include arbitrary implementation details merely because those
details are easy to serialize.

A supported exported interface must bind each relied-upon commitment to its
source-owned contract and exact semantic subject, including applicable
revision or compatibility conditions. It distinguishes derived choreography
facts from imported public-model rules and from explicit trusted or opaque
assumptions. A caller must not treat a source name, matching fingerprint,
structurally valid summary, or an uninspected guard as proof of physical
implementation or of the original policy. Derived facts may be used only
within the verifier's actual supported language and property scope.

For Claim, the normalized interface identifies the helper's actual entry
knowledge, the trusted principal/authority handoff, the public model operation,
and its result contract. It separates four *boundary-facing command-resolution
categories*. Their epistemic status and incorporation implications differ:

| Category | Interface meaning |
|----------+-------------------|
| =:committed= | Qualified authoritative evidence that this exact bound command incorporated and has a recorded outcome |
| =:rejected= | A justified rejection of this command under the operation contract, rather than an inference from missing delivery or another actor's result |
| =:already-incorporated= | Compatible repetition of the same bound command with a permitted recorded outcome; no new incorporation |
| =:commit-unknown= | This observer cannot establish the command's disposition from currently admissible evidence; **not** a fourth durable database status |

The payload states the command binding, disclosed disposition, and any scoped
observation or progression evidence. A successful callback or HTTP response is
not sufficient to construct one of these outcomes. =:commit-unknown= denotes
uncertainty of the observing boundary, not a third database truth between
committed and uncommitted. It may arise from indeterminate delivery even when
the authoritative transaction has a definite result.

=:confirmed= and =:reconciled= may describe protocol events if explicitly defined;
they are not substitute names for the canonical command-resolution categories.
The presentation mapping to =:accepted=, =:rejected=, and =:unknown= remains an
explicit checked contract. A recorded result does not imply permission to
disclose it. In particular, compatible =:already-incorporated= resolution
requires the original command binding, the authorized recorded disposition,
and current disclosure permission; it must not re-run the business operation
or be rejected merely because the previously successful operation changed
the Request's status. =:commit-unknown= requires command-specific
reconciliation or another declared recovery path, not an arbitrary fresh
command ID or a fabricated =:rejected= outcome.

The semantic interface does not encode incidental realization details such as:

#+begin_example
specific Ring handler var
specific Reitit route index
specific DOM node id
specific XTDB Java class
specific browser bundle offset
#+end_example

Those belong to later realization layers unless independently required by
runtime semantics.

*** External commitments versus derived structure

Operation declarations should state only semantic commitments that cannot or
should not be safely inferred.

The verifier/projector should derive:

- reachable continuations;
- behavior-sensitive branch obligations;
- knowledge propagation caused by declared communication;
- terminal paths;
- local plan structure;
- resource obligations that follow from the body.

It should not require the author to restate those facts manually in metadata.

Redundant declarations are dangerous because each copy may remain individually
plausible while disagreeing with the others.

The preferred rule is:

#+begin_quote
Declare external semantic commitments; derive internal control-flow facts from
the choreography body.
#+end_quote

For each exported fact, a checked interface should distinguish:

- =:derived=: obtained from the exact admitted choreography and the named
  structural, knowledge or projection check actually performed;
- =:imported=: supplied by the specified public-model or authority contract,
  retaining its source binding and any uninspected predicates;
- =:runtime-required=: requiring a concrete authenticated principal, current
  guarded model transition, actual message receipt, resource ownership or
  qualified authoritative acquisition;
- =:assumed/open=: a typed external premise or unsupported proof/physical
  correspondence, still visible to every downstream consumer that relies on
  it.

These are specification-level evidence classes, not a claim that current
Gesso already exposes this exact record format. No category grants trust by
being written into caller-controlled metadata. Where a composition includes
several operations, each imported guard, invariant premise and read/write
footprint remains attributable to its original source; possible shared
state interference is not evidence that transitions commute or execute
atomically together.

*** Exported-interface counterexamples

| Apparent interface success | Required judgment |
|----------------------------+-------------------|
| A role-local verifier proves that a branch selector is definitely available | Preserve that specific knowledge/availability result; it does not prove the opaque selector producer's policy or the authenticated caller |
| A successful HTTP callback says Claim was accepted | Require command-bound authoritative disposition evidence; callback success is not incorporation proof |
| A duplicate Claim presents the same command ID but a different principal or arguments | Reject incompatible binding rather than treating it as =:already-incorporated= |
| A prior successful command is replayed after its Request changed state | Resolve from a compatible recorded command under current disclosure; do not impose new-incorporation guards on historical resolution |
| A network timeout has no readable command record | Retain =:commit-unknown= or a qualified recovery state; silence does not prove rejection or safety of a new command |
| Two operation interfaces have compatible types and overlapping read/write sets | Preserve interference and open concurrency assumptions; structural composition is not serializability or noninterference proof |
| A report repeats a model rule or invariant name but has not inspected its implementation | Retain the original source-owned opaque/physical premise; a summary is not a policy certificate |

The interface must remain suitable for independently checked composition
without converting unknown provenance, an assumed predicate, or a physical
deployment gap into a guarantee. Downstream diagnostics should identify
which specific imported or derived claim cannot be established.

*** Availability is not authorization

An operation may be present in a model or choreography registry without being
currently executable by a particular principal.

Likewise, a prepared or bound operation may be rendered as an affordance without
becoming durable authorization.

These distinctions must remain explicit:

| Established fact | What it does not establish by itself |
|------------------+-------------------------------------|
| Operation exists | Its use is valid at this choreography state |
| Use is structurally valid at this state | The current principal is authorized now |
| View renders an affordance | The browser has authority over protected state |

The trusted side re-establishes dynamic authorization at execution and protects
the relevant model premises at the authoritative transition boundary where
intervening changes matter. A rendered affordance, a successful earlier check,
and a valid operation contract do not reserve future eligibility.

This separation lets Gesso reason strongly about operation identity and
distributed protocol structure without pretending to statically prove current
business facts that can change between render and commit. A valid source-level
operation interface also does not attest that its named handler, current
authenticated principal, or transactional guard was actually installed and
enforced. An admission diagnostic must identify whether it checked
choreography structure, current model authorization, or the physical join;
an unavailable independent premise is not a successful authorization.

*** Malformed operation type versus wrong business policy

The unified design distinguishes structural/type malformedness from product
correctness.

If a declaration says that =:request/status= is an enum and a rule attempts:

#+begin_src clojure
(< (:request/status request) 10)
#+end_src

the expression is malformed relative to its semantic types and should be
rejected.

If the application states:

#+begin_src clojure
(= (:request/status request) :open)
#+end_src

when product policy actually intended another valid status test, the expression
may be perfectly well formed while encoding the wrong policy.

Gesso should make the former difficult or impossible to certify *within the
supported typed rule language*. It cannot infer the latter merely from syntax.
The two Clojure-shaped examples illustrate the semantic distinction;
they do not imply that arbitrary host-language Clojure expressions are
automatically translated into a closed proof language. The admitted typed
rule language has explicit reader types and operators, so a numeric
ordering comparison against a nominal enum fails admission under
the corresponding type discipline. Arbitrary Clojure policy functions instead retain
an opaque typed/effect contract unless their relevant source is elaborated
into an inspected language or checked by another admitted mechanism.

This distinction becomes more explicit in the later compositional-proof part,
where typed business-rule expressions and certified semantic interfaces are
defined in detail.  It is already a language-design constraint here because
Choreo must not confuse "type checks" with "is the policy humans intended."

*** A valid rule may still be inadmissible at a distributed program point

Intrinsic rule validity is not enough.

For example:

#+begin_example
rule:
    (= (:request/private-review request) :approved)

intrinsic type:
    Boolean
    VALID

browser use before browser knows :request/private-review:
    INVALID AT THIS PROGRAM POINT
#+end_example

A distributed use of a rule therefore creates obligations beyond ordinary type
checking, including:

- the role knows every required fact;
- required provenance is available;
- trust or authority requirements are satisfied;
- optional values have been refined before use;
- authoritative-basis requirements are met where the rule depends on
  authoritative observation.

This is one of the central reasons Choreo needs a semantic type/knowledge layer
richer than ordinary function typing. A global value store's containing a
binding does not make it definitely available to the chosen role on every
incoming path. Nor do a claimed authoritative basis and a correctly typed
provenance record prove the observation was actually acquired or permits this
use. A role may depend on an optional value only after its applicable presence
refinement. A program point may therefore be rejected even when its predicate
is intrinsically well typed and its runtime inputs would happen to be present
in one favorable execution.

*** Operation declarations should remain inspectable data

The ordinary authoring surface may eventually include macros or helpers, but the
semantic representation after elaboration should remain explicit enough to:

- inspect;
- normalize;
- verify;
- project;
- fingerprint semantically;
- explain on failure;
- compare against dependent declarations.

Convenient syntax must not create an opaque second semantics.

Normalization must preserve the information needed to identify the owning
declaration and explain failed obligations without making source coordinates
part of semantic identity when they are diagnostic-only. The compiler or
elaborator must not synthesize an undeclared trusted assertion simply to make a
knowledge, authority, or continuation obligation pass.

The desired direction is:

Author-facing syntax elaborates into explicit normalized semantic data. That
same subject supports verification, projection, diagnostics, and certification;
none may silently interpret a different policy hidden in the syntax layer.
The checked normalization retains the owning source and the identity of the
semantic contract, even if locations and pretty source snippets remain
diagnostic-only rather than contributing to semantic identity. A generated
plan, copied checker report, or friendly syntax cannot independently become
an authoritative second definition of a business rule.

*** Operation-admission diagnostic classes

| Classification | Evidence and response |
|----------------+-----------------------|
| =:invalid= | A contradiction is established in the admitted syntax, type, value-flow, role knowledge, or stated interface; reject at the earliest competent boundary |
| =:unsupported= | The construct is outside the checker’s supported language or bounded capacity; do not report a successful check or silently discard it |
| =:open-premise= | A needed model/authority, external Clojure, runtime producer, or physical deployment correspondence was not established; preserve the premise and the owning boundary |
| =:runtime-required= | A declared dynamic fact such as authenticated eligibility, current transaction state, or resource ownership must be established when the relevant action occurs |
| =:runtime-rejected= | An actual guarded operation was rejected by its trusted boundary; do not reinterpret this as a static type failure |
| =:indeterminate= | Delivery or outcome evidence is insufficient; retain command-specific uncertainty and a permitted recovery path instead of inventing rejection |
| =:policy-mismatch= | Product policy is allegedly wrong despite type-correct syntax; require a source-owned rule, product acceptance criterion, or counterexample rather than pretending the type checker knows intent |

These names describe required diagnostic distinctions; an API may
expose them using an equivalent typed representation rather than
these literal enum keywords. A useful report identifies
the declaration and semantic subject, the role and program point where
relevant, required versus available knowledge/provenance, the supported
checker property, the failing or open premise, and the correct next
construction, runtime, or product-acceptance boundary.

*** Admission counterexamples

| Apparent success | Required outcome |
|------------------+------------------|
| A nominal status enum is passed to a numeric comparison in the admitted typed-rule AST | Reject the malformed type use, without claiming arbitrary Clojure compilation was analyzed |
| A well-typed predicate tests =:open= but product policy intended another permitted status | Type checking may succeed; the policy-intent discrepancy needs independent rule/acceptance evidence |
| A browser branch reads a private fact known only to the server | Reject that role-local use; global value availability does not grant browser knowledge |
| An optional receive field was present on one path but omitted on another | Require path-specific presence refinement before unconditional use |
| An input claims an authoritative basis with the expected schema | Do not upgrade a claimed token to a trusted acquired observation on shape alone |
| A valid choreography has a declared but physically uninstalled required command handler | Report the missing implementation/assembly premise, not successful physical admission |
| A command times out after being sent | Preserve the indeterminate outcome and reconciliation contract; do not classify timeout as a rejected business rule |

A failed supported check, an unsupported check, an open external obligation,
and a genuine runtime rejection must remain separate. None can be laundered
into success through a structural fingerprint, a synthesized assertion, or an
exception path that swallows a checker limitation.

*** Full-language computations retain their contracts

An action reference binds a semantic interface and its concrete realization.
The interface distinguishes the role that executes it, required scoped inputs,
declared outputs, effects, failure/uncertainty alternatives, resource use, and
applicable termination or progress assumptions. The implementation may be an
ordinary Clojure function, a supported CLJC/CLJS realization, or a declared
external effect. JVM code is not automatically executable in a browser; target
placement and communication remain checked obligations.

For inspectable typed expressions, elaboration derives required inputs and
produced bindings. For opaque code, the declared footprint and functional
contract remain explicit premises unless another mechanism establishes them.
The framework should derive ordinary plumbing while avoiding duplicate manual
inventories; it must not claim to discover all effects of unrestricted Clojure.

The executable machine yields a declared effect request and later admits its
completion or failure. It does not run arbitrary application functions while
canonicalizing a declaration, checking its shape, or computing an artifact
fingerprint. Callback execution belongs to an explicit realization boundary.

A realization can report completion only for the pending declared effect,
through the machine's admitted event path and the applicable owner-relative
execution or resource binding. It cannot directly edit a projected machine,
select a different continuation, introduce undeclared outputs, or treat a
previously retired callback as current. The receiver must enforce the
operation's applicable output, identity, provenance and runtime guard contract;
a correctly shaped callback map is not that enforcement. An uncertain physical
effect retains its declared unknown/recovery alternative rather than becoming a
fabricated failure or a second automatic invocation.

Validating an output and recording its dependency list does not prove that the
implementation calculated the promised result. Runtime validation, instance
observations, checked derivations, and asserted contracts retain the evidence
classes established in Parts I and II. A named local result cannot become
authoritative solely because the caller supplied an =:authoritative= label.

Nontermination, blocking, nondeterminism, and external effects are expressible
but affect the available claims. If a required continuation depends on such an
action finishing, the plan needs an applicable completion premise, enforceable
bound, isolation/cancellation contract, or recovery path. A timeout callback in
the same blocked event loop does not by itself enforce a bound on synchronous
code. No universal totality proof is required for the host language.

*** Definite values, knowledge, and provenance are separate analyses

The abstract global value store describes values in the specification; it is
not a shared runtime context and does not make those values known to every role.
Entry contracts establish role-local facts with their actual provenance. A
low-level test fixture that gives all roles the same input set is not the
ordinary application entry policy.

The verifier computes at least these distinct judgments:

| Analysis | Question |
|----------+----------|
| Value availability | Is the required binding defined on every applicable path? |
| Role-local knowledge | Does the consuming role have a permitted knowledge path for that binding? |
| Provenance and support | Do its subject, scope, observation, and retained premises justify this use? |
| Optional/presence refinement | Has this path established presence and the required variant before use? |
| Causal eligibility | Has the predecessor observation or result required by this continuation been established? |

A forward must-analysis is a useful baseline. At a join, unconditional facts
must be justified on every reachable incoming path. Each environment-event or
receive alternative contributes only its own established fields. Optional fields
are not definitely present merely because one alternative can supply them.
Path-sensitive refinements may establish stronger facts on selected edges.

Agreement on a value at a join does not erase differing assumptions. The result
retains the appropriate support for each path, or a conservative combined
premise, without upgrading an asserted path to a proved one. Unreachable edges
may be excluded only under the checker mechanism that establishes their
unreachability; a branch being unlikely is insufficient.

Bindings have explicit version and scope semantics. An implementation may use
fresh immutable binding identities or a checked replacement discipline. Reusing
one context key for a new value invalidates support that depended on the old
binding; it does not silently inherit the old value's authoritative status.
Omitting an optional field on a later receive must not accidentally reuse an
earlier message's value unless the contract deliberately preserves that binding.
Equal status values for different Requests are still different scoped facts.

Required transmitted fields, and every optional field actually sent, must be
known to the sender with the declared meaning. Static analysis establishes what
it can; runtime construction checks the actual payload and remaining premises.
An optional field is not an exception to knowledge or disclosure requirements.

Loops require analysis to a sound fixed point over the chosen abstract domain.
A bounded analysis reports its bound and unresolved obligations; resource
exhaustion is not successful verification. Graph reachability of a terminal
establishes neither that all executions terminate nor that an environment event
will occur. Recursion, parallel composition, or another extension must carry
explicit semantics and a checking contract; unsupported syntax cannot become
an unchecked alternate path. Full-language work remains available behind the
declared action boundary without pretending the checker understands it all.

*** Executable plans and explanation artifacts

Projection emits one canonical executable plan per role and an optional
explanation artifact tied to that exact plan. A plan may use compact,
non-negative integer state locators. Those locators are local to that artifact,
not semantic operation identities and not stable across rebuilds.

The executable plan retains every runtime-semantic guard, contract reference,
binding, correlation requirement, event distinction, and outcome needed to run
the admitted transition. Diagnostic source coordinates and proof derivations
may live in a sidecar. Stripping a field merely because it was introduced by
verification is invalid if runtime execution still needs its meaning.
In particular, a guard on a delayed effect or message completion remains a
runtime admission obligation even when the proof explaining that guard is
compiler-only. Conversely, a diagnostic sidecar must not be read by the
production machine as a source of new permissions, facts, or continuations.

The exact plan identity binds the versioned canonical representation and all
relevant contract identities. The explanation artifact maps exact emitted
coordinates to source states and judgments under that identity. A compatible
but different plan cannot inherit an old coordinate map as though it were exact.
Compatibility for continuation remains a separate judgment.

A plan-shaped map, a current version tag, and a dense locator range establish
only their checked representation properties. They do not establish projection
correctness, provenance, authorization, or host realization. Admission binds the
generated artifact to the verified subject, selected assurance policy, admitted
assembly, and concrete runtime contracts needed for the claimed use.

*** Acceptance counterexamples for full-language boundaries

These examples state construction-checking obligations under the
selected supported assurance profile. Unrestricted Clojure does not
become fully proved merely because an opaque action has a declared
type or effect contract:

| Apparent shortcut | Required checking or runtime outcome |
|-------------------+--------------------------------------|
| A test harness supplies every entry value to every role, and the browser later uses a server-only input | Test-fixture convenience does not justify role-local production knowledge; reject or require an admitted acquisition path |
| A receive branch omits optional =:approval= but later code reuses a value left under that key by an earlier receive | Require definite binding/presence on this path and the proper observation scope; reject accidental stale knowledge |
| Two Requests have equal =:status=, and a continuation borrows the first Request's authoritative provenance for the second | Reject unsupported subject/scope substitution even when values and Malli shapes match |
| A sender adds an optional message field it does not know, although the payload schema accepts that field | Reject the actual transmitted payload at sender admission; optionality is not a knowledge or disclosure exemption |
| A host callback writes the projected machine's =:state= or jumps to a success continuation without a declared completion | Reject the alternate transition path; realization reports effects, while the machine owns continuation |
| A late callback from a retired effect instance returns a shape-valid completion | Apply its declared owner-relative admission guard; do not let shape equality or a reused state locator revive ownership |
| A transport timeout follows an authoritative effect that may already have committed | Preserve indeterminate disposition and reconcile through the original command binding; do not claim rejection or silently repeat incorporation |
| A loop has an apparent terminal edge but no justified termination or progress assumption | Do not claim liveness; exhausting a sound configured analysis bound leaves an explicit unresolved obligation |
| A plan-shaped map is paired with a certificate or coordinate map from another exact artifact | Reject the mismatch; format validity and a copied proof sidecar do not establish projection correctness or executable authority |

Successful admission must preserve useful ordinary Clojure effects behind
declared contracts, while requiring independent evidence for guarantees the
chosen checker and physical realization do not establish.
** Malli as the value-schema substrate
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-value-schema-boundaries
:END:

Malli provides the ordinary value-shape layer underneath choreographic
types. Its supported schemas describe data and runtime value obligations; the
model and Choreo remain the owners of business meaning, knowledge, authority,
and legal transitions. A schema reference is not a separate semantic registry
that can redefine the owning operation's contract.

A choreographic value may conceptually carry:

#+begin_src clojure
{:schema ClaimCommand
 :known-by #{:helper :request-authority}
 :agreement #{#{:helper :request-authority}}
 :provenance :communicated}
#+end_src

or:

#+begin_src clojure
{:schema RequestProjection
 :known-by #{:helper}
 :provenance :authoritative
 :basis AuthoritativeBasis}
#+end_src

These are conceptual interface sketches, not self-authenticating runtime
envelopes or necessarily literal Malli schema expressions. The schema
references and the =:known-by=, =:agreement=, =:provenance=, and =:basis=
claims have different semantic owners. Malli can check their declared shape;
trusted admission, derivation, communication, authoritative acquisition, and
agreement must establish the corresponding claims. Copying the map, adding a
tag, or passing Malli validation cannot upgrade any of them.

Malli is responsible for questions such as:

- is this a UUID?
- does this map contain the required keys?
- is this settlement one of the allowed values?
- does this intermediate machine context have the promised shape?

Malli is not responsible for questions such as:

- did this value come from a trusted actor rather than merely having a valid
  shape?
- may this helper claim this Request?
- is this Organization currently open?
- does this principal have authority?
- is this stale basis still semantically compatible with the operation?
- did communication actually establish knowledge?
- did an authoritative observation justify the actor's knowledge?
- does this branch satisfy the global choreography?

Those remain domain, FX, Choreo, or authority/progression concerns.

Runtime validators are compiled and reused for the admitted schema and
effective registry definitions whose identities they reference. Their
applicability includes the relevant schema form, registered references,
closedness and refinement options, custom predicate contracts, and target
runtime semantics when these affect validation. A stable schema name, an
unchanged source filename, or a cached compiled function is not sufficient
evidence that the same contract remains in force.

If those bindings belong to an admitted assembly generation, validator use
remains coherent with that generation. A changed schema, registry entry,
extension, or predicate realization requires an appropriately recompiled or
independently established compatible validator before relying on it. A
canonical description may name and compare known semantic dependencies; it
does not pretend that arbitrary host-language functions can be proven
equivalent by hashing their names or serializing them. JVM/CLJS or other
runtime realizations may need separately qualified predicate semantics.
Validator correspondence does not depend on a browser execution identity or
resource-instance counter.

The admitted application validates important runtime boundaries in production,
including:

- externally supplied choreography inputs;
- message payloads;
- authoritative observation/projection envelopes where they cross a runtime
  boundary;
- FX input/output boundaries;
- projected-machine state transitions;
- settlements;
- provisional/canonical protocol payloads.

The successful path uses compiled boolean validators where applicable. Rich
Malli explanations are constructed on failure or explicit diagnostic request.

These validations remain enabled in production. Optimizations based on
measurement may remove redundant work only where an equivalent check has
already been established at a trusted admission boundary and the binding,
value, schema contract, and consumer scope remain valid. No optimization may
drop a required check where untrusted or dynamic values can enter.

*** Schema-boundary acceptance cases

| Tempting shortcut | Required behavior |
|-------------------+-------------------|
| A browser sends a shape-valid principal and authoritative-basis map | Admit the shape only; authentication, observation provenance, and basis progression require their trusted semantic evidence |
| Two schema references have the same name but different registered enum sets | Use the admitted effective registry for the consuming artifact; a cached validator from the old contract cannot silently accept the new boundary |
| A custom predicate changes behavior while retaining its registered keyword | Preserve a changed or unresolved predicate-dependency premise; a matching name or hash of its name is not semantic equivalence |
| A JVM predicate is unavailable or behaves differently in CLJS | Require a supported target realization or explicit qualified premise; do not infer portable meaning from a JVM validator |
| A closed command has an extra field that later code would interpret | Reject the undeclared semantic field or apply an explicitly admitted extension contract; do not merge it into trusted machine knowledge |
| A value was validated under an old assembly but enters a different operation after deployment | Revalidate under, or establish compatible evidence for, the receiving contract; prior validation alone is not transferable authority |
| An implementation disables a required boundary validator after a benchmark | Preserve an equivalent trusted check and its applicability, or reject the optimization; speed does not discharge a runtime premise |

These are finished-system acceptance obligations. They do not claim that
plain Malli validation itself certifies source-to-runtime correspondence,
trust, or the correctness of arbitrary custom predicates.

*** Shape validation is necessary but not semantic certification

Successful runtime validation establishes conformance to the particular schema
under the validator and predicate contracts being relied upon. It is classified
as runtime enforcement, not automatically as a formal theorem. A custom
predicate's name does not establish an authority or provenance claim that its
implementation never checks. Custom predicates are executable code: their
determinism, side effects, exception behavior, and target portability are part of
their contract when relied upon. A schema used for formal reasoning must have a
supported semantic interpretation or explicit premises; serializing a function
reference does not give the prover that interpretation.

It does not prove:

- that a browser supplied the value honestly;
- that the value has authoritative provenance;
- that the current principal may use it;
- that another actor knows the same value;
- that a model operation may be invoked;
- that a choreography branch is globally legal.

This is not a limitation to work around.  It is a deliberate separation of
correctness scopes.

The unified design therefore treats ordinary value schemas as one layer in a
larger semantic interface:

| Layer | Separate obligation |
|-------+---------------------|
| Value shape | The actual value satisfies its admitted schema |
| Semantic type/refinement | Its category and applicable refinements are established |
| Knowledge/provenance | This role has the required scoped justification |
| Authority/operation contract | The trusted boundary permits this operation under current premises |
| Choreographic admissibility | This local action and continuation obey the distributed specification |

These are independent, composable judgments, not a mandatory sequence of
runtime calls or a ladder in which passing one automatically proves the next.
A supported static theorem about a schema may discharge an identified
shape-related premise; a runtime validator enforces a shape-related premise
for a particular admitted value. Neither conclusion entails the other
semantic judgments. Each stronger claim needs its own applicable
construction, proof, trusted admission, or explicit open premise.

A success at one layer cannot be silently promoted into a stronger proposition
owned by a later layer.

The same rule applies when a stronger semantic category happens to use a nominal
host-language representation.  Malli may validate the fields inside an
=AuthoritativeBasis= representation, for example, but shape validation does not
establish that the value came through the trusted authoritative acquisition
path.  Conversely, an admitted value may still use ordinary immutable Clojure
data internally.  What matters is the checked construction/admission relation,
not whether the physical carrier is a record or a map.

A discriminator such as =:type :verified= is therefore useful only as data unless
the supported API also establishes why callers are entitled to rely on that
category. A trusted semantic category is established by an admitted producer,
derivation or authority-bound construction and is usable only under the
subject, scope, issuer, version and consumer constraints of that judgment.
A caller-created lookalike must not be promoted into the same judgment by
deserialization, rewrapping or matching a registry key. This does not require
a special physical wrapper for every value: ordinary maps remain valid
carriers when the trusted construction and evidence remain enforceable.

*** Closed value boundaries are the default

Where data crosses a semantic boundary, the declared schema normally
describes a closed payload. Closedness is an admission property of the
effective boundary contract, not a comment attached to an example map.
It applies to the semantic fields that the receiver is permitted to
interpret, including nested declared structures where their contract is
closed. The source-owned schema and its admitted version, optional fields,
variants and extension points determine the accepted semantic vocabulary;
the sender may not select a weaker schema by supplying its name.

Closedness is especially important for:

- client commands;
- choreography messages;
- provisional-state envelopes;
- settlement envelopes;
- authoritative projection metadata;
- FX inputs and outputs.

An open map is not harmless merely because consumers ignore unknown keys.
Unexpected fields can become an accidental covert semantic channel when later
code begins to read them.

When an application genuinely needs an open payload, that must be explicit
for the particular boundary, and its semantic treatment must be visible
to verification and diagnostics. Merely permitting extra physical fields
does not extend the machine's semantic payload or give a receiver new
knowledge. An explicitly open transport envelope may retain undeclared
adapter data without entering it into role-local fact storage, deterministic
semantic history, branch decisions, operation arguments, authorization,
semantic correlation, or proof conclusions.

If an extension field is to acquire any such semantic role, its source-owned
contract, required shape, knowledge/provenance path, disclosure restrictions,
and compatibility with existing consumers must be admitted. A planned
extension cannot retroactively reinterpret unknown bytes under an old
closed semantic contract. Physical routing or telemetry may use separate
admitted transport metadata rules; those rules do not grant domain authority.

Semantic payload closure is distinct from rejecting every byte of physical
transport metadata. The decoder separates declared semantic payload from
permitted transport metadata through an explicit, qualified contract.
The consumer enforces the closedness of the *decoded semantic fields*, not
merely the spelling of their wire representation. Decoding and normalization
must not allow incompatible encodings, duplicate physical fields, alias
collisions or coercions to create an ambiguous or attacker-chosen semantic
binding; an ambiguity affecting the admitted contract is rejected rather
than silently resolved into new authority.

At a closed semantic boundary, undeclared semantic fields are rejected.
At an explicitly open physical boundary, permitted undeclared data stays
outside semantic context unless separately admitted. This applies whether
the data arrives through an HTTP command, participant message, event,
FX callback, authoritative projection, settlement or reused artifact.
Both admission and later consumers preserve the same division; a generic
map merge, cache retrieval or serialization round trip cannot silently
make excluded fields usable semantic inputs.

*** Shape-and-closure acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent success | Required admission or rejection |
|------------------+---------------------------------|
| A browser posts ={:type :verified :basis B}= matching a registered Malli schema | Validate its shape if applicable, but require trusted issuer, scope and observation evidence before treating B as authoritative |
| A different principal copies another actor's correctly shaped command envelope | Reject unauthorized use despite field-level equality; schema success does not transfer the original caller binding |
| A receive contract is closed, but the sender adds an undeclared =:admin?= field | Reject the semantic extra field; downstream consumers must not interpret or retain it as role knowledge |
| A message expressly allows open physical data with an undeclared =:hint= | Preserve the allowed adapter field only outside the semantic machine; it grants no role-local fact, branch, or operation input |
| A newer consumer starts branching on the old open =:hint= field without changing its admitted interface | Require a new checked semantic contract and knowledge/disclosure path; physical carriage is not retroactive admission |
| A wire decoder maps both ="request-id"= and =:request/id= into one semantic key with conflicting values | Reject an ambiguous decoded binding; do not let key aliasing or last-write-wins select an authority-relevant value |
| An untrusted command includes a schema/registry selector that relaxes required or closed fields | Enforce the receiver's admitted contract; an attacker-selected label does not determine the semantic schema |
| A value passes Malli validation, but a custom predicate has no supported semantics for the chosen proof profile | Retain the predicate's premise or reject unsupported proof admission; runtime shape acceptance is not a theorem |
| A shared cache or callback returns a map containing valid declared fields plus stale foreign-scope metadata | Recheck the consuming scope and boundary; closure and field shape do not establish identity or permitted reuse |

These cases constrain the finished system without prescribing a single
transport decoder, container representation, or proof backend.

*** Runtime validation preserves static premises

Static reasoning depends on runtime values continuing to satisfy the contracts
under which the reasoning was performed.

Consequently, runtime checks are not "extra tests" after proof. They enforce
the assumptions on which static reasoning depends wherever untrusted or
dynamic values enter. The admitted checker identifies which premises require
runtime enforcement; the physical application binds those checks to its actual
input, effect, acquisition, and continuation paths. A validator invoked in an
unrelated test, omitted route, or obsolete artifact does not discharge that
deployed boundary obligation.

Representative boundaries include:

| Runtime input | Required admission before use |
|---------------+-------------------------------|
| Browser command bytes | Bound operation lookup, decoding/schema checks, command/caller binding, and trusted invocation contract |
| External read result | Projection-envelope validation; authorized acquisition, subject/scope/basis/provenance admission; then only the justified role-local knowledge |
| FX implementation result | Declared output validation, effect/owner correlation, and admission of the next semantic step under its current guards |
| Settlement payload | Schema, trusted source, command binding, current owner and disposition-to-settlement checks before the admitted browser transition |

A successful shape check is not itself authorization, authoritative
observation, sender knowledge, current resource ownership, or permission to
install a browser result. The receiver establishes the separate semantic
premises applicable to the particular operation, execution and consumer.
Where an earlier trusted admission is reused, its subject, version, scope,
lifetime and consuming boundary must still apply. The same rule covers
cache hits, deferred effects, and cross-deployment requests.

If a runtime premise fails, execution rejects, defers or recovers according
to the declared protocol without manufacturing a terminal business result.
It must not continue on the theory that static verification made runtime
validation unnecessary.

*** Validators should be reusable and diagnostic failure should be rich

The successful path should use precompiled validators where possible.

The failure path should preserve enough information to explain:

- which semantic value failed;
- which schema or refinement was expected;
- where the value entered;
- which operation or transition depended on it;
- whether failure concerns shape, provenance, authority, or another layer.

A raw Malli explanation may contribute to internal diagnostic evidence,
but downstream error reporting must not force users to reconstruct semantic
context from a low-level schema mismatch alone. Diagnostic construction,
exception metadata, persistence, logging, traces, and responses must each
respect the applicable disclosure boundary: credentials, raw command data,
private payload fields, and protected model values cannot leak merely because
validation failed. A low-level exception containing a full rejected value is
not automatically safe to log or return. Stable semantic identifiers,
authorized/redacted value summaries, checker/issuer scope, and the failed
obligation are normally sufficient. Diagnostic denial must not itself be
misreported as a rejected authoritative operation.

*** Schema changes participate in semantic compatibility

A schema is not merely runtime validation configuration when another semantic
artifact relies upon its meaning.

Changes such as:

- removing an enum member;
- changing required versus optional;
- changing cardinality;
- changing a field's nominal semantic type;
- changing a closed variant set;

may invalidate dependent operation, rule, or choreography artifacts.

The canonical interfaces therefore have semantic identities independent of
source filenames. Dependency analysis tracks the effective schema and registry,
nominal/refinement meanings, closedness and extensions, runtime predicate
contracts, and the actual consumers that rely upon them. Changes invalidate
or require new correspondence evidence for affected validators, type judgments,
operations, projected plans, browser artifacts, application assemblies and
assurance products. A matching name, digest label, file path, or well-formed
version tag alone cannot discharge that dependency.

Compatibility is directional and consumer-relative. An old producer accepted
by a new reader, a new producer consumed by an old reader, continuation of an
in-flight execution, and reuse of a proof may have different requirements.
A schema widening or narrowing can affect required fields, accepted variants,
trusted refinements, disclosure rules, and downstream branch meaning even
when every observed test value still passes both validators. An admitted
compatibility relation must identify the source and target interfaces, the
relevant consuming semantics, and the qualification evidence; otherwise the
dependent claim is invalidated or remains explicitly unresolved.

These obligations apply independently of when the proof machinery is
implemented. The language/model boundary exposes semantic dependencies
rather than hiding them inside unrestricted host-language callbacks.

*** Runtime-premise and compatibility counterexamples

| Apparent success | Required outcome |
|------------------+------------------|
| A schema-valid browser command arrives on an unregistered or bypassing route | Reject or report the missing installed boundary; passing the validator elsewhere does not prove application closure |
| An authorized board read passes its schema but belongs to a previous principal or Request scope | Do not establish current authoritative knowledge or permit installation without matching acquisition, disclosure and owner evidence |
| An FX completion matches its output schema after its resource instance was retired | Reject the stale completion or apply a declared recovery; valid shape does not revive an execution |
| A valid-looking settlement reports =:rejected= after the command may already have committed | Preserve unresolved incorporation and reconcile the original binding; a payload enum does not decide database truth |
| A failure diagnostic includes a protected field in an exception map destined for logs | Redact or withhold at the relevant logging boundary; hiding the field only from the HTTP response is insufficient |
| A new schema admits an extra enum variant used by a branch in an older executable plan | Check the actual direction and consuming semantics; test-value agreement and the same schema name do not establish continuation compatibility |
| A predicate implementation changes but the schema identifier and file name remain unchanged | Requalify the predicate contract and affected evidence, or leave compatibility unresolved |
| A compiled validator and proof sidecar come from different application generations | Reject mismatched dependency applicability; a matching top-level tag is not correspondence |
| An older writer emits a closed payload accepted by a newer reader, but new business guards changed | Shape compatibility may hold while authorization and operation correspondence still fail; check the distinct obligations |

These cases are part of the finished-system acceptance contract. They do not
prescribe one implementation of the validator cache, proof backend, or deployment
migration mechanism.

** Projection and branch knowledge
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-projection-branch-knowledge
:END:

Projection creates a local machine for each role.

Projection must preserve whatever mechanism is required to justify local
behavior.

Sometimes that mechanism is communication.

Sometimes the actor already has justified entry knowledge.

Sometimes the relevant fact is learned from authority outside the current
execution and enters a later choreography as authoritative input knowledge.

The projector/verifier must therefore reason in terms of knowledge requirements,
not merely message presence. For each local use, the obligation identifies the
role, the scoped fact or proposition, the required provenance and authority,
and the program point before which the fact must be available. A globally
defined value or a proof that another actor possesses it cannot serve as
this role's local knowledge. An entry assumption is usable only under its
declared role, source, scope and admission premises.

A role may branch only when it knows enough information to choose the branch.

The verifier should use behavior-sensitive choice analysis:

- if a role behaves differently between two branches, it must know the
  discriminator or an equivalent fact before its behavior diverges;
- the required local evidence may be supplied through declared communication,
  authorized authoritative observation, role-specific entry knowledge,
  supported derivation, or an explicit assertion within its admitted contract;
  assertion establishes that the source asserted a proposition, not the
  proposition's independent truth or authority;
- if its behavior is equivalent across the branches, it does not need to learn
  the choice merely because it is syntactically mentioned.

This is intentionally more precise than a simple "every role mentioned in either
branch must know the condition" rule.

For example:

If authority chooses an approved or rejected continuation and the helper's
behavior differs, the helper needs evidence distinguishing those continuations
before it performs that differing behavior.

That requirement does not imply a dedicated authority-to-helper choreography
message if the helper can instead obtain the required approval fact through a
declared, authorized authoritative observation that satisfies the same branch
obligation.

An alternate knowledge path is valid only when it establishes the *specific
proposition required by this continuation*, with its subject, scope, provenance,
causal predecessor, and temporal/authority qualification. Reading a currently
approved Request does not necessarily establish which earlier command was
approved or which historical branch was selected. If later model changes can
erase or supersede the needed fact, the protocol needs a suitable retained
outcome, receipt, communication, or different continuation contract.

Knowledge-path equivalence is not automatically protocol equivalence.
A required global communication cannot be silently replaced by a reread
simply because both yield equal values: the replacement must be admitted by
the authored choreography or by a proved, contract-preserving refinement
that accounts for observable communication, correlation, participant
waiting, control flow, failure and progress obligations. The projector must
neither invent missing history nor add a different physical action to rescue
an otherwise inadmissible declaration.

Messages should be closed by default.

A message contract declares the fields that may cross the boundary. Arbitrary
extra fields should not be merged into choreography context.

An explicit =:open-payload? true= may exist for cases that genuinely require an
open payload, but closed contracts are the default.

Within the admitted choreography semantics, a sender must have justified
knowledge of every semantic fact it transmits. Receipt from an adversarial
participant still requires the trusted-boundary treatment defined in
[[#req-knowledge-provenance][the knowledge model]].

Receiving a message establishes only the declared communicated values
after the message's required source, channel, correlation, payload and
owner-relative admission. The same-shaped bytes from a different sender,
scope or execution do not establish the original sender's proposition.
Required and actually transmitted optional semantic fields remain subject
to sender knowledge and disclosure checks; undeclared fields in explicitly
open physical payloads do not become semantic knowledge.

Projection must not erase communication that is genuinely required to justify
knowledge.

Conversely, projection should not manufacture communication merely because the
global choreography contains a choice when the target role's behavior does not
depend on it or its knowledge is established through another valid path.

*** Projection is a semantic compilation boundary

Projection is not a convenience transformation that happens to produce smaller
maps.

It is the boundary at which the global specification is turned into the local
information and actions available to a role.

A correct projector must therefore preserve every fact required to justify
local behavior, including when relevant:

- message schemas;
- correlation identity;
- entry knowledge;
- knowledge-producing communication;
- authoritative-observation requirements;
- branch discriminators;
- provenance requirements;
- resource ownership;
- failure/terminal structure;
- executable guards required by local semantics;
- the causal prerequisites that distinguish local authorization to act from
  mere presence of an identically shaped fact;
- scope and disclosure restrictions on what the role may actually receive;
- explicit alternatives for unavailable communication or observation, when
  the global contract requires recoverable continuation.

At the same time, it omits global facts the role neither knows nor needs.
A globally available private discriminator is not permitted in a local
artifact merely because it would make branch selection convenient.
Preserving global semantics does not authorize revealing private premises
or relying on information that the recipient is not allowed to acquire.

The target is neither "copy the whole global machine to everyone" nor "erase
everything inconvenient."  It is a justified local program.

*** Projection cannot create knowledge

A projector may rearrange or encode information justified by the global
choreography.  It may not manufacture a fact merely because the local machine
would be easier to implement if the fact were present.

Bad:

Only authority knows =:approved?=, but the browser projection contains it
without any admitted input, communication, or observation. This projection
manufactures knowledge and must be rejected.

Good:

Authority communicates the permitted command outcome, or the browser obtains
an authorized observation establishing the required proposition. The local
branch becomes admissible after that declared knowledge path succeeds.

The same principle applies to provenance.  Projected data cannot silently
upgrade:

| Supplied support | Unjustified upgrade |
|------------------+---------------------|
| Asserted premise | Mechanically derived proposition |
| Communicated value | Authoritative truth without a supporting authority contract |
| Client-supplied principal id | Trusted authenticated principal binding |

Projection preserves or weakens claims according to explicit semantics; it does
not strengthen them by serialization. Distinguish a valid local use of an
*asserted* fact from a claim of authoritative truth, a known value from
a definitely present optional binding, and a current observation from a
retained command-specific decision.

*** Knowledge-preservation acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent local justification | Required checker outcome |
|------------------------------+--------------------------|
| The global state contains =:approved?=, but the browser has no entry fact, receive or observation path | Reject the local use; global existence does not create role knowledge |
| The server asserts that a Request is approved, and projection marks it authoritative | Preserve asserted provenance or require an admitted authority-bound justification; do not promote assertion into truth |
| The helper sees the Request's current =:approved= status and resumes an earlier command-specific approved branch | Require a retained result/correlation fact or an explicitly different continuation contract |
| The helper reads a result for Request A but continues Request B, whose status value is equal | Reject subject/scope substitution despite value equality |
| A participant sends an optional =:review/result= field without having established its value | Reject the actual transmitted field or require its legitimate sender knowledge |
| A received message carries the right payload from an unbound sender or old execution | Do not establish knowledge before source, correlation and owner-relative admission |
| A compiler replaces a required global notification with an authoritative reread that yields the same status | Require an authored alternative or justified semantic refinement preserving the communication and continuation contract |
| The browser knows a private discriminator only because projection copied the authority's local state | Reject the projection; a global fact cannot bypass permitted acquisition or disclosure |
| Two local branches look identical after ignoring an effect or timeout outcome | Do not erase the distinguishing observable or recovery obligation to claim branch equivalence |

These are finished-system obligations. Their enforcement may be distributed
among construction-time knowledge verification, refinement checking, and
trusted runtime admission; no one layer may silently assume the others have
already discharged its premises.

*** Behavior-sensitive choice is the central branch rule

A role needs evidence sufficient to select its continuation when its observable
local behavior depends on a choice. The required knowledge must exist before
the first differing behavior, not merely become available eventually.

This avoids both extremes:

- projecting too little and leaving a role unable to decide its next behavior;
- overcommunicating every global discriminator to every mentioned role.

The analysis should compare continuations semantically rather than merely by
source syntax.

If two global branches project to equivalent local behavior for role R, R need
not learn the branch solely because R appears somewhere inside both branches.

If they diverge, the projector/verifier must establish a valid knowledge path
before the divergence. Whether behavior differs is judged against the
declared local semantic observations and obligations, including waiting,
effects, outcomes, correlation, resources and relevant causal order. Equal
final values or superficially equal message maps are not sufficient.
A branch choice may remain private only when the projection also respects
its disclosure and information-flow contract; equivalence under a coarse
trace alphabet does not alone prove confidentiality.

A focused expense example separates private choice from public behavior.
Suppose an accepted manager review has one of two recorded results:

| Manager result | Notifier behavior | Finance behavior in an explicitly participating short session |
|----------------+-------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Awaiting Finance | Notify that a manager review was recorded | Receive review-required, acquire current permitted facts, and offer the Finance action |
| Approved by manager | The same notification with the same declared payload | Receive not-required and terminate local participation |

This is a *separately authored neutral notifier protocol*, not a
relabeling of the result-bearing =:expense/review-disposition= message in
the earlier worked review. Its message says that a manager review was
recorded for this Expense and admitted session, not which operation,
result, or current Expense status it produced. The neutral contract
correlates the common session and disclosed Expense subject; it does
not transmit =:review/result=, a route-dependent command identity,
or another route-specific field to the notifier by unnoticed metadata.
The two branches need not create identical command identities in
the authoritative model, but those command identities cannot
implicitly leak through this neutral notification's admitted envelope.

The sender must still establish the coarse proposition *a manager review
was recorded* through its actual admitted command result. A trusted
outcome-derived event or a trusted notification boundary that verifies
the recorded result may establish this proposition without disclosing
the route-specific outcome to the notifier. An adversarial manager
browser's assertion of review completion is not equivalent evidence.
The notifier's effect and result-handling contracts are the same on
both successful branches. It does not need the private threshold
or route selector. If the application changes notification text,
destination, or other relevant observable behavior by branch, that
difference requires a justified, disclosure-permitted input. A
neutral payload establishes no unconditional noninterference guarantee
for timing, occurrence, transport metadata or other observations
outside its expressly checked disclosure and observation contract.

The two notification paths must also have compatible causal prerequisites.
Here each notice follows an authoritative result establishing a recorded review
of the same Expense under the declared message contract. Merely finding equal
payload shapes in two branches would not establish that meaning. If a
notification occurs in only one branch, or if observable destination,
cardinality, delay or resource behavior differs in a way the contract
distinguishes, the notifier may learn something about the choice even from
identical bytes. Any such distinction requires its own admitted knowledge
and disclosure path rather than an unsupported equivalence assertion.

Finance is different when it is already a participant in the short session.
The global contract must distinguish both continuations, for example through a
declared disposition message:

#+begin_src clojure
{:message/id :expense/finance-disposition
 :from :manager
 :to :finance
 :required #{:expense/id :review/command-id :review/disposition}
 :correlation #{:expense/id :review/command-id}
 :disposition-type [:enum :review-required :not-required :review-unavailable]}
#+end_src

This is a schematic intended message interface, not an assertion that
=:disposition-type= is itself an implemented or independently sufficient
Choreo constructor option. Its admitted payload schema must bind the
required =:review/disposition= to this closed variant set and establish
disjoint received alternatives where the projected receiver needs them.
The sender must know the actual disposition and its correlation to the
original review command, and must be permitted to disclose it to Finance.
A matching enum tag and command id supplied by a different actor cannot
establish that binding.

The projected receive can branch on an admitted =:review/disposition= after
trusted source, subject, session and command correlation are established. A
=:review-required= message justifies entering a current observation/action
path; it does not itself authorize Finance approval. A =:not-required= message
permits local return without claiming that Finance acted. =:review-unavailable=
closes this session's participation when the manager's *already bound*
command could not produce a usable result. That variant requires the
real =:review/command-id= referenced by this message contract; it
must not fabricate one to describe failure before command allocation.
If an earlier acquisition or entry admission fails before a command
is allocated, the session needs an independently authored
session-only closure/retirement alternative, or Finance must not
have been admitted as a waiting participant. That closure has
its own declared payload, causality and knowledge contract; it
cannot masquerade as a command-specific disposition.
=:review-unavailable= does not assert that the command never committed;
unknown command resolution remains with its declared recovery owner.

These are additional messages deliberately chosen by this session protocol.
They must not be inserted by the projector merely to rescue an otherwise
unrealizable declaration. Their delivery, timeout, and session-retirement paths
must also be defined. A role that simply waits for =:review-required= forever
cannot claim completion of both branches; absence of a message is not knowledge
that Finance was unnecessary.

The invalid alternative is to give Finance a branch on =:needs-finance?= simply
because the projector knows the manager's selector. Finance has neither that
selector nor its private policy inputs. The projector must report the missing
knowledge rather than copy either into Finance's context.

The normal durable alternative avoids this participating short session. The
manager commits =:awaiting-finance=, and a later Finance actor starts a new
execution from an authorized queue observation. Selection from that queue is
an explicit user/application input, not automatic approval of every visible
Expense. The chosen operation rechecks current eligibility at incorporation.

That alternative establishes a new workflow entry condition; it is not proof
that a particular old command or branch occurred unless the observation also
establishes that command-specific link. Projection may use it only where the
global contract declares that continuation semantics.

Behavior-sensitive projection therefore controls both how much knowledge is
distributed and when it must exist. Equivalent local continuations can share a
plan; different continuations require evidence before the first difference,
including a difference between waiting, performing an effect, and terminating.

**** Supported equivalence and conservative rejection

Behavior-sensitive checking must define the local equivalence relation used by
its supported language subset. The observables include whichever messages,
effects, authoritative invocations, resource actions, and terminal or failure
outcomes the semantic contract distinguishes. Relevant ordering and timing
constraints must not disappear merely because the final values happen to match.

Permitted internal steps may be abstracted only under an explicit semantic
relation. The checker is not required to decide equivalence of arbitrary
Clojure functions or unrestricted programs.

A concrete conservative baseline compares normalized continuation graphs up to
renaming of diagnostic state identities and local binding names. It preserves
operation/action contract identities, bound inputs and results, message
destinations and payload meanings, observable effects, owner scopes, and
terminal/failure alternatives. Ignoring source coordinates does not ignore
causal dependencies or substitute one implementation contract for another.
Cycles require a checked graph relation rather than recursive unfolding without
a termination bound. Stronger equivalence rules must identify their mechanism
and premises; equality of final values or of function names is not such a rule.

A receiver may merge remote alternatives when admitted incoming messages
distinguish the alternatives before their different behavior. The branch
selector must be established at the receive boundary, rather than inferred
from the absence of a message or from a value in a mutually overlapping
optional field. If alternatives
share a sender, event, channel, and correlation scope, their admitted payload
contracts must be disjoint or an explicit discriminator must select them.
Optional fields alone commonly overlap. A supported checker rejects a known
ambiguity and reports an unresolved obligation when opaque schema predicates
prevent establishing disjointness. It must not choose the first matching branch
by map or registration order.

When the supported analysis cannot establish equivalence or a sufficient
knowledge path, it must reject or report an unresolved obligation at the
appropriate admission boundary. It must not silently assume equivalence,
disclose a private discriminator, or introduce an undeclared message to make
projection succeed. An explicit supported communication path or visible trusted
assumption may be authored where permitted by the relevant contract. An asserted
equivalence remains a premise of the resulting projection claim; it cannot
silently create missing input data, discharge a mandatory failed owner check,
or authorize private disclosure.

**** Trace preservation and progress are separate obligations

Excluding forbidden local traces is a safety claim. It does not by itself prove
that a projected program can perform every required interaction, avoid deadlock,
or eventually complete. A projection that admits no useful behavior must not
satisfy the required operation merely because its empty behavior is safe.

Each claimed projection result must state the global/local behavior relation
and the entry and environmental premises under which it applies. Required
realizability, terminal behavior, and progress obligations must be discharged
with the appropriate evidence. Liveness remains conditional on its named
delivery, availability, fairness, or application-action assumptions.

These obligations supplement, rather than replace, the independent application
capability acceptance defined in Part I. A concrete witness that one schedule
projects correctly is not a proof about every admitted schedule; a proof of
trace inclusion is not necessarily trace coverage, absence of deadlock,
liveness, authorization, or secrecy. Completion and acceptance use the
specific theorem and environmental premises that their claims require.

*** Branch-choice acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required construction or execution outcome |
|-------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| Both branch continuations emit the same neutral notification payload, but only one branch emits a notification | Preserve the observable occurrence difference or justify it under the actual abstraction and disclosure contract; equal bytes are not local equivalence |
| Finance reads a manager-only threshold from the global branch selector | Reject the projection unless Finance has an authorized knowledge path; a global choice cannot grant private input |
| Finance waits only for =:review-required=, while the other branch has no message or local terminal path | Reject the claimed total participation or require the authored not-required/disposition path; silence is not knowledge |
| Finance receives a permitted =:review-required= variant from a different session or with a copied command id | Reject mismatched sender/subject/session/correlation admission despite valid enum and payload shape |
| A notifier's destination or effect changes by route while its payload remains the same | Require justified discriminator and disclosure rights for the changed observable effect |
| A supposedly neutral review message hides =:review/result= or route-specific command metadata in its envelope while showing identical notification text | Do not claim notifier route ignorance; remove such fields through an authored neutral contract and qualify every remaining observable under the disclosure property |
| Two receive alternatives have overlapping optional-field schemas and the runtime picks the first match | Reject ambiguous alternatives at the checked boundary; declaration order is not a semantic discriminator |
| The manager's command is unresolved and a =:review-unavailable= message is treated as proof of rejection | Preserve unknown-incorporation reconciliation and its owner; unavailable is not uncommitted |

The cases express the finished language's admission requirements, without
prescribing a particular projection algorithm or proof backend.

*** Communication, causality, and completion

Global communication and physical transport have different granularity. A
global =:communicate= describes the semantic transfer under its message contract;
the local plans expose sending and admitted receipt separately. The realization
relation identifies which concrete event establishes receipt and receiver
knowledge. A local =:complete-send= boundary may permit the sender to
finish without waiting for the peer, but semantic communication has
not thereby occurred: its realization witness is the admitted receiver
consumption of that same message occurrence. A send callback may mean
enqueued or accepted by a transport, not delivered to the peer. It must
not establish the stronger event by implication.
Nor does acceptance by a receiving transport alone establish that the correct
role-local machine admitted and consumed the message. The realization relation
identifies the admissible receiver event, its actual message origin, and the
resulting knowledge transfer. Acknowledgment meaning is contract-specific:
transport receipt, machine consumption, and business adoption establish
different propositions.

The channel contract states relevant ordering, loss, duplication, replay,
correlation, and disclosure semantics. Where loss is possible, a required
receive needs an appropriate retry, timeout, durable handoff, or other recovery
path, with any eventual-delivery claim stated as a premise. Each retry or
handoff preserves the declared occurrence, command and session semantics,
or explicitly establishes a new occurrence under the applicable admission rule.
A timeout establishes that a deadline elapsed locally; it does not prove
non-delivery, remote rejection, non-incorporation, or peer termination.
The receiver's silence is not evidence that it chose an alternate branch.
A sent closure/disposition message is likewise not an actual consumed
closure. Where a participant terminates locally after sending, the
global terminal claim still accounts for the receiver's required
communication and subsequent effects, or an authored terminal/handoff
alternative that explicitly owns the undelivered obligation. The
terminal relation must not manufacture the missing receiver event,
assume fair delivery without a premise, or erase an outstanding
durable effect because its initiating process has retired.

Correlation binds the message occurrence to its admitted conversation, operation,
participants, and semantic subject as required. Command, execution, transport,
and message-occurrence identities are distinct unless the contract deliberately
identifies particular instances. Correlation fields learned from the first
message still need an admitted routing/source scope; accepting an unknown id
does not let an arbitrary message choose which authority or session it belongs to.
At first contact, an unbound correlation key may be learned only after
trusted routing, origin and scope admission; the sender's claim of a session
identifier cannot itself establish the authority to bind that session.
The required relation concerns the intended occurrence and participants,
not merely equality of supplied key strings.

Duplicate handling is explicit. Reaccepting a transport delivery must not repeat
an already consumed local transition or authoritative effect unless the declared
repeat semantics permit it. Rejecting a stale private callback does not erase
independently usable command-outcome evidence; reconciliation can admit it under
its own owner and disclosure contract. Duplicate suppression is qualified by
the protocol's semantic occurrence identity and admitted replay scope; a
repeated delivery of an already consumed occurrence cannot become a second
command or effect through a new transport attempt. Conversely, independent
legitimate occurrences with equal payloads must not be collapsed merely
because their bytes match. Retrying an uncertain authoritative command uses
the declared stable command identity and reconciliation contract, not a fresh
incorporation disguised as message redelivery.

Projection also preserves causal dependencies across foreign authoritative
steps. If role B may act only after command C has a recorded successful result,
B needs a message or authoritative observation establishing that exact premise
before acting. Merely traversing a foreign authoritative node in the compiler
does not establish runtime evidence at B. A message reporting transport
acceptance or an attempted invocation is not proof that C committed. The
authoritative result contract determines which outcomes establish the
specified predecessor; an unresolved result retains reconciliation rather
than satisfying success or definite failure by default.

A completion descriptor naming an authority and basis is insufficient by itself.
An admitted read of an unrelated card, a read before C became visible, or a
current state compatible with several commands cannot discharge C's dependency.
The witness must bind the predecessor, subject, required result, scope, and
relevant progression. Where the continuation requires only current eligibility
rather than C's historical success, the global contract must state that weaker,
different premise.

For a wait with several alternatives, dependency discharge is edge-specific.
An ordinary timeout or cancellation alternative does not inherit the evidence
provided by a successful authoritative-observation alternative. Nor does
receipt on a different session edge establish the intended predecessor. A compact plan
must retain that distinction or reject the unsupported projection, not discard
the dependency because one edge could establish it.

This rule does not impose a global lockstep order on independent work. If an
abstraction permits reordering independent local actions, its dependency and
effect contracts must justify that commutation. If the global meaning really
requires receipt before a sender's later effect, the protocol needs evidence
such as a declared acknowledgement; local send completion is insufficient.

The refinement claim names its observation alphabet. A coarse distributed trace
may hide local calculations or reads, but that does not prove local branch
correctness, confidentiality, resource safety, or presentation behavior.
Nor can it prove an externally significant notification simply because
the receiver admitted the message that *requested* one. The
notifier's issued/fulfilled/failed effect evidence belongs to its
declared effect contract and the physical realization relation.
Where an application promises a durable notification, the
authoritative command's required publication/work intent and the
notification worker's later physical attempts have distinct,
stable, correlated identities and separate disposition milestones.
An acknowledged message, a broker acceptance, and a provider
fulfillment cannot be collapsed into one semantic event. Those
properties require their own retained observations and judgments. Choosing an
alphabet that hides all useful behavior cannot establish application acceptance.

A required durable publication may be realized by a separate
model-work-driven notification execution after the initiating actor
has retired. This is not a license to forge that actor's
=:send= or the recipient's corresponding =:receive= in the
original projected execution. The authored protocol either retains
and recovers its required sender occurrence, admits a checked
delegation/alternative with valid origin and knowledge, or uses a
separately declared worker/receiver path whose observations are
correlated to durable work. The global terminal and effect evidence
must follow the chosen path; structural similarity of messages or
work identities cannot establish refinement across the paths.

*** Message payload closure protects knowledge boundaries

Closed messages are not only a schema preference.  They are a knowledge-flow
boundary.

If a message declares:

#+begin_src clojure
{:request-id UUID
 :outcome ClaimOutcome}
#+end_src

Here =:request-id= identifies the domain Request involved in the Claim outcome;
it is not =:transport-request-id=, which identifies a transport attempt.
Receiving this message may establish only the declared communicated facts.

The receiver does not acquire arbitrary sender context, and the implementation
must not merge unknown payload keys into machine context by convenience.
Declared required fields are present on an admitted message; optional fields
establish knowledge only when actually sent and received under their contract.
A sender must know each transmitted semantic field and be entitled to disclose
it. Receipt establishes *communicated* provenance, not independent authority
of the transmitted assertion. A claim that the message carries a committed
outcome also requires trusted source and command-result correlation.

An explicitly open payload must follow the extension-data contract from the
[[#req-value-schema-boundaries][value-schema boundary]]. Openness alone does not
grant undeclared fields semantic knowledge status or permission to influence a
branch. Nonsemantic transport extensions remain outside admitted fact storage,
branch inputs, operation arguments and semantic history. Promoting any extension
to a semantic field requires a new admitted contract and its knowledge,
disclosure and compatibility obligations.

The example's =:request-id= is domain correlation, not a transport attempt
identity. A command-specific Claim settlement also requires the applicable
command/result and owner binding; matching only the Request id cannot justify
reusing another Claim's disposition. Any weaker guarantee actually selected
remains visible in the result.

*** Communication and causality acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required admission or evidence |
|-------------------+--------------------------------|
| A send adapter reports successful enqueue and the sender records peer receipt | Preserve transport completion only; require an admitted receiver consumption event for semantic receipt |
| A sender completes a required closure send and returns locally while the receiver remains waiting | Do not report the authored global terminal from the sender's local completion; require actual receive or separately justified terminal recovery/handoff |
| A proxy acknowledges receipt and the application treats the payload as installed in a browser | Require the separate installation/adoption contract; transport receipt is not UI adoption |
| A global trace hides the notifier's local effect and a proof of message receipt is advertised as proof of external delivery | Reject the unsupported physical completion claim; observe and check the registered effect stage under its own evidence contract |
| A message from untrusted first contact contains a plausible session id | Establish routing, origin and scope before binding the new correlation value |
| A dropped message times out and the sender declares the remote authoritative effect rejected | Preserve uncertainty and original command reconciliation; local timeout does not prove non-incorporation |
| Two deliveries of one semantic occurrence arrive through different transport attempts | Prevent a second semantic transition unless the protocol explicitly admits repeating the occurrence |
| Two independent occurrences have equal payload bytes | Do not suppress the second solely by payload equality; occurrence identity and scope determine deduplication |
| A notification refers to Request A and claims Command C committed, but the only trusted read is of Request B | Reject predecessor discharge; value similarity and a named authority are insufficient |
| An await timeout and an authoritative-observation event converge, and both inherit the observation's knowledge | Keep dependency discharge edge-specific; timeout cannot inherit unobserved evidence |
| An open message includes undeclared =:admin?=, later read from a generic context merge | Exclude the field from semantic knowledge and control flow absent an admitted extension |
| A sender transmits optional =:outcome= but knows only the Request id | Reject the actual transmitted field; optionality does not exempt sender knowledge or disclosure |

These examples constrain the finished system's semantic admission and
correspondence obligations without prescribing a particular wire format,
queue implementation or distributed transport.

*** Projection preserves necessary communication but does not worship messages

Communication is one way to establish knowledge, not the definition of
knowledge.

A projector preserves a declared communication when that occurrence is part of
the global observable protocol, establishes a necessary causal predecessor, or
is the knowledge source required for another role's behavior. It does not
discard a required send/receive merely because an unrelated later read could
return an equal value. Only an authored alternative or a checked refinement
under a specified observation and recovery contract can change how a global
communication obligation is realized.

Nor does the projector invent a redundant direct message merely because one
actor's choice eventually affects another actor. If the *authored* continuation
requires a proposition that the other actor may instead establish by another
admitted path, the legitimate sources include:

- justified entry knowledge;
- authoritative observation;
- supported deterministic derivation from facts already known to that role;
- another declared and justified knowledge path.

These paths are not interchangeable merely because their values have the same
shape. Each source must establish the proposition actually required by the
continuation, including its subject, role, authority/provenance class,
decision or command correlation when needed, temporal scope, and disclosure
permission. The evidence must arrive before the role's first dependent action.
A declared assertion is usable as an *assertion* premise when that is the
contract; it does not automatically establish authoritative truth.

An authoritative reread can establish current permitted state, subject to its
acquisition, freshness/basis, scope, and progression contract. It does not
automatically prove that a particular earlier command committed or that a
specific historical branch was taken. Where the continuation depends on that
history, the design supplies a command-bound receipt or outcome, a retained
authoritative fact with the necessary correlation, or a different explicitly
authored continuation. A current observation and a command-specific decision
may be compatible without being equivalent.

The choice of knowledge path also determines recovery. A durable authority
rendezvous requires suitable initial acquisition and reconnect/missed-advisory
repair so a dropped notification does not permanently hide a required fact.
An advisory may justify starting or repeating an authorized acquisition; it is
not itself the new authoritative data, proof of visibility, or proof of browser
installation/adoption. By contrast, a genuinely ephemeral report may permit
loss and expiry without a durable reread: reconstructing old transient intent
from a later database value would change its semantics.

Neither optimization grants an unlimited liveness claim. If progress depends
on eventual acquisition, fair delivery, an active subscription, or a user's
Refresh action, the guarantee names those premises. A projector cannot solve
a missing semantic producer by assuming that a future read or message will
eventually appear.

This matters deeply for Gesso's web architecture because durable business facts
often rendezvous naturally through model authority and reread rather than
long-lived direct participant messaging.


HumanHelp Request publication and a collaborative typing indicator illustrate
two different recovery contracts, detailed in the following sections. Their
contrast is a construction requirement, not a heuristic that all durable data
must use rereads or all transient data must use direct messages.

| Required local meaning | Permissible coordination, subject to its contract |
|------------------------+---------------------------------------------------|
| Current authorized Request projection | Scoped advisory or discovery followed by authoritative acquisition and justified adoption |
| Outcome of one particular Request creation command | Command-bound authoritative result/receipt or other admitted correlated evidence |
| Recent admitted typing intention | Authorized ephemeral report with source ordering and expiry; loss may be acceptable |
| Required authored participant communication | Admitted receipt of that semantic occurrence, or an expressly authorized refinement preserving its relevant observables |

*** Necessary-communication acceptance counterexamples

| Tempting substitution | Required result |
|-----------------------+-----------------|
| A compiler removes an authored participant message because a later query may return the same field | Preserve the message or demonstrate an admitted contract-preserving refinement; shape equality is insufficient |
| A helper rereads =:approved= and claims its own earlier command was approved | Require command-specific correlated evidence or an explicitly different continuation |
| A new Request is committed but the advisory is lost and no discovery/reconnect path exists | Reject the claimed required convergence under loss, or name the missing delivery premise |
| A browser receives a board advisory and marks an unacquired card as adopted | Do not establish authoritative content or installation from an advisory alone |
| A typing report is dropped, and a later database query is claimed to reconstruct that transient report | Preserve the declared ephemeral loss/expiry semantics; do not manufacture historical intent |
| A role has a valid fact from another principal's or Organization's scope | Reject unrelated knowledge even if its value equals the required fact |

These are obligations of completed Gesso. An application may deliberately
choose either communication or authority-mediated rendezvous when the chosen
path satisfies the actual semantic, disclosure and recovery contract.

**** Durable Request publication and selective adoption

For a newly created Request, the authoritative result belongs to the public
Request model. The creator learns the permitted command outcome and reacquires
the declared card or board projection. Other clients need not receive the whole
Request from the creator's execution. A command-bound accepted result can
also justify immediate presentation of the creator's newly created card when
the result and membership-adoption contract supply the required content and
scope. The framework does not force an additional board reread merely to
display an already admitted result. Updating one card, introducing a card
into a filtered or sorted board, and adopting complete board membership are
different propositions; each requires the corresponding declaration and
completion evidence. An unresolved command cannot be shown as a committed
creation solely because an optimistic view was rendered.

#+begin_src clojure
{:operation/id :request/create
 :authority :request
 :caller-role :customer
 :command-protocol :stable-bound-command
 :accepted-continuation
 {:creator-acquisition :request/created-card
  :remote-coordination :request/board-advisory}
 :rejected-continuation :request/create-rejected
 :unknown-continuation :request/reconcile-create}
#+end_src

This is an inspection view of imported contracts. The admitted create
operation supplies the authorized caller and command-specific created Request
identity. An arbitrary new row found by a query does not establish which Request
this command created.

Remote coordination is triggered from the authoritative incorporation or a
recoverable publication handoff under its declared contract. Required
invalidation cannot depend solely on the creator's browser receiving a response
and deciding to notify peers. Publication failure after commit leaves a
committed command plus delivery debt; it is not a rejected create command.
Required publication is secured by an atomic authoritative change/publication
intent or an equivalently qualified durable discovery mechanism. A best-effort
post-commit notification alone cannot establish no-lost-change recovery when
a process can fail between commit and publication. The recovery contract
preserves work identity, scope and required progression across retries and
router or subscriber replacement. Part IV owns the atomicity, propagation
and repair obligations of that handoff.

An advisory identifies a permitted scope and justified acquisition requirement.
Before the browser records it, the shared-event admission establishes the
current subscription or fragment owner, source registration and instance,
source provenance, permitted transition, and scope/requirement applicability.
Matching a subscription identifier or accepting a transport event by itself
does not establish those facts. Advisory payload data cannot relax the
registered producer, recipient or disclosure contract.
Its disclosure does not reveal a hidden Request by implication. A lower bound
must be supplied by the admitted producer or a declared fence protocol, not
invented from the receiver's old basis. Subscriptions and reacquisition cover
initial registration, reconnect, and missed-notification gaps through a declared
read/registration fence, durable cursor, buffered event interval, periodic
authorized rediscovery with stated premises, or an equivalent protocol.
Neither a new subscription alone nor an old snapshot plus best-effort events
establishes gap-free awareness. If a producer lacks a minimum progression
bound, a tokenless advisory needs another qualified fence or read rule;
"newer than the recipient's last displayed basis" can be too weak.

The browser uses the pure transition and effect-shell separation from Part II.
The following calls illustrate the semantic steps, not a second independent
business-rule authority or a requirement to use these exact helper names:

#+begin_src clojure
(def after-advisory
  (board-runtime/step current-board admitted-board-advisory))

(def refresh-start
  (board-runtime/step
    (:state after-advisory)
    admitted-user-refresh-event))

;; The shell executes the emitted acquisition effect. It retains its
;; registered owner, concrete instance, scope, and principal-context binding.
(:effects refresh-start)

;; The completion is admitted against the latest board state, not the state
;; captured before the request. A later installation completion may be needed.
(board-runtime/step latest-board admitted-acquisition-completion)
#+end_src

The server derives the caller from its actual authentication context. The
browser effect cannot grant a principal identity by carrying a field. The
returned observation is bound to the full query, projection, visibility,
temporal context, authority, and basis required by the acquisition contract.

Acquisition, permitted installation, actual installation, and semantic adoption
are separate where the realization makes them separate. An acquire-only
obligation may be discharged by an admitted read, but an acquire-and-adopt
obligation that requires physical installation is not discharged by a completed
HTTP request, validated response, proposed swap or mere installation permission.
A pure state update does not itself swap a DOM node. An asynchronous
installation must report its admitted completion or failure, under the same
owner, target instance, scope and observation that authorized the effect.
The last installation-right check and the physical mutation must share an
appropriate serialization or equivalent protection against target replacement. Adoption discharges only requirements that the
installed/adopted observation actually covers, preserving later or unrelated
requirements and respecting the current owner. Coverage is about the
authorized query and its visibility, projection, basis and temporal semantics,
not about the presence of a particular hidden row in the rendered HTML.
An observation can correctly cover an advisory without exposing the Request
when the current principal or active filters exclude it. A stronger admitted
acquisition may discharge more than the requirement that initiated it, but
not an unrelated scope or a requirement whose adoption milestone has not
been reached. Physical effects run outside
retriable state-update functions. The worked X18/X19/X20 race in Part II fixes
the intended interpretation: a permitted X19 installation may satisfy X18
while retaining an intervening X20 requirement under partial-advance policy.
Under an all-current-requirements policy, that X19 installation instead
defers until sufficiently covering evidence is acquired. The policy is
declarative and must not change according to which asynchronous callback
runs first. An owner, principal, query, resource or target replacement
invalidates the old installation right even if the response's basis is newer.
The accepted pure transition is the only source of emitted physical effects.

Bob may therefore retain the old board while showing a Refresh affordance.
The notification does not establish the new Request's title or visibility;
authoritative reacquisition does. Eventual adoption remains conditional on the
declared automatic or user trigger, not merely on the database being available.
The creator's immediate accepted-command update and other viewers' selective
Refresh policy can therefore coexist without giving the creator's browser
authority to publish trusted facts for those viewers.

*** Durable-publication acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required construction or execution outcome |
|-------------------+--------------------------------------------|
| A Request commits, then the web node crashes before an unprotected best-effort advisory | Preserve required publication/recovery debt or provide qualified durable discovery; do not reinterpret the committed command as rejected |
| A viewer subscribes after the initial read and misses a change in the registration gap | Require a fence, replay, buffering or qualified rediscovery under the claimed awareness guarantee |
| A correctly shaped advisory arrives for a retired subscription instance | Reject the obsolete shared event; payload validity does not restore source/owner rights |
| A remote advisory includes a hidden Request title that the recipient is not entitled to see | Reject prohibited disclosure; advisory permission is not permission to reveal domain content |
| A tokenless advisory is satisfied by reading merely later than the viewer's old basis | Require qualified coverage of the triggering change or preserve unresolved reacquisition debt |
| The creator's command has an unknown disposition and the optimistic card is treated as a committed new row | Reconcile the stable command identity; optimistic display cannot prove authoritative incorporation |
| A correlated accepted create result updates one card, then code assumes the entire board's sorted membership is current | Check and admit the separate membership/query scope or reacquire as the contract requires |
| An X19 result is installed while X20 remains outstanding | Discharge only requirements X19 actually covers under the declared adoption policy; retain or defer X20 accordingly |
| An HTTP response completes but its HTMX swap is cancelled or its target retires | Retain acquire-and-adopt debt unless another admitted installation/adoption path actually satisfies it |
| A valid newer observation returns for an old principal context or replaced board owner | Reject obsolete installation rights; newer basis does not overcome scope or resource-instance mismatch |
| A complete authorized board observation omits a Request hidden by access policy or selected filter | Permit coverage only under the actual query/visibility contract; do not reveal the hidden row or mistake omission for failed propagation |
| An effect is executed inside a retriable pure state update | Reject duplicate or uncontrolled host effects; interpret only effects of the accepted state transition |

These are requirements of completed Gesso. They do not prescribe a single
database, routing topology, browser shell, or selective-adoption policy.

**** Ephemeral typing reports

A typing indicator has a different meaning. It reports that an admitted editor
channel recently sent a typing intention; it does not prove that a human is
physically typing and need not have a durable database fact to reread.

The wire payload carries intent, not sender identity. An untrusted client
cannot select the admitted actor, authenticated principal, source instance,
recipient, or ordering epoch by adding fields to this payload:

#+begin_src clojure
(def TypingPayload
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:document/id uuid?]
   [:typing? boolean?]])

(def valid-typing-payload? (m/validator TypingPayload))

(def typing-contract
  {:message/id :document/typing
   :from :editor
   :to :peer-editor
   :payload TypingPayload
   :sender-binding :authenticated-registered-document-channel
   :receiver-binding :authorized-document-subscription
   :delivery {:durability :ephemeral
              :delivery-guarantee :best-effort
              :replay :none}
   :ordering {:source :registered-actor-stream
              :duplicates :ignore
              :older-sequence :ignore}
   :display {:meaning :recent-admitted-typing-report
             :expires-after-receipt-ms 3000}})
#+end_src

The trusted boundary uses the registered channel and current document policy.
The following functions are contract sketches: the complete implementation
may elaborate them from admitted declarations, without requiring applications
to write a second, unchecked authentication or schema language:

#+begin_src clojure
(defn admit-typing-message!
  [registered-channel decoded-message]
  (when-not (valid-typing-payload? decoded-message)
    (throw (ex-info "Invalid typing payload"
                    {:error/type :typing/invalid-payload})))
  (let [document-id (:document/id decoded-message)
        sender (channels/require-current-sender! registered-channel)
        permission
        (document-model/require-typing-permission!
          (:trusted-context sender) document-id)]
    (typing-boundary/issue!
      {:contract :document/typing
       :registered-source registered-channel
       :sender sender
       :permission permission
       :payload decoded-message})))
#+end_src

These API names specify boundary responsibilities. The channel supplies the
authenticated principal, admitted actor, current source instance, and their
binding; the payload supplies none of those. The model permission result binds
that principal and document under the declared authorization timing policy.
Issuance checks their correspondence and constructs the receiver envelope.
Recipient routing separately checks each subscription's permission to receive
this document's indicators. Source admission and recipient admission are
distinct: a legitimate editor report is not automatically authorized for
every peer, document view, or account context. Later revocation follows the
explicit subscription policy; initial registration is not an indefinite
disclosure grant. The admitted receiver envelope binds the registered
sender/actor, document, source instance and applicable ordering evidence.
These fields are issued or verified by the trusted boundary, not accepted
as additional assertions from the adversarial wire payload.

The issuer assigns a sequence within the admitted actor-stream instance, or
uses another admitted ordering mechanism with the same contract. The
sequence is part of the trusted report envelope, not a client-chosen field in
=TypingPayload=. For the sequence-based realization, the issuer supplies a
strictly advancing value for accepted reports in one registered stream; the
receiver compares it only against that stream's admitted high-water mark.
Delivery remains best-effort and may be reordered; the sequence rule means
older accepted reports cannot reverse newer display state, not that every
issued report will be delivered.

Reconnection requires an admitted new source binding and a declared retirement
or succession rule. Neither a random larger sequence nor an unfamiliar epoch
may replace the current source merely by arriving. An old stream's delayed
=:typing? false= or =true= must not change the replacement indicator. Different
source instances have no implied numeric ordering, even for the same actor;
their precedence comes from the source/owner admission contract. This
provides ordering of admitted reports, not proof of when the sender actually
typed.

The peer maintains one indicator owner for the document, admitted actor, local
principal context, and source instance. The following table is its semantic
transition contract:

| Event | Required admission | Result and effect |
|-------+--------------------+-------------------|
| New =typing? true= report | Correct source and owner; sequence exceeds last accepted within that stream | Retain sequence, show indicator, retire old timer, allocate a fresh expiry resource |
| New =typing? false= report | Same checks | Retain sequence, retire timer, hide indicator, remain ready for later reports |
| Duplicate or older report | Same source but non-advancing sequence | No display change and no expiry extension |
| Expiry callback | Current timer instance still owns this indicator and its deadline has elapsed | Hide indicator and retire that timer; retain the accepted source sequence |
| Retired timer/source callback | No current ownership | Reject/no-op; do not hide or revive a replacement indicator |
| Navigation, account change, or subscription retirement | Owner lifecycle transition | Retire indicator resources and reject later events under the old owner |

The sequence high-water mark remains while that registered stream can deliver
messages, including after a stop or expiry. Otherwise a delayed duplicate could
resurrect an indicator. A =typing? false= report changes presentation, not the
fact that a higher-sequence report was accepted. Timer expiry likewise does
not reset report ordering. Safe reclamation of a stream's ordering state
requires its admitted retirement/fencing contract to exclude future delivery
into the retired owner; mere invisibility or elapsed display expiry is not
such a fence. Bounds and cleanup follow that lifecycle, and identities from
unrelated streams are not numerically ordered.

The timer uses a local elapsed-time contract. The display claim is three
seconds after the last accepted =typing? true= report, not three seconds after
an unverifiable human action. The admitted receipt determines the local expiry
deadline; a =typing? false= report retires the timer rather than scheduling a
new display deadline. Newer admitted =true= reports replace both the deadline
and its owning timer instance. A timer callback must pass current owner,
instance and deadline admission before requesting the hide effect; checking a
callback's timer id alone is insufficient if its target or subscription was
replaced. A delayed first delivery can still show a recent-receipt report;
if the product instead requires bounded age from issuance, it needs additional
age and clock/delivery premises. A suspended browser rechecks expiry when it
resumes rather than treating a late timer callback as evidence of continued
typing. Timer scheduling alone is not a hard wall-clock guarantee. If the
physical shell cannot run at the intended deadline, its eventual completion
must still be admitted under the current semantic state. Physical timer and
DOM effects run only from accepted transitions, not inside retriable pure
state updates or direct callback mutations of projected machine state.

Envelope consumers bind =:document/id= and =:actor/id= to distinct local names,
such as =document-id= and =actor-id=. Their UUID-shaped representations must not
erase the distinction between the document being discussed and the sender. This
is the ordinary authoring consequence of Part II's identity categories.

If a report is lost, a database reread two minutes later cannot reconstruct it
usefully. The indicator may simply remain absent or expire; there is no required
durable replay. That differs from required acquisition of durable Request state:

| Property | Request publication | Typing report |
|----------+---------------------+---------------|
| Durable semantic truth | Public Request model | None required for typing intent |
| Remote coordination | Scoped advisory and authoritative acquisition | Admitted ephemeral message |
| Loss handling | Declared repair/discovery path for required convergence | Absence or expiry is permitted |
| Local resource protection | Acquisition and installation owner checks | Source ordering and timer-instance checks |

Projection preserves the selected knowledge and recovery path. It must neither
replace genuinely ephemeral semantics with a later database query nor require
direct peer delivery where a declared authoritative rendezvous is sufficient.
It also must not turn a best-effort presence hint into a guaranteed delivery,
proof of ongoing human activity, or an authoritative statement of editor
membership. An indicator is a scoped presentation of an admitted recent report.

*** Ephemeral-report acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required admission or result |
|-------------------+------------------------------|
| A sender adds =:actor/id= and =:principal/id= to the client payload | Reject undeclared fields and derive sender identity from the registered channel, not client bytes |
| A valid document-scoped typing report is sent to a peer whose document access was revoked | Enforce the recipient subscription's current disclosure policy; legitimate source admission is not universal recipient authorization |
| A delayed =true= sequence 8 arrives after admitted =false= sequence 9 from the same stream | Ignore the older report without restarting display or expiry |
| An old stream sends sequence 9999 after reconnection established a new admitted source | Reject the retired source; unrelated stream counters do not establish precedence |
| A =false= report hides the indicator and its sequence high-water mark is cleared | Retain replay protection while that source can still deliver; delayed earlier =true= must not revive it |
| An expiry timer from the previous =true= report fires after a newer report replaced its resource instance | Reject the retired callback; it must not hide the replacement indicator |
| The browser suspends beyond expiry, then handles a delayed callback as proof of fresh typing | Check the local deadline and current owner; no callback establishes continuing human activity |
| A timer callback updates DOM directly from captured, obsolete document/account state | Require an admitted current-owner transition and physical effect; no direct bypass of semantic ownership |
| A report is lost and a later Request-like database reread is treated as a replay of that typing intention | Preserve permissible ephemeral loss and expiry; an unrelated current read cannot reconstruct the missing occurrence |
| A typing report passes the shape validator and is treated as proof that a human is actively typing | Retain only the declared recent admitted intention meaning and its local expiry contract |

These are finished-system acceptance obligations, not claims that any
particular backend or browser timer already realizes them.

*** Local executable guards belong in the executable projection

Proof derivations and global proof trees do not belong in the production
browser.

However, a local predicate or guard whose result is required to execute the
projected semantics is runtime-semantic data and must remain in the executable
projection or in a trusted, admitted realization bound to that projection.
The generated local program retains the guard's operational contract,
required inputs, evaluation point, and failure continuation. A trusted
server-only authorization or authoritative progression check must not be
weakened into a browser-computed Boolean merely to make every guard portable.
For each physical role, the assembled application binds the guard to the
place with the authority and information needed to enforce it.

A compilation or proof artifact can establish that a guard is necessary or
sufficient under stated assumptions. It cannot replace the actual check
when the premises depend on the current runtime owner, resource, principal,
observation, or target. A guard used to establish a proof premise is also
part of the exact semantic contract on which that proof depends.

The distinction is:

| Material | Required location |
|----------+-------------------|
| Guard checking that resource instance I still owns the right to resume execution E | Executable semantics where that transition needs it |
| Derivation explaining why that guard contributes to a property | Proof or diagnostic artifact, bound to the exact executable subject |

The guard identifies the execution and resource instance required by this
callback's admission contract; it is not a universal generation comparison.
Shared fragment or subscription events use their own declared ownership checks.
An effect completion may also require the effect invocation, target, current
plan, scope, operation or command identity according to its contract.
Conversely, a boundary that does not depend on a given identity category
need not manufacture a corresponding universal counter.

Passing an ownership guard does not by itself establish the knowledge,
provenance, or authority required for the resumed branch. The owning runtime
also validates the normalized completion or failure, current transition,
declared input/output schema, and applicable admission evidence. Guard
evaluation and the state transition it protects must be serialized or
otherwise protected against relevant owner replacement; a check followed
by an unprotected asynchronous mutation reopens the race. The physical
effect interpreter executes only effects of accepted transitions, with
declared retry, idempotency, and resource-retirement behavior.

The executable semantic core evaluates runtime guards as part of the admitted
transition semantics. A proof kernel or dedicated semantic checker establishes
its stated judgment about those semantics; it does not replace the runtime
admission check or supply missing runtime facts.

The production machine must never depend on an optional proof sidecar to decide
a transition. A sidecar can carry semantic origins, property judgments,
assumptions, and diagnostic paths when bound to the exact executable subject,
but it cannot become a hidden production guard, authority token, or alternate
state store. Removing optional proof/diagnostic material cannot alter the
runtime transition relation. Conversely, removing an executable guard from
a supposedly optimized plan changes the semantics and invalidates claims
that depended on that guard.

*** Projection failure should identify the missing justification

When projection cannot produce a legal local machine, diagnostics should explain
the actual semantic deficit.

Useful failure information includes:

- role;
- global state/transition;
- local behaviors that diverge;
- required discriminator;
- facts currently known;
- available provenance;
- missing knowledge path;
- candidate communication or authoritative-observation paths when derivable;
- the violated obligation and the admission stage that failed;
- whether the conclusion is proven invalid, unresolved under an opaque
  predicate or assumption, or blocked by an unsupported language construct;
- relevant source-to-projected locations and contract identities, where
  available.

A generic "projection failed" error throws away precisely the information that
the verifier already computed. Diagnostics must also distinguish a missing
knowledge path from an actual failure of source authentication, guard
ownership, resource correspondence, or authoritative realization. A private
discriminator's absence may be named as an obligation without disclosing its
value or the private policy itself.

These explanations are compiler/assurance evidence, not a permission to expose
every source fact to a runtime user. Diagnostic rendering, structured errors,
logs, and proof reports enforce their respective disclosure contracts. A
redacted error retains useful obligation and location identifiers where
permitted. If a checker cannot establish the property, it must reject or
expose an unresolved obligation; it must not label it proved or quietly
supply a fabricated guard or knowledge path.

*** Host-language callbacks may not bypass projected semantics

A local runtime may invoke host-language functions to realize declared local
effects, but arbitrary callbacks must not become an alternate transition path.

The admitted interaction is:

#+begin_example
projected semantic transition
    -> declared effect with owner, resource and contract identity
    -> registered host implementation
    -> normalized completion or failure
    -> owner-relative event admission
    -> projected semantic transition and accepted effects
#+end_example

The forbidden alternative is:

#+begin_example
host callback
    -> mutate choreography state or protected DOM target directly
       using captured or unchecked authority
#+end_example

The latter reopens the proof and knowledge boundary. A host callback can
produce a message or normalized event, but cannot unilaterally confer its
acceptance. An ignored stale completion must not be reinterpreted as proof
that the corresponding authoritative command rejected, never committed,
or lost an independently recoverable result. Execution-private effects,
shared advisories, and physical installation callbacks each retain their
own actual owner and acceptance conditions.

The common boundary is
[[#req-owner-relative-event-admission][owner-relative event admission]] in Part II.
Part VI develops its browser implementation. An execution-private effect
completion must not bypass that boundary, and a declared shared event must use
its actual fragment or subscription owner rather than an invented execution.

*** Guard-and-diagnostic acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required completed-system outcome |
|-------------------+-----------------------------------|
| A projected browser callback resumes after its resource instance was retired | Reject the stale completion; equality of a command id or plan name cannot restore the resource's owner rights |
| A fragment advisory is associated with the last active command execution because the event lacks an execution id | Apply the registered fragment/subscription owner and source contract; do not invent an execution |
| A browser claims its local =:authorized?= guard passed and submits an authoritative mutation | Bind current authentication, authorization, and authoritative execution at the trusted server boundary; client Boolean is not that evidence |
| An effect callback checks a current target, yields, then installs into a replacement DOM target | Enforce owner check and mutation under an admitted serialized or equivalent protected boundary |
| The projected machine consumes a normalized failure that lacks the required effect invocation correlation | Reject or recover under its declared contract; a value with the right shape is not a matching completion |
| A production optimization omits a runtime guard because the proof sidecar once stated it was sufficient | Preserve the executable admission contract and requalify affected evidence; the sidecar is not a runtime replacement |
| A proof/diagnostic sidecar is absent, but the runtime attempts to load it to decide the next branch | Keep executable transitions independent of optional sidecar data or reject the defective runtime assembly |
| Projection cannot justify a branch, and the compiler inserts a synthetic authority observation to make it type-check | Reject or expose the actual unmet obligation; only an authored path or justified refinement may change the semantics |
| A projection error includes a private threshold and an authorized user's role lacks permission to view it | Redact protected facts while retaining permitted location and obligation context |
| A stale browser callback is ignored and the command status is recorded as rejected | Preserve independent command outcome reconciliation; event retirement does not establish authoritative disposition |

These are normative acceptance cases for finished Gesso. Runtime guard
placement and implementation languages may vary, but every claimed
projection and deployed realization must preserve the corresponding
admission, resource, evidence, and disclosure obligations.

** Long logical workflows and short runtime executions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-durable-workflow-boundary
:END:

A choreography may describe a logical workflow that spans human-scale time.
Its business meaning can remain continuous while its participating runtime
executions start and finish independently. Neither the global description nor
the presence of an =:await= operation implies that one Aleph node, browser, or
other process must retain a suspended machine in memory for hours or days.

An in-execution wait and a durable business wait have different contracts.
An active role-local =:await= suspends that execution until an admitted event,
retirement or recovery decision. A durable business wait instead records a
model-owned pending condition or obligation from which later authorized actors
can establish new entry facts. That distinction is explicit in the assembly;
neither form silently acquires the guarantees of the other.

For example, a business flow may conceptually involve:

An approval workflow requests approval, waits under its domain policy, and
continues when an admitted decision establishes the next condition.

A robust HumanHelp realization may instead be:

| Period | Short execution and durable connection |
|--------+----------------------------------------|
| Request submission | E1 incorporates pending approval, binds its command result, and records required durable follow-up under the authoritative operation contract |
| Human-scale delay | Pending domain state and any deadline/work obligation remain discoverable and recoverable under their declared authority and retention contract |
| Approver action | E2 starts with its own authenticated principal, admitted plan, permitted current observation and explicit selection, then incorporates a guarded decision |
| Later requester action | E3 separately acquires its authorized entry facts, if further work is needed, without inheriting E1's or E2's execution-private resources |

The durable wait lives in model state, along with whatever command/outcome,
pending-work, deadline or notification obligations the business contract
requires. Durable existence alone does not guarantee that a later actor will
discover or act on it. Where progress is required, the application declares
the discoverable query, permitted recipients, wakeup or scheduling strategy,
missed-notification repair, and assumptions about authorization, actor
action, availability and fairness. A pending state without any permitted
continuation is not a completed workflow.

Subject to the authoritative store's declared durability, recovery,
retention and compatibility assumptions, the durable workflow fact can survive:

- web-node failure;
- deployment;
- load-balancer rerouting;
- client disconnect;
- browser suspension;
- long offline periods.

The ordinary Gesso choreography contract does not promise durable persistence,
transparent relocation or automatic migration of arbitrary suspended
server-local executions. A durable model record, an admitted command receipt,
and a recoverable task are not snapshots of all in-flight runtime state.

Applications that require persisted workflow-machine continuations use a
separately admitted, opt-in capability. Its contract covers stable checkpoint
identity, atomicity relative to authoritative decisions and pending effects,
recovery discovery, owner/fencing transfer, plan and data compatibility,
replay and idempotency, uncertain effect disposition, resource reconstruction,
expiry, cancellation, and eventual retirement. Restarting from a serialized
machine map without these guarantees is not durable-continuation admission.
The capability may be realized by a dedicated workflow engine or a
Gesso-managed facility, provided the same semantic obligations hold.
No such facility is imposed on ordinary short choreographies.

*** Durable waiting belongs in authoritative state by default

Human-scale waiting is normally represented as durable domain state rather than
a suspended in-memory choreography execution.

This has several consequences.

First, subject to those persistence, retention and compatibility assumptions,
the durable workflow meaning can survive:

- deployment;
- process loss;
- browser suspension;
- load-balancer rerouting;
- reconnect;
- long periods without any participant online.

Second, later actors can acquire the relevant facts by authorized
authoritative observation rather than requiring the original process to resume.
The observation establishes current, scoped eligibility; the actor's fresh
execution still checks its own authorization and operation guards at
incorporation. A queue entry or notification is not a grant to approve every
visible item, and the later execution does not automatically inherit the
historical outcome or transient authority of a prior command.

Third, a deadline belonging to business policy is durable authoritative
meaning, not merely a browser or process-local timer. If the contract requires
action after a deadline, a qualified durable scheduler, recovery scan or
equivalent admitted mechanism owns that obligation. The scheduled attempt
must recheck current eligibility and distinguish deadline occurrence,
effect dispatch, and successful incorporation. A delayed callback cannot
retroactively turn an expired authorization or retired resource into a valid
operation.

Fourth, a durable operation with unfinished publication, external-provider
work or command resolution retains that work's stable identity and recovery
owner after its initiating execution retires. A process failure or timeout
does not prove the corresponding effect never occurred. Reconciliation and
retries follow the public operation's idempotency and disclosure rules;
a new execution identity does not silently create a new command intent.

Finally, the framework avoids making one runtime's memory an accidental
source of business truth. The later-execution and expense-history examples
in the following section elaborate those identity and command boundaries.

*** Durable-wait acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required semantic outcome |
|-------------------+---------------------------|
| E1 records =:pending= only in an in-memory Choreo machine and its web node crashes | Do not claim durable workflow survival; persist the required authoritative state and recovery obligations |
| A durable pending record exists but no actor, queue, schedule or discovery path can reach it | Do not claim required eventual completion; supply a qualified continuation strategy or state the missing progress premise |
| E2 starts from an authorized pending queue but directly approves every row visible in it | Require explicit admitted selection where the application needs it and recheck current operation guards at incorporation |
| A user was authorized when E1 started, so E2 inherits that principal and capability days later | Reject borrowed transient authority; E2 establishes a fresh principal, knowledge, plan and current permission |
| An E1 notification fails after commit, and the application reports E1's authoritative command rejected | Keep the committed outcome and separately recover required publication debt |
| A process-local deadline timer fires after the workflow's authority, owner or policy changed | Recheck durable deadline and current business eligibility; local timer arrival is not authorization |
| A worker times out on an external effect and starts a new command to perform the same business intent | Preserve stable command/effect binding and reconcile uncertainty before any retry or newly admitted intent |
| A persisted continuation is loaded with an incompatible plan and reused effect capabilities from its old process | Require checked migration or retirement, owner fencing, effect recovery, and reconstructed resources |
| Two approvers concurrently observe pending state and both submit approval commands | Authoritative incorporation must guard the current state and command binding; earlier read eligibility is not a shared commit permit |
| The workflow has a graph path to an approved terminal but every eligible actor may remain offline forever | State the environmental fairness and actor-action premises; graph reachability is not a liveness proof |

These requirements describe completed Gesso independently of one particular
process manager, workflow engine, database, transport, or scheduler.

*** A later choreography is not automatically the same execution

When durable state connects two periods of activity, the later protocol may be
a new execution with a new execution identity.

For example:

| Execution | Command | Durable effect when accepted |
|-----------+---------+------------------------------|
| E1 | C1 | Submission becomes pending |
| E2, later | C2 | Approval decision is recorded |

The business process is continuous in domain meaning, but E1 and E2 need not be
one persistent protocol execution. E2 must establish its own authenticated
principal, current model eligibility, required knowledge, and applicable plan.
The existence of E1's durable result does not carry E1's transient authority or
resource ownership into E2. Its accepted result can establish durable facts
about the domain and the command that caused them, within the model's
authorized observation contract. It is not a reusable principal credential,
an open effect handle, or evidence that the later actor may commit without
rechecking current business rules. A new protocol execution may nevertheless
continue the same logical workflow without a suspended E1 machine.

This distinction avoids conflating:

- semantic command identity;
- protocol execution identity;
- durable domain identity;
- workflow/business identity.

An operation's command identity also differs from each physical invocation,
transport retry, authoritative basis and resource instance used to carry it.
The assembly must preserve the distinctions relevant to each boundary, even
when several belong to one domain workflow. A compatible retry can have a new
transport/request identity while retaining its original semantic command.
A later deliberate business action has its own command, even when it targets
the same Expense and uses an equivalent-looking payload.

Where an application truly requires a durable suspended execution, that feature
must define its own:

- persistence;
- ownership;
- versioning;
- migration;
- replay;
- resource cleanup;
- liveness;
- recovery semantics.

It is not silently inherited from ordinary Choreo.


The expense example extends across several days without keeping a suspended
server process. Its three periods of activity have these contracts:

| Period | Entry knowledge and action | Authoritative obligation | Completion meaning |
|--------+----------------------------+--------------------------+--------------------|
| Monday, employee submission | Selected owned Expense and admitted submit intent | Guard current ownership, draft status, and submission policy; record submission and command result atomically | Recorded submission of C1, or explicit rejection/unknown handling |
| Thursday, manager review | Authorized queue observation, explicit selection, coherent Expense/Policy observation | Guard current manager permission, submitted status, policy relation and selected route; incorporate either manager approval or escalation, with C2 bound to the selected operation | Recorded manager approval *or* recorded awaiting-Finance disposition; rejected and unknown outcomes take their own continuations |
| Friday, Finance review, if escalated | Authorized pending-Finance observation and explicit selection | Guard current Finance permission, awaiting-Finance status, and applicable policy; record C3 with the Finance decision | Recorded Finance approval, or explicit rejection/unknown handling; no Finance execution is required for the manager-approved branch |

Each row expands to a short choreography with the same explicit model-result
mapping used earlier. Queue selection is an input event that may cancel or become
stale. A manager's authorized observation can justify proposing one route, but
the authoritative model must enforce the selected operation's current manager
permission, status and policy at incorporation. If the policy, expense status,
actor or selected route becomes invalid, the operation follows its declared
rejection or uncertainty semantics; a prior queue read is not a commit-time
guard.

The manager-approved branch is already a completed business decision for
this example. The escalation branch makes the Expense eligible for a distinct
Finance execution later, subject to then-current authorization and state.
No silent automatic Finance approval or queue-wide action is implied.
Queue visibility does not authorize bulk approval, and a normal return
from an invocation is not by itself proof of successful incorporation.

Public model calls expose domain arguments and the bound command identity:

#+begin_src clojure
(expense-model/submit!
  trusted-employee-context
  {:expense-id E :command-id C1})

;; These are mutually exclusive manager alternatives, not two writes.
;; The admitted decision chooses one semantic operation before C2 is bound.
(case admitted-manager-route
  :approve-as-manager
  (expense-model/approve-as-manager!
    trusted-manager-context
    {:expense-id E :command-id C2})

  :escalate-to-finance
  (expense-model/mark-awaiting-finance!
    trusted-manager-context
    {:expense-id E :command-id C2}))

;; Only an Expense with admitted awaiting-Finance state can enter this route.
(expense-model/approve-as-finance!
  trusted-finance-context
  {:expense-id E :command-id C3})
#+end_src

These calls illustrate public operation contracts, not a standalone workflow
engine or a claim that these exact function Vars already exist. C2 is bound to
the chosen semantic operation, its canonical arguments, the appropriate
principal/authority scope and interface version before first submission.
It cannot be repurposed to try the opposite manager branch after an uncertain
result; doing so would be incompatible command reuse. The registered model
realization owns its guards, principal interpretation,
time source, and atomic command protocol. A policy may be evaluated from a
coherent authoritative Expense/Policy observation to present the permitted
choice, but the actual incorporation must protect every mutable dependency
on which that decision depends. A compatible snapshot, version expectation,
or atomic predicate/effect design may realize that contract; an unconstrained
read followed by a stale write may not. The business policy and operation
meaning are owned by public model interfaces, not duplicated by the UI
or the projected manager choice. Callers do not pass a mutable list of
commit guards that can omit ownership or status checks. The trusted context
comes from the admitted invocation boundary; constructing a map with a familiar
=:principal= field is not authentication.

If submission time is generated by the server, it follows the operation's time
contract. The model distinguishes a command's effective domain time,
recording/transaction time, and incidental invocation time where they differ.
A transport retry does not acquire a new semantic command merely
because the wall clock advanced. The recorded outcome retains the time actually
incorporated; compatibility of replay is checked against the intended semantic
arguments, not a fresh incidental timestamp.

A compatible retry checks command binding, retained-result applicability and
current disclosure permission before returning the recorded outcome. It does
not require Monday's Expense to remain a draft on Thursday. The historical
recorded result does not assert that the Expense is *currently* submitted or
that an earlier actor's authorization remains current. A new command requires
the current guards. If command evidence was pruned, restored from another
authority incarnation, or migrated incompatibly, the operation applies its
declared retention, identity and unavailable-result policy rather than
guessing non-incorporation.
Competing attempts of one command are interpreted against authoritative outcome
evidence; one failed attempt does not by itself reject the whole command.

When a result is unknown, recovery retains enough authorized command identity
and binding to reconcile later. Retiring E1's UI or process is allowed only under
the declared resolution or handoff contract; it must not erase the unresolved
obligation or create a new command to retry the same intent accidentally.
Current Expense state may have advanced beyond submission and is not by itself
an answer to whether C1 was incorporated. A pending reconciliation obligation
belongs to an admitted durable owner or recoverable reference after local
retirement; the UI's disappearance is not command resolution. A later
compatible retry or authorized command-result lookup must preserve the
original intent's operation and canonical argument binding.

An illustrative history and identity view is:

#+begin_src clojure
{:expense/id E
 :events
 [{:status :submitted
   :effective-at monday-time
   :recorded-at monday-recording-context
   :caused-by {:command C1 :operation :expense/submit}}
  {:status :awaiting-finance
   :effective-at thursday-time
   :recorded-at thursday-recording-context
   :caused-by {:command C2 :operation :expense/mark-awaiting-finance}}
  {:status :approved
   :effective-at friday-time
   :recorded-at friday-recording-context
   :caused-by {:command C3 :operation :expense/approve-as-finance}}]}

{:monday {:execution E1 :command C1 :principal employee-P
          :plan :plan/submit-expense-A12}
 :thursday {:execution E2 :command C2 :principal manager-M
            :plan :plan/review-expense-A13}
 :friday {:execution E3 :command C3 :principal finance-F
          :plan :plan/finance-review-A13}}
#+end_src

This is a permitted inspection history, not a requirement to store one growing
vector or a universal event DAG. It shows the *escalation branch* of manager
review; a manager-approved branch records its own approved outcome for C2
instead of inventing a Thursday escalation or a Friday Finance command.
The model's actual persistence, retention, correction, and command-evidence
contracts determine what can be reconstructed. Effective and recording time
remain distinct as in Part II. A current =:approved= status alone is not
evidence of which actor or command caused it; history and causal claims require
the relevant admitted retained records, not inference from status equality.

The business process is continuous because the authoritative Expense model
retains its meaning. The displayed escalation-history E1, E2 and E3 separately
establish their current principal, knowledge, permissions, resources, and
admitted plan. The business may be resumed under a newer compatible plan
without pretending that a paused E1 artifact survived unchanged. A new plan
can change permitted UI affordances or current policy under its declared
migration/compatibility contracts; it cannot retroactively rewrite C1's
recorded historical outcome. Historical workflow continuity does not transfer
a retired execution's capability or resource rights.

A durable workflow facility is an additional opt-in contract if an application
needs persisted continuations. It must state checkpoint atomicity relative to
effects, ownership or fencing, replay and deduplication, effect uncertainty,
schema/plan migration, recovery discovery, and resource reconstruction. Serializing
an in-memory machine map is not enough to establish those properties.

*** Later-execution and command-history acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required admission or evidence |
|-------------------+--------------------------------|
| A manager opens a pending queue on Thursday and bulk-approves all displayed Expenses | Require declared per-selection intent and current authoritative operation guards; queue visibility is not commit authority |
| A manager selects =:approve-as-manager= but the runtime invokes =:mark-awaiting-finance= using the same C2 | Reject operation/binding mismatch; C2's immutable semantic intent includes the chosen operation |
| A manager's policy read once said Finance was unnecessary, but Expense or Policy changes before commit | Enforce the actual current policy/Expense relation at incorporation or reject under the declared guard |
| The manager-approved Expense is later sent automatically to Finance because the global workflow describes a Friday period | Do not invent E3; Finance applies only to a properly recorded awaiting-Finance branch |
| Friday's Finance actor reuses Thursday's principal, plan capability or callback owner | Require fresh E3 identity, knowledge and permission; E2's runtime rights have retired |
| A compatible C1 retry arrives after Thursday's manager decision and fails because Expense is no longer draft | Resolve C1 using its retained compatible command record; replay does not require original new-incorporation guards |
| An unauthorized caller guesses C1 and demands its stored result | Enforce current disclosure policy; possession of a command id is not permission to learn its outcome |

| A server retry recalculates a later timestamp and changes the recorded submission outcome | Return the originally incorporated time under the command's time contract; transport retry is not a new event |

These are finished-system acceptance conditions. They do not require one
universal event log representation, an always-resident workflow machine,
a particular database engine, or these illustrative operation function names.

*** Durable model state is the normal rendezvous point

For long workflows, authoritative state provides a stable rendezvous point
between actors who were never concurrently connected. A later short execution
acquires permitted current facts through the model's declared observation
contract; it does not need a live communication channel to the earlier
participant or a preserved local machine from that participant's execution.

The durable subject, business workflow, command intent, protocol execution,
actor, plan, and transport attempt have distinct identities. A later actor can
continue work on the same durable subject without inheriting any earlier
principal credential, resource instance, command capability or projected
branch. A durable business fact establishes only the proposition its
authoritative source and observation scope justify. It is not an unbounded
certificate of every event in the subject's history.

A later actor may know what happened because authority establishes:

#+begin_example
:approval/pending
:approval/approved
:request/claimed
:request/done
#+end_example

not because it receives a packet from the actor who caused that state.
These keywords illustrate model-owned domain judgments, not privileged
universal Gesso statuses. An accepted =:request/done= observation establishes
the current status under its source, scope, visibility and temporal contract;
it does not automatically establish which earlier command or actor made it so.

This follows [[#req-authoritative-observation][Part II's authoritative observation
model]]: observation is a first-class knowledge path. A subsequent execution
receives only facts it is authorized to acquire. A scoped query, authorized
projection and justified basis may establish current eligibility, but the
public model operation still protects its current mutable business guards
at incorporation. Reading a queue does not grant the right to act on every
item in that queue.

Durable pending state is not itself a progress guarantee. The application
declares the opportunity for an eligible actor to discover and act on it,
or states the environmental premises on which progress depends. That contract
covers relevant authoritative queries, recipient visibility, initial and
reconnect acquisition, missed advisories, work/deadline scheduling if required,
and retry or reassignment after an owner disappears. It identifies the owner
of required unfinished work rather than assuming that some browser or
process-local callback will remain alive indefinitely.

Where loss of a required participant can strand the workflow, the
runtime-condition and recovery requirements from Part I apply. Any lease,
reassignment, cancellation, escalation, expiration, or other successor policy
remains model-owned and must preserve its current authorization, fencing,
command binding and atomicity requirements. An expired lease is not a
permission to repeat an uncertain effect under a new command. Lost
notifications cannot erase required durable work; failure to discover a
pending item cannot silently change it into a terminal business result.

A durable model can be available without satisfying liveness if all eligible
actors remain offline or decline to act. Progress claims name their delivery,
scheduler, availability, fairness and human-action premises. A proof of
reachable terminal states does not by itself prove eventual completion.

*** Deployment compatibility remains explicit

A durable business fact may outlive the executable plan that originally
produced it.

That is normal.

What cannot be assumed is that a suspended *runtime execution* from an older
deployment remains compatible forever. The corresponding contracts have
different persistence and compatibility boundaries:

| Subject across deployment | Required independently checked relationship |
|---------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| Durable domain state | The current model still interprets its admitted historical and current meanings, or a protected model-owned migration establishes them |
| Retained command/outcome and unresolved work | Original command binding, disclosure, retention, disposition and durable recovery owner remain interpretable or follow a declared typed unavailability/reconciliation contract |
| Projected execution and resource ownership | Continuation is explicitly supported by the old-to-new plan/assembly relation, or old rights are retired and a fresh execution is admitted |
| Physical client/server pairing | Actual route, protocol, codec, asset, producer/consumer and permitted rolling-release direction are supported or rejected before incompatible behavior |
| Assurance or diagnostic evidence | A claim remains applicable to its exact artifact, interfaces, assumptions and physical coverage; mere functional compatibility does not transfer an exact-coordinate proof |

The later stale-plan/recovery design therefore treats durable model truth,
command reconciliation, execution rights, deployed routing, and proof
applicability as separate dimensions. A matching plan name, URL, source file,
version number or copied digest label is not sufficient compatibility evidence.

A deployment may invalidate continuation of an old projection while leaving
the authoritative business state entirely valid. A safe recovery may retire
old execution-private callbacks and resources, then admit a *new* execution
under a current plan after authenticating the principal, acquiring justified
entry facts and checking command/observation compatibility. Where a
particular suspended execution is allowed to continue, a checked directional
continuation or migration contract establishes its state mapping, owner
transfer, pending effect treatment, input/output interpretation and
recovery premises. Neither path may silently reissue an unresolved
authoritative command with a fresh command identity.

Compatibility is directional and conditional on the installed realization.
An older browser talking to a newer server and a newer browser talking to an
older server can require different evidence; unsupported combinations must
be excluded by an enforced routing contract or receive typed retirement and
recovery behavior. An offline browser cannot retain indefinite continuation
rights simply because it once had a valid plan. Conversely, retiring the
old execution does not erase committed business work or command-result
records whose recovery lifetime outlasts that plan.

If deployment changes the meaning or representation of persisted domain
state, the public model requires its own compatible interpretation or
protected migration. Migration is an authoritative writer under the
applicable invariants, concurrency and authorization rules, not an
incidental side effect of running a new browser plan. Rolling upgrade and
rollback must account for the actual old/new writers and durable state
they can encounter; rollback is not an automatic undo of incorporated
domain migrations. Diagnostic sidecars may require exact artifact identity
even when a declared continuation compatibility relation permits a
different runtime artifact.

*** Durable-rendezvous and deployment acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required completed-system outcome |
|-------------------+-----------------------------------|
| A Request remains =:pending= after server restart, but no permitted query or recovery worker can rediscover it | Do not claim durable workflow progress; supply qualified discovery/ownership or name the missing premise |
| A new approver reads an authorized queue and uses the previous approver's principal or resource grant | Require current identity, permission and model incorporation guards; old execution rights do not transfer |
| An observer sees =:request/done= and attributes it to a particular old command without a retained outcome | Require command-specific evidence for causality; current status is not proof of that historical disposition |
| A required notification disappears during a router failover and the pending work becomes undiscoverable | Preserve durable publication/discovery or explicit weaker guarantees; local interest memory is not gap-free recovery |
| A lease expires while an external effect might have committed, and a replacement worker creates a new command | Reconcile the stable intent and fence retired owners; lease expiry is not proof of non-incorporation |
| A newer plan rejects the old runtime representation and the application deletes its durable domain object | Retire or reconstruct the runtime separately; keep valid model truth and perform protected domain migration only when needed |
| A stale client presents a matching plan name to a newer server with incompatible command outcomes | Require the scoped old-to-new continuation contract or typed recovery; name equality has no authority |
| A current client is routed to an older server that lacks its required decoder | Exclude the pairing through enforced routing or reject it before unsafe execution; old-to-new support alone is not symmetry |
| A proof sidecar for an old runtime artifact is attached to a merely compatible newer plan | Preserve exact-artifact proof binding or establish new evidence; continuation compatibility is not proof correspondence |
| The application retains a command ledger after old assets expire, then treats lookup failure as proof the command never committed | Apply declared retained-result, disclosure and unresolved/typed unavailability semantics; do not manufacture rejection |
| A release changes persisted domain status representation without validating old writer and rollback behavior | Enforce model migration and writer-coexistence obligations; browser reinitialization does not migrate business truth |
| A new plan automatically adopts all refreshed board membership, bypassing the application's explicit Refresh policy | Preserve the declared acquisition/adoption contract through deployment recovery, rather than silently adopting remote changes |

These obligations specify completed Gesso independently of an individual
deployment, particular browser, server framework, database, or routing
topology.

** Semantic acceptance obligations

The definitions above require executable judgments, constructive admission
paths, and focused conformance cases. Part IX owns compositional certification;
Part X owns soundness and implementation correspondence. The semantic
definitions used by construction, static verification, projection, the
portable machine, host realization, and assurance tools must agree on their
shared meaning. They must not silently introduce alternative meanings for
a role, value, message, transition, authority, or terminal result.

A case is evaluated against an identified semantic subject, application
assembly where relevant, selected assurance profile, and declared environment
and trust assumptions. The outcome distinguishes a justified admission, a
specific rejected violation, an unresolved obligation, and a capability
outside the selected supported profile. Calling a case "accepted" never means
that all stronger properties have been established. Rejection cannot be used
to claim success when the system should have accepted an ordinary valid
application under the same declared contract.

The following cases constrain Gesso's completed semantic language and its
realizations. Each case remains a mandatory conformance obligation:

| Case | Required result |
|------+-----------------|

| Missing operation binding or malformed continuation | Reject; an assertion cannot supply the absent structural edge |

Tests exercising these cases are conformance evidence for the tested subjects
and profiles. They do not replace a proof of the general projection relation,
prove arbitrary host callbacks, or establish every external-system premise.
Negative and mutation cases must reject for the intended semantic reason, not
merely because the fixture became malformed in an unrelated way.

*** Acceptance evidence and claim boundaries

Each exercised obligation records enough stable information to understand
which judgment was tested or checked:

| Evidence dimension | Required distinction |
|--------------------+----------------------|
| Subject | Authored normalized declaration, verified global machine, emitted role plan, assembled application, physical handler, or observed execution, as appropriate |
| Claim | Exact structural, knowledge, authority, projection, trace, safety, progress, privacy, deployment or model-operation proposition being assessed |
| Scope | Relevant actors, data, roles, operations, schema/predicate definitions, application generation, deployment pair and environment assumptions |
| Result | Admitted, rejected with a relevant semantic counterexample, unresolved with named premises, or outside a declared supported profile |
| Origin | Semantic locations and exact artifacts/interfaces on which the evidence depends, with applicable trusted-issuer and disclosure constraints |
| Coverage | Whether the evidence is a test fixture, bounded model check, finite structural theorem, concrete witness, conditional general proof, or installed-application correspondence result |

The generator and assembly checker expose these distinctions without making
ordinary Gesso application authors handwrite a second formal-methods language.
When a model checker, proof kernel, static analyzer, runtime validator, or
physical test discharges an obligation, its actual property and premises remain
visible in the assurance record. A stronger dependent judgment cannot be
claimed merely because a weaker component returned success. A conditional
proof remains conditional, and an unsupported assertion about arbitrary
host-language behavior cannot be relabeled as verified implementation fact.

Cross-layer conformance includes both directions needed for the selected
property: valid authored actions must have justified projected/runtime paths,
and observable projected behavior must trace back to admitted semantics.
A generated runtime cannot introduce a hidden authority, a new observable
effect, or an alternate terminal result merely because no positive fixture
exercised the difference. Tests for message loss, duplication, stale callbacks
or deployment changes exercise the relevant admissible schedules and
recovery premises; they do not assume fair delivery by choosing only
successful schedules.

An accepted physical realization establishes no stronger claim than its
covered installed entry points and writers, runtime artifacts, decoders,
owners, effect implementations, persistence providers and trust bindings.
A test of a mock operation or selected route cannot certify every
production invocation path. The release-level proof/coverage obligations
remain owned by Parts VII, IX, X and XI. The semantic requirements here
are independent of a particular implementation revision.

*** Positive construction acceptance

Acceptance also includes positive cases. The framework must not achieve
apparent soundness by rejecting every meaningful application or by requiring
every application author to implement their own formal toolchain.

| Valid construction | Required positive capability |
|--------------------+------------------------------|
| An ordinary Clojure/ClojureScript function has a complete admitted operation/effect contract but is opaque to the closed prover | Permit it under the selected qualified assurance profile, retaining the relevant explicit premises rather than claiming a proof of its body |
| Two remote branches have equivalent authorized local behavior | Project the legal local continuation without requiring an unnecessary discriminator or revealing private global values |
| An application chooses either a result-bearing or a truly neutral notifier contract for the same manager decision | Admit the chosen protocol with its actual knowledge, authorization, source evidence and disclosure policy; do not force all apps to reveal the branch or to hide it |

Success requires both the expected rejections and these permissible
constructions within their named profiles. The acceptance matrix is a
minimum cross-layer conformance basis, not a closed enumeration of every
valid Gesso program or a substitute for general correctness arguments.
The finished design is accepted only with consistent semantics,
property-specific justified claims, explicit boundary premises, and
nonvacuous support for meaningful application construction.

#+TITLE: Gesso Canonical Design — R5.1 Revision Charter
#+SUBTITLE: Standing goals, editorial method, and acceptance standard for the complete Gesso specification
#+OPTIONS: toc:3 num:nil
#+STARTUP: nofold

* Executive mandate

The purpose of the R5.1 effort is to produce a /timeless, complete, coherent,
and technically exact specification of the finished Gesso framework/.

The canonical design is a plan for what Gesso /will be able to do when the
project is complete/. It is not a progress report, a snapshot of a particular
Git commit, or documentation limited to features already implemented.

Use the real Gesso implementation, the earlier R4 plan, the R5 candidate, test
failures, and hard-won development experience to improve the /target design/.
Do not use the present implementation as an artificial ceiling on that design.

This charter governs the R5.1 /revision process/. The twelve-part canonical
specification governs the /finished system/. Keep those distinct.

#+begin_quote
Describe the right finished Gesso; use implementation experience to make that
description more correct. Never confuse “not yet implemented” with “should not
be specified.”
#+end_quote

* Non-negotiable interpretation

** The canonical document describes the finished framework

Use present-tense, normative statements for the intended complete system:
“Gesso derives...”, “The verifier rejects...”, “Application assembly
requires...”. This is not a claim that the current source already does so.

The specification may define future language constructs, public APIs,
intermediate representations, checkers, certificates, runtime adapters,
recovery mechanisms, and verification guarantees.

An unimplemented capability is /not/ a specification defect. A contradictory,
undefined, unjustifiably unconditional, unachievable, or improperly evidenced
claim /is/ a defect and must be corrected.

Do not add repetitive, version-bound sentences such as “the current Gesso
implementation does not yet support this” throughout the canonical text.
Put observations about implementation status in separate preservation audits,
implementation-correspondence notes, issue trackers, or progress reports.

** Future promises and proof claims are different things

The document may /require/ Gesso to establish a theorem or correspondence
property in the finished system. It must still accurately state the theorem's
subject, assumptions, trust boundaries, quantifiers, scope, and evidence
standard.

Distinguish, wherever relevant:

- a specified guarantee of the completed framework;
- a property of the abstract semantic model;
- an actually discharged proof obligation;
- a bounded model-check result;
- a test or concrete witness;
- a runtime-enforced condition;
- a trusted premise or uninspected host-language contract;
- a property that is unsupported or not yet established.

A future design may prescribe stronger evidence than is currently available.
But neither the present nor future design may promote one evidence class to
another without a valid justification. In particular, finite checking does
not automatically prove an unbounded theorem; shape equality does not prove
provenance; source acceptance does not prove physical execution; and a passed
suite does not prove the intended business policy is correct.

** The finished system retains an ambitious developer experience

Application authors express business rules and assemble applications in
ordinary Clojure and Gesso constructions. They should not be required to write
TLA+, Quint, Recife, proof DAGs, or manually duplicate information already
owned by their model, choreography, or views.

Gesso may employ formal-model backends, proof checking, executable reference
semantics, model-based testing, and runtime evidence internally. Choosing one
backend is an implementation decision unless the canonical semantics genuinely
depend on its distinguishing behavior.

The aim is unusually strong /domain-specific/ application checking, not
omniscient analysis of arbitrary general-purpose computation. Opaque Clojure
and external effects remain available through declared interfaces whose
unproved premises, realization boundaries, and enforcement contracts are
accounted for explicitly.

** Completeness includes the whole application and the whole system

Do not narrow the target to whichever subsystem is presently most mature.
The complete plan covers, at the appropriate level of rigor:

- domain rules and their independent product-intent acceptance criteria;
- choreography, knowledge, role projection, and executable transitions;
- authoritative operations, atomicity, identity, replay, and unknown outcomes;
- provisional presentation, Live, acquisition, reconciliation, and continuity;
- browser/server coordination, actual effects, and physical ownership;
- whole-application assembly, admission, policy closure, and source ownership;
- HumanHelp as a real production-model proving application;
- compositional assurance, counterexamples, and coherent proof obligations;
- correspondence to actual Clojure, XTDB, Biff, HTMX, browser, and other
  admitted realizations;
- deployment, release, migration, recovery, and multi-node behavior;
- a dependency-ordered implementation strategy and defensible completion gates.

Implementation work may be staged or explicitly deferred. An implementation
estimate that temporarily excludes HumanHelp or multi-node work never removes
them from the finished-system specification.

* Sources and decision hierarchy

** Distinct roles of R4, R5, R5.1, and current code

| Source | How to use it | What it does not imply |
|--------+---------------+------------------------|
| R4 (=main=) | Preservation baseline for normative obligations, counterexamples, examples, and explanations | That the older design is always superior or must be copied verbatim |
| R5 (=r5=) | Preferred architectural starting point, to be carefully improved | That every R5 example, boundary, or omission is already correct |
| R5.1 (=r5.1=) | Evolving reviewed candidate and eventual canonical successor | That a completed sectional audit alone establishes whole-plan correctness |
| Gesso source (=application-assurance= or successor) | Implementation evidence, feasibility lessons, actual semantics, gaps, tests, and adversarial cases | That currently missing implementation forbids a future requirement |
| HumanHelp and other adopters | Independent product demands, real model ownership, and end-to-end acceptance | That a simplified example or synthetic fixture replaces production evidence |

The specified future behavior has priority over accidental details of current
code. Conversely, genuine counterexamples uncovered in code, tests, or the
underlying mathematics may require changing the intended specification.

** Choose the best design, not the union of every sentence

Do not blindly merge R4 and R5. Treat the stronger R5 architecture as the
starting point, then check whether R4 contributes an essential requirement,
example, exception, or explanation that R5 lost.

Preserve R5 improvements even when restoring R4 content. Replace obsolete R4
machinery with an equivalent or stronger requirement when direct restoration
would contradict the superior design.

If the best resolution differs from /both/ R4 and R5, revise the design and
record the reason. Earlier decisions may be revisited when later work exposes
real contradictions or superior alternatives.

This is a substantive design revision, /not/ the strict additive-only R4
example-enrichment exercise. Deleting, rewriting, reorganizing, or replacing
inferior material is permitted when it improves the final semantic result.

* What makes a revision worthwhile

Every focused review should ask:

1. Is the intended behavior unambiguous and independently meaningful?
2. Which component owns each semantic fact or policy, and which components
   derive or import it? Is anything duplicated as competing authority?
3. Do the stated inputs, outputs, identities, knowledge paths, permissions,
   provenance, resources, and failure alternatives actually compose?
4. Is a guarantee unconditional, or does it depend on authorization, qualified
   backend behavior, deployment, fairness, a trusted function, or other premises?
5. Can a hostile actor, stale callback, race, retry, schema change, missing
   message, unknown commit, or incompatible artifact violate the claim?
6. Are construction rejection, runtime rejection, uncertainty, and unsupported
   analysis reported distinctly and truthfully?
7. Does the section describe the complete intended capability without
   pretending that currently available implementation evidence is stronger
   than it is?
8. Is a useful R4 requirement or worked case missing? If so, where does its
   semantic obligation belong under the improved architecture?
9. Is the design actually usable by a Clojure application author without
   duplicating Gesso-owned declarations or writing proof languages?
10. What concrete acceptance case would establish that this part of the
    finished system works, beyond an internally self-consistent example?

Add detail when it prevents an actual misinterpretation, identifies a required
boundary, provides an instructive counterexample, or establishes a necessary
acceptance condition. Do not expand merely to make the document longer.

* Small-section revision workflow

** Split at semantic join points

Work on one naturally coherent section at a time. Prefer subsections small
enough for serious analysis of every claim and example rather than attempting
a superficial rewrite of a multi-thousand-line part.

Split on existing Org heading boundaries whenever possible; do not split an
Org block or sever the meaning of a dependency. A typical working section can
be around 80–160 lines, but coherence matters more than a fixed quota. Larger
or smaller sections are justified when the underlying concept demands them.

Preserve a lossless reconstruction of the chosen R5 candidate before changing
its normative text. A source-unit split is itself structural preparation, not
an assertion that the candidate has passed review.

** One focused revision at a time

The conversational command =next= requests one complete, carefully reviewed
revision. =nextnext= has the same meaning. An interjected =next= while a
revision is still underway means continue that same revision to completion,
not start an overlapping new edit.

One revision should generally:

1. Read the current R5.1 source section and its immediate cross-references.
2. Read the relevant R4 counterpart, including useful examples that may be
   located in other parts or under different headings.
3. Inspect the relevant actual Gesso implementation, contracts, and tests when
   those would resolve a design question.
4. Identify improvements, omissions, contradictions, misleading assumptions,
   open proof obligations, and meaningful negative cases.
5. Make a focused, full-file revision that preserves the stronger architecture.
6. Write the corresponding R4 preservation and implementation-context audit.
7. Rebuild the affected published Org part from the numbered sources.
8. Validate structural integrity, scope, references, and the committed result.
9. Commit the bounded change to the working R5.1 branch and report the result.

Spend sufficient attention on the chosen scope; do not sacrifice review depth
to produce a larger number of superficially completed sections.

** Treat examples as design tests

Use both successful and adversarial examples. Include ordinary developer-facing
usage, rejected constructions, runtime and transport failures, and clear
recovery behavior where relevant.

An example of the /future intended API/ is legitimate even before that API
exists. Mark genuinely schematic notation when doing so prevents confusion
between illustration, exact proposed interface, and executable implementation.
Do not delete a useful future API or its guarantees just because there is no
current Var with that name.

When a proposed interface is meant to be normative, define its intended
contract precisely enough to implement and test. When exact spelling is an
unsettled ergonomic choice, preserve the meaning without prematurely freezing
incidental syntax.

** Record preservation and correspondence separately

Each sectional audit should contain, as applicable:

- the relevant R4 requirements and examples considered;
- the stronger R5 features retained;
- normative changes made in R5.1 and why they are improvements;
- material intentionally superseded rather than silently dropped;
- what the present implementation and tests actually establish;
- unresolved implementation, proof, deployment, or application obligations;
- cross-part issues requiring later reconciliation.

Audits are /working historical records/. They may reference Git commits,
versions, branch names, missing APIs, test results, and current limitations.
Such references belong here, not in the timeless normative specification.

Do not invent independent evidence for a guarantee based on a consistency
check, an illustrative code sample, or agreement between two generated models
that share the same unchecked assumption.

* Multi-level reconciliation

** Section-level review is necessary but insufficient

A sound section may conflict with another sound section. Treat section audits
as local progress, not proof that the whole part has accepted semantics.

** Reconcile each complete part

After all numbered sections of a part have been reviewed:

- examine the joins between sections for conflicting identities, obligations,
  causality, proof assumptions, recovery rules, and term definitions;
- check that R4's important requirements survive in their best R5.1 form;
- verify that positive examples and negative tests use consistent contracts;
- resolve ambiguities, returning to earlier numbered sections as necessary;
- produce a distinct whole-part acceptance/reconciliation record;
- run the structural checks on the mechanically published part.

A part may be declared a /reviewed candidate/ with explicit cross-part work
remaining. That label is not implementation certification or a theorem.

** Reconcile the full twelve-part design

After all part reviews, perform a separate whole-plan examination:

- Verify a single consistent conceptual vocabulary and ownership model.
- Follow representative complete application paths from business intent
  through choreography, authority, browser, live acquisition, deployment,
  and verification.
- Trace the proof and assumption dependencies across parts without hidden
  trust upgrades or independent-but-inconsistent duplicate semantics.
- Check adversarial concurrency, stale ownership, failover, recovery, privacy,
  and permitted liveness claims against the whole system.
- Confirm that HumanHelp's product acceptance and the formal assurance goals
  can both be pursued under the same proposed interfaces.
- Resolve or explicitly scope every normative contradiction, missing bridge,
  false claim of impossibility, and incomplete completion criterion.
- Confirm the implementation roadmap leads to the actual finished-system
  contract rather than merely the portion already built.

Revisit an earlier accepted part openly when warranted. A previous editorial
acceptance is not an obstacle to fixing a newly discovered design error.

* Document-integrity and delivery rules

** Canonical structure

The finished design consists of twelve ordered Org parts. Numbered sectional
source units are an editorial mechanism for reconstructing the published parts,
not independent competing normative plans.

Maintain one authoritative source for the intended content. An assembled part
must match its numbered sources byte for byte. Preserve meaningful existing
=CUSTOM_ID= anchors and cross-part links unless an intentional, checked
migration replaces them.

Check Org headings, source/example block pairing, link resolution, duplicate
anchors, complete content, and exact generated assembly after each change.
Validate examples using appropriate real tooling when an example is claimed to
be runnable. Schematic examples must not be misrepresented as executable tests.

Run the repository's assembly checker, currently
=python3 script/assemble_r5.py --check=, or its validated successor when the
editorial source inventory expands. The checker must not be treated as proof
of semantic correctness; it checks document construction.

The established filenames retaining =r4= are transitional publication names,
not a declaration that the R5.1 content has R4 semantics. Rename them only as
part of a coordinated, checked publication migration.

** Repository and changeset discipline

Keep canonical R4 (=main=) and the original R5 (=r5=) available as comparison
baselines. Perform R5.1 revisions on the =r5.1= working branch or its explicit
successor, not by rewriting either baseline branch.

Prefer one focused changeset containing the relevant numbered Org section,
its preservation audit, and the mechanically reassembled canonical part.
Include an updated index or cross-part record if the change genuinely needs
one. Editorial process documents such as /this charter/ are separate from the
twelve normative parts and do not belong in their assembly.

Write explanatory commit messages. Never claim that a tool, test, proof, or
branch update succeeded without confirming the resulting state. Deliver
complete files rather than ambiguous partial patches when full-file workflows
are expected.

** Handover across conversations

A later conversation should be able to recover the process by reading this
charter and checking the live repository. Do not rely on the previous model's
memory or a stale progress count as the source of truth.

A handover needs only:

- this charter and the active branch;
- the latest committed branch state;
- the next incomplete source section or pending reconciliation;
- relevant audit findings and cross-part issues;
- any actual failed structural check or blocked repository operation.

Do not ask the user to restate this charter's governing principles every time
work resumes. If the user says =next=, infer the next incomplete revision from
the repository and established workflow.

* Final acceptance standard for the R5.1 specification

The editorial project is complete only when:

- all twelve parts have been thoroughly reviewed against R4 and the superior
  R5 architecture, with preservation decisions recorded;
- every section has received attention proportional to its semantic risk;
- all whole-part and whole-plan consistency reviews are complete;
- all necessary target-system interfaces and semantic obligations are
  specified clearly enough to guide implementation and acceptance;
- future capabilities and proposed APIs remain specified where worthwhile,
  independent of how much code already exists;
- proof claims, trust boundaries, unsupported cases, liveness assumptions,
  and physical correspondence obligations are precise and non-contradictory;
- the end-to-end application story, HumanHelp proving requirements, deployment,
  multi-node design, and completion roadmap are coherent together;
- no important R4 requirement or explanatory counterexample has been silently
  lost, and obsolete mechanisms have been deliberately superseded;
- the assembled Org document and its source fragments pass the defined
  structural, reference, and publication checks;
- the final artifact is understandable as a statement of the /finished
  Gesso/, with implementation-status commentary confined to separate records.

These are /design-document acceptance/ conditions. They do not by themselves
establish that the finished framework has been implemented or that every
specified theorem is proved. Those are separate engineering and verification
completion gates /defined by/ the canonical plan.

* Standing instruction

When revising Gesso's canonical design, assume the following without further
prompting:

#+begin_quote
Build the best complete and timeless specification of the finished Gesso.
Start from the stronger R5 architecture; preserve or improve the genuine R4
requirements; apply what real implementation work has taught us; and never
reduce the intended design merely to match what is currently implemented.

Work in small, deeply reviewed semantic units. Check both successful and
adversarial cases. Keep audit/status evidence separate from normative target
semantics. Reconcile sections into parts and parts into one coherent system.
Preserve ambitious but well-defined future guarantees, while qualifying every
proof, trust, and physical-realization boundary accurately.

One =next= means one carefully completed, validated, committed revision.
#+end_quote

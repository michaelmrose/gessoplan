* Part XI — Release and completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-11-release-and-completion
:PART_REVISION: 11-20261108
:END:

This part defines incremental evidence reuse, release admission, activation,
bounded compatibility, and completion. A release claim concerns an identified
application, supported scope, actual artifacts, and declared environment. It is
not established by a successful build, an empty error log, a large test count,
or a report containing a successful status.

Parts I–X define the semantics and enforcing boundaries. This part makes their
acceptance obligations concrete without introducing a second application model.
Part XII owns realization ordering. Operational usability, compositional
certification, external formal correspondence, and production multi-node support
remain four separately reportable completion scopes.

The plan is normative. Examples in this part illustrate release mechanisms and
hypothetical records; they do not declare the release complete.

** Incremental certification, build, and release
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-release-evidence
:END:

The ordinary application constructor performs structural admission and installs
the required production gates. A release profile adds its claim-specific proof,
qualification, acceptance, and deployment requirements. Omitting an optional
stronger proof profile does not disable structural construction, rendered-surface
checking, runtime authorization, or artifact correspondence.

A selected stronger profile must be enforced by the actual candidate and
installation path. Defining a strict checker or obtaining its report in a test
does not establish that production invoked it. A disabled or absent policy
cannot be treated as successful evaluation of that policy.

*** The selected claim is not the generated gate list

The release request is a declaration of *what is being claimed*, not a
summary reconstructed from checks that happened to pass. Its canonical
requirements originate in the actual supported application and independently
selected product obligations. Construction, elaboration, proof generation,
test discovery, and installation *derive* relevant gates from that request;
they cannot redefine the requested claim by omitting source operations,
writer paths, protected read surfaces, deployment roles, or product journeys
that are difficult to establish.

A release candidate has an immutable claim identity during one evaluation.
The release's exact subject, qualifying evidence, and intended runtime
realization are separately identified: a model/source identity is not a
handler implementation identity; an assembly fingerprint is not browser
asset bytes; a test fixture's host is not the actual production listener;
and a source-checked certificate is not a runtime permission. Reusing a
previously admitted result always requires rechecking its declared
applicability to these distinct subjects.

The minimum normal construction contract applies regardless of optional
stronger milestones. A user may request a bounded operational profile
without claiming an external formal proof or multi-node completion. But
the ordinary constructor must still check its mandatory whole-application
closure, generated/runtime artifact agreement, rendered affordance
boundaries and required current-authorization guards. An omitted advanced
proof option is not permission to bypass a necessary server/XTDB gate.


*** Declare the claim before evaluating its gates

A release intent identifies at least:

- the application and canonical requirement-set identity;
- the required product capabilities, independently of the exposed-operation list;
- the requested milestone or explicitly narrower supported scope;
- the versioned assurance profile, mandatory judgments, permitted premise
  classes, and required runtime enforcement;
- the semantic, implementation, artifact, provider, and configuration subjects;
- the intended deployment topology and supported compatibility directions;
- the required test environments, fault scenarios, resource limits, and product
  decisions;
- the admission, installation, activation, and retirement conditions.

**** Freeze the intended requirement denominator

The versioned release intent fixes the canonical requirement inventory and
which selected application/product capabilities count as fulfilled, excluded,
blocked, or unimplemented *before* expensive checking starts. Its denominator
includes obligations that an incomplete implementation fails to discover;
discovery is itself an independently checked coverage relation against the
original declarations. A discovered empty set of proof tasks, zero failing
tests or zero registered business operations is not evidence that the
canonical requirement inventory is complete.

An intentional narrow release is a new identified claim. It names the
excluded capabilities and evidence families, why the narrower claim is
valid for its supported users and environment, and which original
milestones remain unmet. A missing requirement is never relabeled
not-applicable merely because its handler is absent. A profile may
explicitly accept a trusted premise under Part IX's rules; this is not
equivalent to silently deleting a mandatory theorem or physical
qualification from the requested milestone.

**** Bind the same candidate across its evidence edges

A current candidate manifest binds the versioned source/requirement set,
normalized semantic and proof-subject identities, admitted operation and
application assembly generations, implementation/configuration identities,
compiler/codec versions, actual emitted browser artifacts, chosen provider
and topology, required runtime gates, and each mandatory test/proof/
qualification witness. The manifest is an obligation/identity record,
not an invented universal certificate. Physical installation and
activation still need independent observations of the actual running
handler, worker, writer, served bytes and credentials.

Evidence is applicable only if its checked subject and dependency closure
match the *current* candidate within the selected relation. Exact source
equality, structural semantic equality, binary artifact identity,
implementation conformance and installed host correspondence are different
checks. A sound abstract invariant can remain reusable after a compatible
implementation change even while previous physical and browser tests become
stale. A copied successful report or a digest asserted by the subject under
test does not establish these relationships.

The release evaluator must report the origin of every mandatory gate from
canonical requirements or admitted application construction, including
an explicitly justified dynamic/opaque premise when a static catalog is
incomplete. It must record unavailable, unsupported, incomplete and stale
required evidence *without* converting it into success. The exact decision
vocabulary and distinction between admitted, installed, activated and
completed are owned by 11.02, not redefined here.


The release gate derives the concrete obligations from that intent and the
canonical application construction. It joins them to the original obligation
ledger, rather than replacing the ledger with a list of successful checks.
Required capabilities omitted from the implementation remain required.

Changing the requirement set, narrowing a proof domain, removing a capability,
or weakening an assurance profile is an explicit change of release intent. It
produces a distinguishable claim with its exclusions visible. It cannot make the
original full-completion claim pass by shrinking its denominator during gate
execution.

**** A concrete scope-bound counterexample

Suppose the full HumanHelp release still requires a separately selected
response-flow interaction, but the candidate demonstrates only Create,
Claim and explicit remote-board Refresh. Those features can be valuable
positive evidence for an appropriately named partial operational slice.
They cannot produce a completed *full HumanHelp* requirement set by
deleting response-flow from the discovered feature list. Similarly, a
proof of the Claim abstract transition cannot be presented as evidence
for all model operations, installed XTDB writers, real-browser behavior
and independent multi-node recovery.

Conversely, a fully specified response-flow product requirement that has
not yet been implemented is a missing application capability, not an
unsupported proof checker; a checker incapable of evaluating one declared
proof judgment is a distinct machinery gap. The release diagnostics and
repair owner must reflect the real deficiency rather than steering an
application author to rewrite sound product requirements around a tool.


A missing product decision is different from unsupported framework machinery.
Either may block the requested claim, but diagnostics identify the actual owner.
**** Nonvacuous release-intent challenges

| Positive candidate | Forbidden alternative |
|--------------------+-----------------------|
| Complete canonical requirement set produces its source-derived gate inventory | Empty or incomplete discovered operation set is declared complete |
| Named narrow profile explicitly excludes unimplemented journeys | Missing feature is silently omitted while full completion is retained |
| Current semantic proof is admitted for its checked proposition | Proof-looking report, old digest or mismatched dependency grants admission |
| Real source/assembly/artifact identities match the qualifying witnesses | Changed physical handler or served JS reuses stale conformance evidence |
| Actual protected runtime route enforces current admission | Disabled gate or never-installed wrapper is reported as successful enforcement |
| Original model and application requirements yield all required negative/positive tests | Reject-all or never-run test suite is used as readiness evidence |
| HumanHelp remote changes retain explicit Refresh and protected read policy | Successful SSE send is reported as canonical browser adoption |
| Selected stricter profile is genuinely evaluated before candidate admission | Strict failure is silently downgraded to a weaker profile or accepted premise |
| Required backend assumption is qualified within its declared version/scope | Caller-claimed or expired provider status is treated as production proof |
| Accepted operational scope names its four-milestone exclusions | One green build is called formal and multi-node completion |

Each row is a prospective acceptance obligation; it does not claim that any release has passed it. The real
positive case traverses the named construction and (where claimed) physical
boundary, preventing a reject-everything implementation from passing a
negative-only gate.


HumanHelp's selected remote-adoption policy is already part of Part VIII's
requirements. Its response-flow contract remains a product decision until it is
substantively selected and tested.

*** Four release decisions, with different evidence

The release process distinguishes these decisions:

| Decision | What must be established | What it does not establish |
|----------+-------------------------+----------------------------|
| Candidate admission | The constructed semantic/build candidate satisfies its profile and applicable pre-installation gates | That a particular server has adopted it |
| Installation eligibility | Actual private host bindings, providers, workers, assets, and enforcement satisfy the installation contract | That all public traffic has switched or that runtime premises stay true forever |
| Activation eligibility | The installation is current, exposure and overlap are permitted, and transition prerequisites hold | The success of every future user operation |
| Completion claim | Every required capability and evidence gate for the named milestone is satisfied for its scope | Completion of other milestones or later changed releases |

These are architectural decisions, not a requirement for four public API calls.
An implementation may combine phases while preserving their obligations.

*** The four decisions are subject-specific judgments

These decisions cannot be represented by a single `:ready` bit.
The release intent and source-derived requirement ledger of
11.01 identify what was requested before gates execute.
Every later phase binds its *own current* subject,
evidence, qualified premises and first enforcing
boundary.

| Decision | Current subject / required observation | Forbidden inference |
|----------+----------------------------------------+---------------------|
| Candidate admission | Full source/capability ledger, admitted semantic assembly and required proof/build/asset obligations for the chosen profile | Success of one test run or a smaller generated inventory proves all required capability coverage |
| Installation eligibility | That exact candidate actually bound to this host's Biff handlers, protected writers, provider, worker, credential, asset and runtime gate inventory | An in-process system map or staging snapshot proves the production listener is using it |
| Activation eligibility | Current installed generation, actual traffic exposure, overlap and session/command/continuation compatibility, retirement and recovery premises | Installation alone proves public traffic has switched and in-flight intents can safely continue |
| Completion claim | Original requirement set, named supported milestone, real product capabilities, independent acceptance and exact physical/formal evidence required by that scope | Operational success automatically completes compositional, external-formal or multi-node milestones |

A future implementation may combine phases in one procedure,
but it preserves individual decision subjects, acquired
observations, failure results and invalidation conditions.
A candidate is not an installation credential; an
installation is not a traffic-activation receipt; an
activated generation is not a proof that all later
requests, real users or full product journeys succeed.

The release ledger retains the exact current normalized
assembly, checker/profile and artifact identities where
required. It never substitutes an application name,
timestamp, Git commit, browser-reported digest or
caller-provided host label for an independently
qualified subject. A staging test proves only its
specified installed staging environment unless
a separate checked transfer justifies more.


A candidate can be exercised in a controlled integration environment only after
that environment's own admission and installation gates hold. Testing an admitted
staging installation is not the same as admitting the final production host.
The final host establishes its environment-specific bindings before exposing
managed behavior. This avoids both circular acceptance rules and a practice of
exposing unchecked application paths merely to see whether they work.

*** Gate results are closed, explicit, and scoped

The release-report vocabulary distinguishes the following results. An adapter
may translate a tool's native statuses into these meanings, but it must not turn
an inconclusive result into success.

| Result | Meaning | Satisfies a required gate? |
|--------+---------+---------------------------|
| =:passed= | The required check completed successfully for the applicable subject and scope | Yes, after evidence and applicability validation |
| =:failed= | The check established the specified violation or unsuccessful outcome | No |
| =:incomplete= | Work began but did not establish the required result, including timeout or exhausted exploration | No |
| =:unavailable= | The required checker, environment, dependency, or resource could not be used | No |
| =:unsupported= | The mechanism cannot establish the requested judgment for this input or scope | No |
| =:skipped= | The check was intentionally not executed | No |
| =:stale= | Existing evidence no longer applies to the current subject or premises | No |
| =:missing= | No required result or resolvable evidence exists | No |

These are release-check results, not model outcomes or evidence classes.
=:passed= may describe a test, a kernel check, a qualification run, or a proof
check; the record still names which mechanism ran. A passing test does not become
a proof because its status shares the same word.

*** A gate result is not self-authenticating evidence

For every required gate, the report identifies its
canonical originating requirement, exact subject,
selected mechanism and version, expected proposition
and scope, independent evidence/issuer provenance,
observed result, dependency applicability and open
premises. A map containing `:passed`, a successful
test count, an unchecked digest or a copied prior
report cannot prove the required checker executed
or that the deployed system used its result.

A proof gate needs an actually admitted derivation
bound to the current original source and its
assumptions. A physical effect gate needs evidence
that the actual installed writer, serving handler,
provider and artifact were challenged and remain
the selected installation. A browser result needs
the current resource's completed effect, not an HTTP
200 or attempted SSE send. A required runtime
authorization mechanism must be installed at the
protected action boundary; a release report
cannot preapprove every later principal.

A successful negative test is a positive *test
result* only when the specified invalid input reached
its required first boundary and produced the
expected refusal without forbidden effects. It
does not make the tested invalid command an admitted
business operation. A later failure of diagnostics
after possible XTDB submission cannot be converted
into business rejection or permission to resubmit.


An expected rejection passes only a negative test whose input, first enforcing
boundary, and expected diagnostic or prohibited effect were specified. The
candidate rejected by that test is not thereby admitted. A test that succeeds
because an unrelated wrapper rejected earlier does not establish enforcement
by the intended boundary.

*** Closed result vocabulary and complete mandatory inventory

The eight result values above describe what a named
checker or test established. They do not themselves
decide whether a requirement applies. Gate
applicability is derived independently from the
canonical source requirements and approved release
intent; each required gate must have an accounted
result. A missing original-source obligation cannot
disappear because the generated gate inventory or
the discovered operation list is incomplete.

Unexecuted required work is `:missing` or
`:skipped` as appropriate, unavailable providers
are `:unavailable`, an unsupported checking
capability is `:unsupported`, and a bounded or
timed-out incomplete exploration is
`:incomplete`. None becomes `:passed` merely
because no counterexample was observed. A real
failed check remains `:failed`; result provenance
is retained so an unsupported checker, missing
business rule, failing guard and infrastructure
fault have distinct repair owners.

A selected profile may explicitly exclude a gate
only through its authorized applicability rules
and an independently identifiable narrower claim.
An exclusion is a separate scoped decision, *not*
a ninth successful checker result. Unknown
applicability, undiscovered privileged writers,
an unselected required product decision or
unqualified source completeness blocks the stronger
release claim. A full HumanHelp completion claim
cannot be preserved by dropping response-flow or
another unmet original capability after evaluating
its checks.


A gate can be excluded only through the declared scope and applicability rules.
It is recorded as excluded with a reason and requirement reference, not as a
passing check. A gate mandatory for the canonical milestone cannot be excluded
while retaining that milestone's complete status. Unknown applicability blocks
the relevant decision until resolved.

*** Mandatory obligations and accepted premises are not one counter

The profile decides which obligations require checked derivations, which require
runtime mechanisms, which require qualification or test evidence, and which may
remain explicit trusted or asserted premises. A conditional admission can be
valid while displaying such accepted premises.

The report therefore separates:

- mandatory unmet obligations, which must be empty for the requested decision;
- accepted premises with their exact proposition, scope, owner, and dependent
  claims;
- runtime obligations, whose enforcing mechanisms must be installed and whose
  concrete conditions are evaluated when required;
- unsupported, stale, or refuted claims;
- exclusions and narrower scopes;
- original source obligations and the evidence relationships that account for
  their current disposition.

*** Exact conditionality and fail-closed mandatory gates

The preselected profile separates mandatory
derivations and tests, current runtime enforcement,
physical provider qualifications, and specifically
permitted trusted or asserted premises. An accepted
premise remains in the report with its proposition,
provenance, scope, qualifying observation, affected
claims and invalidation triggers. An assumption
known false or requiring unperformed qualification
cannot be called successfully accepted simply
because an abstract theorem was checked.

A required proof obligation cannot be moved into
the accepted-premise column after its checker fails.
A current source-relative conditional theorem can
remain valid even when physical installed-host
eligibility is unknown, but it does *not* establish
that eligibility. Physical XTDB contention tests
do not themselves establish independent proof
kernel soundness; a closed theorem does not
authenticate a production client. Report each
evidence class and its current applicability,
not an aggregate success percentage.

If the selected strict profile requires a proof,
qualified backend, live guard or installed handler,
missing, stale, unsupported, incomplete, unavailable
or failed evidence withholds that specific release
decision. The trusted product owner can request a
*different*, expressly narrower release intent;
the original request never silently downgrades.
After potentially committed work, missing diagnostic
evidence cannot undo the effect or turn
`:commit-unknown` into rejection. Mandatory
pre-effect safety gates fail closed for new
prohibited operations instead.

*** Required positive/adversarial release decision controls

| Positive control | Invalid shortcut to reject |
|------------------+----------------------------|
| Source-complete candidate checks every mandatory gate | Omitted Claim/response-flow operation disappears from generated check list |
| Staging candidate executes valid positive test at admitted staging host | Staging success authorizes an unrelated production listener |
| Real installed route, writer, provider and served JS match accepted candidate | Checked Biff map or copied artifact receipt is called installed enforcement |
| Active traffic uses a compatible installed generation and recovery context | Old incompatible host/session remains exposed after install pass |
| Named full completion includes all required user workflows | Green Claim demo is called all HumanHelp, formal and multi-node milestones |
| Invalid action is refused at the required physical gate | A test-only early wrapper refusal is counted as physical coverage |
| Real authorized operation succeeds at the same checked gateway | Reject-all handler passes negative-only acceptance |
| Exact finite state graph is completely checked within its stated scope | Timeout or truncated search is promoted to universal proof |
| Approved preview preserves explicit excluded full-milestone debt | Failed strict gate is reclassified as optional after it fails |
| Current qualified backend premise remains visible and scoped | Stale or known-false atomicity premise is treated as proof |
| Lost post-submit response retains unknown settlement | Observer exception is reported as rejection and triggers new command |
| Changed installed handler/provider/artifact requalifies dependent decisions | Old copied successful release report remains perpetual authority |

These are physical and semantic acceptance obligations. Written cases do
not establish that the corresponding checks have been executed or passed. Part X 10.15 specifies their
independent positive/adversarial observation ladder.

“Zero open obligations” is not a substitute for this accounting. It can hide the
fact that an implementation premise was merely accepted, or demand an impossible
proof of all external systems. Mandatory obligations cannot be moved into the
premise list because a proof failed. An accepted backend premise still needs
its required qualification and must not be known false.

A current principal, valid command binding, or permission to mutate a resource
cannot be frozen as a build-time fact. Release evidence establishes the proper
runtime mechanism and its scope, not the truth of every future invocation's
premises.

*** Deterministic regeneration and identity layers

For unchanged complete declared inputs, the normalized semantic construction
must reproduce the identities it claims deterministic: semantic subjects,
required dependency closure, supported projections and plans, assembly
interfaces, and browser manifest. Relevant normalizer, rule, checker, codec,
compiler, and format versions enter the appropriate dependencies.

The identities answer different questions:

| Identity or relation | Question answered |
|----------------------+-------------------|
| Semantic subject identity | Is this the same normalized meaning in the supported interpretation? |
| Required dependency closure | Which exact subjects and premises support this claim? |
| Implementation identity | Which concrete callable, lowering, or provider realization is bound? |
| Exact artifact identity | Which actual bytes were built, tested, stored, or served? |
| Assembly generation | Which immutable admitted whole-application realization owns this invocation? |
| Continuation compatibility | May this client or execution safely use that target realization? |
| Activation record | Which admitted realization was exposed in this environment and transition? |

Source paths, line numbers, Git commits, timestamps, and human version labels are
useful provenance. They are not substitutes for those meanings. Identical source
text under a changed primitive or compiler may not preserve the relevant claim.
Conversely, a comment edit need not change a semantic subject.

Semantic reproducibility does not imply byte-identical compiler output. Exact
reproducible builds additionally control every byte-affecting input. Otherwise
the build records and verifies the actual emitted bytes separately. Timestamp
or diagnostic exclusions from an identity must be explicit and justified.

**** Determinism has a declared semantic boundary

Identity is computed from the *complete meaning-bearing input* for
the specific subject and interpretation, not merely its displayed
name or serialization. The versioned normalizer must declare
which fields, primitive definitions, imported contracts, schema
types, feature/profile settings and admissible external
assumptions affect the relevant meaning. Equivalent ordering
of supported unordered maps/sets should not introduce accidental
drift; relevant sequence order, literal distinctions and
whitespace inside semantic strings must not be erased.

The identity contract also distinguishes a syntactically
normalized interface from denotational equivalence: a structural
digest is not a proof that arbitrary Clojure code, a database
lowering or two different implementations behave identically.
Changes to interpretation, trusted primitive rules or
normalization semantics require versioned reconsideration
even when a human-readable rule or identifier looks unchanged.

Compiler, formatter, build toolchain and runtime dependency
changes are tracked by the evidence and artifact edges that
depend on them. A comment or source location edit that is
excluded from the semantic representation need not invalidate
a theorem, but it may still affect provenance, diagnostics
or an actual byte-producing compiler input. The build either
controls every byte-affecting input for reproducible bytes
or measures the bytes it *actually* emitted and qualifies
their current binding. A timestamp-sensitive artifact
cannot inherit a previous byte identity from a matching
semantic subject alone.

The identity and dependency ledgers must be compatible with
the immutable release intent of 11.01 and the different
candidate, installation, activation and completion subjects
of 11.02. None of those decisions is represented by one
global monotonic release number or a Git commit alone.

A changed dependency requires reconsideration of the affected artifacts; it does
not guarantee that every artifact's bytes change. A server-side policy change
may leave an executable browser payload unchanged if its complete relevant
contract and dependencies remain satisfied. A new manifest or receipt is still
required wherever its binding changes. Reuse follows checked facts, not an
assumption that every source edit either rebuilds everything or changes nothing.

*** Incremental invalidation follows actual dependency edges

The dependency index is derived from admitted source and the required claim
closure. It includes semantic dependencies and the separate implementation,
provider, configuration, build, and qualification dependencies needed by each
claim. A structural graph supplied by the caller cannot establish its own
completeness.

**** Dependency closure includes the boundaries that can invalidate a claim

The dependency index must be derived from the canonical admitted
application and the required original-source obligation catalog,
including invariant, operation, role/knowledge, model read/write,
guard, plan, projection, rendered affordance, application assembly,
proof/checker/profile and client artifact contracts. Where relevant,
the dependent evidence graph also includes the actual bound
implementation, physical writer/entrypoint, provider, compiler,
configuration, credentials, host installation, activated generation
and compatibility/retention assumptions.

These are not assumed to be one uniform graph. A semantic
contract-dependency edge, proof-dependency edge, implementation
correspondence edge, build-input edge, physical qualification
edge and active-installation edge can have different recheck
policies. The checker must establish every required edge of
the declared graph; that proves *declared* graph consistency
only. Independent closure obligations still govern original
source completeness and undiscovered physical writers.
A caller-supplied dependency map cannot close its own
missing-edge obligation merely because it is acyclic.

A renamed, removed or newly discovered required subject
is a change to the corresponding obligation/closure,
not an excuse to retain downstream success because an
old identifier disappeared from the graph. Unresolved
references, contradictory dependencies, and cycles
that cannot be admitted under the selected graph
contract are typed construction/release failures
rather than silently ignored edges.

A dynamic permission, current Membership, request
status, user input or command outcome should not
be frozen as an immutable build dependency. The
release dependency is on the *installed mechanism*
which checks those facts at the authoritative
read/write boundary; actual invocation must still
check the current values. A pure build graph
cannot replace runtime authorization or liveness.


When a dependency changes, reconsider its transitive dependents. Retain a result
only when the current consumer establishes its applicability, including profile
and interpretation compatibility. Rechecking a join may show that an unchanged
interface theorem remains reusable while physical evidence must be renewed.
The system need not regenerate unrelated subjects because they share a file or
Git commit.

Invalidation records distinguish these actions:

- reconsider a claim whose dependency changed;
- reconstruct a semantic subject or executable artifact where required;
- recheck evidence or its applicability;
- rerun a concrete qualification or acceptance case;
- reacquire installation evidence for a changed physical environment;
- retain an immutable result with the reason its applicability survives.

They must not collapse all of these into “rebuild” or imply that a retained
abstract proof establishes unchanged production behavior.

**** Reconsideration is not synonymous with recompilation

A changed input expands the candidate *reconsideration cone*,
not necessarily the set of artifacts whose bytes must change.
For each affected subject, the current consumer determines
which action applies:

| Action | Required reason |
|--------+-----------------|
| Retain an immutable semantic conclusion | Current source subject, checker/rule contract, original obligations, dependency closure and premises are still admissible |
| Reissue or recheck semantic proof evidence | The relevant source, inference/checker, proof dependency, profile or interpretation changed |
| Rebuild a plan or browser artifact | Its executable contract or byte-affecting build inputs changed, or reuse cannot be established |
| Revalidate concrete implementation/XTDB behavior | A handler, transaction lowering, real writer, provider or related physical assumption changed |
| Recheck installation/activation | Exact served assets, registered routes, live host/worker/credential binding or overlap conditions changed |
| Reacquire product/test evidence | The required capability, execution subject, test harness or assurance scope changed |

An implementation can optimize this by retaining
immutable checked results under the cache-admission
rules of 11.04. It may not skip required current
validation based solely on an old success map.
An unavailable required recheck remains unavailable,
not a pass. Retrying a read-only proof query is not
the same as resubmitting an uncertain committed
business command.


**** Executable illustration: the affected dependency closure

This pure Clojure example computes a reconsideration set over a supplied finite
graph. The graph uses subject labels, not cryptographic identities. It assumes
that its nodes and edges have already been validated and that the graph is the
complete relevant dependency index. It does not check those assumptions, issue
a certificate, or decide which reconsidered result can ultimately be reused.

#+begin_src clojure
(def release-dependencies
  {:claim-rule #{}
   :model-schema #{}
   :claim-contract #{:claim-rule :model-schema}
   :claim-proof #{:claim-contract}
   :claim-adapter #{}
   :claim-conformance #{:claim-contract :claim-adapter}
   :claim-plan #{:claim-contract}
   :search-projection #{}
   :search-plan #{:search-projection}
   :browser-manifest #{:claim-plan :search-plan}
   :application-root #{:claim-proof :claim-conformance :browser-manifest}})

(defn affected-subjects [dependencies changed]
  (loop [affected changed]
    (let [expanded
          (into affected
                (for [[subject inputs] dependencies
                      :when (some #(contains? affected %) inputs)]
                  subject))]
      (if (= affected expanded)
        affected
        (recur expanded)))))

(def policy-change
  (affected-subjects release-dependencies #{:claim-rule}))

(def adapter-change
  (affected-subjects release-dependencies #{:claim-adapter}))

(assert (= #{:claim-rule :claim-contract :claim-proof
             :claim-conformance :claim-plan :browser-manifest
             :application-root}
           policy-change))
(assert (= #{:claim-adapter :claim-conformance :application-root}
           adapter-change))
(assert (not (contains? policy-change :search-plan)))
(assert (not (contains? adapter-change :claim-proof)))
#+end_src

In this graph, the policy change reaches the browser manifest, while an adapter
change reaches physical conformance and the application root without invalidating
the abstract Claim proof. A real manifest that also binds the adapter would add
that edge and therefore be reconsidered. A search implementation that relies on
the changed policy would likewise add the dependency. The example does not
justify omitting either edge when it exists.

For the illustrative graph, the two assertions test only
reachability in a finite, supplied dependency map.
A real implementation must independently show that
=release-dependencies= was derived from the complete
relevant original source and that every implementation,
source, browser and environment edge was discovered.
The helper eligibility change must also supply a typed,
authoritative =:helper/not-suspended= input; merely adding
an unvalidated keyword to a set is not an admitted rule.

The example's `:claim-adapter` is deliberately distinct
from `:claim-contract`. Its implementation replacement
reconsiders conformance and application assembly while
permitting the original abstract proof to survive only
after the current checker verifies the unchanged proof
subject and dependencies. If the real browser manifest
binds adapter identity, the manifest must depend on it:
the illustrative absence of that edge cannot justify
omission from the production closure.


Reconsidering the manifest does not force recompilation if the actual required
payload and build binding can be reused under the current contract. The example
computes impact, not an optimal build schedule or a proof of cache validity.


**** Positive and adversarial invalidation controls

| Positive control | Prohibited inference or regression |
|------------------+------------------------------------|
| Two equivalent normalized maps reproduce one supported structural subject ID | Unordered input iteration changes proof identity arbitrarily |
| Relevant string, vector-order or primitive-meaning change produces a requalified subject | Normalizer erases a semantic distinction and retains an old theorem |
| Diagnostic-only edit retains abstract proof where its subject remains the same | Actual byte-affecting build change is omitted from artifact dependencies |
| Claim eligibility policy change reconsiders Claim proof/plan and root | The unrelated Request search plan is rebuilt without a dependency reason |
| Claim adapter A is replaced by B under the same checked abstract contract | Old A-only physical conformance result is reported for B |
| Current proof checker re-admits an unchanged semantic subject and complete dependencies | A copied digest, subject label or cached success is trusted without re-admission |
| Browser manifest changes trigger exact artifact and receipt correspondence checks | Changed manifest is treated as equivalent solely because source operation name matches |
| New privileged XTDB writer is discovered and added to physical coverage obligations | Old complete declared semantic graph is called complete deployed writer inventory |
| Deleted or renamed required capability remains an unresolved original obligation until accounted for | Removed graph node silently disappears from release requirements |
| Incompatible graph cycle or missing required edge rejects admission with a typed error | Traversal terminates and is mistaken for proof that the graph is valid |
| Installation provider, credential or activated host changes requalify affected live claims | Unchanged semantic fingerprint permanently certifies new deployment |
| Current Membership is checked at Claim effect time by installed authorization | Build-time cached Membership or a release report grants permanent permission |
| Optional unrelated file edit retains unaffected evidence when current admissibility is shown | Entire proof graph is regenerated solely because two subjects shared a Git commit |
| Expected unsupported/unavailable proof recheck preserves the original required gate as unsatisfied | Release system silently downgrades a strict profile to maintain a green result |

These are design acceptance obligations, not reports of executed tests. They require independently chosen
positive and counterexample cases, actual source-derived
coverage and physical validation at the boundaries claimed
by the selected release profile.
*** Cache admission preserves the original trust boundary

A persisted cache entry is candidate evidence. Its key, filename, recorded digest,
and successful status cannot confer issuance. The trusted consumer reconstructs
or validates the current subject and required closure, then applies the relevant
source, derivation, assumption, interpretation, and issuer rules from Part IX.

The supported reuse paths are:

| Reuse path | Required validation |
|------------+---------------------|
| Immutable in-process issued result | Genuine issuer binding, unchanged subject and required closure, current applicability |
| Serialized proof/evidence package | Safe decoding, complete original source/proof material, current dependency and profile checks, required semantic rechecking |
| External formal evidence | The supported import/checking contract, exact subject representation, proof/checker identity, and applicability |
| Previous concrete test or qualification | Exact tested subject/configuration or a justified coverage-preserving relation, with remaining environment checks identified |
| Previous installation record | Only the installation facts it still establishes; a new host or changed binding acquires its own evidence |

**** Cache lookup and proof admission are different events

A cache lookup yields candidate evidence, not a checker-issued proof. The
trusted consumer constructs its expected subject from the *original current*
Gesso source and full declared obligation/dependency closure; it cannot
accept a serialized package's own claim about that closure as its sole
authority. The present checker contract, required inference rules, proof
profile, assumptions and precise proposition determine whether reuse is
admissible. A matching cache key, checksum, success flag or subject name is
only a lookup/correlation aid.

A genuine immutable issuer-registered, in-process result can avoid repeated
expensive derivation while the current consumer independently establishes
its continued applicability. Persisted serialized proof material has crossed
a new trust boundary and requires the supported current source/proof checker
or external proof-import contract before it can be issued again. The system
need not rerun unrelated immutable proofs on every HTTP request, but cannot
replace a mandatory current check with a trusted-looking deserialized map.

**** Worked counterexample: a green cache file is not a certificate

These two maps are *schematic comparison data*, not new callable Gesso
APIs or evidence that a checker was executed:

#+begin_src clojure
(def expected-claim
  {:subject :operation/request-claim-O13
   :requires #{:rule/claim-eligible-R8 :schema/request-S9}
   :checker :checker/operation-v7
   :profile :profile/compositional-v3})

(def persisted-candidate
  {:cache/key [:request/claim :profile/compositional-v3]
   :subject :operation/request-claim-O12
   :requires #{:rule/claim-eligible-R7 :schema/request-S9}
   :checker :checker/operation-v6
   :status :passed
   :derivation-hash "71ad..."})
#+end_src

The candidate fails current re-admission for *separate reasons*: subject
O12 does not match O13, declared rule R7 is stale relative to R8, checker
v6 is not the required v7, and the digest is not the proof derivation or
genuine live issuer binding. A fail-fast implementation may report the
first problem, but cannot infer the rest were satisfied.

Even copying O13, R8 and v7 into a forged green file does not supply an
original source derivation or prove its premises and checking rules. The
trusted consumer must derive its expected inputs independently. Corrupted
proof bytes can be rejected or reconstructed from admissible originals,
never promoted to successful certification.


An opaque JVM handle is process-local. Printing or serializing it cannot make it
a durable certificate. Copying a report into a new map cannot recover its issuer
or the origin of its physical observations. The cache must not accept executable
reader forms, arbitrary callbacks, or unbounded structures merely because they
arrive under an evidence filename; its parser and resource contract are explicit.

**** Closed serialization and observation provenance

Serialized evidence has a versioned passive-data format, bounded size and
depth, permitted reference syntax, supported checker/import versions and
strict resource limits. Cache loading cannot evaluate executable EDN reader
forms, arbitrary callbacks, user code, unbounded collections or unknown
extensions. Unsupported, truncated and unreadable packages fail with typed
diagnostics. A safe cache miss is permission to reconstruct from trusted
original input, *not* to ignore a required proof or a known counterexample.

Test and physical-qualification receipts have their own trust rules. A
copyable report, checksum or signed-looking record cannot independently
prove that a designated test actually ran on an identified physical XTDB
provider, browser build, served host or privileged writer. If a selected
release profile requires independently attributable execution or current
installation, the evidence issuer, tested subject, environment, bounds,
method and qualification dependencies must be established at the original
boundary. A signature can authenticate a defined statement but cannot
make an incomplete statement about writer coverage exhaustive.

Persisted evidence never grants live authority. A cached proof is not
a current Membership check, retained command result, principal, permission
to disclose, DOM resource-owner capability or permission to submit another
mutation. Real protected operations retain their effect-time authorization
and post-submit unknown-outcome recovery obligations.


A corrupted or incomplete cache entry may fail admission or trigger fresh
reconstruction from trusted inputs. It cannot relax the original check. A
reconstruction failure leaves the required gate unsatisfied. Recovering from a
cache miss is different from suppressing a counterexample or a known invalid
premise.

An unchanged immutable result can avoid repeating expensive work under an admitted
cache contract. This does not require reparsing an unchanged proof on every
request. It also does not cache current Membership, command disposition, live
resource ownership, or permission to disclose a prior result as permanent facts.

**** Proof retention, physical requalification, and cache freshness

The cache contract must distinguish a proof's *current abstract subject*
from a particular Clojure handler, XTDB lowering, compiler, provider,
deployment host, issued browser plan and actual served asset. A change
to any applicable physical dependency requalifies the corresponding
physical claim without automatically invalidating an independently checked
unchanged abstract theorem. Matching semantic identity is not sufficient
until the current checker validates the source meaning, proof inputs,
dependency closure, interpretation, profile and premise scope.

Cache re-admission does not collapse 11.01's original requirement catalog,
11.02's four release decisions, or 11.03's different reconsideration actions
into a permanent green release flag. A previously successful staging report
cannot qualify a different production installation. An obsolete artifact
receipt cannot qualify newly emitted browser bytes. Unavailable current
qualification leaves the required release decision unmet, while unaffected
evidence can remain useful under its actual checked applicability.


**** Unchanged contract, changed implementation

Suppose the normalized Claim contract and proof remain unchanged while its XTDB
adapter changes from implementation A to implementation B. The release must
separate the following questions:

| Item | Reuse decision |
|------+----------------|
| Abstract Claim theorem | Reusable if its actual subject, dependencies, interpretation, and profile still apply |
| A's transaction-lowering correspondence | Does not establish B's correspondence |
| A's backend qualification | Reusable only for the portion whose complete applicability envelope B preserves; changed adapter behavior requires qualification |
| Handler and gateway binding | Re-establish for the actual issued B realization and installed host |
| Browser plan and bytes | Reconsider their actual dependencies; rebuild only where needed |
| End-to-end Claim evidence | Establish applicability to B; an A-only execution cannot be silently relabeled |
| Whole-application root | Recheck the joins and report B's remaining premises |

Implementation identity is acquired from the actual bound construction and its
relevant dependencies. Assigning a new label in a map does not establish which
handler the server executes. A successful source-relative join does not prove
complete physical writer coverage or the final serving process's adoption.
Those remain the separate production obligations defined in Parts VII and X.

**** Worked implementation contrast: unchanged Claim, changed adapter

The A44-to-B45 adapter counterexample can be stated without
pretending that a public `release/reconsider-implementation` or
`certificate/admit-cached` API already exists:

| Evidence | A44-to-B45 decision |
|----------+---------------------|
| Current abstract Claim theorem | Retain only if the issuer/checker re-establishes its original normalized subject, proof, complete declared dependencies, profile and premises |
| Original A44 XTDB lowering and guard/frame correspondence | Not evidence of B45's different physical behavior; check B45 independently |
| Backend isolation, codec and transaction qualification | Retain only the scope demonstrably preserved; requalify changed provider/adapter/transaction assumptions |
| Installed Ring handler and privileged writer inventory | Acquire evidence of B45's actual bound callable and complete relevant host writer paths |
| Browser plan, manifest, build receipt and emitted bytes | Reconsider actual dependencies and verify current stamp/byte correspondence; recompile only when justified |
| End-to-end Claim test | Verify validity on B45 and re-execute any required physical case not covered by a sound transfer |
| Root release and active host claim | Rejoin current conditional proof, B45 implementation/installed host, exact artifact and applicable live qualification |

The useful optimization is preserving a genuine theorem independently
of the handler refactor, not retaining A44-only conformance as proof of
B45. Conversely, it is wrong to throw away unrelated Request-search
proofs or browser plans just because both implementations share a
repository commit.

**** Positive and adversarial cache-admission controls

| Positive case | Prohibited shortcut or counterexample |
|---------------+---------------------------------------|
| Current live issuer handle is bound to its original proof and dependencies | Caller-created handle or copied EDN report becomes a certificate |
| Original O13/R8/v7 proof rejects stale O12/R7/v6 cache candidate | Green filename, digest or `:passed` flag bypasses validation |
| Forged package with new labels lacks checkable original derivation | Merely copying a current semantic ID manufactures checker admission |
| Closed bounded data package safely decodes under selected version | EDN reader execution or arbitrary callback runs at cache load |
| Corrupt stored proof fails or reconstructs from trusted originals | Truncated cache turns mandatory release gate into success |
| Genuine reusable theorem retains explicit current assumptions | Known-false backend premise becomes discharged through cached result |
| Qualified physical receipt applies only to its observed subject | Staging test log alone certifies the production deployment |
| Changed B45 actual transaction requalifies physical conformance | A44's successful XTDB test is relabeled B45 |
| New installed handler and writer closure are qualified at the host | Caller-supplied B45 name authenticates the serving process |
| Current browser manifest, receipt and actual built bytes agree | Stale JS bundle is reused because semantic Claim name matches |

| Unavailable mandatory strict re-admission blocks exact profile | Weaker operational success silently substitutes for demanded proof |

These are future positive and adversarial acceptance obligations, not
recorded test results. Physical claims require the real first
enforcing boundary and supported passing operations, not only a
test-only wrapper or a reject-all implementation.
** Root release evidence and candidate integrity

The root report has machine-readable data and a human-readable explanation of
the same records. It is generated from checked construction and observed evidence;
it is not a second hand-maintained application registry.

*** Required report fields

The report identifies:

1. Release intent, canonical requirements, requested milestone/scope, assurance
   profile, and independent required-capability inventory.
2. Normalized subjects, required dependency closure, original obligation ledger,
   accepted premises, mandatory unmet obligations, and excluded claims.
3. Actual implementation bindings, source/build provenance, provider/configuration
   identities, compiler/runtime versions, and qualification applicability.
4. Issued plans, browser manifest, build receipt, exact artifacts, and the admitted
   assembly generation to which they belong.
5. Every required gate's result, evidence class, evidence reference, subject,
   environment, bounds, and enforcing boundary where relevant.
6. Candidate, installation, activation, compatibility, and retirement decisions,
   including the actual serving-host evidence for the claim made.
7. Product decisions, resource limits, retention contracts, security boundaries,
   and conditional-liveness premises.
8. Completion results for each requested milestone and a change-impact explanation
   relative to any reused evidence.

*** The root report carries a typed claim/evidence ledger

A root report has a stable, versioned representation whose human-readable
explanation is generated from the *same checked underlying records* as its
machine-readable form. It does not reconstruct source or release policy from
the checks that happened to pass, and a user-edited human explanation cannot
update the checked decision. The report is about a specific named release
intent, original requirement denominator, candidate, physical environment,
evaluation window and claimed milestone; it is not a global `certified`
flag on an application name.

Each required claim and capability has a source-derived original requirement
reference and a current disposition: which checked proposition or physical
effect is asserted, what evidence class can support it, who issued or observed
that evidence, what exact subject/configuration was examined, and what the
selected profile requires. A required obligation absent from the emitted
evidence ledger remains *missing*; an admitted narrower scope names the
excluded original requirement and its approval. The ledger preserves
unknown, failed, unavailable, unsupported, incomplete, stale and
intentionally skipped cases according to 11.02 rather than erasing them
from the denominator.

| Report dimension | Must distinguish and bind |
|------------------+---------------------------|
| Original source and coverage | Declared business rules, models, operations, invariants, initial states, roles, protected writers, rendered affordances, all required product capabilities, omissions and source-completeness assumptions |
| Semantic proofs and issue authority | Exact theorem subject, proof family, original derivation/checker source, proof kernel and dedicated checker identities/versions, inference/profile scope, genuine issuer, qualified premises and independent checker-soundness status |
| Assembly and actual implementation | Normalized construction and issued assembly generation, real guarded operation, route, installed handler and writer/worker bindings, source/implementation dependency closure and open physical-refinement edges |
| Browser and build | Plan/manifest, generated bootstrap source, compiler/toolchain/format versions, exact emitted JS identity, receipt, tested bytes, installed/served bytes and client acquisition assumptions |
| Real provider and environment | Selected XTDB/backend profile, transaction lowering and codec, provider configuration/version, host/credential/OS/network/transport and physical qualification envelope |
| Gate observations | Required gate ID and expected boundary, actual test/checker type and result, provenance and execution identity, tested source/configuration, positive/negative oracle, bounds, coverage limitations and allowed premise classes |
| Release decisions and rollout | Separately identified candidate admission, specific installation eligibility, exposure/activation and required overlap/retirement/continuation recovery facts, with requalification triggers |
| Product and milestone | Independently selected product policy, required HumanHelp workflows, positive user-capability evidence, named operational/compositional/external-formal/multi-node milestones and remaining unselected or unmet requirements |

The report also classifies the relevant *trusted computing base* (TCB)
by responsibility: source semantics and normalization, proof/checker,
compiler/build, authenticated application/XTDB writer, installed host,
browser/transport and external environment. For each dependent claim,
it states which pieces have actual checked evidence, which are
explicit trusted premises and which are still unsupported or unknown.
A backend premise may be accepted under a selected profile but cannot
be presented as an independently proved universal property.

A useful evidence graph contains edges from the original obligation
to the admitted semantic/proof subject, concrete implementation,
selected artifact, observed deployed gateway and resulting milestone
decision. Such edges describe the *specific correspondence evidence*
and its limits. Their presence in a report does not establish the
edge's truth: the trusted consumer obtains issued proof or physical
observation from the owning boundary, then validates the resulting
reference and current applicability before any release decision.

*** Identity and provenance are claim-specific

The canonical release subject from 11.01 remains immutable while
evidence is collected. Semantic/proof identities, dependency
closure, issuer identity, implementation identity, exact bytes,
assembly generation, installation and public activation are
distinct coordinates. A Git commit, timestamp, source filename,
shared operation name, matching digest or supplied report status
cannot substitute for any missing coordinate.

A test made against a fixture assembly, old server implementation
or staging host may be valuable evidence *for that stated scope*.
It cannot be reused as production evidence merely because the
current candidate has the same logical operation name. Concrete
test-to-actual-installation correspondence requires an expressly
checked applicability relation, including the compiler, emitted
artifacts, provider, runtime and resource boundaries required by
the claimed behavior.

A built artifact's receipt binds the selected generated bytes to
its declared manifest under the build contract. It does not by
itself prove that the deployed listener serves those bytes, that
a user browser loaded them, or that the browser completed a
permitted DOM effect. Installation and activation evidence from
the actual serving environment belongs to the separate 11.02
decisions and Part VII's physically enforced lifecycle.

An immutable historical release evidence package is useful for
reproducibility, comparison and audit. When source, checker,
policy, provider, credential, served artifact, active host or
retention condition changes, the report must distinguish historical
facts from *currently applicable* evidence. A current release
decision reacquires or revalidates exactly the dependent
observations required by 11.03–11.04, rather than editing an old
successful report to appear current.

*** Root reports are descriptions, not new authority

The reporting process consumes genuine checked construction,
proof-issuer and observation interfaces. A caller-supplied map,
test list, original source graph, physical route inventory,
reported issuer handle or manually assembled root report is
neither a second application registry nor a release authority.
The selected trusted owner must obtain the actual source and
installed observations from their appropriate boundaries, with
unknown physical enumerator completeness recorded as an open
obligation rather than assumed away.

The human and machine presentations may arrange information
differently, but must represent the same claims, evidence
classes, failed or unrun gates, accepted premises, exclusions,
enforcing boundaries, and limitations. A concise summary can
link to checked detailed evidence, but may not call a weaker
status `certified` or omit a blocking gate. The executable
source, proof package and physical installation—not the printed
report—remain the authoritative inputs for the corresponding
decisions.

Proof/diagnostic values must not be fed into runtime semantic
transition decisions, browser effect authorization, Membership
evaluation or XTDB guarded writes. Genuine proof of a conditional
abstract proposition does not convert it into current physical
permission. Runtime guards enforce current authority even when
release reports are unreadable or unavailable.

*** HumanHelp and release-report challenge cases

| Valid root-report statement | Forbidden promotion or omission |
|-----------------------------+---------------------------------|
| The original full HumanHelp capability catalog is named, including unselected response-flow work | The successful Create/Claim/Refresh subset becomes the full completion denominator |
| Claim theorem is admitted under its precise original source, kernel version and remaining premises | A copied proof report becomes universal XTDB/CLJS implementation soundness |
| Selected Biff/XTDB handler and complete current writer inventory have their own physical evidence or open gap | A source-derived route list asserts that every external writer was observed |
| Exact browser manifest, tested built JS bytes and actually served artifact have distinct references | A valid build receipt is presented as proof of real client installation |
| Fixture/browser staging positive and negative tests retain their precise tested subject and environment | A passing staging suite certifies a different production host |
| A named required physical qualification is stale or unavailable and remains blocking | A current human summary hides the failed gate while machine results say `:passed` |
| A strict profile lists accepted source assumptions and separately unmet physical obligations | Accepted backend premise is silently rendered as fully proved |
| Four release decisions and four completion milestones have separate status/subjects | One green root record is interpreted as permission to activate every host |
| A supported remote HumanHelp lifecycle advisory awaits explicit Refresh and current protected read | An SSE send is labeled canonical browser adoption or participant receipt |
| Changed provider or serving handler triggers requalification of its dependent release claim | An old timestamp/digest permanently qualifies the changed deployed gateway |
| A known committed command remains committed after delivery/diagnostic failure | Missing release report turns unknown/committed domain command into rejection |
| Positive valid Claim succeeds under the actual protected gateway, and forbidden attempts are rejected | A reject-everything handler passes solely because its negative tests are green |

These are independent report and physical conformance requirements, not
evidence that the tests have run or that any release is certified. The small aggregation illustration
in 11.06 intentionally checks no such issuance or physical evidence:
its output remains downstream explanatory data.

Every reference must resolve to evidence of the stated subject and scope. A
passing fixture test, an old browser bundle, a supplied route inventory, and an
actual installed handler are different evidence. The report must preserve the
origin and limits identified in Part X rather than combining their green labels
into an unsupported production claim.

The root report is explanatory. An admitted candidate or installation uses the
genuine checked construction result or supported revalidation contract. It does
not trust a caller-edited =:passed= or =:eligible= field. The report never becomes
an authorization capability for Claim or a semantic input to browser transitions.

*** Executable illustration: aggregate status is not evidence admission

The following pure function illustrates only the final aggregation of an
already validated, nonempty required-gate set and gate-result records. It does
not validate proof evidence, authorize deployment, or replace the profile's
source-derived gate construction. Its output explains blockers to a caller.

#+begin_src clojure
(def release-result-kinds
  #{:passed :failed :incomplete :unavailable :unsupported
    :skipped :stale :missing})

(defn summarize-required-gates [required results]
  (when-not (and (set? required) (seq required) (map? results))
    (throw (ex-info "Expected a nonempty required-gate set and result map."
                    {:reason :invalid-gate-input})))
  (let [blockers
        (into {}
              (keep (fn [gate]
                      (let [present? (contains? results gate)
                            row (get results gate)
                            status (if present? (:status row) :missing)]
                        (cond
                          (not (contains? release-result-kinds status))
                          [gate :invalid-result]

                          (= :passed status) nil

                          :else [gate status]))))
              required)]
    {:summary (if (empty? blockers) :requirements-passed :blocked)
     :blockers blockers}))

(def small-required-set #{:assembly-closure :real-claim :response-contract})

(def incomplete-results
  {:assembly-closure {:status :passed}
   :real-claim {:status :passed}})

(assert (= {:response-contract :missing}
           (:blockers (summarize-required-gates
                       small-required-set incomplete-results))))

(assert (= :blocked
           (:summary
            (summarize-required-gates
             small-required-set
             (assoc incomplete-results
                    :response-contract {:status :skipped})))))

(assert (= :requirements-passed
           (:summary
            (summarize-required-gates
             small-required-set
             (assoc incomplete-results
                    :response-contract {:status :passed})))))
#+end_src


The example's green result is deliberately *non-authoritative*. Two
additional pure tests reveal why: (1) a caller can fabricate passing
rows without any proof or physical test; (2) the supplied set may be
nonempty but omit the actual original requirements. Both produce a
`:requirements-passed` summary even though neither demonstrates
admission of the intended release.

#+begin_src clojure
;; This looks green solely because the caller supplied green rows.
(def invented-green-rows
  (zipmap small-required-set
          (repeat {:status :passed})))

(assert (= :requirements-passed
           (:summary
            (summarize-required-gates
             small-required-set invented-green-rows))))

;; A nonempty subset need not be the complete canonical required set.
(assert (= :requirements-passed
           (:summary
            (summarize-required-gates
             #{:assembly-closure} incomplete-results))))

;; Present malformed rows are distinct from genuinely missing rows.
(assert (= {:response-contract :invalid-result}
           (:blockers
            (summarize-required-gates
             small-required-set
             (assoc incomplete-results
                    :response-contract {:status :unknown})))))

;; Every recognized nonpassing check result remains blocking.
(assert
 (every?
  (fn [status]
    (= {:response-contract status}
       (:blockers
        (summarize-required-gates
         small-required-set
         (assoc incomplete-results
                :response-contract {:status status})))))
  [:failed :incomplete :unavailable :unsupported
   :skipped :stale :missing]))
#+end_src

These assertions are *negative illustrations*, not assertions that
Gesso has verified any actual release. Their purpose is to show that
the same pure helper can report green when its inputs are forged or
incomplete. The helper cannot learn the missing requirement from
the map it is given; preventing that failure belongs to independent
source/requirement closure and current proof/physical evidence
validation from 11.01–11.05, not to another success flag in its output.

An arbitrary caller can fabricate the example's =:passed= rows. That is precisely
why aggregation is downstream of evidence validation and is not an issuer. The
real gate checks each result's evidence, subject, profile, scope, and applicability
before it reaches this stage. An extra passing row for an unrelated capability
cannot satisfy a missing required gate.

The real required set is derived from the requested canonical scope and must not
be replaced with the three-gate illustration. Rejection of an empty set is a
small guard against vacuous aggregation, not a proof that the set is complete.

**** Check status, applicability, and authority are not interchangeable

The example's eight result kinds classify the *outcome of a named
required check*. They are not command outcomes, evidence types,
exclusions, or issued release decisions. A timed-out exploration
is `:incomplete`, not a successful proof from seeing no
counterexample. Unavailable or unsupported mandatory machinery
blocks the selected claim; the original strict profile cannot
be silently downgraded to keep its green label.

An independently authorized *narrower release intent* may
exclude a particular requirement only with its own scope,
original requirement reference, responsible decision and
visible remaining milestone debt. Exclusion is a separate
applicability result, not a ninth check outcome or a passing
check. Unknown applicability remains unresolved.

An expected refusal is a positive result only for a specified
*negative test*: it must observe the intended first enforcing
boundary and absence of the prohibited physical effect. It is
not authorization of that invalid command, nor does a
reject-all implementation meet the ordinary positive operation
requirements. Similarly, failure to retrieve release
diagnostics after a potentially committed Request mutation
does not change its domain outcome to `:rejected` or
authorize duplicate submission.

The release root of 11.05 preserves original requirement
coverage, source-bound derivations, proof assumptions, actual
test/XTDB and installed Biff/browser evidence, and the
candidate/install/activate/complete distinctions. A passing
aggregate can be displayed only as a *summary of independently
validated evidence under one declared claim*. It cannot
produce the evidence or make an incomplete source inventory
complete. Even a genuine historical aggregate is not
permanent evidence that a changed host or provider remains
qualified.

**** Required positive and adversarial acceptance pairs

| Positive case | Counterexample to reject |
|---------------+--------------------------|
| Entire original requirement inventory drives the mandatory gate set | Nonempty reduced set omits failing HumanHelp response-flow and still appears green |
| Admitted original proof/checker and real test evidence bind each passing row | All-passing caller-made status map becomes a certificate |
| Missing required gate remains `:missing` | Absent original requirement disappears from denominator |
| Malformed present row is reported `:invalid-result` by sample | Unknown status treated as a passing result |
| All seven nonpassing statuses block a required gate | Timeout, unsupported checker or stale result is counted as passed |
| Positive supported Claim operation succeeds at actual effect boundary | Reject-all handler passes solely negative tests |
| Defined invalid Claim reaches its designated protecting boundary | Earlier unrelated test wrapper rejection counts as actual physical enforcement |
| Approved preview exposes original full-plan exclusions | A small operational slice is called full HumanHelp completion |
| Named abstract theorem and accepted backend premises stay distinct | Source proof success claims physical writer and transaction correctness |
| Candidate, installation, activation and completion retain separate evidence | Pure aggregate green status activates a live host |
| Real served JS/XTDB evidence is checked for exact current subjects | Copied fixture success authorizes production |
| Commit-unknown remains pending authorized historical reconciliation | Missing report is read as rejection and triggers duplicate write |

These are prospective test requirements, not evidence that the specified conformance tests have run. The executable assertions
above test only the arithmetic of a user-supplied finite map,
not live proof, release admission, or production behavior.
*** The tested object must be the activated object

A release acts on the admitted candidate built from the application's original
source and selected assurance profile. Candidate identity binds the admitted
declarations, operations, source and dependency closure, actual prepared server
realizations, route/decoder and provider selections, proof and runtime gates,
generated plans, browser manifest, and exact emitted artifacts. The binding is
immutable for the claim being checked. A mutable registry, later Var
redefinition, stale cached report, or plausible matching name may not replace
an admitted component between checking and exposure.

The four judgments in 11.02 apply to different subjects. Candidate admission
checks the declared assembly and constructed artifact under its selected
profile; installation checks the actual host-bound handler, routes, providers,
writers, workers, and asset-serving relationships; activation authorizes
specified externally reachable service under a current installation; completion
evaluates the separately declared product and assurance milestone. Evidence for
one judgment does not issue the next. These judgments may be implemented by
one coordinated constructor, but its claimed observations and enforcement
boundaries remain distinguishable. A displayed root report is explanatory
evidence, not a transferable activation capability.

*** Establish the real candidate and installation relation

The build binds the generated bootstrap and BrowserAssemblyManifest to the
source-derived application assembly. Artifact verification checks the actual
bytes and the issuing build receipt, including the relevant compiler and
byte-affecting inputs, rather than trusting a filename, embedded stamp,
timestamp, digest supplied by the candidate, or a successful status map.
A cryptographic digest under its declared assumptions supports byte identity;
it does not prove compiler correctness, runtime semantics, or deployment
adoption. A selected toolchain remains a named qualification premise unless
its correspondence is established by stronger evidence.

The installation then establishes that the exact checked realization is
actually selected for serving. It accounts for the final route dispatcher and
protected execution gateway, the guarded Ring/Biff handler actually consumed
by the serving host, actual writer and provider bindings, background entrypoints,
response and render paths, and the exact browser assets made available to
clients. A system map containing a correct =:biff.ring/handler= does not prove
that the running listener invokes it; a build receipt does not prove that a
browser received those bytes. Later route override, unchecked raw Ring HTML,
service-worker cache, reverse-proxy rewriting and alternative server entrypoints
must either be covered by the qualified installation or remain explicit,
potentially blocking, deployment premises.

The selected environment has its own qualified dependencies. Staging results
can be reused for a production release only along an explicit checked transfer:
the semantic/implementation subject must still match, while production
credentials, backend incarnation, endpoint, installed handlers, served assets,
provider qualification and topology receive their required current evidence.
A successful staging request, a manifest copied to a new host, or equivalent
Git source does not by itself establish production installation.

The release gate retains the original predeclared requirement denominator,
including required real product paths and privileged writers. A candidate
cannot become eligible by failing to discover an operation, excluding a
mandatory physical path, changing the requested profile after failure, or
relabeling a trusted premise as proof. Conversely, a reusable abstract theorem
may remain valid across a qualified implementation change while the changed
adapter, emitted asset and installed deployment obtain fresh conformance
evidence. These are separate dependency and applicability judgments.

*** Coherent activation and failure handling

Installation may privately allocate dependencies before activation. The
selected lifecycle nevertheless fences every externally effecting application
path until its applicable checks are complete. This applies to listeners,
HTMX entrypoints, scheduled and background writers, Live publishers, stream
callbacks, and managed browser asset delivery. An init phase that opens a
listener or starts a worker before a later preflight start step must prevent
unadmitted protected behavior at that earlier boundary. Separately qualified
health or unavailable responses are allowed; a purported health path must not
invoke unchecked Claim, reveal protected content, or submit a privileged write.
Private preparation that itself needs a durable effect has its own independently
admitted effect contract.

For a selected installation scope, activation chooses one coherent
new-entry assembly and its serving relationships. The activated binding
contains route selection, actual executors, providers, policy and render
checks, guarded output, resource ownership, asset references and the chosen
compatibility policy. Publishing these as independent mutable replacements can
expose a combination that no candidate admitted. A compare-and-activate
transition, a fenced quiescent handoff, or an equivalently checked host mechanism
prevents a candidate admitted against an older predecessor from silently
superseding a newer active choice. The comparison includes the actual expected
active identity and its applicability, not just a coincident version number or
a status value in caller-controlled EDN.

The activation authority has an explicit *scope*. One host may switch an
immutable dispatch composite without making the entire load-balanced
deployment globally atomic. Hosts serving different admitted generations
during a rolling release must satisfy the declared placement and directional
continuation contract in [[#req-bounded-release-compatibility]]; the existence
of one successful local switch establishes no cross-host or browser guarantee.
A new active host or new provider incarnation cannot inherit a historical
installation qualification solely because the operation's semantic ID is
unchanged.

An invocation captures the admitted assembly and applicable concrete
execution/response context when it enters. A completed Ring return, the end
of a lexical scope, or a completed request handler call is not necessarily
the end of a future, stream, deferred response, subscription or transferred
job. These lifetimes own the necessary generation and resource references
through completion, cancellation or an explicitly checked transfer; they do
not consult a mutable global lookup for whichever executor, provider or render
guard is newest. New invocations use the activated generation. Older work may
continue only while its own binding, live authority and declared overlap
permission remain valid.

An assembly reference is not a perpetual security grant. Authentication,
Membership, source-of-truth model guards, current backend qualification,
credential validity, resource incarnation, and effect-time permission are
checked at the appropriate current trusted boundary. Retirement or revoked
premises fence new protected effects even if an old request still owns memory
or a callback retains a handler. Physical cleanup alone is not a substitute
for the revocation of semantic execution rights.

Candidate failure before activation leaves the previous assembly exposed
*only if the previous assembly still satisfies its own current conditions*.
A failed replacement cannot justify continued unsafe service after a known
backend or security premise has been revoked. The declared response suspends
the affected operation or enters a separately admitted restricted mode;
unaffected work may continue under a demonstrably valid scope. Failure after
partial physical setup accounts for listeners, workers, credentials, files,
publication and any irreversible effects actually acquired or emitted, even
when no initialization callback returned successfully.

Cleanup retires the failed candidate's owned resources in dependency-safe
order without stopping a surviving generation's shared provider or abandoning
admitted transferred durable work. The primary failure remains visible;
secondary cleanup failures, leaked resources and required operator actions
are separately recorded. An unsuccessful socket close or stale worker stop
does not restore permission to make an effect. A pre-activation effect that
already escaped the intended fence is an incident requiring authoritative
reconciliation, not a clean installation rejection.

A gate failure or response loss after an authoritative command may have been
submitted never manufactures =:rejected=. It cannot undo an incorporated
transaction or clear committed publication debt. The same stable logical
command identity, original binding, and supported historical reconciliation
path govern a possibly committed command; a later transport attempt does not
silently create another domain operation. Part IV owns command outcomes and
[[#req-application-lifecycle]] owns physical lifetime; release activation
must preserve both contracts.

*** Rollback is a new checked activation

Reactivating an older admitted assembly is a *new* installation/activation
judgment at the current time, not a reversal of the prior activation receipt.
The rollback candidate must again match its actual bytes, handler and
provider bindings; pass the currently selected assurance and security gates;
and qualify every old/new client-server direction that routing can actually
expose. An earlier passing test, historical release report or previous host
permit is not perpetual permission. The comparison/fencing rule also applies
when an older artifact is intentionally selected as a rollback target.

Rollback does not reverse durable Request state, accepted command records,
transaction history, outbox obligations, previously disclosed information, or
already installed browser effects. The selected older implementation must be
safe for the state, retained work and clients it can actually encounter.
Schema/state migrations are authoritative writers in the proof and deployment
inventory, even when executed once by an administrative tool. Their admission
names the migration's original source owner, preconditions, protected write
footprint, invariants, transaction/compensation semantics, qualified backend,
and observable committed version or state boundary.

Rollback eligibility is assessed *after observing the actual migration and
writer state*. It includes forward/backward data compatibility, concurrent
older/newer writers, pending durable work and outbox entries, stable command
result decoding and retention, old asset references, client/server envelopes,
current authorization, and the resulting invariants. Schema compatibility
cannot be inferred from an unchanged API route or a label such as
=:backward-compatible=. A compatibility promise may permit an old reader but
not an old writer; the admitted deployment must enforce that distinction.

An irreversible migration, a changed mandatory authorization rule, or an
unrecoverable command/result format may prohibit activating the old writer.
In that case the safe recovery is a qualified forward repair, compatible
read-only reconciliation path, or another separately admitted transition;
it is not an unchecked code rollback. No universal migration engine or
automatic reversal of business effects is required. A retained historical
command lookup can remain authorized even when executing its original
mutation implementation is no longer safe.

alone does not count as physically installed or traffic-active evidence.
Actual host adoption, forbidden-effect absence and live client behavior

to the *actual exposed and, if selected, reactivated realization*.

** Bounded rolling-release compatibility
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-bounded-release-compatibility
:END:

A rolling release preserves *one admitted application semantics* while
several physical realizations, artifacts, hosts or invocations may remain
alive at once. Compatibility is a checked, potentially *directional*
relation between a particular originating client/continuation and an
admitted target server realization for a specified operation and protocol.
It is not a generic property of an application name, a Git branch, or
two nearby build numbers. A successful compatibility judgment preserves
only its named semantic and physical obligations under its declared
premises; it neither grants present authority nor proves that an
unobserved deployment uses the checked artifacts.

*** Name the four independent release identities

A release decision distinguishes:

- the source-derived semantic identity of the operation, projection or plan,
  including the conditions that make its continuation meaningful;
- the exact emitted client/server artifact and protocol realization, including
  actual route/decoder/encoder and browser runtime bindings;
- the admitted application assembly and its concrete installed/activated
  generation at each applicable host and deployment scope;
- the directional continuation/adaptation relation between an origin and
  a target for the operation in question.

A semantic ID may be unchanged while generated bytes, provider behavior,
trusted writer, decoder, installation or security qualification changes.
Conversely, two different generations may support a checked continuation
relation. Neither identity equality nor inequality proves that relation
without the relevant source, physical and current-premise evidence.

The normal baseline is a *finite, declared* overlap between the current
activated assembly and a selected predecessor. Another finite window
may be admitted, but it must name the actual eligible assemblies,
origins, target installations, supported operations, duration or
retirement condition, and recovery policy. =N= and =N+1= may illustrate
activation order; their arithmetic is not an assembly identity and
does not itself mean compatible. A third activation does not magically
make every previously served page disappear or its command uncertainty
resolve. The declared retirement boundary must have an enforcing and
recoverable meaning.

Development may build and admit many candidates without activating
them. The relevant predecessor is determined by the selected activation
history and declared rollout topology, not by earlier commit time,
candidate creation order, or a numerical version label. A candidate
that was never activated is not silently treated as a predecessor
serving real clients. Conversely, a still-reachable older host or
sleeping browser cannot be omitted merely because a newer release
was admitted and activated elsewhere.

*** Check every direction the real topology can expose

For a two-assembly overlap A (selected predecessor) and B (selected
current assembly), the release gate accounts for all four pairings.
"Permitted" always means *qualified for the specified operation*,
not unrestricted permission to invoke arbitrary routes.

| Originating client or execution | Target server realization | Required release judgment |
|---------------------------------+---------------------------+---------------------------|
| A client/plan | A server | Continued actual installation validity and supported lifetime of A/A, or enforced retirement |
| A client/plan | B server | Separate old-to-new semantic/protocol compatibility, a checked directional adapter, or typed retirement/recovery |
| B client/plan | A server | Separate new-to-old compatibility, or a physically enforced routing exclusion with safe failure |
| B client/plan | B server | Current-pair admission, actual installation and active runtime qualification |

The pairs are not interchangeable: an A client may issue an envelope
that B's decoder accepts while a B client uses a new field that A's
decoder cannot safely interpret. An operation-compatible Claim
continuation need not imply compatibility for an unrelated board
query, admin writer, Live subscription, or browser effect. Even
within one pair, protected commands, read-only reconciliation,
authorized collection acquisition and advisory delivery may have
different supported directions. Record their individual scopes.

The real reachable matrix is derived from selected deployment/routing
rules *and* the actual ways clients can reach a host: mixed instances
behind a load balancer, sticky-session expiration, retry and redirect,
host loss/failover, reconnecting SSE, stale page assets, service workers,
CDN/proxy behavior, in-flight requests, delayed async responses and
sleeping browser tabs. A routing rule that excludes B-to-A is an
enforced, qualified premise, not a conclusion from an ordinary
happy-path load-balancer configuration. If failover or a direct
endpoint can reach A, that pairing remains reachable unless an
actual checked guard excludes it before the protected continuation.

The release records the true endpoint, installed route and decoder,
selected source and plan identity, actual client artifact, host
generation, required protocol features, enforcement point and evidence
for every permitted or excluded direction. A shared URL, matching
operation keyword, apparent schema shape, source hash, browser-supplied
generation label or caller-edited compatibility map does not establish
the selected target or safe execution. A manifest checked at build
time does not prove that an already-running host actually serves it.

*** Admit compatibility or stop before the effect

A permitted cross-generation continuation must preserve the
source-derived operation meaning and the selected client/server
representation, including its invocation, result and recovery
contract. Its current target must have an admitted installation,
supported decoder/encoder, available browser/runtime capabilities,
and qualified physical execution or read path. A sound complete
normalized continuation contract may support conservative semantic
equality, but the actual physical and authority joins remain
separate checks. A broader directional transformation requires a
stated representation/refinement relation and the evidence or
explicitly allowed trusted premise appropriate to the selected
assurance profile. Section 11.09 owns that contract and the exact
asset/endpoint retention required to realize it.

Unsupported or unqualified skew is detected at the *first boundary*
where the incompatible continuation could otherwise acquire
protected data, perform an authoritative effect, or install a
browser-owned result. The available outcomes are: admitted
continuation under current premises; checked directional adaptation;
semantic retirement with the declared authorized recovery; or
typed refusal where that recovery is unavailable. A silent fallback
to an ordinary unmarked HTMX request, an old anonymous decoder,
discarded semantic metadata or a guessed operation is never an
acceptable fifth outcome.

A refusal is not sufficient merely because some unrelated outer
wrapper blocks a request in a test. For the claimed enforcement
boundary, negative conformance must show the intended guard
rejecting the specific mismatched pairing *before* the protected
physical effect or disclosure, while a valid compatible pairing
succeeds at that same actual gateway. Unsupported work does not
become successful simply because the attempted transport returned
HTTP 200, a browser callback ran, or an SSE message was queued.

An existing request or stream remains tied to the originating
assembly and its admitted resource context during its declared
lifetime. A host switch does not authorize it to fetch an arbitrary
new executor, outcome mapper, provider, response guard or asset.
A checked transfer may deliberately rebind only the dependencies
its compatibility proof covers. Current principal, Membership,
model authorization, backend qualification and browser resource
ownership are not frozen by either the old assembly or a successful
release check. A revoked effect permission is enforced even for an
otherwise compatible old continuation.

A command submitted under A may have committed even when its
response is lost and B is activated. Failing the A-to-B *mutation
continuation* gate must not relabel =:commit-unknown= as
=:rejected= or authorize a new command ID. A qualified
read-only historical reconciliation path may remain usable after
the old mutation implementation is retired; its current principal,
original command binding, result decoding and disclosure checks
are independent of permission to retry. Section 11.10 owns the
retirement/intent recovery contract and preservation of HumanHelp's
explicit remote Refresh policy.

*** Make the overlap an actual release gate

The candidate and activation gates fix the declared reachable
pairings before traffic moves. Installation and runtime qualification
then establish the exact host bindings, routing exclusions, asset
references and compatible protocol implementations on which the
decision depends. A topology or artifact change invalidates the
affected direction even if the semantic continuation contract
is byte-for-byte unchanged. Unavailable or unsupported required
cross-generation checks block that requested overlap profile;
a separately approved narrower rollout may instead impose
independently enforced exclusions. The original requested
strict profile is not silently downgraded.

Rolling releases are not globally atomic merely because one host
uses a coherent compare-and-activate transition. Mixed-host operation
requires its own finite compatible window, exposure/failover
coverage and qualified retirement conditions. For a supported
direction, any claimed progress depends on stated fairness,
availability, retry and retention premises; safety cannot be
replaced by an assertion that a client will eventually reconnect.
When the promised overlap expires, the old execution rights are
retired by actual enforcement and explicitly recoverable intent
is reacquired under the current admitted semantics. Neither
elapsed wall time nor server request reference count by itself
establishes that all sleeping clients have been handled.

*** Derive a complete continuation contract

The continuation contract is derived from the *same admitted source*
that owns the operation, Choreo projection and executable client plan.
It is not a second application-authored catalog of convenient version
labels. For an originating continuation and a target realization,
the contract identifies every dependency whose change can alter the
meaning, authority, physical representation or safe completion of
the continuation. An opaque dependency is explicitly an assumption,
a separately qualified implementation contract or an unsupported
boundary; it is never silently omitted from the source-derived
inventory because static analysis cannot inspect its Clojure body.

The normalized contract includes, at the applicable scope:

- original operation, role, placement, rule/invariant interfaces and
  source-derived semantic identity, with the permitted state/transition
  family and any open proof or backend premises;
- typed argument and presence semantics, canonical logical command ID
  binding, deduplication, authority scope, result vocabulary, rejection
  and uncertain-incorporation/reconciliation obligations;
- event, knowledge/provenance and allowed information-flow conditions,
  including which participant may receive or use each observation;
- progression and acquisition source/basis, query and coverage contract,
  advisory versus canonical observation, adoption grants and required
  authorization for collection membership and rendered content;
- selected optimistic resolution, causal outcome ordering, cancellation,
  response ownership and browser effect/resource-incarnation constraints;
- the specific endpoint, route/method/template, transport envelope,
  parameter/codec versions, decoder and encoder, outcome mapping,
  HTML/HTMX semantics and actual emitted browser runtime requirements;
- resource lifetime, transfer and retirement behavior for requests,
  streams, event subscriptions, deferred callbacks, old assets and workers;
- persistent document/state and durable-work encodings that the target
  can encounter, including migration/reader/writer compatibility and
  historical command-record formats;
- provenance of selected providers, installed executors and runtime
  capabilities, plus the declared applicability and invalidation edges
  needed to qualify the physical realization.

This is a *closed and source-derived* relevant-dependency interface
for the selected operation and continuation, not a demand that all
arbitrary host code be proved. If a required dependency or reachable
state/representation is absent, the stronger compatibility judgment
is open or rejected under the requested profile. Treating incomplete
discovery as an empty dependency set would make every plan appear
compatible and is forbidden.

*** Normalize semantic equality without turning hashes into proofs

A conservative compatibility rule compares complete, type-aware
normalized continuation contracts for their *semantic* subject and
checks all remaining current physical and trusted-premise edges.
Normalization has a versioned definition, accounts for defaults,
missing-versus-null fields, role/operation identity and dependent
rule semantics, and excludes diagnostic-only source metadata only
where the source's declared meaning demonstrably does not depend on
it. A digest is a compact identifier for the normalized bytes under
its hash assumptions, not an independent derivation or current
execution capability.

Equality of source names, Git revisions, route strings or a
caller-supplied digest does not establish semantic equality; equal
semantic contracts need not imply equal emitted JavaScript,
transaction adapters, decoder implementation, host installation or
permission to disclose historical results. A changed provider,
implementation, backend or artifact may preserve the abstract
operation theorem but invalidates its dependent physical
correspondence until requalified. A changed current Membership
does not change the semantic Claim contract, yet may prohibit the
particular operation or protected acquisition at effect time.

A more permissive *directional* adapter may translate an originating
plan or protocol into a target representation. It specifies its
domain of admitted origins, target assemblies, operation and
argument/result/state mapping, preservation obligations, error and
unknown outcome behavior, and the point at which the actual
transformation is enforced. Required refinement or simulation
evidence is bound to those exact subjects and assumptions. A
selected profile may explicitly accept a qualified trusted
implementation premise where permitted; it may not silently
replace required checked refinement with a handwritten
=:compatible?= flag. Reversing a proved old-to-new adapter does
not infer new-to-old compatibility.

The selected release evidence reports which rule admitted each
operation-specific direction: exact semantic equality, checked
adapter, explicitly permitted trusted premise, or exclusion.
It ties that rule to exact origin/target identities, current
dependency and implementation qualification, open assumptions
and the concrete first enforcing boundary. A proof-looking map,
human report or old successful compiler stamp cannot act as an
adapter, proof issuer or server/DOM authorization.

*** Bind actual route, artifact and browser realization

An admitted continuation can be enacted only when its origin's
real client artifact and the target's *actually installed* route,
decoder, response encoder and supported runtime form a compatible
physical pair. The build manifest, compiled bootstrap, artifact
receipt, exact built bytes, selected host and actual served/loaded
bytes remain distinct evidence subjects. The actual bytes must
match their qualified identity; their digest alone does not prove
the compiler's behavioral correctness. A retained file at a
build path is not an actually reachable versioned browser asset,
and a valid system-map =:biff.ring/handler= does not prove which
handler a running host invokes.

Versioned asset references are immutable during the promised
lifetime, including all byte-affecting dependencies needed to
execute their admitted plan. A mutable URL such as one that
always serves only the latest JavaScript cannot be relied on for
A's client while B is active unless the release establishes an
independent checked content/compatibility and cache relation.
Proxies, CDNs, service workers, browser caches and alternative
entrypoints are part of the stated actual serving/loading
qualification; an origin that self-reports a manifest ID does
not authenticate what code the client actually runs.

The release policy identifies exactly which admitted server
handlers, route selectors, envelope/codecs, result/command
decoders, subscription endpoints, provider contracts and
browser artifacts must stay available to service each permitted
A/A, A/B, B/A or B/B direction from 11.08. It also names the
mechanism ensuring they remain actually reachable, not merely
present in a deployment folder. The selected code/runtime
compatibility envelope is bounded by a versioned admission
policy; old packages are not kept executable forever solely
because an ancient tab can wake.

A browser returning from sleep, back-forward cache or offline
storage is not assumed still current. Before a protected send,
authoritative adoption or stale callback effect, its current
resource/assembly contract, permitted server pairing and
trusted runtime conditions must be established or the old
execution is retired. A compatible client plan does not freeze
the current actor, Organization Membership, route authorization,
protected disclosure, or DOM target ownership.

*** Specify distinct retention horizons and their owners

The release derives an explicit lifetime and owner for each
required resource and separately records its withdrawal rule:

| Retained subject | Required lifetime/owner relation | What retention alone cannot establish |
|------------------+----------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| Exact old browser asset and matching dependency bytes | Named supported client execution/overlap window and serving owner, including sleeping-client recovery promise | That any browser actually loaded it, remains authorized or can continue past expiry |
| Old installed route, decoder, encoder and compatible executor | Every permitted origin-to-target operation direction during admitted overlap, under the owning host's current qualification | That an old writer remains safe after schema/authorization changes |
| Live subscriptions and event/reconnect protocol | Declared overlap, replay/fence and current interest/authorization recovery contract | That queued SSE delivery is current protected collection adoption |
| Resource-owned pending browser/request/stream work | Its valid current operation lifetime or checked transfer; retirement may end actuation rights sooner | That a reference count measures every remote sleeping browser |
| Durable command-result and historical decode/reconciliation path | Declared command retention and authorized resolution horizon, potentially longer than executable-plan overlap | That old mutation execution is still allowed or the observed state attributes a command |
| Persistent schema, migration and queued durable work formats | All admitted readers/writers and recoverable work that can encounter the format | That backward read compatibility grants backward write permission |
| Current protected content acquisition and DOM effects | Only the named current knowledge, principal, grant and resource scope | That possession of old data or a cached credential extends disclosure rights |

The overlap horizon, asset availability, command-record horizon,
authoritative state/format support, replay/registration windows
and required recovery horizon need not coincide. They must be
jointly satisfiable for the selected release promise. A release
cannot promise recovery from an old command while retiring
its only permitted decoder or losing its durable retained
record, nor can it call an old browser executable after
withdrawing the only compatible runtime endpoint.

When a horizon expires, the policy defines the reachable
typed refusal, semantic retirement and current authorized
reacquisition path. Expiry must be enforced by a current
boundary; the absence of an HTTP request, a zero process-local
reference count, a successful cache purge or a wall-clock
label does not prove that no old browser exists. A sleeping
client may be safely *refused* at the next contact without
extending its right to perform effects. Physical garbage
collection follows the actual eligible rights and retained
recovery obligations rather than creating a silent gap.

Command resolution often outlives executable-plan compatibility.
A new generation may expose a qualified, protected **read-only**
decoder and resolver for an original C42 command record even
after rejecting A's old mutation implementation. Resolution
validates the bound operation version, canonical arguments and
original target, stable command identity, current authorized
principal/disclosure and genuine trusted historical evidence;
a browser-held ID or a current Request state does not prove
C42 incorporated or identify which attempt caused it.
Where evidence is absent or outside the declared retention
guarantee, the status remains unknown or explicitly blocked;
it is not changed to =:rejected= by expiration or a network
exception. Compatible retry of C42, where separately admitted,
still must use the original binding and deduplication contract
rather than silently creating C43. Part IV owns command truth
and 11.10 owns recovering the old participant's intent.

*** Retention must preserve product acquisition policy

Release reconstruction is not a hidden source of extra
authorization or an automatic grant to adopt unrelated remote
changes. For HumanHelp, remote Request Create and lifecycle
changes remain advisory until Bob's explicitly granted
Refresh, except for precisely scoped own-command outcome and
affected-card recovery. Asset reload, SSE reconnection,
new host selection, plan retirement or recovered command
status does not discharge unrelated advisory debt or create
a new one-use acquisition grant. A newly initialized page
may have its independently declared initial-load grant; it
must be distinguished from a transparent successor read
performed merely because an old asset expired.

Retained local route, filters and an explicitly supported
draft can assist current reconstruction, but none retains old
authority. A valid current read must cover the required
scope/basis, remain discloseable under present policy, and
install only into the new resource's authorized target.
Out-of-window execution artifacts, stale optimistic rollback
callbacks and obsolete plan-private knowledge cannot be
restored by copying bytes from storage. Section 11.10 owns
the exact retirement, debt and participant-facing recovery
dispositions; this section requires the necessary retained
physical paths and evidence to make them feasible.

Section 11.08 owns *which directional pairings are reachable and
admitted*. This section owns *which source-derived contract,
physical realization and retained resources make each pairing
safe and recoverable*. Section 11.10 owns retiring the
execution and restoring only authorized semantic intent.
No separate global compatibility registry, arbitrary suspended
machine migration or universal durable participant-message fabric
is required by these responsibilities.

*** Retire execution rights while preserving unresolved intent

Release retirement is a semantic transition, not the moment a physical
resource finally disappears. It ends the selected obsolete execution's
permission to continue, emit protected commands, adopt a browser result,
consume a knowledge/acquisition grant, or mutate a resource it no longer
owns. The browser coordinator and host activation boundary establish
this disposition *before* best-effort cancellation of timers, HTTP
requests, listeners, streams, worker callbacks or old DOM resources.
Cancellation may race or fail; a surviving physical handle does not
recover its revoked effect permission.

Retirement identifies the old assembly and runtime, execution and
resource incarnations, affected continuations, and any still-owned
physical cleanup obligations. Private callback and provisional-effect
rights are retired or explicitly transferred through a checked current
owner. A late transport completion may provide admissible evidence
about a historical command, but it cannot resume a retired plan or
perform the old swap, rollback or refresh. Evidence admission,
authoritative command settlement, scoped acquisition and actual DOM
installation remain distinct decisions.

*** Preserve only explicitly recoverable semantic context

A recovery contract derived from admitted application semantics
classifies what may survive as *intent*, what may survive as
*evidence*, and what must lose execution authority:

| Surviving subject | Permitted recovery under a newly admitted owner | What does not survive |
|-------------------+-------------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Logical page/route and domain Request identity | Locate the currently valid authorized route and subject | Old plan program counter, route handler or principal permission |
| Explicitly recoverable local options or draft | Revalidate typed values, user intent and relevant current schema before use | Stale DOM snapshot, uncontrolled arbitrary JS state or automatic command issuance |
| Retained previously adopted observation | Reuse only its still-valid known scope, disclosure and progression where the new contract permits it | Currentness, proof of absence, arbitrary board membership or permission to refresh |
| Stable unresolved command C42 and original binding | Reconcile its historical result or separately re-admit a compatible retry | Old transport attempt, UI optimistic rollback right or a new logical command ID |
| Live advisory and unresolved acquisition obligation | Transfer the correct scoped debt and user grant under the current coordinator | A new grant, canonical observation or completed adoption merely from reconnect |
| Durable worker/outbox obligation | Transfer or recover under a qualified durable owner and compatible format | Old process-local scheduling callback or a fabricated successful send |

The contract does not demand persistence of arbitrary suspended Choreo
machines or restoration of an old browser's whole executable state.
A recoverable draft is not an already admitted domain mutation. A
stable domain Request ID may select a current protected observation
but does not prove that an earlier command incorporated. An invalid
new principal, changed Organization membership, removed route or
unsupported codec may force protected content and obsolete context
to retire rather than remain displayed or executable.

On recovery, each selected capability is re-admitted at its real
current boundary: the route and selected plan at construction or
serving, authentication and Membership at protected use, model guards
at authoritative write, command result at its trusted historical
resolver, acquisition/coverage at its permitted read, and resource
ownership at physical installation. Matching names, old proof
reports, persisted snapshots, application-version labels or browser
flags cannot stand in for those current checks.

*** Keep command uncertainty independent of execution retirement

A logical command and a browser execution have different identities
and potentially different lifetimes. The stable =:gesso/command-id=
belongs to one original operation, canonical arguments, command
binding and authorized source context. A transport retry can have a
new =:transport-request-id= or =:http-request-id=; neither changes
the command identity, its domain Request target or its historical
incorporation question. A process restart, replacement assembly,
retired callback or timed-out response does not imply that the
trusted operation failed or did not commit.

When an original result is unresolved, the recovery owner retains
the applicable command-specific obligation and the exact retained
record/decoder/reconciliation scope from 11.09. A current authorized
resolver examines trustworthy command evidence under the original
binding and current result-disclosure policy. A compatible durable
record may justify the permitted historical recorded outcome; a
current Request state alone cannot attribute that state to C42.
A missing record at one observation, a lost response, failed SSE
publication or expired browser plan does not prove nonincorporation.
A negative test must distinguish an actual authoritative rejection
from an unknown outcome and from a currently nondiscloseable record.

A compatible retry, where the authoritative operation explicitly
supports it, preserves C42 and the original immutable binding under
the existing deduplication and current permission checks. If the
old mutation interface is no longer eligible, a qualified read-only
historical lookup can remain supported without executing its old
writer. If binding, retention, authorization or provider evidence
does not admit either lookup or retry, recovery returns a truthful
unknown, unavailable or blocked disposition with any permitted
current-state repair path. It does not synthesize =:rejected=, invent
C43, claim a successful historical outcome or loop indefinitely.
The specific external availability and retention premises for
eventual resolution are recorded; safety is required even when those
premises fail.

Command outcome, current-state acquisition, durable publication and
browser adoption do not collapse into one success. A retained
committed C42 record can support an authorized report to Alice
while its old callback has no effect rights and its prior card
representation is not installed. A currently qualified Request
read can repair the visible card without proving C42 caused that
state. Publication and remote Live interest recovery retain
their independently required obligations after the original
server process or browser execution retires.

*** Release recovery preserves HumanHelp's adoption policy

HumanHelp's product policy distinguishes creator-local Create,
own-command settlement, specifically admitted local
rejection/unknown card repair, and unrelated remote creation or
lifecycle changes. Remote changes are *advisory* to another
participant's adopted board until an explicitly permitted
Refresh under [[#decision-humanhelp-remote-lifecycle-adoption]].
Release retirement, reconnection, a new host, resumed visibility,
asset reload and a newer runtime are not themselves authorization
to acquire and install an unrelated fresh collection.

The current recovery plan accounts for separately named:
- the adopted board's existing authorized membership and coverage;
- newer current local card/Request observations already installed;
- pending remote advisory and knowledge/progression debt;
- available explicit user Refresh permission and its one-use scope;
- own-command and locally admitted recovery exceptions;
- the effect/target owner to which an authorized response may install.

An old one-use Refresh grant is neither duplicated nor silently
restored when its former execution retires. A completed grant may
discharge only the obligations actually covered by the completed
and authorized acquisition. Unrelated advisory debt remains open,
including advisory work arriving during the in-flight read or
during a deployment boundary. An unconsumed grant or unresolved
read may be transferred only under the declared owner/expiry and
continuation contract; an incompatible plan must ask the user for
a new authorized action rather than simulate a successful click.
A new page initialization may have an independently declared
baseline read grant; that is not permission to disguise an
unrequested remote Refresh as a silent successor acquisition.

Known principal/Organization authorization changes may require
retirement of previously displayed protected content and
subscriptions. An earlier adopted observation is not a perpetual
right to disclose after an authority context changes. Conversely,
a newer already justified local card installation cannot be
regressed by a late board response merely because that response
was generated by the newly active assembly. Current source,
coverage, progression, grant, resource incarnation and physical
installation must all justify the proposed adoption.

A representative history is:

#+begin_example
A serves Alice's eligible Claim C42 for Request R17.
C42 may have incorporated, but its HTTP response is lost.
A's browser execution and callback rights retire as B activates.
B admits a current, authorized historical C42 readback.
The returned historical result is distinguished from R17's current card.
A qualified current card acquisition may repair Alice's own view.
Bob receives the remote lifecycle advisory; no automatic board adoption.
Bob's explicit authorized Refresh can read and adopt its covered scope.
Late A callbacks cannot swap Alice's card or consume Bob's grant.
#+end_example

The recovered Claim outcome is truthful only to its recorded
historical evidence. Alice's old optimistic rollback is not
restored, and Bob's board membership changes only under the
product's actual acquisition grant. A lost command-result lookup
must not be interpreted as rejection; an unavailable Refresh
service retains appropriate blocked/advisory status rather than
inventing convergence or a successful installation.

*** Rolling-release acceptance follows actual physical histories

A release requesting overlap or retirement qualifies the actual
reachable A/A, A/B, B/A and B/B pairings specified in 11.08, the
source-derived continuation and retained physical resources of
11.09, and the activated and potentially rolled-back assemblies
of 11.07. It observes real installed route/decoder and browser
artifacts, selected provider and gateway behavior, actual source
and target incarnations, and each applicable trusted authority
boundary. Positive permitted behavior and nearby forbidden cases
must be challenged at their actual corresponding enforcement
seams; a passing isolated decoder, report map, fabricated
staging fixture or reject-everything wrapper cannot issue
a deployed rolling-release result.

| Required permitted history | Adversarial history to reject or report honestly |
|----------------------------+--------------------------------------------------|
| A valid A-origin continuation completes under a retained qualified A or checked B context | A retired callback performs a new protected effect under whichever global handler is current |
| A late A response carries separately admissible C42 evidence | Its obsolete physical response installs into B's current card or marks C42 rejected |
| A real C42 record is resolved under current disclosure permission | Browser-held ID or current Request snapshot substitutes for a trusted historical record |
| No C42 result is visible yet and recovery retains unknown | One negative snapshot is called proof of noncommit and a new C43 is submitted |
| A compatible retry uses C42's immutable arguments and deduplication contract | An old command is silently reinterpreted as a new business operation |
| Qualified B historical lookup outlives A's revoked mutation interface | Reconciliation requires reviving unsafe A writer or grants a replay permit |
| In-flight A stream completes only with its checked surviving rights | A's server response adopts B's new runtime/provider without transfer |
| New principal revalidates authorized historical result or receives nondisclosing status | Old session or Membership snapshot exposes another user's protected command |
| Valid source-derived A/B and B/A directions operate with installed artifacts | Sticky routing test hides a failover to an incompatible old server |
| Excluded direction refuses before an actual guarded model write | A test-only early wrapper masks an unchecked production writer |
| Actual compatible browser asset remains served until eligible overlap expires | CDN purges A's runtime while sleeping A pages were promised continued service |
| Expired A execution is typed-retired and offered authorized current recovery | Missing JavaScript file or decoder exception silently terminates claimed recovery |
| Failed B preserves still-qualified A; revoked A rights remain fenced | B's failure keeps a known-unsafe old worker accepting mutations |
| Old/new workers and migrations preserve admitted durable formats | Backward-compatible read is treated as permission for an obsolete writer |
| An owned valid current acquisition installs the selected covered card | Stale old optimistic rollback overwrites a newer authoritative card |

The selected release profile lists mandatory observations and real
enforcement owners for each reachable case, including resource and
asset retention, backend/provider changes, unknown command outcomes,
current authorization, durable-work formats and explicit user
acquisition. Missing, unsupported, stale or unavailable mandatory
evidence blocks the corresponding release claim without silently
reclassifying it as success. A narrower rollout may be separately
admitted under its explicit exclusions and current enforcement
contract. Bounded runtime tests establish only their exercised
conditions; universal cross-host or liveness guarantees require
their additional assumptions and appropriate stronger evidence.

11.07 owns candidate activation, failed installation and rollback.
11.08 owns the directional reachability and incompatibility gate.
11.09 owns source-derived compatibility, exact artifacts and
retention horizons. This section owns *semantic retirement,
surviving command/recovery intent, HumanHelp's selective adoption,
and their final rolling-release acceptance histories*. It adds
no universal suspended-workflow persistence and no second authority
for business policy, command results or browser knowledge.

** Global non-goals and retained design freedom

Gesso's assurance boundary is deliberately narrower than the set of
possible Clojure programs, distributed databases, durable workflows and
formal systems. This limits *what the framework requires universally*,
not what an application is allowed to use or what a selected release
profile may require. A domain can opt into stronger persistence,
communication, proof, migration, consistency or verification where
its declared semantics demand it. The chosen mechanism then enters
the relevant source, dependency, qualification, writer and physical
evidence boundaries.

A non-goal is not an exclusion that can be applied after a failed
mandatory gate. A required capability remains required by its original
business source and selected release intent; an unsupported mechanism
makes the corresponding stronger claim open or blocked. Conversely,
the presence of a powerful optional mechanism does not automatically
upgrade an unrelated operation to a proven or deployed claim.

*** No universal persisted fact graph or model history

Gesso does not mandate a second persisted directed acyclic graph
containing every authoritative fact, provisional presentation,
supersession, command, witness and dependency. The domain models and
their selected authoritative backend own durable business facts,
the identities that make those facts meaningful, and precisely the
history and retention required by their operations.

Proof and assembly dependency graphs are *assurance structures*,
not a second system of record for arbitrary application state.
They must still account for the original-source obligations,
applicable assumptions, participating writers and invalidation
of dependent evidence when these change. Calling the graph optional
does not permit a required operation, outbox effect, command result
or recovery obligation to vanish from that account.

Nor is every model required to persist Git-like branches, commit
ancestry, three-way merge ancestry, event sourcing or a CRDT.
These can be chosen when the domain's consistency and conflict
semantics benefit. Gesso can expose a typed, checked interface for
conflict detection, eligible resolutions, idempotent handling and
reconciliation outcomes; the model supplies the meaning and
authorization of those resolutions. The framework never selects
a universally valid "ours wins" policy merely because it has a
provisional state and an authoritative observation.

A model that requires an audit trail, idempotent durable command
record, compensating operation, migration log or historical
outcome lookup must preserve it under the declared backend
contract. The absence of a *universal* fact DAG or event log is
not permission to erase those selected durable obligations.

*** No single global revision or automatic knowledge equality

No global integer represents, all at once, authoritative XTDB
transaction progression, domain entity revision, valid/effective
time, a command's causal history, a participant's knowledge
frontier and a browser resource incarnation. These have different
owners, comparison relations and retention lifetimes.

A provider may expose a sound scoped monotonic basis or a model
may declare and enforce its own entity revision. A composed
observation uses only the ordering and coverage relations
justified by its provider and query scope; incomparable
frontiers are not coerced into one scalar "freshness".
The browser cannot equate the latest timestamp, transport
response or invalidation with authority to install an
unobserved collection or prove a command's historical effect.

Gesso also does not require every role to know every fact.
Distributed knowledge, relevance, privacy, authorization
and permitted acquisition intentionally create different
valid projections. Agreement or convergence claims concern
only the declared reachable information, justified source
and progression frontier, permitted adoption and
environmental assumptions. Deliberately undisclosed data
is not a disagreement to be repaired by an unauthorized read.

*** No universal direct participant fabric or durable actors

An ordinary cross-user interaction can rendezvous through
authoritative domain state and later short choreographies.
Gesso does not require a global directory mapping every
actor to a current server node, universally routable
participant messages, or persistence of every suspended
server/browser machine as an independently durable actor.

If a protocol *does* require direct delivery, confirmed message
receipt, durable actor execution or a long-running workflow,
its selected module must declare those semantics, failure
alternatives, durable owner, authorization, replay/duplicate
rules and the evidence on which its claim depends. Those
capabilities remain valid application choices rather than
mandatory infrastructure for all Gesso deployments.

Single-node and multi-node realization preserve the *same*
operation, command, knowledge, Live and recovery semantics.
Scaling across nodes may introduce routing, interest discovery,
subscription fencing, shared publication and redelivery
mechanisms; these do not invent a second model of authority.
An implementation may begin with a Clojure router and later
replace a physical routing component without changing the
source-owned invalidation or permitted acquisition contract.

An SSE advisory is not necessarily a domain command, and
delivery of one message is not proof of durable database
incorporation or browser adoption. A required invalidation
must still have a justified discovery, publication, coverage
and recovery path through router loss or late interest
registration. A deployment cannot cite this no-global-fabric
non-goal as a waiver for lost required Live updates.

*** No arbitrary stale-machine migration

Gesso does not transparently deserialize or migrate arbitrary
suspended browser or server execution state through incompatible
releases. The baseline is finite checked release overlap,
typed semantic retirement, transfer of explicitly recoverable
intent and current authorized reconstruction under the
newly admitted plan.

An old execution loses its private timer, callback, resource
and optimistic rollback rights when retired. A supported
logical route, Request identity, user-owned draft or local
choice may survive only with revalidation and current owner
admission. A still-unresolved command retains its original
identity and historical reconciliation obligation where
promised; it does *not* regain old executor permission or
become a new command. A current Request read can repair a
view but is not proof of the older command's incorporation.

A checked domain-specific state transformation or compatible
long-lived workflow is permitted. Its source-derived scope,
state/codec interpretation, current principal checks,
invariants, and relevant installed effects must be qualified.
A release must not pretend that an unavailable old decoder
or an expired asset can migrate a private machine through
a "compatible" version label.

For HumanHelp, reconnection, new assets and host replacement
do not automatically grant the user a new remote-board
Refresh or silently adopt advisory-only changes. Recovery
follows the same owner, coverage and one-use grant semantics
as an ordinary running application.

*** No universal proof of arbitrary host-language computation

The finished framework has closed, purpose-built semantic
interfaces for selected rules, operations, choreography,
projection, application composition and checkable evidence.
A Clojure or ClojureScript function can participate through
an admitted interface without becoming a theorem simply
because it was called from a certified operation.

Gesso does not require a universal Clojure type checker,
unrestricted dependent types, arbitrary Clojure/JVM theorem
proving or an application author writing Lean, TLA+, Quint
or Recife scripts for ordinary development. Application
authors declare source business rules and construction,
select explicit assurance profiles, and provide genuinely
necessary premises or domain-level specifications. Gesso
derives supported proof/checker obligations and can invoke
appropriate external checking machinery as an implementation
mechanism, without making those languages a second mandatory
application specification.

A closed checker rejects unsupported expressions *for that
judgment*. The application may use a general host-language
implementation under an explicitly weaker, qualified
interface where the selected profile permits it. A mandatory
stronger proof obligation cannot be silently rewritten as a
trusted assertion because the checker cannot inspect a function.
Likewise, a proof kernel's successfully checked derivation
is not proof that a separate opaque executor implements it.

The framework does not claim to have proved the entire
JVM, JavaScript engine, browser, HTMX, Biff, XTDB, operating
system, hardware, network or every third-party dependency.
A precise source theorem can remain valid *conditionally*,
with each relevant compiler, adapter, runtime, physical
writer and deployment contract separately classified and
qualified. "Unproved" is not the same as "assumed correct":
the release report names actual unresolved, accepted or
unsupported premises and the claims depending on them.

*** No inference that a coherent business rule is the desired rule

A rule can be well typed, compositionally coherent, preserved
by every declared transition, and faithfully executed while
still expressing the wrong product choice. Gesso checks
the declared semantic and application obligations; it
does not infer the product owner's intended meaning from
a successfully type-checked syntax tree or the subset of
controls the implementation happens to expose.

Independent required capabilities, authorization choices,
user-facing interaction and positive acceptance remain
part of the original product requirement set. In HumanHelp,
missing Response interaction, Reassign or remote Refresh
cannot become "optional" because another feature passed
formal or browser checks. The product owner decides open
business choices; checking verifies the resulting selected
policy, not a guessed policy invented to pass a gate.

*** No elimination of current enforcement or physical testing

Construction and certificate checks do not freeze future
authentication, Membership, current domain guards, backend
qualification, browser target ownership or transport
compatibility. Those must be evaluated at the corresponding
trusted effect, disclosure or adoption boundary. A proof
report does not grant runtime permissions, while the installed
runtime guard must implement its selected policy without
silently downgrading an unsupported managed action.

Nor do proofs eliminate integration, concurrency,
failure-injection, real browser, selected backend, load,
startup or deployment tests. The independent production
correspondence checks which routes, writers, handlers,
assets and provider incarnations actually perform the
checked operations. A fabricated passing test map, one
successful negative-only fixture or an unused built asset
cannot replace a genuine observed boundary. Conversely,
hundreds of passing tests cannot turn an unsupported
universal theorem into a checked derivation.

No unconditional distributed liveness, recovery or
convergence follows from a connected transport or a finite
machine check. Any positive progress guarantee retains
its availability, fairness, retention, user action,
reconnect and disclosure premises. Safety must remain
accurate when those premises fail: unknown incorporation
cannot become rejection, and inaccessible recovery
cannot be described as completed delivery.

*** Identity, evidence sidecars and backend freedom

Source file, namespace, Git commit, line number, assembly
identity, exact asset bytes, normalized semantic subject,
issued proof handle and current resource incarnation are
different coordinates. Their correctly bound relations can
support verification and diagnostics; replacing one with
another because two strings look alike is not assurance.

Optional explanatory proof or diagnostic sidecars do not
change domain transition meaning or authorize effects.
A selected mandatory preflight, issuer, admission or runtime
guard *does* enforce its independent boundary using the
appropriate validated evidence. Required durable audits,
publication outbox writes and command records are actual
declared effects, not optional logs simply because they
help with diagnosis. No passive report gains the right
to mutate a Request or install a protected DOM target.

Database-neutrality is not a mandatory end in itself.
The semantic rules are separated from accidental XTDB
storage representation, but a qualified XTDB-specific
transaction, history, readback or basis-fencing mechanism
may be the most direct correct realization. An alternative
provider may be installed when its actual atomicity,
history, consistency, failure and qualification premises
establish the needed relation. It is not accepted merely
because it implements a similarly named protocol.

*** Non-goals do not weaken selected release obligations

| Deliberate non-goal | Mandatory boundary that remains |
|--------------------+---------------------------------|
| No universal fact DAG | Original business facts, writers, command outcomes, audit/outbox and proof dependencies remain complete for their selected contracts |
| No global revision integer | Every selected source, progression, causal and knowledge ordering is checked in its actual domain |
| No universal event sourcing or CRDT | Model-selected conflict and durable reconciliation semantics are enforced with appropriate authority |
| No global actor directory or cross-node message fabric | Required Live interest/invalidation and durable-work failure/recovery obligations still hold on every admitted topology |
| No arbitrary suspended-machine migration | Supported bounded compatibility, honest retirement and retained command-specific intent still hold |
| No arbitrary Clojure theorem prover | Closed-source proof obligations and explicitly classified opaque implementations still meet the selected assurance profile |
| No proof of the entire software/hardware stack | Named trusted premises, qualified deployed bindings and independent physical acceptance remain visible |
| No inference of product intent | Original independent product requirements and positive user capabilities remain the acceptance denominator |
| No unconditional convergence | Progress is conditional; safety and truthful blocked/unknown statuses survive outage |
| No mandatory database portability | Each selected backend has qualified, source-compatible transaction and observation behavior |
| No semantic sidecar authority | Required admission/runtime gates and actual durable effects still enforce their independently owned contracts |

The negative design constraints preserve room for ordinary,
lightweight server-rendered Clojure/HTMX applications as well
as applications selecting stronger formal, distributed or
durable capabilities. What may be omitted is *unselected
machinery*, never the source-derived obligations or actual
enforcement necessary for a guarantee the release claims.

** Progress metrics and completion boundaries
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-completion-boundaries
:END:

Progress is measured by the original required behavior, supported
semantic judgments, usable physical capabilities and justified
evidence for the exact subject claimed. Revision counts, file size,
passing assertions, the number of emitted reports and the fraction
of a *conveniently discovered* operation list do not establish
release eligibility or any completion milestone.

A progress measure is a diagnostic view over source-derived
requirements and genuinely observed evidence. It is not a separate
issuer, an authorization capability, or a way to alter a release
profile after an inconvenient failure. Section 11.05 owns the
underlying root claim/evidence ledger; 11.02 owns the eight
gate-result dispositions and the four independent release
decisions. This section specifies what honest counts and
comparisons are allowed to say about that ledger.

*** Freeze the denominator before measuring the numerator

Every coverage measure identifies:

- the original independent product or assurance requirement set,
  its source owner and immutable identity for the claim being made;
- the exact claim scope, selected profile, required milestone,
  operation or artifact class, and applicable generation, host,
  provider and evaluation window;
- the predeclared inventory of required subjects, obligations,
  successful paths, failure classes and execution environments;
- the admissible evidence type, issuing checker or observing
  boundary, applicability and trust premises for each item;
- the numerator's actually qualified items, the denominator's
  complete required items, and an explicit inventory of not-yet-
  credited, failed, stale, unavailable or unsupported items;
- the validation of denominator completeness against the
  *original* source or an independently specified product
  capability contract, not merely a generated catalog; and
- any approved scope change, separate preview profile or
  genuinely inapplicable item, with the resulting changed
  claim identity and independently reviewable rationale.

A missing item has not become inapplicable just because the
application failed to construct its route, render its button,
discover a writer or issue a proof obligation. An implementation
with only three exposed operations may close those three
correctly and still fail the independently required six-operation
product scope. The exposed-operation denominator and the
required-user-capability denominator are two distinct metrics;
neither silently substitutes for the other.

A denominator may change when an independently authorized
business or release requirement actually changes. Reports
preserve the previous and new inventories, the approval and
effect on claims, and recompute coverage against the new
subject. They do not call a shrinking denominator successful
implementation progress on the unchanged original goal.
A selected stricter assurance profile cannot silently turn
a missing proof into a trusted premise, skipped test or
new smaller profile after the check fails.

A ratio is reported only where its denominator and
classification make sense. For an empty selected inventory,
report "not requested" only if that inventory was validly
excluded by the claim; report "missing requirement inventory"
where source completeness cannot be established. Empty,
unavailable or unobserved is not automatically 100% coverage.
Unknown and incomparable outcomes remain explicit rather
than receiving arithmetic credit.

*** Keep different progress dimensions separate

| Metric | Stable required denominator | What may count toward coverage | Invalid shortcut to expose |
|--------+-----------------------------+--------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Usable original product capabilities | Independently selected canonical user capability and decision inventory | Eligible positive actual-user histories and applicable recovery/rejection cases | Missing Response, Reassign or Refresh vanishes because its control is absent |
| Declared assembly closure | Managed source operations, affordances, routes, acquisitions, effects and required dependencies for the selected whole application | Each actual original-source obligation assembled and discharged at its named construction boundary | Caller-supplied closed subset replaces undiscovered source obligations |
| Runtime/production enforcement | Required real route, handler, writer, output and privileged effect-boundary classes for the actual installation | The intended current gate is reached on the production path, with exercised positive and negative cases | Test-only wrapper or locally constructed handler substitutes for deployed adoption |
| Typed semantic judgment coverage | Required type, placement, knowledge, authority, refinement and progression subject/judgment pairs | A supported checker establishes its precise source-relative conclusion | Unsupported Clojure body is relabeled a checked theorem |
| Required dependency and assumption closure | Original business, proof and application obligations, with their transitive dependencies | Every required input or explicit admissible qualified premise is accounted for | An acyclic, incomplete evidence DAG is called closed |
| Framework theorem/proof-kernel coverage | Predeclared properties and supported semantic artifact classes for the selected assurance profile | Exact required properties discharged with qualified issuers, subject identity and applicable premises | A successful one-operation finite fixture counts as whole-framework proof |
| Formal-to-executable correspondence | Named semantic-to-implementation, writer, transaction, browser, installation and deployment transfer obligations | Independently valid evidence of the specific correspondence and trusted boundaries | Source proof or matching digest is treated as physical execution |
| Mutation/defect rejection quality | Predeclared representative defect classes and intended first rejecting boundaries | Each defect is rejected at its correct boundary with useful diagnosis and a paired eligible positive | Reject-all checker, flakiness or rejection far downstream counts as success |
| Qualified substrate coverage | Actually selected backend, transaction adapter, compiler/browser runtime, host, configuration, providers and topology envelopes | Required tests/premises for those exact implementations and current conditions | A shadow adapter, old artifact or different XTDB environment is credited |
| Recovery and interruption coverage | Required loss, uncertainty, reconnect, replay, retirement and provider/host failure histories | Truthful outcomes, usable current recovery and correct owner/authority constraints under stated premises | Happy-path HTTP/SSE delivery is called eventual convergence |
| Release overlap/rollback coverage | Every actually reachable permitted or excluded client/server direction and retained work/format dependency | Qualified real routing exclusions, compatible operations, retirement and rollback state | One host switch is called globally atomic |
| Evidence reuse/impact accuracy | Applicable unchanged obligations plus affected semantic, physical and environmental change classes | Current reusable evidence and required rechecks both accounted for | An old green cache suppresses required invalidation |
| Test execution inventory integrity | All required jobs, cases, environments, shards and accepted result/receipt records | Each genuine completed and attributable test result in its declared scope | Missing worker result, truncated inventory or fabricated status is counted as passing |

Each row is a *separate* claim dimension. A release can
have complete semantic closure but lack actual installation
or user-facing acceptance; it can exercise every browser
capability without establishing a theorem; it can prove a
model invariant while its full physical writer set remains
unknown. Any combined presentation retains those separate
blockers, required evidence classes and dependency links.
No weighted "overall assurance %" can promote a missing
mandatory gate to success.

*** Measure coverage with evidence status, not optimistic arithmetic

The root evidence ledger retains the status of *every*
required gate as one of the eight classifications from
11.02: =:passed=, =:failed=, =:incomplete=, =:unavailable=,
=:unsupported=, =:skipped=, =:stale= or =:missing=.
Passing credit is possible only when the checked evidence
actually refers to the intended subject, source, current
installation/environment and selected profile. A copied
"passed" map is not evidence and has no numerator authority.

A report distinguishes at least:

| Category | Meaning for the original requested claim |
|----------+------------------------------------------|
| Required and verified in scope | Eligible to receive its precise checked credit |
| Required but not executed/observed | Still missing or incomplete; no success credit |
| Required but unavailable/unsupported | Blocking unless an independent new profile explicitly changes the claim |
| Executed and failed | Report failure, intended boundary and reproducible/unknown diagnosis |
| Previously checked but stale | Re-evaluate dependencies and required physical or source changes before reuse |
| Skipped by an approved narrower profile | Visible exclusion under a different claim, not completion of the original |
| Trusted external premise accepted by the selected profile | Conditional assumption disclosed with all dependent claims, not counted as independently proved |
| Truly inapplicable under original requirements | Exclude only with a justified, traceable scope rule, not a failing implementation |

For example, suppose the independent product requirement
set contains Create, Claim, Unclaim, Reassign, Response
and Refresh. If four have eligible actual positive-browser
evidence and Reassign and Response do not, the product
coverage is four of six, with the two missing controls
named. If the assembled UI exposes only the four successful
operations, it may have four of four *exposed-route closure*
but still has only four of six required product capability
coverage. The latter denominator does not shrink to make
the application look complete. An incomplete Response
product decision is an explicit blocker until the owner
selects and the application demonstrates its contract.

The same discipline applies to formal proof: a finite
check of selected states counts toward its *finite
subject* only. It does not count as an induction theorem,
universal checker soundness, independent proof-kernel
verification or the whole installed XTDB/browser refinement.
Counts of assumptions and trusted-base components are
audit aids, not a scalar probability of correctness.
An accepted environmental premise stays visible and
transitively attached to all claims that depend on it.

*** Diagnose first-boundary rejection and positive usefulness

Rejection quality measures the difference between a defect
discovered at the earliest justified owner and one allowed
to fail late, at an unrelated wrapper, or only after a
protected physical effect. For every included mutation
or controlled-defect family, state the intended violation,
first sound checking boundary, expected typed diagnostic,
physical effect/non-effect oracle and paired valid case.
The defect inventory is selected independently of the
mutations the implementation happens to reject.

An invalid forged principal must fail at the trusted
authorization boundary, not be "proved safe" because the
browser happened not to show the button. An incompatible
old-client command must be rejected before the real
protected writer, not merely by a test-only interceptor.
A malformed model rule rejected by a compiler only after
a missing provider crashes has not demonstrated the
intended early diagnosis. Conversely, a checker that
blocks *all* Claims, or only accepts trivial rules, has
excellent rejection arithmetic but fails positive
usefulness and the selected capabilities.

Failed or flaky tests remain accountable under the
declared test isolation, schedule and evidence policy.
A rerun that happens to pass is new observed evidence;
it does not erase a genuine unresolved nondeterministic
failure or justify excluding its original job from the
required inventory. A first-boundary requirement may
be structurally proved, physically observed, or still
an open transfer obligation; the reporting level is
not silently upgraded.

*** Preserve performance, reproducibility and reuse context

Performance measurements identify hardware, JVM/browser,
compiler, storage/backend version and configuration,
dataset and data distribution, worker count, routing
topology, resource limits, warm/cold state and executed
workload. A fast deterministic developer gate is
valuable for iteration; it is not a substitute for
required complete JVM, finite/formal, Chromium, real
XTDB, fault/reconnect or multihost coverage.

Parallel tests retain a deterministic selected inventory,
the exact job-to-shard assignment and result accounting,
isolated side effects and proof that required preflight
ran for the proper scope. Missing, timed-out, duplicated
or otherwise unauthenticated worker completion cannot
turn a release gate green. A summary from an orchestration
tool must be tied to actual executed tasks and failures
under its declared trust boundary; a copyable report
cannot authenticate execution by itself.

Incremental proof or test reuse is measured by correct
dependency invalidation and current applicability, not
only by jobs avoided. When source meaning remains stable
but actual browser bytes, handler, provider or backend
incarnation changes, the abstract theorem may remain
reusable while physical correspondence evidence must
be requalified. Source, environment, trusted premise,
toolchain and original-required-set changes have
distinct invalidation effects. Report both avoided
work *and* required reruns; reducing elapsed time by
dropping required work is a false optimization.

The release and completion milestones in 11.13–11.18
use these metric definitions to explain partial
checkpoints, not to replace the respective mandatory
gates. Project management can track iteration count,
time and document size as resource measures; these
are never evidence that a business behavior, theorem
or installed browser effect is correct.

*** Positive and adversarial measurement controls

| Honest measurement | Invalid or misleading nearby measurement |
|--------------------+------------------------------------------|
| Six independent product capabilities remain required when four are implemented | Four exposed controls are reported as 100% of the original product |
| Missing Response product decision is shown as a blocker | Response is silently removed from the requirement inventory |
| Exposed operations close against their actual original assembly source | A caller-generated subset is called complete whole-application closure |
| Current installed protected routes and writers are independently challenged | A test-only wrapper is counted as production-path enforcement |
| Supported checked rule form earns its exact theorem/judgment credit | Unsupported arbitrary Clojure is relabeled checked because it was executed |
| Trusted premise remains attached to dependent abstract proof | Accepted assumption is counted as discharged independent theorem |
| A finite model run credits only its bounded initial/transition domain | Finite sample is shown as universal XTDB/browser correspondence |
| Original mutation inventory and positive cases are predeclared | Reject-all verifier records perfect negative test percentage |
| Invalid route fails before the intended effect with useful diagnostics | Late crash after write or unrelated wrapper rejection is called first-boundary success |
| A valid real Reassign executes under the same guard challenged by rejection | Disabled or unavailable button is counted as positive capability |
| Actual compiler/host/backend configuration is measured | A passing different adapter or staging deployment counts as production |
| Command C42 remains unknown with explicit permissible recovery | Timeout is scored as a successful rejection or terminal resolution |
| A/B and B/A actual routing exposures are measured or excluded by tested guards | Happy-path sticky session is called full rolling-deployment qualification |
| New browser asset invalidates required physical checks while reusing sound abstract proof | Cached source-equivalent theorem is treated as current loaded-asset evidence |
| Each required parallel job is accounted for and its failures remain inspectable | Missing shard, stale log or rerun success silently cleans the denominator |
| Physical load measurement includes hardware, topology, limits and data | Fast one-worker test is advertised as production throughput |

These are prospective measurement and acceptance obligations;
the table is not itself observed test evidence. A complete
and accurate report can be useful while the release is
blocked. It is the distinction between *what is required*,
*what has actually been established* and *what remains
open*, not a decorative percentage, that makes progress
visible and keeps completion claims honest.

*** Operational application completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: milestone-operational-application
:END:

The first bounded completion milestone is a *usable, structurally
closed, actually installed* Gesso application, established through
the HumanHelp proving application and its public production-model
contracts. The milestone is not the successful creation of an
ApplicationAssembly, a passing Claim fixture, a running Biff
server, a green release summary, or an acceptance decision about
this specification. It requires the independent original product
capabilities and the actual supported production paths that make
them work.

This milestone is deliberately distinct from full compositional
certification, independent external formal verification and
production multi-node completion. Those have separate completion
subjects in 11.14–11.16. The operational milestone nevertheless
honors the ordinary semantic/runtime checks and every stronger
assurance obligation explicitly selected by its release profile;
it cannot obtain a green result by downgrading an unavailable
mandatory theorem or a required physical test after failure.

*** Bind one original product target to an admitted release

The requirement set is fixed from independent HumanHelp business
and interaction obligations in Part VIII, not inferred from the
example's currently implemented routes, rendered buttons, or
discovered command catalog. The acceptance profile identifies
actual required controls, successful user histories, rejection
and recovery paths, authoritative model outcomes and real
environmental premises. A valid partial checkpoint names
what is missing against that same denominator.

The canonical construction closes the source-declared models,
rules, operations, choreography, executable plans, HTTP routes,
trusted execution and outcome mapping, authoritative acquisitions,
rendered affordances, Live subscriptions/publication, generated
browser assembly and exact artifact relationships. Part VII
owns the underlying construction and its mandatory checks.
The installed application must use the *same qualified semantic
subject and actual compiled/served realization* for the checked
operation. A system map, build receipt or completed constructor
does not independently prove a real listener, worker or browser
uses those checked bindings.

Completion joins three separately evaluated claims from
[[#req-application-assurance-claims]]:

| Claim subject | Required evidence | What cannot substitute for it |
|---------------+-------------------+-------------------------------|
| Declared closure | Source-complete construction and render/operation-plan relations for the admitted application | A closed subset with missing original capabilities, or a caller-edited manifest |
| Actual production enforcement | Installed route, trusted gateway/model writer, response/asset/HTMX paths and required effect guards behave at their real physical boundaries | Test-only wrappers, a valid unconsumed Ring handler, fabricated status rows or a staging artifact |
| Required usable behavior | Eligible user actions visibly succeed with authorized source-of-truth results, plus their relevant negative, concurrency and recovery cases | Disabled controls, HTTP 200, provisional DOM changes, isolated unit tests or console silence |

A release may pass a structural test yet fail product behavior,
or pass one browser scenario while the actual authoritative
writer bypasses the checked model. Each failure remains a
separate blocker. Root evidence reports do not issue runtime
permission, certify externally observed tests merely from
caller-provided data, or grant HumanHelp actor authority.

*** Prove the public production model owns the result

The proving app uses the actual public production
User, Membership, Organization, Location, Request and
RequestAssignment owners and their declared interfaces;
it does not replace them with an example-only Claim model,
browser-local permission rules or a separate store invented
to make a fixture green. Trusted server authentication binds
the acting principal, and production-model guards check
current eligibility, actor/target distinction, required
Organization/Location association and protected Request
and assignment dependencies at the authoritative effect
boundary.

For each admitted lifecycle mutation, the public model
determines current Request truth, permitted assignment
transitions and outcome meaning. The primary assignment
and the Request state agree in the authoritative transaction
and authorized projection. The completed lifecycle state
is =:done=. Reassign selects a *different eligible target*
through its real permitted manager/supervisor authority,
closes the superseded primary responsibility and establishes
the new one under guarded state; it is not a relabeled
self-Claim or a fabricated target parameter.

The command path keeps stable logical command identity,
canonical binding, authorized effects, compatible
incorporation/replay, readback and disclosure distinct from
the transport attempt and displayed card. Real XTDB
transition behavior must satisfy the selected transaction,
contention and consistency assumptions, including the
actual production writer inventory. A lost response is
=:commit-unknown= where incorporation is not established;
a missing readback is not a domain rejection or permission
to allocate another command ID. Required durable publication
and external writer obligations remain visible even if the
browser promptly renders the desired card.

The observed user result is established through the operation's
qualified authoritative outcome and acquisition contract.
A separate post-commit read is required *where that contract
needs one*; it is not an unconditional ritual after every
successful transaction. Conversely, a provisional HTMX
swap or copied optimistic state is not production-model
truth or a substitute for an independently justified
installation of current data.

*** Exercise the full independent HumanHelp capability set

Part VIII owns the exact product meaning and acceptance
contracts. The operational profile includes, at minimum,
authenticated access and relevant protected disclosure,
Create and immediate permitted creator-local visibility,
details and local interaction, the six production Request
lifecycle actions, response interaction, search and ordering,
board visibility/terminal options, Live advisory and explicit
Refresh, optimistic feedback, selection/scroll continuity
and honest rejection, uncertainty and reconnection recovery.
An implementation cannot omit a difficult capability and
make its own smaller feature catalog the completed product.

Each lifecycle action has an eligible positive real-browser
history and applicable forbidden, stale-state or concurrent
histories:

| Operation | Required successful user result | Essential contrary boundary |
|-----------+---------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| Claim | Eligible helper becomes the guarded primary assignee | Two concurrent helpers cannot establish two active primaries |
| Unclaim | Permitted current owner relinquishes responsibility with coherent Request/assignment state | Stale or unauthorized actor cannot clear another current assignment |
| Mark on the way | Eligible current assignee performs the declared transition | Old or unrelated actor cannot advance a protected Request |
| Complete | Permitted actor reaches authoritative =:done= and closes required assignment state | A provisional completed card or duplicate completion is not a second successful mutation |
| Cancel | Authorized requestor performs the allowed cancellation and dependent closure | Unrelated user or invalid current lifecycle cannot cancel |
| Reassign | Authorized manager chooses a distinct eligible helper and persists correct replacement history | Forged target, self-claim substitute, stale eligibility or missing manager scope cannot pass |

The product's Response interaction is a separate *required
but not yet independently selected* product contract at
[[#decision-humanhelp-response-flow]]. Before full operational
completion, the designated product owner must select
the observable user intent, eligible actor, model/external-effect
ownership, privacy/disclosure, success, error and recovery
behavior, then that selected contract must pass independent
acceptance. Gesso does not invent email, messaging, contact
disclosure or a second Request model merely to fill the gap.
A placeholder button or an existing Claim handler is not
accepted as Response without the independently authored
equivalence and positive user result.

Remote Create and remote lifecycle changes are **advisory**
to another participant's board until explicitly adopted
under [[#decision-humanhelp-remote-lifecycle-adoption]].
An own-command settlement and specified affected-card
rejection/unknown recovery have only their declared
exceptions. The explicit one-logical-query Refresh grant,
its coverage, possible user-authorized successor intent
and remaining advisory debt are governed by
[[#req-humanhelp-refresh-coverage]]. Successful SSE send,
reconnect, presence of a new card in storage or server
restart is not a new user Refresh grant. A newer justified
local card observation must not regress under a late,
incomparable board response.

Search, creation ordering, mine-first, unclaimed-first,
terminal visibility, selected card/detail state and
permitted local input are tested over **adopted**
information, not by secretly acquiring unrelated remote
changes. Re-render and continuity preserve appropriate
local state without retaining revoked principal authority,
stale optimistic callback rights or an obsolete browser
resource's permission to install content.

*** Qualify the actual server, browser and backend path

The selected Biff startup and application-module order
must install the guarded handler and render-validation
path actually consumed by the serving listener. Actual
source and render affordances must close to the intended
Choreo operation, execution gateway and typed outcome.
Alternative routes, raw Ring HTML, scheduled or background
writers, migration/admin entrypoints and other selected
physical escape paths either receive required coverage
or remain named blocking premises for the claim. Private
initialization must not expose an unadmitted effect.

Exact generated browser bytes and manifest/receipt
relations are checked under their stated compiler and
toolchain premises, while actual serving and browser
loading are separately observed. The real physical
browser path exercises the admitted semantics through
HTMX, SSE, resource ownership, continuity and the
selected optimistic protocol. A fixture that directly
posts hidden command metadata without using the actual
rendered control is not a valid substitute for a user's
operation. Conversely, a visible successful control does
not independently prove every physical XTDB effect or
command-record causal relation.

Independent product acceptance runs real Chromium or
an equivalently declared supported browser against
the selected served assets and actual routes, with
appropriate authenticated contexts. Multi-client
advisory, concurrent Claim, authorization, session
changes and Refresh require genuinely distinct actors
and correct source-of-truth observations. The selected
XTDB backend, adapter, schema, consistency/progression,
command records, provider qualification and deployment
premises must match the application being claimed;
a passing in-memory fake or unrelated local backend
cannot issue this production conclusion.

Representative controlled defects prove that the
intended *production* construction and effect paths
invoke their promised checks. Invalid operation
binding, missing original requirement, stale route,
unqualified writer, forged principal, incompatible
client generation, stale DOM resource, and incorrect
Refresh use should be caught by their first
appropriate boundary. Nearby valid cases must work;
a reject-all route and a test-only protective wrapper
are not successful product or enforcement evidence.

*** Demand honest interruption and recovery behavior

Usable operation includes the declared interruption
and uncertainty paths, not only an uninterrupted
positive script. Test at relevant boundaries:
concurrent conflicting updates; timeout or response
loss after possible commit; unknown incorporation
with retained original command identity; Live delivery
interruption and required publication recovery;
invalidation arriving during Refresh; disconnect
and reconnect; stale resource callback, superseded
optimistic effect or newer adopted card; principal/
Membership changes; and failed preflight, installation,
asset or provider qualification. A case may finish
with a truthful blocked or unresolved disposition
under stated availability assumptions rather than
a fabricated successful convergence.

A runtime transition can retire old browser execution
rights while preserving only explicitly recoverable
semantic intent. Release compatibility, asset/decoder
retention and current result disclosure follow
11.07–11.10. An old callback cannot undo a newer
authoritative installation, invent a duplicate Claim
or consume someone else's Refresh grant. Required
resource caps, bounded subscriptions/queues and
recoverable user-facing errors remain part of the
selected operational acceptance profile.

The operational milestone does not silently include
the full general multi-node proof and production
deployment profile of 11.16. It *does* require
every client, server, backend and failure condition
actually selected for its own operating environment,
including real multi-client behavior and any physical
routing constraints it claims. If an operational
profile chooses a multi-host topology, its applicable
cross-host premises and acceptance cases cannot be
waived merely because 11.16 remains a separately
named broader milestone.

*** Compositional certification completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: milestone-compositional-certification
:END:

The compositional-certification milestone establishes a *working,
non-vacuous and source-complete chain of specified semantic judgments*
for its declared artifact classes, operation catalog, application
scope and assurance profile. It is neither a generic =:certified=
flag nor a promise to prove all Clojure programs. The authoritative
definitions of each judgment, its proposition, issuer/checker
boundary and dependency obligations are in Part IX; the
production-correspondence frontier is in Part X.

A completion decision must name both what was genuinely checked
and what remains conditional. A verified derivation, a conditional
abstract induction, a qualified model executor, an installed
handler, an observed browser interaction and an independently
proved checker meta-theorem are *different* claims. Their evidence
can be linked through correctly established relations, never
identified merely because the application report lists them
under one operation name.

*** Complete the supported source-to-judgment chain

For the selected supported artifact and operation classes the
milestone requires the following actual capabilities:

| Semantic boundary | Required operational result | Not established by that result alone |
|-------------------+-----------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| Typed rule construction | Closed versioned syntax, value types, short-circuit semantics, safe presence/refinement reads, interpretation and conservative normalization | Correctness of an arbitrary opaque Clojure callback or the desired business policy |
| Typed rule and guarded-operation judgments | Exact original source, input/reader paths, permitted transition and frame obligations with typed rejection of malformed cases | That actual XTDB writers implement those transitions |
| Distributed use-site admissibility | Role and program-point knowledge, provenance, disclosure, authority, refinement, progression and resource rights are checked at their source-owned uses | That a queued transport observation was authorized, delivered or adopted by a real browser |
| Global/projection/executable-plan relations | Projection and lowering are tied to the original Choreo subject, selected observable behavior and checking scope | Unconditional physical browser/HTMX/SSE trace refinement |
| Proof kernel and dedicated checkers | The admitted closed inference rules and required semantic judgments are actually checked, with recognized unknown/unsupported cases | Independent soundness of the implementation of the proof kernel itself |
| Conditional invariant/induction checking | Checked initial-state and preservation obligations for each invariant and required operation in the declared catalog | Complete physical writer coverage or all real initial states without separate evidence |
| Source-derived obligation and dependency closure | Original declarations determine every required proposition, branch, producer, dependency and premise for the selected profile | A graph supplied by its own claimant is complete merely because it is acyclic |
| Issuance, consumption and currentness | A genuine checked result is bound to the exact proposition, source, profile, checker versions and applicable dependency generation | Permission to run a model command, install a DOM target or infer a deployed release |
| HumanHelp real Claim pilot | One substantial public-model Claim produces traceable semantic, construction and qualified production/browser evidence | Every HumanHelp product capability or universal deployed-system refinement |

A checker implementation may fuse these stages or use several
focused namespaces. Completion concerns their observable
semantics and trusted boundaries, not a mandatory number of
compiler passes, a required external proof language or a
particular public function name. Arbitrary host-language
implementation remains available through an explicit,
profile-permitted implementation premise; an unsupported
closed judgment cannot silently become a proved theorem.

*** Require independent source completeness and useful positives

The accepting boundary obtains the *original* model, rule,
operation, choreography, projection and application
declarations with their actual source owners, relevant
imports and independent required-capability inventory.
A proposed derivation does not get to define the set
of propositions it must prove. Required obligations
include every supported operation and applicable branch,
read/write/frame condition, role-local use, required
initial state, guarded outcome, resource exit, relevant
dependency and expressly selected invariant.

Obligation and dependency coverage is checked independently
against that original source. A cyclic proof-justification
graph is invalid; an acyclic graph that omits Reassign,
the rejection branch, an original Organization rule or
an opaque implementation dependency is *also* invalid
for a claim that requires those subjects. Runtime
choreography loops are not automatically circular proofs.
An admitted abstract subset may legitimately pass only
when its narrower subject and exclusions are explicit;
it does not meet a larger original application
denominator by changing the reported label.

Certification is not satisfied vacuously by an
unsatisfiable initial-state specification, a rule
checker that rejects every Claim, unreachable
user-facing positive operations, or an inconsistent
collection of admitted premises. Where the selected
claim requires inhabited initialization, reachable
allowed transitions or real participant usefulness,
these are separately checked or explicitly qualified.
Correct rejection is paired with an eligible positive
under the same chosen checker and application
boundary. Product policy remains independently
owned: a sound derivation can enforce a coherent
but unintended HumanHelp rule.

*** Distinguish derivation, assumption and proof soundness

The small kernel checks exact propositional or other
supported derivations under their named hypotheses.
Dedicated checkers establish their own defined
typing, guarded-transition, finite-model, induction,
distributed or composition judgments. A developer
or elaborator cannot issue a certificate merely
by returning =:valid?=, =:proved=, a matching hash,
a copied checker summary or a self-described
dependency graph.

A checked derivation says that its conclusion follows
from the named premises under the *specified calculus*
and the implementation's checker trust contract.
It does not prove that every atomic business predicate
holds in live Request data, that the selected business
policy is desirable, or that the kernel's own algorithm
is independently sound. Independent kernel soundness
and production-to-formal checker correspondence are
separate external formal obligations of 11.15.
When that stronger scope is selected they cannot be

For conditional induction, each declared invariant

operation** in the required catalog, with the
actual transition, simultaneous writes, readable
prestate and unchanged frame interpretation.

Assumptions are propagated along the particular
accepted justification for the guarantee consumed.

other admitted entailment from evidence of the

source, parameter, principal, authority, temporal
basis, implementation and issuer boundaries.

Checked premise identities, dependencies and
rejected substitutions remain inspectable.

*** Bind real issuance and safe evidence reuse

The ordinary construction path checks source shape,
typing, elaboration, coverage, dependency closure,
proof rules, premise policy and coherent currentness
before issuing a source-bound, typed admitted product
for the exact supported claim. A human-readable root
report is derived from the same genuine checked
records but is not an issuer or authorization
capability. A raw map or test fixture can describe
a proposition; it cannot impersonate the issuer
of a checked proof or an admitted executor.

Process-local handles require their intended current
issuer/registry and dependency context. Any durable
serialized package is treated as untrusted input
until decoded and independently re-admitted or
authenticated and rechecked under its declared
threat, rule-version, source and premise model.
A signature can authenticate attributed bytes under
its trust contract without proving the signed
business proposition. Reuse is valid only when the
exact source and applicable semantic/checker
dependencies remain qualified; changes to
normalization, trust assumptions, domain rules,
issued executor, backend, compiled artifact or
installed route invalidate the *affected kinds*
of evidence. A reusable abstract theorem does
not requalify changed physical implementations.

The application assembly may consume the admitted
result only for its matching selected operation,
plan and source generation. The trusted command,
installed Ring/HTMX and browser boundaries separately
enforce current authority, execution correspondence
and physical resource ownership. Certificates and
optional sidecars do not determine runtime business
transition outcomes or grant permission to replay
a logical command. Mandatory construction/installation

their own stage; those gates do not derive it from
caller-editable reporting fields.

*** External formal verification completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: milestone-external-formal-verification
:END:

External formal verification is a *separate milestone with an
explicit theorem and implementation-transfer frontier*. It
establishes independently checked mathematical results about
the exact closed Gesso semantics named by the claim and, where
the requested claim includes production acceptance, an
adequately justified connection to the real checker and
issuer. It does not prove every possible Clojure program,
whole HumanHelp product behavior, every XTDB transaction,
the deployed browser or every possible multi-node history
merely because one formal calculus has been mechanized.

The initial target is the seven-rule *closed propositional
proof kernel* and its acceptance relation from Part IX and
[[#req-formal-production-correspondence]]. It is deliberately
small enough to specify independently of the runtime and to
remove a specific trust gap. The independently accepted
semantics and exact first-target obligations are in Part X,
including 10.10; this milestone describes which results
and transfers must exist before completion can be declared.
It does not create a second formal semantics or authorize
Gesso authors to maintain a separate handwritten policy
model merely to obtain checking.

*** Bind the external theorem to the original semantic subject

The formal package identifies its actual source-owned
proposition, inference language, nominal formula/atom
identities, premises, proof-step structure, Boolean
interpretation, admitted checker rules, proof-goal
relation, and the precise versions of normalization
and original-source interpretation under examination.

The first calculus consists of exactly the following
seven admissible *inference rules*:

| Rule | External meaning and side condition |
|------+-------------------------------------|
| =:premise= | A declared named premise P permits derivation of P, retaining that premise as open |
| =:truth-introduction= | The distinguished true formula is derivable without assuming an unproved atom |
| =:implication-reflexivity= | A well-formed P entails P implies P |
| =:conjunction-introduction= | At least two checked premises yield their ordered conjunction |
| =:conjunction-elimination= | A checked conjunction yields exactly its permitted indexed constituent |
| =:implication-elimination= | Checked P implies Q, followed by checked P in the required reference order, yields Q |
| =:disjunction-introduction= | A checked P yields the declared disjunction containing P in its permitted position |

The formal language includes the syntax and valuation of
the admitted formula constructors, even when a constructor
has no corresponding inference-introduction rule. No
unimplemented arithmetic, SMT entailment, arbitrary
rewriting, hypothetical-context discharge, excluded-middle
rule, atomic-truth axiom or Clojure evaluation can be
implicitly added to make a proof pass. Source well-formedness,
ordered backward references, no cycles, exact rule arity,
index and conclusion, supported limits and the required
premise/step-use discipline remain explicit. A stronger
inference language requires its own theorem and production
correspondence rather than inheriting this theorem by name.

The baseline independent *soundness proposition* is:
for every admitted well-formed original-source derivation
D of goal P from named premises Gamma and every Boolean
valuation v of its opaque atoms, if the inference relation
accepts D and each premise *actually used by D* holds
under v, then P holds under v. The formal inference
relation is defined independently of the production
Clojure checker; its acceptance is not a trusted call
to that checker. A derivation with remaining open
premises is a conditional entailment, not a theorem
that those business propositions are true. A proof
whose assumptions have no reachable model may still
be logically sound but does not establish a useful
or satisfiable business operation.

*** Complete and distinguish the five proof/transfer claims

The milestone ledger identifies the following distinct
S0–S4 results and their evidence status:

| Claim | Required result | What it does not establish alone |
|-------+-----------------+----------------------------------|
| S0 — inference-rule soundness | Independently specified and machine-checked preservation of Boolean entailment by each of the seven legal rule schemas, arities and side conditions | An accepted sequence of concrete production proof maps is valid |
| S1 — derivation-system soundness | Machine-checked induction over all well-formed finite ordered derivations, including exact referenced premises, goal, and rejection of unsupported derivations from the admitted relation | The Clojure checker actually implements the formal relation |
| S2 — production acceptance correspondence | A checked or explicitly qualified relation from every *supported production-accepted input* through its actual parser, normalizer, rule checker and admission path to the S1 relation | Unconditional implementation soundness when this transfer is only a trusted premise |
| S3 — typed atom/meaning bridge | The supported typed business-rule evaluator, source-reader context, presence/nil and result interpretation correspond to the opaque-Boolean atoms relied upon by the claim | Arbitrary opaque Clojure policy truth, current Membership or real domain data |
| S4 — issuer/composition/realization binding | Formal subject, original-source-required obligations, dependency/premise closure, current issuer/checker version and consuming application artifacts are linked with separately justified implementation and installation boundaries | Direct permission to execute a command, arbitrary physical XTDB/browser refinement or complete product acceptance |

For the initial independent *logical* milestone, S0 and
S1 must be genuinely machine-checked, not replaced by
ordinary tests or a handwritten assertion that each
inference rule appears sound. The declared **production-
transferred milestone** additionally requires its exact
selected S2–S4 obligations to be discharged by checked
relations or clearly named, independently qualified
trusted premises *where the original selected profile
allows them*. Any still-trusted edge remains visible
in the final claim; the milestone cannot call its
production correspondence unconditional.

A stronger profile that requires proven S2, S3, S4 or
full execution refinement cannot be satisfied by
downgrading those duties to tests or assumptions after
failure. A narrower S0/S1 theorem package can be
reported as valuable partial progress, but it does
not meet a selected release claim that requires the
production transfer. Neither S2 nor S4 is established
by simply pasting the same digest into a Clojure
proof report and a formal result.

*** Establish the correct direction of correspondence

The crucial safety direction is *production acceptance
implies formal admissibility* for every concrete input
the actual selected production path can accept. That
requires sound decoding of all input forms the path
admits, source and nominal identity, typed proposition
normalization, reference resolution, premise tracking,
ordered step checking, resource-limit handling, and
the particular certificate/issuer admission behavior.
A theorem quantifying over inputs to an *ideal* formal
checker does not rule out a real production parser
accepting a malformed unsupported input.

The transfer mechanism must name its own trusted
boundary: verified implementation/extraction,
machine-checked translation, proof-producing
compilation with independently checked output, or
an explicit qualified translation/checker assumption.
An independently formalized derivation is not linked
to production simply because both tools accept a few
test vectors. Calls from the formal semantics into
the unchecked production checker would assume away
the very implementation gap the theorem is meant
to close.

A soundly *rejecting* production checker may be
incomplete relative to the mathematical calculus.
Soundness of accepted results does not require it
to accept every valid formula, but a release claim
for a useful supported fragment also needs the
selected positive/control and coverage obligations.
Unsupported syntax, invalid derivation, source
mismatch, resource exhaustion, tool outage and
search timeout remain distinct non-success statuses.
A changed accepted proof calculus, checker
implementation or parser interpretation needs a
new applicable S2 relation even if an earlier
S0/S1 theorem remains mathematically valid.

S3 and S4 do not silently close further execution
claims. Proving a typed atom's abstract evaluation
does not authenticate actual HTTP input or the
current principal. Binding the issuer to the
operation does not prove the XTDB guard was used,
the browser loaded the admitted asset or every
physical writer refines its declared transition.
Those additional claims require the specific
source-to-physical and runtime observations of
Part X and the selected operational profile.

For a *safety* theorem transferred from an abstract
model to concrete execution, the relevant
initial-state coverage, concrete-to-abstract
step/trace simulation and property reflection
are separate quantified duties. For liveness,
fairness, divergence, delivery, environmental
availability, retained history and user-action
conditions must also be justified. A single
successful observed history, or model state
equality without history, cannot establish
those universal transfer directions.

*** Import genuine external proof evidence reproducibly

For each S0–S4 claim the evidence package records:

- the original semantic subject, exact theorem statement,
  quantifiers, formula/rule versions and intended result
  scope, not just a convenient theorem nickname;
- the generated formal input's source provenance,
  translation/extraction mechanism, original required
  proposition and checked normalization/encoding relation;
- proof assistant or checker product, exact version and
  supported language, its trusted kernel/axioms,
  imported libraries, options and declared resource
  budgets;
- the genuine proof term/checkable artifact, observed
  machine-check result, checking command, environment,
  reproducible inputs and dependency/certificate
  fingerprints with their provenance;
- issuer and evidence-import controls, current source,
  operation, application, toolchain and runtime
  applicability, versioned accepted premises and
  named invalidation/rechecking triggers;
- any still-open or explicitly trusted S2–S4 relation,
  assumptions relevant to installed execution,
  negative controls and the final conditionality
  of the exported claim.

A checker may be Lean or another suitable mechanized
system; no one vendor, tactic script or notation is
part of Gesso's universal application programming
contract. Application authors specify their
business rules and Choreo/Gesso construction.
Generation of appropriate formal subjects,
verification tasks and import metadata belongs
to the framework and its selected formal backend.

A process exit code, text claiming "proved",
borrowed screenshot, copied theorem hash or
unverified serialized report is not sufficient
evidence of checked theorem origin. A local
independent checker must validate imported
proof material under its declared trust contract,
or the output remains explicitly conditional
on an authenticated external checking service.
Signatures authenticate specified bytes and
issuer origin only under their key assumptions;
they do not prove the proposition being signed.

The release gate rechecks the source/evidence
binding and actual current applicability rather
than treating formal verification as permanent
authority. Semantic rule changes may invalidate
S0/S1; source/proposition or atom-interpretation
changes can invalidate S3; checker/parser changes
can invalidate S2; new issuer, dependency,
operation or installed artifact generations can
invalidate S4. A source-compatible abstract proof
may survive a physical adapter change while
the actual physical transfer becomes stale.
An outdated theorem remains historical until
the named relation to the new subject is
re-established.

*** Preserve exact finite and conditional evidence classes

An exhaustive finite exploration proves its named
property only for the completely enumerated
initial-state and transition graph of the selected
finite model under its stated domain and history
semantics. A bounded-depth search, sampled
valuation corpus, incomplete enumeration, timeout,
unavailable backend, truncated branch, or
unsupported rule cannot be reported as a
completed proof. Conversely, a genuine finite
counterexample may be useful before enumeration
finishes; its exact model and trace remain
explicit.

An unbounded theorem needs the corresponding
quantified derivation or a separately justified
abstraction/refinement from the concrete
state/input range. A restricted Boolean atom
theorem is not a general arithmetic theorem.
A source-relative conditional induction is
not a universal physical writer theorem.
A tested current backend is not a proved
unbounded XTDB implementation. The report
preserves those evidence classes and their
trusted assumptions without compressing
them to one global "formally verified" bit.

The design must also guard against a common-mode
semantic mistake: if an automatically generated
formal model and the production checker both
implement the same incorrect interpretation,
matching test results may conceal the bug.
Independent specification of the closed inference
rules, a distinct formal semantics, mutation
oracles and typed atom counterexamples are
required. Their coverage is fixed before
selecting only examples that pass.

*** Multi-node production completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: milestone-multi-node-production
:END:

This milestone realizes the same application meaning across actual independent
application processes under the qualified topology from
[[#req-multi-node-realization]]. Host identity does not confer semantic authority.

Completion requires all of the following for the stated production envelope:

| Area | Required evidence |
|------+-------------------|
| Physical topology | Actual independent hosts/processes, routing, qualified shared authority, and declared failure model |
| Command semantics | Stable binding, cross-host replay/disclosure, concurrent delivery, retention, and honest uncertainty |
| Publication recovery | Atomic durable intent where promised, dispatcher ownership, retry/acknowledgment semantics, duplicates, and crash recovery |
| Subscriber continuity | Initial and reconnect fence/replay/gap handling, including silent missed notices and retention expiry |
| Progression-safe reads | Another host obtains sufficient real provider evidence or reports insufficiency without false discharge |
| Selective adoption | HumanHelp remote changes remain advisory until permitted Refresh across reconnect and host replacement |
| Current authority | Replacement hosts enforce current authentication, Membership, scope, protected read, and command-result disclosure |
| Release overlap | Actual reachable client/server combinations, assets, workers, state formats, retirement, and recovery satisfy the selected bound |
| Resource envelope | Measured capacity, queue/debt/retention bounds, backpressure, and failure behavior under the declared load |
| Root evidence | Actual topology and provider assumptions enter the same obligation and release report |

The required fault schedule includes loss before submission, after possible
submission, after commit before publication, after publication before completion
recording, and during response delivery. The outcomes are not all equivalent.
A surviving authoritative commit remains incorporated even while a browser is
unknown. A later current-state read does not automatically attribute its command.

Advisory receipt, distribution acknowledgment, acquisition, physical installation,
and adoption are separately observed. At-least-once dispatch to a named boundary
does not establish delivery to every browser. Initial interest registration and
reconnect gaps require their own evidence even when no advisory was received.

Load testing must exercise relevant resource limits and recovery behavior, not
only maximum successful request rate. Independent concurrent requests must not
share a one-shot writer capability or leak invocation context. Where deferred
work is supported, its explicit ownership and assembly context are exercised.

Progress tests state when their availability, fairness, retention, authorization,
and user-action premises become satisfied. Under explicit Refresh, an inactive
user is not required to adopt remote state. After authorization is revoked, the
expected result may be nondisclosure rather than successful reconciliation.

A two-host demonstration or early prototype can expose valuable semantic defects
without satisfying this production milestone. The completion claim requires the
actual failure, resource, and compatibility envelope, not merely a load balancer
and a shared database.

*** The four scopes do not collapse into one status

Operational completion can precede external proof and production multi-node work.
A compositional pilot can proceed while other application capabilities remain
unfinished. A kernel theorem does not establish deployment resilience, and a
resilient deployment does not establish kernel soundness.

Scheduling can overlap where dependencies permit. Each completion record still
needs its own scope, requirement set, and evidence. The operational definition
cannot be weakened because proof work is advanced; the formal definition cannot
be weakened because the UI works.

*** Completion records and continued applicability

A completion record names the milestone, canonical requirements, supported
scope, admitted assembly and actual artifacts, accepted premises, evidence for
every required gate, environment, open product decisions, and exclusions. Its
complete status is computed from those records, not independently authored.

Historical completion remains evidence about the release and environment that
were checked. Later changes trigger dependency-based reconsideration of the
applicable claims. Unaffected results may be reused where their applicability is
established. Completion is neither a permanent property of a repository nor a
requirement to repeat every unrelated test after every edit.

Known premise violations invalidate dependent applicability even when the abstract
theorem remains mathematically valid. The record retains both facts. Operational
policy determines how affected work is suspended, recovered, or explicitly
restricted; an old completion badge cannot override that policy.

Documentation changes define or clarify requirements. They do not establish that
an implementation meets them.

options, explicit Refresh, and enforced assembly bindings, but whose response
interaction still lacks a selected product contract. It may also have useful
proof and two-host experiments. Its report must preserve the different answers:

| Completion scope | Hypothetical evidence | Reported conclusion |
|------------------+-----------------------+---------------------|
| Operational application | Several actual browser capabilities pass; required response contract and its scenario are missing | Partial and blocked for the full Part VIII milestone |
| Compositional certification | Typed Claim guard and source-bound conditional theorem pass; required broader judgment or production joins remain missing | Partial for the stated certification scope |
| External formal verification | One finite derivation audit passes; universal kernel/production correspondence is absent | Finite result reported; external baseline incomplete |
| Multi-node production | Two processes can read shared state; publication-loss and reconnect-gap cases are untested | Prototype; production milestone incomplete |

This example asserts no present implementation status. Each hypothetical passing

If a later candidate selects the response contract and satisfies every remaining

retain their own incomplete results. It may release under the permitted
operational profile. It cannot silently satisfy a requested stronger profile
by renaming its claim after that profile failed.

A downstream consumer can inspect the actual supported scope, what was checked,
which premises remain, and what blocks the stronger claim. The record should
make that answer simpler than reverse-engineering a long list of test names.

** Acceptance for changes to the release mechanism

The release system itself needs mutation and positive-control evidence. At
minimum, exercise:

- a semantic change invalidating its complete required dependency cone while an
  independent subject stays reusable;
- an implementation-only change retaining a valid abstract theorem while
  invalidating affected physical evidence;
- a forged or corrupted cached report failing at the issuer/re-admission boundary;
- a missing required capability remaining missing despite all exposed operations
  being closed;
- failed, skipped, unsupported, unavailable, stale, and incomplete required checks
  each blocking the claimed decision;
- a disabled stronger policy failing a release intent that requires that policy;
- a valid conditional claim retaining its accepted premises without inventing
  mandatory unmet obligations or erasing the original ledger;
- a tested artifact differing from the served bytes failing correspondence;
- a checked local handler not adopted by the actual server failing or narrowing
  the installed-host claim;
- concurrent activation, partial installation failure, and cleanup preserving
  coherent current bindings;
- a known invalid old premise preventing fallback to an otherwise previously
  admitted assembly;
- compatible rolling directions succeeding and unsupported directions retiring
  or rejecting before incompatible effects;
- unresolved command identity surviving execution retirement without a fresh-ID
  resubmission or false rejection;
- protected recovery honoring current disclosure and HumanHelp's adoption policy.

The test report names the first intended enforcing boundary, actual subject and
artifact, observation origin, fault schedule, and remaining premises. Rejection
of every candidate is not success: ordinary supported positive releases and
reusable unchanged evidence must continue to work.

** Working rule for every semantic or release change

For each changed component, identify the proposition it establishes, the premises
it needs, the evidence mechanism, the actual enforcing boundary, and the dependent
claims that must be reconsidered. If the next layer cannot mechanically check
the required interface and evidence relationship, that portion of the chain
remains an explicit premise or an unmet obligation according to the profile.

The intended result is a framework that knows which claims are proved, checked,
qualified, runtime-enforced, trusted, asserted, or tested; which concrete
realizations those claims concern; and when they stop applying. Conditional
liveness retains its environmental premises alongside its evidence class.

A release is admitted because its declared requirements and applicable evidence
compose through the actual construction and installation path. Completion is
claimed because the required behavior and assurance scope have been established.
Neither conclusion follows from an application merely appearing to start.

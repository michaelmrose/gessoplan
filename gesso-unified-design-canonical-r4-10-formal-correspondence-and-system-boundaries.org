* Part X — Formal correspondence and system boundaries
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-10-formal-correspondence-and-system-boundaries
:END:

This part specifies the scope of stronger proof claims and their connection to
production, together with the trusted boundaries and multi-node contracts that
those claims must acknowledge. It consolidates the repeated formalization
material into one correspondence contract without treating test coverage as a
substitute for a theorem.

** Formal strengthening and system boundaries

This part connects the compositional architecture of Part IX to system-wide
assurance claims, external formalization, trusted implementation boundaries,
and physical multi-node realization. It introduces no second semantic layer.
Release/completion gates and the ordered realization roadmap follow in Parts XI
and XII. Historical plan ordering does not override the canonical contracts.

*** Assurance is layered rather than binary

Gesso does not have one global state named =proved=.

Different claims may legitimately have different evidence.

The accepted assurance classes are:

- *machine-proved / externally machine-checked* -- a theorem follows from an
  explicit formal semantics under named assumptions;
- *kernel-checked* -- a production proof term or derivation is accepted by the
  small trusted proof kernel;
- *exact-finite checked* -- the stated property was exhaustively checked over a
  finite semantic state space, with the scope of that claim stated precisely;
- *assembly-verified* -- a closed application graph/artifact correspondence
  check succeeded;
- *runtime-enforced* -- a concrete premise is validated or supplied at runtime;
- *trusted* -- correctness depends on an external implementation or boundary
  outside the current proof;
- *asserted* -- the application explicitly supplies a domain proposition Gesso
  cannot derive;
- *tested* -- behavioral evidence exists, but the property is not promoted to a
  stronger assurance class.

The root assurance report may contain all of these at once, with the meanings
fixed by [[#req-assurance-classification][Part I]]. Conditional liveness qualifies
a progress claim and its environmental premises; it is not an additional
evidence mechanism. A conditional-liveness claim can be theorem-backed, model-
checked, or supported by tests, and the report must say which.

The design must not collapse them into one marketing label.

*** Stronger proof never changes semantic ownership

Formalization may strengthen confidence in:

- type safety;
- knowledge/provenance use;
- projection preservation;
- certificate composition;
- browser-adapter transition invariants;
- progression algebra;
- stale-dependency rejection;
- whole-application correspondence.

It does not move HumanHelp business truth into Gesso.

For example, Gesso may prove that a distributed program invokes the declared
=:request/claim= operation only under the modeled knowledge/authority conditions.

It does not prove that HumanHelp product policy chose the correct Claim rule
unless that rule itself is separately specified and verified against a stronger
external requirement.

*** The final formal boundary follows the production semantics

The object of formal reasoning must remain the semantic object production uses.

The architecture rejects:

#+begin_example
formal model A
    |
proof of A
    |
handwritten production semantics B
#+end_example

when the intended correctness claim concerns B.

The desired direction is:

#+begin_example
explicit production executable semantics
        |
        +--> executable use
        |
        +--> model checking
        |
        +--> proof-term generation
        |
        +--> external formal correspondence
#+end_example

The same rule applies to projected plans, browser transition semantics, and
whole-application assembly.

The executable semantic core realizes production transitions. The proof kernel
checks derivations, while dedicated semantic checkers establish their particular
judgments through declared mechanisms. These roles may share canonical semantic
definitions without being one component or one assurance class. An external
proof-assistant kernel is another explicitly named checking boundary, not an
alternate application runtime. Correspondence connects the checked subject to
the production object; terminology alone does not establish that connection.

** System-wide assurance target matrix
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-system-assurance-targets
:END:

The complete architecture aims to establish the following guarantees.

Each guarantee must be accompanied by an honest assurance classification in the
implementation/root report.

The section states the desired property.

It does not imply that every property has already reached its strongest target
classification.

*** Choreography language and projection

The supported global choreography language has explicit transition semantics.

The system should establish that:

- a global choreography has a closed, inspectable semantic representation;
- projected machines arise from an accepted global choreography;
- the supported projection algorithm preserves the declared global/local trace
  or refinement relation;
- projected behavior does not invent undeclared communication;
- projection preserves every knowledge-producing mechanism required by local
  behavior;
- behavior-sensitive branch divergence is allowed only after the local role has
  justified knowledge of the required discriminator or an equivalent fact;
- unsupported semantic constructs are rejected or explicitly classified outside
  the proved subset rather than silently inheriting stronger guarantees.

Target evidence:

- explicit semantics;
- proof-kernel checking or a dedicated exact-model checker for the stated
  construction judgments;
- external theorem support for stable projection rules where practical.

*** Knowledge, provenance, and agreement

The system should establish that:

- senders know the values they send;
- consumers know the facts they consume;
- communication establishes knowledge only for the declared payload;
- authoritative observation establishes only the facts justified by the
  observed authoritative projection and its provenance/basis;
- deterministic derivation establishes only declared outputs from known inputs;
- asserted knowledge remains visibly asserted/trusted;
- weaker provenance cannot silently become stronger provenance;
- browser assertion alone cannot become authoritative provenance;
- agreement claims are justified through declared communication, compatible
  authoritative observation, checked derivation, or explicit assertion;
- agreement over evolving facts is scoped to a meaningful frontier rather than
  assumed from co-presence or shape equality;
- privacy/authorization may intentionally produce different valid knowledge
  projections for different actors.

Target evidence:

- proof-kernel-checkable knowledge/provenance rules for the closed semantic subset;
- runtime enforcement for concrete authoritative reads and authentication;
- application assertions where domain truth is external to Gesso.

*** Types, schemas, and rule use

The system should establish that:

- message payloads satisfy declared Malli/value schemas;
- machine contexts satisfy state schemas at checked boundaries;
- supported typed rule operators are used only on values in their semantic
  domains;
- unknown fields/variants are rejected;
- optional values are not dereferenced without a valid refinement;
- required closed matches are exhaustive;
- relationship predicates use valid endpoint/cardinality contracts;
- operation output fields cannot be consumed before they are produced;
- a rule that is intrinsically well typed is still rejected at a distributed
  program point when required knowledge, provenance, authority, refinement, or
  progression is absent.

Target evidence:

- runtime schemas;
- typed rule elaboration;
- kernel-checked semantic judgments;
- mutation tests for unsupported/malformed expressions.

*** Authoritative model transitions

The system should establish that:

- public model operations own authoritative business semantics;
- authoritative transitions advertised as atomic have one atomic commit boundary;
- failures established to precede commit cannot expose half of the promised
  atomic authoritative transition;
- failures after commit cannot reclassify or implicitly revert that transition;
- lost outcomes after transaction submission preserve participant-relative
  uncertainty rather than inventing rejection;
- known post-commit publication/settlement failure creates delivery/recovery
  obligations, not evidence that the mutation failed;
- FX inputs and outputs are explicit and do not rely on hidden context mutation;
- model operation contracts distinguish required capability, authority, typed
  input/outcome, atomicity, and trusted assumptions;
- Choreo depends on public model contracts rather than private model FX/graph
  internals.

Target evidence:

- runtime-enforced transaction guards;
- model/property tests;
- certified operation contracts;
- implementation conformance trusted initially where arbitrary Clojure bodies
  are not formally verified.

*** Progression and authoritative observation

The system should establish that:

- authoritative progression is distinct from domain revision, valid time,
  command identity, and browser execution/resource-instance identities;
- progression requirements are canonical;
- composition of progression requirements is conservative;
- commit-derived invalidation and reread preserve sufficient scoped progression
  requirements when delivered;
- loss between commit and publication follows the declared recovery contract,
  with the assumptions needed for eventual acquisition made explicit;
- advisory invalidation never fabricates authoritative content or claims that
  the recipient has already adopted the advertised progression;
- a raw browser-supplied basis is not proof of prior observation; any admitted
  observation receipt establishes only its explicitly validated issuance and
  scope contract, not delivery, installation, or human awareness;
- stale basis is exposed to model policy rather than interpreted by Gesso as an
  automatic conflict;
- convergence may skip intermediate advisory events when a newer authoritative
  observation satisfies the application's semantics.

Target evidence:

- explicit progression algebra;
- XTDB integration tests;
- exact finite/proof checks for algebraic properties where practical;
- trusted XTDB consistency contract.

*** Optimism

The system should establish that:

- optimistic state is distinguishable from authoritative state;
- every optimistic projection carries semantic command and authoritative-basis
  provenance;
- provisional state cannot increase server authority;
- every provisional trajectory has a declared resolution/recovery path;
- direct settlement loss permits later authoritative supersession through the
  declared recovery path under its availability and scheduling assumptions;
- current-state supersession does not by itself settle command attribution;
- newer authoritative installation revokes stale rollback rights;
- multiple browsers may hold conflicting provisional projections without
  creating multiple authoritative histories;
- a semantic optimistic operation never silently downgrades to an anonymous
  ordinary request because basis/command/plan data is absent.

Target evidence:

- portable transition semantics;
- browser adapter invariant checking;
- whole-application rendered-surface enforcement;
- real Chromium adversarial scheduling tests.

*** Browser transition safety

The production browser semantic adapter should itself be the checked transition
system.

The system should establish that:

- every semantically active async effect has one declared owner or explicit
  shared-ownership class;
- execution-private callbacks are admitted only for the current
  execution identity and relevant resource-instance ownership state;
- stale or duplicate events cannot resume an illegal machine state;
- stale callbacks cannot acquire validity merely because a logical target still
  exists;
- authoritative install cannot be undone by an obsolete provisional callback;
- semantic retirement revokes callback authority before best-effort physical
  cleanup;
- physical cancellation is an optimization rather than the sole safety gate;
- fragment coordination satisfies its stated bound on logical active/queued
  slots; retained metadata and retired physical requests have separately stated
  bounds or abstractions;
- shared fragment/subscription events use their declared owner-relative
  admission path without fabricated command execution identity;
- deferred physical mutation revalidates the relevant effect permission before
  changing managed DOM, not only when its callback begins;
- continuity may preserve local UI state but cannot alter domain/choreography
  semantics;
- incompatible stale projected plans retire and use the declared recovery or
  authoritative reconstruction path, preserving the product's acquisition and
  adoption policy rather than treating reconnect as automatic permission.

Target evidence:

- exact transition-system/model checking;
- property tests over adversarial event orders;
- JVM/Node/Chromium conformance;
- real browser integration for FFI/HTMX assumptions.

*** Portable execution and artifact identity

The system should establish that:

- the same portable semantic transition core runs under JVM, Node, and Chromium
  for the supported subset;
- production and proof-enabled tests execute the same canonical
  =ExecutablePlan=;
- proof/diagnostic sidecars are runtime-nonsemantic;
- exact sidecar lookup requires exact executable-artifact identity;
- compatibility identity remains distinct from exact artifact identity;
- generated browser artifacts correspond exactly to the current required browser
  manifest before the application is served.

Target evidence:

- deterministic plan/artifact identities;
- conformance tests;
- build/startup closure checks;
- structural noninterference by runtime API shape, with optional diagnostics
  isolated from semantic control flow and failures;
- equivalence for the same initial state and admitted event stream, without
  claiming identical wall-clock scheduling or external resource behavior.

*** Whole-application closure

The system should establish that every exposed Choreo-managed affordance has a
coherent path through:

#+begin_example
view affordance
    -> semantic operation
    -> verified trajectory
    -> projected browser plan
    -> exact generated artifact
    -> declared transport
    -> trusted route
    -> trusted server operation
    -> authoritative model operation
    -> settlement/progression
    -> authoritative acquisition
    -> renderable authoritative projection
#+end_example

The system should reject:

- unknown semantic operations;
- missing browser plans;
- stale generated artifacts;
- missing/incompatible transport;
- missing trusted route/server operation;
- missing required server capability;
- missing acquisition path;
- anonymous ordinary HTMX use of an assembled semantic route;
- implicit semantic downgrade;
- duplicate/incompatible identities.

Target evidence:

- =ApplicationAssembly= closure checking;
- runtime rendered-surface enforcement;
- lifecycle-enforced preflight before serving;
- real-browser acceptance through the same admitted assembly generation.

Closure quantifies over declared affordances. Independent required-capability
acceptance must also fail when a promised action is omitted. Production
entrypoint enforcement must demonstrate that the checked assembly and artifact
are those actually used. These three claims remain separate under
[[#req-application-assurance-claims]].

*** Browser/server authority boundary

Under named authentication/model/XTDB assumptions, arbitrary browser behavior
must not grant additional authoritative privilege.

The browser may propose:

- operation;
- arguments;
- command ID;
- observed basis;
- correlation metadata.

The browser may not establish:

- authenticated principal;
- server role entitlement;
- authoritative provenance;
- current model authorization;
- protected read/subscription permission;
- trusted server machine state.

Target evidence:

- trusted-side operation registry;
- authenticated principal binding;
- runtime authorization;
- adversarial browser tests;
- semantic authority-flow theorem for the modeled protocol boundary.

*** Liveness and convergence

The architecture should support explicit conditional claims such as:

#+begin_quote
If relevant authority eventually becomes reachable, the actor continues to
execute, the declared acquisition/adoption policy permits the required work,
any required user action occurs, the scheduler fairly selects enabled recovery,
required messages/reads eventually succeed, and the authoritative world is
quiescent long enough, then the specifically named presentation or agreement
obligation resolves. Command attribution additionally requires command-specific
outcome evidence when current-state observation is insufficient.
#+end_quote

The system must not infer unconditional liveness merely because:

- a terminal path exists;
- a timeout transition exists;
- a refresh route exists;
- a message could theoretically arrive.

Every liveness/convergence claim names its environment assumptions and the exact
obligation that eventually resolves. A reachable terminal state proves the
possibility of progress, not eventual progress on every fair execution. Any
argument over all fair executions must justify the fairness and progress steps
it uses. Safety must continue to hold when liveness premises fail; remaining
unresolved is preferable to inventing an outcome or permission.

Target evidence:

- graph/state-machine analysis;
- conditional theorem statements;
- integration/fault testing for the external assumptions.

** Framework theorem families

The following are the principal theorem families over the stable semantic
definitions. Their subjects and evidence mechanisms must be stated separately;
the executable semantic core is not itself the proof kernel.

*** Certified composition soundness

If certificate B is accepted using certificate A, every premise B attributes to
A must follow from A's exported guarantees under checked scope/substitution and
compatibility rules. Additional premises admitted at B remain explicit
assumptions of the resulting conclusion, not guarantees attributed to A.
Required obligation coverage and subject binding follow Part IX. A conditional
composition theorem does not establish its external premises or the conformity
of arbitrary implementation bodies.

*** Business-rule type safety

A certified rule cannot apply a supported operator to values outside the
operator's declared semantic domain.

*** Rule-use knowledge safety

If a certified projected role branches/acts on a rule result, all facts required
to evaluate that rule are justified by the role's certified knowledge at that
point, modulo named assumptions.

*** Provenance safety

A stronger provenance class is never derived solely from a weaker class unless a
declared inference rule permits it.

In particular, browser assertion alone cannot become authoritative provenance.

*** Authority derivation and non-escalation safety

No projected/browser-supplied semantic artifact can increase the authority of the
authenticated principal without a trusted server/model rule that establishes the
capability.

*** Projection preservation

For the supported Choreo subset, accepted local projections preserve the stated
global trace/refinement property.

*** Executable-plan provenance

Every certified production plan corresponds to one accepted projection under
the recorded semantic dependencies and plan-format version.

*** Stale certificate safety

A certificate whose required semantic dependency identity no longer matches
cannot be used to produce a certified downstream artifact.

*** Progression preservation

The existing progression algebra should eventually participate in the same proof
story:

- requirements are canonical;
- composition is conservative;
- success/failure rules preserve unsatisfied requirements appropriately;
- advisory invalidation does not fabricate authoritative progression.

*** Browser semantic adapter safety

Existing adapter state-machine properties should eventually be connectable to
the root proof report while the DOM/HTMX/browser shell remains an explicit
trusted/tested realization boundary.

** Formal proof strategy and production correspondence
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-formal-production-correspondence
:END:

External formalization follows the stable production semantics. The intended
progression is explicit semantic data, compositional Clojure certificates, a
small proof kernel, proof-producing elaboration, stable proof-kernel semantics,
external soundness theorems, and checked production/formal correspondence.

Formalization aims to reduce trust in Gesso's own verifier. It does not replace
Clojure, Malli, XTDB, HTMX, or Chromium, and does not move HumanHelp business
policy into the framework. This is a target architecture, not a statement that
its theorems or correspondence mechanisms already exist.


**** Worked correspondence slice: HumanHelp Claim has one production meaning

A Claim slice makes the correspondence direction concrete.  The formal and
production objects may differ in representation, but the transferred theorem
must still concern the production semantics actually admitted.

#+begin_src clojure
(def claim-subject
  {:operation :request/claim
   :state-type :humanhelp/Request
   :input-type :humanhelp/ClaimInput
   :preconditions
   [[:= [:field :request/status] :request.status/open]
    [:known? :actor :membership/current]
    [:authorized? :actor :request/claim]]
   :transition
   {:request/status :request.status/claimed
    :request/helper-id [:input :actor/id]}
   :outcomes #{:accepted :rejected :unknown}})

(def claim-formal-subject
  (gesso.formal/translate-operation claim-subject))

(def claim-production-plan
  (gesso.compile/executable-operation claim-subject))

(def claim-correspondence
  (gesso.formal/check-correspondence
   {:formal-subject claim-formal-subject
    :production-subject claim-production-plan
    :claim
    [:forall-supported-inputs
     [:production-accepts? :request/claim]
     [:formal-judgment-holds? :claim-safe]]
    :representation :gesso.correspondence/operation-v1
    :direction :production-acceptance-implies-formal-validity
    :dependencies
    {:rule-set :semantic/claim-rules-C21
     :operation :semantic/request-claim-op-O31
     :kernel :gesso-proof-kernel/K7}}))

(def admitted-claim
  (gesso.application/admit-operation
   {:plan claim-production-plan
    :correspondence claim-correspondence}))

;; Wrong shape: prove F while production independently executes G.
(def formal-F {:when :open :then :claimed})
(defn production-G [request actor]
  (if (:request/priority-lock? request)
    request
    (assoc request
           :request/status :request.status/claimed
           :request/helper-id (:actor/id actor))))
#+end_src

The second shape leaves an unproved semantic gap even if =F= and =G= share a
name, examples, or copied digest metadata.  The desired architecture checks the
representation/refinement path to the executable production object.  Where an
arbitrary Clojure implementation remains behind the public operation contract,
its conformance remains an explicit trusted or tested premise until stronger
evidence exists.

*** External proof assistant role

Lean is the leading candidate, but the architectural role matters more than the
particular proof assistant. Initial formal objects should remain smaller than
the full implementation:

- proposition syntax and semantic type judgments;
- knowledge/provenance entailment;
- authority/capability entailment;
- the declared progression requirement algebra;
- certificate dependency composition;
- selected transition invariants and projection properties.

Application developers continue to author Clojure/Clojure data and Gesso's
closed semantic languages. Ordinary development does not require Lean, tactic
knowledge, manual proof trees, or hand translation of HumanHelp rules into a
second language. The framework derives the relevant formal objects where their
meaning is mechanically available.

*** Exact claim and correspondence direction

A theorem about a formal checker is insufficient if production accepts different
derivations or executes a different plan. Every transferred claim must identify:

- the formal subject and proposition, including assumptions and supported subset;
- the concrete production object and its immutable identity/version;
- the representation relation between formal and production subjects;
- the checking, translation, or refinement relation used for transfer;
- the direction and scope of that relation;
- the remaining implementation and toolchain trust premises.

For sound admission, a typical required direction is: whenever the production
boundary accepts a subject for guarantee G, the corresponding formal judgment
holds under the recorded premises. Agreement on rejected examples alone does
not establish this direction. Completeness, meaning acceptance of every valid
supported subject, is a different property. A sound checker may be conservative;
usability and supported-feature acceptance must still prevent “reject all” from
being treated as a successful implementation.

For execution safety, a trace/refinement argument must account for every
relevant concrete behavior and its observable meaning. Mere acceptance-set
agreement does not prove that the runtime executes the certified plan correctly.
For liveness, correspondence must additionally address divergence, scheduling,
and fairness as needed; a safety simulation that allows arbitrary stuttering
cannot alone establish progress.

*** Correspondence mechanisms and their evidence

Select the smallest mechanism that demonstrably reduces the trusted gap. The
following mechanisms have different assurance implications.

| <24> | <38> | <38> |
| Mechanism | What it can establish | Remaining obligation |
|-----------+-----------------------+----------------------|
| Externally checked proof terms | The particular admitted proposition follows from accepted premises | Bind proof, subject, dependencies, and actual production artifact; enforce the check |
| Generated or extracted checker | Implementation originates from the formal definition | Justify translation/extraction and execution toolchain, or identify them as trusted |
| Mechanically proved comparison/refinement | A stated relation holds for all objects in the proved domain | Identify domain, direction, representation relation, and assumptions |
| Exhaustive comparison over a finite domain | Acceptance/results agree throughout that specified finite domain | Do not extend to unbounded inputs without a sound coverage argument |
| Generated conformance vectors | Tested implementations agree on those vectors | Tests alone do not prove correspondence outside the exercised cases |

Using every currently generated vector, or covering every inference-rule tag,
does not establish coverage of all rule parameters, combinations, recursive
proof trees, malformed encodings, or production paths. Such results remain
conformance tests unless a separate argument establishes exhaustive coverage
for the claimed domain. More than one mechanism may be needed.

A source review saying “the Clojure rule looks like the Lean theorem” is review
evidence, not formal correspondence. Similarly, using a formally specified
algorithm does not prove a handwritten implementation conforms to it.

*** Bind formal checking to the admitted production artifact

The correspondence path must bind the normalized subject, complete required
obligations, derivation, dependencies, proof-kernel semantics, and result to the object
actually admitted. The boundaries identified in [[#req-proof-kernel-boundary]]
remain relevant: normalization, projection, executable lowering, and runtime
realization each need appropriate checked correspondence or named trust.

An independently valid proof about subject A must not authorize construction of
subject B. Copying A's identity onto B is insufficient. Build/startup must bind
the admitted plan and generated artifact to the admitted assembly generation actually served,
following Part VII. Immutable references or revalidation must prevent a later
mutable lookup from substituting a different object after checking.

If an external check is a required construction gate, unavailable checking,
timeout, unsupported features, or invalid evidence prevent that admission. The
system may retain an already admitted valid assembly generation under the declared
lifecycle contract; it must not silently waive the requested certification
profile. Proof sidecars remain unnecessary for runtime semantic decisions after
admission and never become authorization capabilities.

*** Formal work follows stable semantics

Make the semantics explicit, use them in production, exercise adversarial cases,
stabilize the relevant checking rules, and then formalize them. The aim is to
reduce a specific trusted surface rather than repeatedly translating unstable
representations or encoding the whole implementation wholesale.

The selected formal scope may grow incrementally. Each report names the actual
subset proved and the properties still checked, tested, or trusted. A theorem
for one primitive cannot silently cover every expression using other primitives.

*** Finite checking and abstraction

Exact finite exploration establishes a property over its specified finite model.
The model must name its initial states, transitions, data domains, property, and
exploration completeness. A timeout or partially explored graph is incomplete
evidence, not a successful exact check or a counterexample.

Checking resource-instance counter values 0 through 3 establishes only that
bounded result unless a sound abstraction argument covers all relevant concrete
resource instances, counter scopes, and reuse/retirement behavior. Opaque instance
identities may require a different abstraction; no universal counter is assumed.
The same
applies to command identities, progression tokens, dependency depth, queue
metadata, and retired physical effects. Bounded queue slots do not imply a
finite overall state space.

An abstraction supporting a concrete safety claim must preserve the relevant
initial states and simulate all concrete transitions needed for that claim,
with the abstract invariant implying the concrete safety property. Merely
mapping each state to some abstract state is insufficient. A liveness transfer
needs the additional progress/fairness/divergence conditions its theorem uses.
An abstract counterexample may require concretization before being reported as
a realizable production failure.


**** Worked abstraction slice: resource ownership without a fake finite universe

Browser resource-instance identities may be unbounded even when the safety
property depends on only a small relation among them.  Checking four chosen
UUIDs would therefore prove only an artificial four-value model.

#+begin_src clojure
(def concrete-state
  {:target :request-list
   :current-resource-instance #uuid "62eae6bf-d8e5-4faa-9c65-5387408ec111"
   :retired-resource-instances
   #{#uuid "e3ff7f3d-a62e-4995-9ef9-314298250812"}
   :pending
   {#uuid "62eae6bf-d8e5-4faa-9c65-5387408ec111"
    {:effect :fragment-fetch
     :phase :awaiting-response}}})

(defn callback-admitted? [state callback]
  (and (= (:resource-instance callback)
          (:current-resource-instance state))
       (contains? (:pending state)
                  (:resource-instance callback))))

(defn abstract-instance [state instance]
  (cond
    (= instance (:current-resource-instance state)) :instance/current
    (contains? (:retired-resource-instances state) instance) :instance/retired
    :else :instance/other))

(defn abstract-callback [state callback]
  {:instance-class
   (abstract-instance state (:resource-instance callback))
   :pending?
   (contains? (:pending state) (:resource-instance callback))
   :event (:event callback)})

(defn abstract-admitted? [{:keys [instance-class pending?]}]
  (and (= instance-class :instance/current) pending?))

(def resource-ownership-obligation
  {:initial :every-concrete-initial-state-has-related-abstract-state
   :step :every-relevant-concrete-transition-is-simulated
   :property
   [:implies
    [:abstract-callback-admitted?]
    [:concrete-callback-owned-by-current-resource?]]
   :scope
   #{:start-resource :retire-resource :receive-callback
     :authoritative-install}})
#+end_src

If the abstraction relation and simulation obligation are machine-checked,
exhaustive exploration of the finite abstract state space can support a claim
about every concrete identity covered by that theorem.  Without that bridge,
the same exploration is merely bounded evidence.  Liveness still needs its own
scheduling/fairness argument rather than inheriting this safety result.

*** Evidence package and maintenance

A formal claim is reviewable only when its evidence identifies:

- theorem statement and formal semantic definitions;
- proof/checker versions, relevant options, and any admitted axioms;
- normalized subject, dependency closure, and artifact identities;
- representation/correspondence definitions and their evidence class;
- exact finite bounds or abstraction soundness evidence where used;
- the production boundary enforcing admission;
- open assumptions and excluded behavior;
- reproducible checking instructions and the observed result.

A changed semantic rule, primitive, checker, translation, or production binding
must trigger the rechecking appropriate to its dependency edges. Unchanged
interface-level proofs may remain reusable while implementation correspondence
needs renewed evidence. The maintenance contract must not confuse a theorem's
continuing mathematical validity with applicability to a changed release.

** Trusted computing base, security boundaries, and failure modes
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-trusted-system-boundaries
:END:

The formal architecture is credible only when the trusted computing base is
named and kept small.

*** Assurance ledger

For each release/milestone, the root assurance report should identify:

- proof-kernel namespaces/size;
- elaborator/compiler namespaces;
- runtime-enforced boundary namespaces;
- trusted external libraries/systems;
- application assertions;
- properties machine-proved;
- properties kernel-checked;
- properties exact-finite checked;
- properties assembly-verified;
- properties runtime-enforced;
- properties supported only by tests;
- exact implementation, dependency, and semantic/checker versions in scope;
- correspondence and admission boundaries that bind the evidence to production;
- conditional-liveness premises and unresolved obligations.

Namespace size is useful audit information, not a proof of soundness. The ledger
must distinguish the logical trusted base from the larger runtime, security,
and availability dependencies. Each claim identifies the relevant subset;
listing every dependency without saying which claim relies on it is insufficient.

A smaller explicitly named TCB is preferable to a much larger system described
vaguely as formal.

*** Typical trusted/external boundaries

The exact list evolves with implementation, but likely boundaries include:

- JVM/Clojure runtime;
- ClojureScript/JavaScript engine;
- browser primitive behavior;
- supported HTMX lifecycle contract;
- Aleph/Ring transport implementation;
- authentication/session implementation;
- XTDB transaction/consistency implementation;
- operating system/network behavior;
- external proof assistant and its proof-checking kernel implementation where used;
- parsing, serialization, normalization, extraction/code generation, and build
  tooling wherever their correctness is not independently checked;
- primitive operator semantics and cryptographic assumptions used for identity
  or authenticated evidence.

These are not ignored.

They are named premises.

Real integration and adversarial testing exists partly to challenge them.

*** Certificate system does not replace runtime authorization

A malicious browser may forge:

- serialized maps;
- DOM;
- JavaScript;
- headers;
- semantic IDs;
- plan-looking objects;
- certificate-looking objects;
- role/principal fields;
- basis/provenance claims.

Therefore:

- server authority comes from trusted server context and current model policy;
- endpoints resolve operations through trusted registries;
- protected reads/subscriptions remain server-authorized;
- cached/persisted certificates re-enter through structural/semantic validation;
- artifact signing may protect integrity but does not prove semantic correctness;
- a structural certificate about a route never substitutes for validating its
  concrete invocation before protected execution.

The security claim is relative to its specified threat model and boundary. A
browser may propose arbitrary bytes; hostile code already holding server-side
transaction authority is a different trust condition. Server authorization must
hold even if checked rendering is bypassed. Authentication, request-origin/CSRF
controls where relevant, input/resource limits, and protected-data disclosure
follow the actual transport contract; a non-escalation theorem about the modeled
protocol does not automatically prove the security of the entire web stack.

*** Hashes are identities, not proofs

A checked digest provides correspondence evidence for specified bytes or
normalized content under the canonicalization and collision-resistance
assumptions. A digest string copied into unverified metadata provides no such
binding by itself.

It does not establish the truth of the proposition associated with those bytes.

The architecture always distinguishes:

#+begin_example
identity evidence
    from
semantic proof evidence
#+end_example


A cache lookup makes the distinction operational.  Matching bytes can establish
artifact identity while old semantic evidence is nevertheless stale under a new
dependency closure.

#+begin_src clojure
(def cached-browser-artifact
  {:artifact/id "sha256:91b..."
   :proof-report/id :proof/request-board-R12
   :dependencies
   {:claim-operation :semantic/request-claim-op-O31
    :membership :semantic/membership-M18}
   :assembly-generation :assembly/H42})

(def current-application
  {:required-artifact/id "sha256:91b..."
   :dependencies
   {:claim-operation :semantic/request-claim-op-O31
    :membership :semantic/membership-M19}
   :assembly-generation :assembly/H43})

(defn artifact-identical? [cached current]
  (= (:artifact/id cached) (:required-artifact/id current)))

(defn evidence-admissible? [cached current]
  (and (artifact-identical? cached current)
       (= (:dependencies cached) (:dependencies current))
       (= (:assembly-generation cached) (:assembly-generation current))))

(artifact-identical? cached-browser-artifact current-application) ;=> true
(evidence-admissible? cached-browser-artifact current-application) ;=> false
#+end_src

The digest still says something useful about exact artifact identity.  It does
not authorize carrying the old Membership-dependent proposition into the new
assembly, and a new digest alone would not prove semantic correctness either.

*** Elaborator/checker shared-bug risk

A rich elaborator and a checker implemented from the same misunderstanding may
share a bug.

Mitigations include:

- deliberately small proof kernel;
- adversarial malformed derivations;
- independent rule tests;
- external formalization;
- production/formal correspondence.

The purpose of the independent proof-kernel/formal step is specifically to reduce the
amount of semantic correctness that depends on trusting the compiler that
generated the evidence.

*** Certificate forgery

Same-shaped map construction must not enter certified APIs.

A public API that accepts:

#+begin_src clojure
{:semantic-id X
 :valid? true}
#+end_src

as equivalent to a checker-issued certified value defeats the architecture.

Certified values use checker-controlled constructors and validated persistence
boundaries.

*** Circular trust

An operation contract cannot prove its own implementation merely by declaring
the postcondition it wants.

Likewise:

- a rule cannot certify itself;
- a certificate cannot accept itself as its own dependency;
- a proof of projection cannot assume the preservation theorem it is intended to
  establish.

Certificate dependencies remain acyclic. Supported recursive semantics use an
explicit checked fixed-point/induction rule within a component, with a soundness
argument for that rule. They do not permit circular certificate justification.
Unsupported cycles reject.

*** Assertion inflation

=:asserted= is a legitimate proof-DAG leaf.

It is not an escape hatch for every difficult obligation.

Root reports should count and surface assertions prominently so an application
whose "proof" is mostly assertion is visibly different from one with derived
guarantees. Counts are diagnostic information, not a confidence score: one
central opaque premise may matter more than many minor assertions. Reports must
show which guarantees depend on each premise. Required proof-kernel obligations cannot
be converted into assertions merely because checking them failed.

*** Semantic-fingerprint under-approximation

If normalization omits a semantically relevant field, a stale certificate may
appear current.

Fingerprint normalization therefore needs adversarial mutation tests.

*** Semantic-fingerprint over-approximation

If normalization includes irrelevant implementation/source details, harmless
edits invalidate too much of the proof DAG and developers are incentivized to
bypass certification.

Semantic interfaces should therefore be minimal.

*** Proof artifacts must remain noninterfering

Proof sidecars, derivation trees, theorem metadata, and diagnostics never become
inputs to production transition decisions.

A proof system that changes runtime meaning when enabled has created a second
semantic channel.

*** "Verified" remains qualified

Public reports should prefer specific classifications:

- machine-proved;
- kernel-checked;
- exact-finite checked;
- assembly-verified;
- runtime-enforced;
- trusted;
- asserted;
- tested.

The unqualified word =verified= should not be allowed to hide materially
different evidence. Conditional-liveness qualifications remain attached to the
relevant claim alongside, rather than in place of, its evidence classification.

*** Arbitrary Clojure is not the formal target

The strongest proof effort concerns:

- the closed semantic rule language;
- semantic interfaces;
- choreography;
- projection;
- adapter transition semantics;
- certificate composition.

It does not attempt to prove arbitrary host-language behavior by pretending all
Clojure is inside the small logic.

Implementation conformance outside the closed semantic subset stays explicitly
trusted/tested until separately constrained or verified.

** Validation ladder

Stronger formal assurance does not remove the need for concrete testing layers.

Retain:

- unit tests;
- property tests;
- exact finite-state exhaustive checks;
- JVM/Node/Chromium portable conformance;
- real HTMX/SSE/EventSource integration;
- adversarial browser tests;
- XTDB integration tests;
- multi-node tests when multi-node Live exists;
- generated-artifact freshness tests.

Their role becomes clearer:

#+begin_example
formal proof
  -> semantic theorem

certificate checking
  -> downstream theorem composition

runtime checks
  -> concrete premises

integration/adversarial tests
  -> scoped evidence about shell correspondence and external assumptions
#+end_example

*** Evidence layers are complementary

A useful failure can live outside the theorem boundary.

For example:

- a proof may establish adapter execution/resource-instance isolation;
- a Chromium test may reveal that an HTMX lifecycle event is normalized
  incorrectly;
- a property test may find an elaborator bug before the proof kernel sees its output;
- an XTDB integration test may reveal an incorrect assumption about snapshot
  propagation;
- a HumanHelp multi-client test may reveal a missing whole-application edge.

The correction may occur in:

- semantics;
- implementation;
- trusted-assumption definition;
- application assembly;
- application policy.

The framework should not distort the proof boundary merely to make every bug a
theorem violation. Acceptance records the first enforcing boundary, the actual
admitted assembly generation, and the evidence scope. Expected network/console failures
in fault scenarios are asserted precisely; unrelated failures still fail the
scenario. A test-only wrapper rejecting before the real boundary is reached is
not evidence that production enforces that boundary.

** Multi-node realization and authoritative rendezvous
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-multi-node-realization
:END:

Multi-node deployment realizes the semantic distinctions already established.

It does not introduce a second authority model.

*** Roles, actors, hosts, and authorities remain distinct

When several Aleph nodes exist:

- a role remains a semantic choreography role;
- an actor remains a role/principal realization;
- a host remains a physical process/node;
- an authority remains the trusted semantic owner of authoritative facts or
  operations.

A concrete node may temporarily realize an authority projection.

The node itself does not become semantic authority.

For example:

#+begin_example
role:
    :request-authority

host for invocation:
    web-node A

retry host:
    web-node C

semantic authority:
    [:authority :request R]
#+end_example

Host replacement must not change command identity or authoritative meaning.
The new host must recover or receive the actual command identity through its
trusted protocol; physical relocation does not reconstruct missing intent.
Retry safety follows the model's explicit idempotency/deduplication and retention
contract. A node-local cache or the same command string alone cannot establish
cross-node exactly-once effects. Outcome loss after submission can remain
unknown even when another node can read current authoritative state.

*** Durable model state remains the ordinary rendezvous point

For ordinary HumanHelp cross-user interactions:

#+begin_example
Alice browser
    |
Aleph node A
    |
Request authoritative operation
    |
XTDB commit
    |
distributed advisory invalidation
    |
Aleph node C
    |
Bob browser receives advisory
    |
product policy or explicit Refresh authorizes acquisition/adoption
    |
authorized authoritative reread
#+end_example

Alice and Bob do not require a persistent cross-node participant-message process
merely because they interact with the same Request.

The durable Request fact is the rendezvous point.

*** Distributed invalidation is not participant messaging

A distributed invalidation says approximately:

#+begin_quote
Relevant authority may have changed. A future acquisition must satisfy the
advertised scope/progression requirement; the application's policy determines
when acquisition and adoption are permitted.
#+end_quote

A choreography participant message says approximately:

#+begin_quote
This execution received this declared message.
#+end_quote

A future implementation may transport both through related infrastructure.

Their semantics remain different.

*** Direct participant routing is opt-in

A genuinely ephemeral protocol may require receipt of a participant message
itself to carry semantic meaning.

That use case may introduce:

- distributed actor-interest registration;
- message routing;
- correlation;
- duplicate/loss semantics;
- retry/delivery policy;
- authenticated routing, protected payload disclosure, and recipient admission;
- resource-instance retirement and the applicable compatibility contract across
  host replacement or a change of admitted assembly generation.

Delivery acknowledgments, broker persistence, execution admission, and domain
commit are distinct facts. Any stronger delivery guarantee must name its scope
and recovery assumptions rather than inheriting them from the broker's label.

Those mechanisms should be added when a real choreography requires them.

They must not redefine ordinary authoritative convergence.


**** Worked multi-node contrast: durable Request versus ephemeral typing message

Two cross-user features may traverse the same cluster while requiring different
semantics.  First, Alice claims a durable HumanHelp Request through node A:

#+begin_src clojure
(def claim-command
  {:command/id #uuid "76d7d7e1-fd03-47f7-82f7-66915ec283d9"
   :operation :request/claim
   :request/id #uuid "412090ce-0e79-4bc8-9d66-a4690ee52022"
   :actor/id #uuid "069ca45d-33de-4d06-b958-ef1a0da69390"})

(def claim-result
  (request/claim! node-a-system claim-command))

;; The committed fact outlives node A.
{:request/id (:request/id claim-command)
 :request/status :request.status/claimed
 :request/helper-id (:actor/id claim-command)
 :progression {:request/id (:request/id claim-command)
               :basis 1842}}

(def advisory
  {:kind :request/authority-may-have-advanced
   :scope [:request/id (:request/id claim-command)]
   :requires {:basis-at-least 1842}})

(defn refresh-through-node-c [principal advisory]
  (auth/authorize-read! node-c-system principal (:scope advisory))
  (let [observation
        (request/read-authoritative
         node-c-system
         {:principal principal
          :scope (:scope advisory)
          :requirement (:requires advisory)})]
    (progression/assert-satisfies! observation (:requires advisory))
    observation))
#+end_src

If publication is lost, node C satisfies the outstanding requirement by reading
the durable model under current authorization.  It does not reconstruct the
Claim from node A's process memory.  The reread may establish current Request
state without proving whether Alice received her direct settlement response.

Now contrast an ephemeral typing signal whose receipt itself is the semantic
event:

#+begin_src clojure
(def typing-message
  {:execution/id #uuid "240e696a-e527-4fd1-80ac-bb104d91ef02"
   :message/id #uuid "0a675872-2ba3-4575-a76f-4024d2c73938"
   :from :actor/alice
   :to :actor/bob
   :kind :collaboration/typing
   :document/id #uuid "389bdfac-48c9-4563-8b83-83bfae42d32e"
   :expires-at #inst "2026-09-21T21:36:04.000-07:00"})

(def typing-route-contract
  {:delivery :best-effort
   :durability :ephemeral
   :duplicates :permitted
   :late-message :discard
   :authorization :current-document-membership
   :semantic-effect :recipient-observed-message})

(defn receive-typing! [recipient message]
  (when (and (routing/current-recipient? recipient message)
             (auth/currently-authorized? recipient (:document/id message))
             (time/before? (time/now) (:expires-at message)))
    (ui/show-typing-indicator! (:from message))))
#+end_src

There may be no durable business fact for node C to reread later.  A stronger
product guarantee would need an explicit stronger routing, persistence,
acknowledgment, or recovery contract.  Reusing Request invalidation semantics
would conflate two meanings merely because both can travel through the same
broker or load-balanced hosts.

*** First physical multi-node topology may be simple

The first useful multi-node environment does not need sophisticated
orchestration.

A development harness may use, as concretely useful:

- multiple Aleph nodes;
- HAProxy or another load balancer;
- shared XTDB/object-store dependencies;
- Valkey or another Live invalidation-routing substrate;
- Redpanda or another log only where a concrete requirement warrants it.

The service list is implementation-specific.

The semantic gate is real host replacement and cross-node propagation through
the same trusted model/protocol boundaries. Naming services is not evidence of
those properties. The selected topology must record the actual dependency
versions and supported consistency/durability contracts; this document neither
requires every listed service nor certifies an unspecified deployment.

*** Multi-node assumptions enter the assurance graph

Once the physical implementation exists, the root assurance report should name
the contracts needed for:

- host replacement;
- distributed invalidation routing;
- interest registration;
- post-commit publication;
- duplicate publication;
- lost publication;
- stale routing knowledge;
- reconnect through another node;
- node-local process loss;
- progression-safe reread from another node;
- command identity/deduplication scope and retention where retry guarantees rely
  on them;
- cross-node session/principal binding and compatible application realizations
  under the declared plan/protocol and admitted assembly contracts;
- acquisition/adoption policy across reconnect and initial reconstruction.

Authority committed while publication was lost must remain discoverable through
the implemented [[#req-post-commit-recovery][post-commit recovery contract]]. If
that contract relies on explicit Refresh, the liveness claim includes that user
action. Reconnect alone is not evidence that lost advisory information has been
recovered. A lagging node must not label an insufficient read as satisfying the
requested progression merely because it returned successfully.

These assumptions arise from the actual implementation.

They should not be invented speculatively before the topology exists.

*** Required multi-node acceptance evidence

The multi-node milestone must identify the actual topology and test at least:

- a mutation on one node becoming authoritatively observable through another;
- an already connected remote browser receiving advisory without unauthorized
  new-Request adoption, followed by successful permitted Refresh;
- a node lost before submission versus one lost after submission/commit, with
  participant uncertainty and any permitted retry handled distinctly;
- crash after commit before publication, followed by the declared recovery path;
- duplicate or reordered advisory delivery preserving scoped outstanding debt;
- reconnect through a different node preserving the declared adoption policy;
- a read unable to meet the required progression preserving recovery rather than
  falsely claiming current authority;
- current authentication and authorization at the replacement host;
- incompatible old/new plan versions following the selected compatibility or
  retirement contract.

These cases use real node/transport boundaries where the claimed failure depends
on physical deployment. Portable model tests remain complementary evidence.
The report separates safety under interruption from progress after the named
recovery conditions hold. A single successful load-balanced request does not
establish the multi-node milestone.

*** Multi-node does not require durable suspended choreography machines

A node may disappear while the durable business process survives.

Ordinary long waits remain represented as authoritative model state and later
short choreographies.

Persisting arbitrary suspended choreography-machine state across process death is
a separate opt-in facility with its own:

- persistence;
- versioning;
- migration;
- ownership;
- recovery;
- delivery semantics.

*** Proof work and multi-node work can overlap

Full multi-node productionization is not a prerequisite for compositional proof
hardening.

Conversely, a small real multi-node prototype may expose missing semantic
distinctions that should be corrected before deeper formalization.

The correct relationship is iterative:

#+begin_example
stable semantic model
    |
small real multi-node realization
    |
new failure evidence
    |
refine named assumptions / semantics if necessary
    |
proof/root report incorporates the actual contracts
#+end_example

The semantic invariant remains constant:

#+begin_quote
Physical topology may change; semantic authority does not derive from host
identity.
#+end_quote


** Formal correspondence and system-boundary invariant

#+begin_quote
A formal theorem applies to production only through its stated representation,
checking, or refinement relation. Conformance vectors remain tests unless their
coverage justifies a stronger claim. Complete obligation coverage, immutable
subject/artifact binding, and explicit translation assumptions prevent a proof
of one object from being attributed to another. Exact finite checking does not
silently become an unbounded theorem, and safety correspondence does not
silently establish liveness.

The root report preserves the evidence class, open assumptions, trusted boundary,
and applicable subject/artifact identities and admitted assembly generation of
each claim where relevant. Runtime authorization and protected
acquisition remain necessary regardless of certificates. Multi-node placement
changes hosts, not authority; retries, publication recovery, current reads, and
command attribution follow their explicit contracts. HumanHelp's selective
adoption policy survives these realizations. Operational success, compositional
certification, external formal correspondence, and multi-node completion remain
distinct milestones with distinct evidence.
#+end_quote

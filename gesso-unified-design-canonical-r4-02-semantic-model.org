* Part II — Semantic model
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-02-semantic-model
:PART_REVISION: 02-20261107
:END:

This part defines the semantic distinctions shared by choreography, authoritative
operations, browser realization, and application assembly. It uses the assurance
and admission vocabulary established in Part I. The definitions do not require
a universal persisted fact graph, a new runtime class for every concept, or a
single global revision coordinate.

The model applies to full-language applications under the
[[#req-full-language-construction][checked construction contract]]. An opaque
Clojure computation has an explicit semantic interface and retained premises;
it is not excluded from the application because its implementation is outside
the proof fragment. This part defines what the surrounding claims mean.
Part IX owns their checking judgments, and Part X owns soundness and concrete
correspondence. The intended semantics does not inherit incidental limitations
of one runtime representation.

Examples use illustrative contract and API names unless explicitly identified
as executable fixtures. Symbols such as R, U, B, and X17 stand for values bound
by the surrounding example. An inspection map shows information that an admitted
boundary must retain; writing that map does not establish admission. Later
parts specify concrete construction, checking, and realization contracts.

** Values and evolving values

A value may be a singular immutable value:

#+begin_src clojure
42
:open
{:user/id user-id}
#+end_src

A value may also be understood as evolving across a time, revision, causal, or
transformation-step dimension.

For example:

#+begin_example
foo = :bar at T1
foo = :baz at T2
#+end_example

or:

#+begin_example
foo = :bar at step S1
foo = :baz at step S2
#+end_example

The observations themselves may remain immutable facts.

The evolving value is the relationship among those observations and the rules
used to select or interpret one observation relative to a particular point of
view.

"Current value" is therefore not an eternal mutable fact.

It is a derived view relative to some combination of:

- valid/effective time;
- causal or transformation relation;
- authoritative basis;
- authoritative progression;
- actor knowledge frontier;
- application policy.

For a HumanHelp Request:

#+begin_example
F1: Request R was unclaimed at valid time T1.
F2: Request R was claimed by user U at valid time T2.
#+end_example

These are *semantic descriptions*, not a claim that a physical Request stores
a =helper-id= field. HumanHelp's actual helper relationship is modeled by
RequestAssignment. A valid interpretation of F2 must account for that
relationship and its authorized temporal/observation context.

Both statements can remain true under the model's declared valid-time and
correction semantics. The historical fact that an observation was made is
distinct from a claim that its interpretation of the world can never be
corrected. Recording an earlier observation does not make an erroneous domain
assertion eternally true.

The Request's current state depends on the point from which the Request is
evaluated and on later facts that supersede, refine, reconcile, or otherwise
relate to the earlier history.

The framework must not confuse:

#+begin_example
fact existed
fact became authoritative
fact became visible at a database frontier
actor learned fact
actor derived another fact
actor displayed representation of fact
#+end_example

These are distinct events and relationships.

*** Conceptual fact graph versus required persistence

The semantic model may reason in terms of facts and relations among facts
without requiring Gesso to persist a universal fact DAG.

The conceptual graph is useful because it allows the design to reason about:

- provenance;
- supersession;
- derivation;
- authority;
- agreement;
- causality;
- command/result relations;
- provisional versus authoritative state.

Actual persistence remains owned by application models and their chosen
authoritative systems.

For HumanHelp, XTDB and public model semantics remain the primary persistence
and authority boundary.

Gesso must not introduce a duplicate universal fact store merely because the
proof vocabulary can describe facts.

*** Immutability and correction

The statement that observations are immutable does not prohibit an application
from correcting its model of the world.

A later authoritative fact may state that an earlier interpretation was wrong,
superseded, withdrawn, or effective over a different valid-time interval.

What must remain explicit is the relationship.

The system should avoid interpreting absence from a current projection as
evidence that an earlier observation never occurred. This semantic distinction
does not require indefinite storage of every observation, prohibit legitimate
data deletion, or introduce an audit-history retention policy. Persistence and
retention remain model-owned.

This distinction becomes important for:

- reconciliation;
- idempotency;
- audit;
- stale-command interpretation;
- provisional settlement;
- authoritative reread;
- domain-specific corrections.

*** Semantic value versus representation

A semantic value is not identical to a wire encoding, DOM representation, XTDB
object, or Clojure map merely because one of those represents it.

Representations may change while semantic identity remains stable.

Conversely, two values with identical serialized shape may have different
semantic meaning because their:

- provenance differs;
- authority differs;
- basis differs;
- principal differs;
- operation context differs;
- plan/protocol compatibility identity differs.

Shape equality alone is insufficient when the semantic judgment depends on
distinctions not established by that shape. Ordinary immutable values may still
use ordinary value equality when the enclosing contract makes it sufficient.
Gesso must not mistake matching schemas or encodings for equality of authority,
provenance, subject, observation scope, or admission status.

*** Typed values, absence, and portable interpretation

The semantic interpretation of a value includes the type and context under
which it is used. A nominal enum, currency amount, principal identifier, and
resource identifier must not become interchangeable because their physical
encodings overlap.

The following cases are distinct:

| Case | Meaning |
|------+---------|
| Field omitted from projection | No value was supplied for the field; its physical presence or absence may be unknown at this scope |
| Field established absent | A complete, admitted schema/read/guard judgment explicitly establishes absence of the field at its stated subject and basis |
| Field present with nil | A present value permitted only when the type admits nil |
| Field present with false | A Boolean value, not absence |
| Fact unknown | The actor lacks a justified proposition; this is not the proposition's negation |
| Observation denied | The observation contract withholds disclosure; this need not establish either presence or absence |
| Resource authoritatively absent | A justified negative result within a declared subject, basis, time, and query scope |

Omission from a partial projection must not be interpreted as known absence;
only an applicable completed observation or admitted representation contract
can establish that negative fact. A read that was denied, timed out, incomplete,
or performed at an inadequate basis does not establish absence by returning no
object. A complete negative observation is limited to its declared query,
authority, visibility, time and basis; it is not a global nonexistence claim.
Likewise, an absent *optional* field in admitted state differs from a missing
*required* reader or malformed input, which must fail the applicable validation.
A defaulting accessor must not collapse absent, nil, and false when the
operation's guard, frame, or outcome depends on their distinction. The schema
and acquisition contract determine which omissions have semantic meaning.

Portable numeric and value profiles specify ranges, overflow, equality,
ordering, strings, nominal categories, and serialization. A mathematically
unbounded integer in a proof is not automatically a safely represented browser
number. A canonical encoding can normalize =1= and =1N= as the same mathematical
integer without proving that every host arithmetic operation preserves that
meaning. Concrete interpretation has its own target and correspondence contract.

Unsupported values at a wire or checked-IR boundary produce a typed rejection
or use an explicitly admitted codec. This does not prohibit using those values
inside an ordinary host-language computation whose exported interface is valid.

*** Representation, semantic category, and admission
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-semantic-admission
:END:

Representation validity, semantic category, semantic admission, and proof are
separate questions.

A Clojure map, record, scalar, wire value, DOM node, or other representation may
carry a semantic value without the representation itself establishing the
stronger status in which later code intends to use it.

The design therefore distinguishes conceptually:

| Question | What a positive answer establishes |
|----------+------------------------------------|
| Does the representation have the required shape? | Structural validity under the selected schema |
| Does it belong to the required semantic category? | The required meaning, such as command rather than execution identity |
| Has it crossed the required admission boundary? | The boundary's checked bindings and applicability |
| Which propositions have been proved? | Only the checked conclusions under their retained premises |

A positive answer to an earlier question does not automatically answer the
later ones.

This distinction matters most where two values may have the same or nearly the
same representation while differing materially in provenance, authority,
lifecycle, or assurance status.  Examples include:

- =ObservedBasisClaim= versus an authoritative basis established by trusted
  acquisition;
- =CommandId= versus =ExecutionId=;
- provisional versus authoritative projections;
- raw versus checked choreography declarations;
- projected plans versus admitted executable plans;
- raw certificate data versus checker-accepted certificate evidence;
- authenticated observation evidence versus an operation capability;
- historical observation evidence versus current authorization.

The supported Gesso APIs must not silently treat one such category as another
merely because the underlying values have equal map shape, equal scalar
representation, matching discriminator keys, or compatible Malli schemas.

This is an admission rule rather than a requirement to turn every semantic
concept into a new runtime class.  Permitted realizations include, where they
satisfy the intended boundary contract:

- nominal types or records;
- opaque wrappers;
- smart or checked constructors;
- values reachable only through a verifier, elaborator, authoritative
  acquisition path, or registry lookup;
- tagged representations whose tags are validated by the appropriate trusted
  boundary;
- representations whose construction and use make the invalid substitution
  impossible or immediately rejectable through the supported path.

The design requires the semantic distinction and its enforcement at the
supported boundary.  It does not prescribe a nominal-type rewrite when a
chosen mechanism provides the required protection.

Nominality is especially useful as misuse prevention inside cooperating trusted
application code.  For example, if =CommandId= and =ExecutionId= are both
physically UUIDs, a supported API should not make accidental interchange look
ordinary merely because both values satisfy a UUID schema.  Similarly, a raw
map containing the fields of a checked certificate must not become accepted
certificate evidence solely by carrying a discriminator such as
=:certificate/type= or =:verified? true=.

A boundary must retain or establish the information required to distinguish
the categories it promises to check. If two untagged UUIDs reach a boundary
with no trustworthy category or correlation information, UUID shape validation
cannot reconstruct whether one originated as a command or an execution.

The supported construction path must therefore preserve the needed distinction
through checked construction, typed or opaque values, declared field bindings,
trusted correlation, or another mechanism that actually enforces the contract.
Merely assigning different variable names to interchangeable unchecked inputs
does not establish that protection. Framework-owned construction should derive
these bindings wherever it already possesses the required information.

Nominal or opaque representation alone is not a security proof.  A value called
=VerifiedChoreography= is trustworthy only to the extent that its construction
or admission path actually establishes the claims downstream code relies on.
Likewise, a Clojure record constructor is not treated as a cryptographic or
process-isolation boundary.

Crossing an untrusted serialization boundary also strips any purely in-process
admission status.  Conceptually:

1. A trusted boundary admits a server-side value under its declared premises.
2. Encoding and transmission produce bytes that may pass through an untrusted
   participant. The original in-process admission status does not travel merely
   because those bytes encode a familiar representation.
3. Decoding reconstructs data. Verification or trusted reacquisition must
   establish the premises required to admit that data for its new use.

Deserializing data into the same nominal-looking representation must not by
itself restore authority, trusted provenance, certification, or admission.

Ordinary downstream application authoring should not pay unnecessary ceremony
for these distinctions.  Gesso should preferentially construct admitted values
behind verification, elaboration, acquisition, projection, model, and assembly
boundaries.  Routine application code should not be required to manually wrap
every Request id, title, status, or other ordinary domain value merely to
satisfy framework ceremony.

The practical acceptance criterion is:

#+begin_quote
Where two same-shaped values have materially different semantic meanings,
accidental substitution through the supported API is either structurally
unavailable or rejected at the earliest legitimate boundary with a diagnostic
that identifies the expected semantic category, the supplied category, and the
missing derivation or admission step. If the supplied category cannot be
established, the diagnostic identifies it as unknown or unadmitted rather than
inventing a trustworthy origin from an untrusted tag.
#+end_quote


An expense-approval path shows what this means without requiring authors to
create three record wrappers and a registry entry by hand for every value.
The application declares an observation contract and supplies its model-owned
acquisition implementation. Gesso owns admission and downstream checks.

#+begin_src clojure
(def EmployeeValue
  [:map
   [:employee/id uuid?]
   [:employee/department keyword?]
   [:employee/risk-band [:enum :ordinary :enhanced-review]]])

(def ExpenseValue
  [:map
   [:expense/id uuid?]
   [:expense/employee-id uuid?]
   [:expense/department keyword?]
   [:expense/amount Money]
   [:expense/status [:enum :draft :submitted :approved :rejected]]])

(def approval-inputs-contract
  {:observation/id :expense/approval-inputs
   :input [:map [:expense/id uuid?]]
   :projection {:expense ExpenseValue :employee EmployeeValue}
   :purpose :expense/approval-preparation
   :authorization :expense/observe-approval-inputs
   :bindings
   [[:= [:expense/id :expense] [:expense/id :arguments]]
    [:= [:employee/id :employee] [:expense/employee-id :expense]]]
   :consistency :compatible-authoritative-read-context
   :implementation #'expense-model/read-approval-inputs!})

(defn observe-approval-inputs!
  [ctx expense-id]
  ;; The contract is resolved from the admitted application. Authentication
  ;; and read authorization come from ctx, never from observation arguments.
  (observation/acquire!
    ctx
    :expense/approval-inputs
    {:expense/id expense-id}))

(defn approval-facts-eligible?
  [ctx expense-id observed]
  (let [{:keys [expense employee]}
        (observation/require-for!
          ctx observed
          {:contract :expense/approval-inputs
           :arguments {:expense/id expense-id}
           :purpose :expense/approval-preparation})]
    (and (= (:expense/id expense) expense-id)
         (= (:employee/id employee) (:expense/employee-id expense))
         (= :submitted (:expense/status expense))
         (= (:expense/department expense) (:employee/department employee))
         (= :ordinary (:employee/risk-band employee)))))
#+end_src

The =Money= reference uses Part I's illustrative nominal USD-cents profile.
It is not automatically an admitted Part IX scalar merely because a Malli
schema names it. An actual checked realization must bind the supported
nominal representation/codec and reader interpretation, or carry a separate
permitted opaque implementation premise. The =:bindings= field paths above
are also *schematic semantic-interface constraints*, not direct syntax of
Part IX's closed =[:read reader-id]= rule AST. If used for a checked-rule
judgment they require a defined, checked elaboration; otherwise they remain
explicit contracts with their applicable checking/enforcement premises.
Unsupported elaboration cannot be reported as successful rule checking.

Schema Vars and the observation map are declarations, not admission
credentials. The public API names above illustrate the required contracts
rather than freeze a particular namespace layout.

The admitted observation establishes the facts returned for this preparation
purpose. The sample eligibility function does not establish that its caller is
a manager or may approve an expense. That is a distinct authorization decision
owned by the authoritative approval operation. If the function is opaque to the
checker, its functional contract remains explicitly asserted even though its
input admission and relationship checks can be enforced.

The acquisition/admission boundary must establish all of the following:

| Obligation | Establishing boundary |
|------------+-----------------------|
| Caller identity | Trusted authentication bound to the actual context |
| Permission to disclose | Model-owned read authorization before releasing protected data |
| Requested Expense | The acquired Expense id matches the admitted argument |
| Related Employee | The acquired Employee id matches that Expense's employee relationship |
| Representation | Non-optional validation of the declared result schemas |
| Observation meaning | Correct authority, projection contract, parameters, temporal context, and compatible read basis |
| Permitted use | Current applicability of the admitted observation for this caller and purpose |
| Realization fidelity | Proof, runtime enforcement, or retained assumptions connecting the acquisition implementation to its contract |

A single compatible read context may be supplied by a model operation or by
several reads under an admitted consistent-acquisition contract. In
particular, reading an Expense from one basis and its Employee from a later
incompatible basis cannot satisfy the combined relationship merely because
the records each passed schema validation. Separate successful reads alone
do not establish a coherent combined snapshot. If that
premise cannot be established, the boundary rejects the stronger combined claim
or exposes separately scoped observations for a contract that permits them.

An implementation may use an opaque object, trusted issuance registry, or other
checked representation for the observation. Its admission binding includes the
result material, contract identity, subjects, basis, temporal/query context,
principal context, purpose, and applicability required by the contract. A record
class or =:admitted?= tag alone is insufficient. A process-local handle may be
valid for a local invocation; continuation across hosts requires a declared
shared, revalidated, or server-verifiable mechanism. None of this requires a
universal persistent observation store.

Now compare an observation with client-carried data containing equal values:

#+begin_src clojure
(def observed
  (observe-approval-inputs! ctx expense-id))

;; Illustration: the browser returns the same domain values it received.
;; Parsing and shape checking are allowed; they do not restore admission.
(def returned-values
  {:expense expense-value
   :employee employee-value})

(= (observation/value observed) returned-values)
;; => true in this fixture

(approval-facts-eligible? ctx expense-id returned-values)
;; => rejected: no admitted observation for the required contract and context

(approval-facts-eligible? ctx expense-id observed)
;; => evaluates the sample eligibility rule under its retained premises
#+end_src

Reconstructing a record with a familiar class or copied fields must fail the
same boundary unless the actual admission binding is re-established. Conversely,
there is no need to reject an ordinary scalar solely because it was serialized
when its use requires only shape-checked value equality. Admission requirements
follow the claimed meaning, not a rule that all decoded values are unusable.

A browser may also return an observation receipt when the protocol explicitly
supports one. Verification can establish the receipt's exact historical issuance
claim and applicability; it does not silently supply a new current observation
or approval authority. Otherwise the supported path reacquires authorized facts.

Finally, preparation is not incorporation. The eventual approval command must
recognize compatible replay and, for a new incorporation, protect the Expense,
Employee relationship, eligibility facts, and current approval authority under
the atomic contract. An earlier admitted observation is not a substitute for
that protection. A successful old approval result likewise does not prove that
the Expense's present state remains unchanged.

The practical authoring surface remains an acquisition contract and ordinary
functions. The richer admission representation is framework-owned, inspectable,
and proportionate to the claims made about its use.

** Business rules and the auditable semantic surface
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-auditable-semantic-surface
:END:

A correctness-relevant business rule should not exist only as an incidental
condition buried inside arbitrary implementation code.

For Gesso's purposes, a *business rule* is a named semantic proposition,
relation, precondition, postcondition, transition restriction, authorization
condition, disclosure condition, or other application judgment whose truth or
applicability affects a correctness-relevant operation.

Possible application rules include the following. This list illustrates the
kind of policy that belongs on the surface; it does not assert that each rule
has already been adopted by HumanHelp:

- a Request may be claimed only while it is open;
- the claimant must have an active Membership in the Request's Organization;
- a requester may not claim their own Request;
- a completed Request is terminal;
- a protected observation may be disclosed only to an authorized principal;
- a command may be incorporated only under the authority and argument binding
  established for that command.

The semantic rule is distinct from any one Clojure expression that happens to
implement or test it.  A condition such as:

#+begin_src clojure
(= :active (:membership/status membership))
#+end_src

may participate in the realization of a rule such as
=:membership/active-for-request-operation=.  By itself, however, this
expression establishes only that the particular =membership= value supplied to
it has status =:active=. It does not establish whose Membership it is, whether
it belongs to the Request's Organization, whether the authenticated principal
is the claimant, or whether the Membership is still active when Claim commits.
Those bindings and timing requirements belong in the rule's meaning. The
expression is one implementation step; the named rule is the semantic
proposition whose meaning should remain stable across refactoring, generated
realization, formal translation, diagnostics, and audit views.

A Gesso application therefore has an *auditable semantic surface*: the compact
set of named rules, operations, authorities, lifecycle transitions, invariants,
knowledge/agreement requirements, and explicit trusted premises that determine
its correctness-relevant behavior.

That surface should normally be substantially smaller than the application's
full implementation.  A large application may contain tens or hundreds of
thousands of lines concerned with rendering, formatting, queries, adapters,
integration plumbing, logging, caching, and incidental computation while its
business semantics consist of a much smaller collection of rules and
operations.  Gesso should preserve that compression rather than require an
auditor to rediscover policy by rereading the entire implementation.

The requirement is not that every conditional expression become a business
rule.  Conditions concerned only with presentation mechanics, formatting,
local optimization, or other behavior outside the claimed semantic boundary
need not be promoted merely because they are written with =if=, =when=, or a
predicate call.

A condition is a strong candidate for the semantic surface when it controls or
materially contributes to matters such as:

- authorization or eligibility;
- protected disclosure or observation;
- ownership or responsibility;
- lifecycle admission or transition;
- authoritative mutation;
- transfer of money, value, quota, inventory, or another conserved resource;
- command acceptance, incorporation, settlement, or retry meaning;
- establishment or use of authoritative knowledge;
- a domain invariant or cross-operation consistency rule.

The application may represent such rules using ordinary Clojure data, pure
functions, checked constructors, existing model contracts, thin macro sugar,
or another representation that preserves their semantic identity.  This design
does not require a second policy language or a macro-heavy pseudo-language.
What matters is that the supported Gesso path can identify the rule and the
semantic judgment it contributes, rather than knowing only that some opaque
branch occurred in host code.

A rule representation may conceptually carry information such as:

#+begin_src clojure
{:rule/id :request/claim-requires-active-membership

 ;; Meaning:
 ;;   The authenticated helper must have an active Membership in the
 ;;   Organization of the Request being claimed when Claim commits.
 ;;
 ;; Rationale:
 ;;   Claiming transfers operational responsibility for the Request.
 ;;
 ;; Correctness significance:
 ;;   Principal binding, Request/Organization association, Membership
 ;;   ownership, and active status must be mutually compatible and
 ;;   protected or re-established at the authoritative commit boundary.
 ;;   A stale browser or server pre-read is not sufficient.

 :proposition
 [:and
  [:= [:membership/user-id]
      [:authenticated-principal/user-id]]
  [:= [:membership/organization-id]
      [:request/organization-id]]
  [:= [:membership/status] :active]]

 :subjects
 {:principal :trusted-authenticated-principal
  :request :current-authoritative-request
  :membership :current-authoritative-membership}

 :required-at :request/claim-new-incorporation

 :applies-to
 #{:request/claim}}
#+end_src

The =:proposition= field paths in this sketch are *semantic-interface
notation*, not expressions directly admitted by Part IX's closed,
reader-based =[:read reader-id]= grammar. In that baseline, a comparable
active-status conjunct would use a declared =:membership/status= reader,
an explicit =[:read :membership/status]=, and =[:literal :active]= under
its nominal status type. A supported frontend must check the elaboration
of the source meaning, operand types, and bound subjects before it may
report that this stronger rule is mechanically well typed or established.
Without that translation, the interface may still identify an explicit
opaque contract and its permitted premises, but it is not certified merely
because the vector is recognizable. The corresponding principal, Request,
Organization, and transaction-current relationship obligations remain
separate from checking the status conjunct.

The exact representation above is illustrative; its =:subjects= and
=:required-at= entries express obligations, not evidence that current facts
were acquired or that a transaction guard was implemented. The three field
tests must refer to the *same* Membership, the trusted current principal, and
the Request selected by this Claim. Part IV determines how an authoritative
realization protects those facts and their relationship at commit. The semantic
requirements are that the rule has stable identity, that its checked meaning
*where supported*, or its explicitly conditional interface where opaque, is
available to the parts of Gesso that rely on it, and that enough correspondence
is retained for later assembly, diagnostics, certification, and formal artifacts
to refer back to the same rule. The business meaning and binding belong to
their public model contract; choreography, views, and generated checking
products must not redeclare subtly different eligibility policies. An opaque
Clojure predicate with the same name does not make that proposition
mechanically established. Its contract is an ordinary supported dependency
whose accepted assertions must remain visible.
The new-incorporation rule is not imposed on a compatible replay merely because
the same public operation processes both cases.

Business rules should also be generously explained in ordinary source.  Where
practical, the declaration or function that defines a rule should be surrounded
by retained explanatory metadata and source comments explaining at least:

- what the rule means in domain terms;
- why the rule exists;
- which subtle correctness boundary matters to its enforcement;
- any intentionally surprising exception or asymmetry.

Comments improve human and model review, but comments alone cannot be premises
of a mechanical assurance claim because ordinary Clojure reading/compilation
need not preserve them.  Prose that must survive elaboration into generated
semantic inventories, diagnostics, or assurance artifacts must therefore be
retained as checked metadata, documentation strings, source metadata, or
another explicit representation.  The machine-checkable rule declaration remains
distinct from its explanatory prose.

This produces two complementary views of the same rule:

#+begin_example
machine-checkable rule declaration
    : request/claim requires a current active Membership held by the
      authenticated helper in this Request's Organization at commit

human-auditable explanation
    : what "active" means here, why it matters, and why the check must
      participate in the authoritative transition rather than rely on an
      earlier browser observation
#+end_example

Neither view should silently substitute for the other.  Explanatory prose does
not prove the rule, and a terse machine predicate is not sufficient by itself
for an application intended to remain auditable by humans.

When a correctness-relevant decision necessarily depends on application code
whose semantics Gesso cannot inspect or derive, that dependency should still
have an explicit semantic name and boundary.  It may be represented as an
opaque or trusted premise rather than being allowed to disappear into an
unnamed implementation branch.  Later certification and whole-application
assembly determine what assurance may be claimed from such a premise; this part
requires only that the semantic dependency itself remain visible.

The completeness of an *audited* semantic surface is a separate, scoped
correspondence claim. The source-owned operation, writer, authorization,
disclosure, and decision inventory must be related to the actual paths
covered by the selected checking or inspection profile. A closed generated
subset may support complete coverage of that subset; an analyzer that did not
inspect an arbitrary Clojure body cannot establish the absence of hidden
policy branches by finding none. An intentionally opaque body is permitted
under a named implementation/effect/dependency contract and admission policy,
with its uninspected internals retained as premises rather than falsely
classified as checked rules. Known consequential decisions outside every
admitted rule, runtime-enforced premise, or approved opaque contract are
semantic-policy escapes and must be reported. An uninspected inventory is
*unknown*, not empty. Part IX owns the precise evidence and rejection rules,
including source-derived mandatory obligations; this section does not create
a second author-maintained policy registry.

The semantic-surface acceptance criterion is:

#+begin_quote
A reviewer should be able to inspect the correctness-relevant business policy
of a Gesso application as a compact set of named semantic rules and explicit
premises without reconstructing that policy from unrelated implementation
code.  Refactoring may change the physical Clojure realization without erasing
or silently changing the identity of the rule being realized.  Any
correctness-relevant dependency that cannot be represented or derived by the
supported semantic path remains explicit rather than being mistaken for an
assured rule merely because opaque host code returned a truthy value.
#+end_quote

*** Rule identity, interpretation, and opaque implementation

A stable semantic name identifies a rule's place in the application. It is not
by itself an identity of unchanged meaning. A change to relied-upon types,
bindings, temporal requirements, effects, or guarantees changes the exported
semantic interface even when its =:rule/id= remains the same.

The interface identity uses a declared versioned canonical representation.
Map/set ordering and irrelevant formatting may normalize; vector order,
meaningful string content, nominal types, presence, and other semantic material
must not be silently erased. Normalization must reject unsupported material or
collisions rather than treat different subjects as the same declaration.

Opaque Clojure has a separate implementation binding. Its contract identity
does not prove that binding implements the contract, and Gesso does not require
deciding equivalence of arbitrary functions. A concrete implementation change
may preserve a theorem about the interface while requiring new correspondence
evidence or assumption applicability for the realization.

The semantic surface must expose the inputs and effects on which a meaningful
decision depends. A deterministic predicate over supplied authoritative values
has different premises from a function that reads a clock, consults an external
service, or writes state. All remain expressible; concealed dependencies do not
inherit the guarantees of an effect-free contract. A result observation, a
contract assertion, and a universal proof have different scopes.

** Facts, time, and authoritative progression

The design distinguishes at least the following dimensions.

*** Valid/effective time

Valid or effective time describes when a domain fact applies in the modeled
world.

Examples:

- Membership became active on one date;
- a Request was open during one interval;
- Organization policy changed at a domain-effective time;
- a correction applies retroactively to an earlier period.

Valid time belongs to domain meaning.

*** Recording time and correction history

Recording or system time identifies the authoritative history under which a
fact was recorded, corrected, or withdrawn. It differs from the valid/effective
time at which that fact applies in the domain. A correction recorded today may
change the authoritative interpretation of an earlier valid-time interval.

An observation that depends on both dimensions must retain both, directly or
through its admitted read contract. A read basis that bounds transaction
visibility does not alone select the valid time or historical correction view.
The database provider defines the supported relation between these coordinates
under its qualified backend profile.

*** Domain or causal relation

Domain or causal relations describe how semantic facts relate.

Examples include:

- based on;
- produced by;
- superseded by;
- reconciled by;
- rejected by;
- confirmed by;
- corrected by;
- derived from.

These relations are not necessarily database transaction order.

*** Authoritative database progression

Authoritative database progression describes the consistency/basis frontier at
which authoritative facts are visible.

For HumanHelp's primary persistent authority, this is supplied by the admitted
XTDB provider under its [[#req-backend-assumption-qualification][qualified backend contract]].
The provider owns token interpretation and the evidence for a comparison or
read guarantee. Generic choreography code does not decode an XTDB token and
invent those semantics independently.

A basis is scoped to its authority/provider context and relevant database
incarnation or equivalent identity. Equal raw tokens from different databases,
tenants where applicable, or reset histories are not automatically comparable.

A provider's **minimum visibility requirement** and the **actual basis of an
acquired observation** are different: a read may be required to include a
confirmed effect at or beyond B1 and return an observation from B2. B1 is not
thereby the exact snapshot used, and B2 does not establish that the Request's
current value equals its immediate post-commit value. The adapter must expose
which relation it established—an exact observation coordinate, an admitted
lower bound, or only a requested minimum—and retain the query/temporal scope.
A transaction submission, allocated log position, or claimed commit token is
not by itself evidence that a particular domain command incorporated.
Part IV owns the concrete commit-to-visibility contract and its backend
qualification.

An authoritative progression value may advance because unrelated data changed.

Therefore:

#+begin_example
database advanced
!=
this Request changed
#+end_example

*** Actor knowledge frontier

An actor knowledge frontier describes what authoritative information a
particular runtime actor has acquired through its admitted observation paths.
It is scoped by the facts, authority, projection, and relevant temporal context
of those observations. It need not be one token covering all actor knowledge.

For the supported browser implementation, the frontier records semantic
acquisition and incorporation by that implementation. It is not a server proof
that an adversarial browser or the human using it retained or understood the
information.

Two browser actors may observe the same authoritative database at different
frontiers.

The same principal may therefore have two browser tabs with different
knowledge.

The database's current state and an actor's current knowledge are distinct.
Acquired evidence, content selected for adoption, and content successfully
installed in a presentation may also be distinct. A protocol must identify
which transition advances each claimed frontier; merely scheduling a read or
allowing a DOM swap does not establish its completion.

*** Physical delivery time

Physical delivery time may matter operationally without becoming semantic
authority.

An SSE notification may arrive after another message.

A callback may fire after an execution has been retired.

A browser may wake after a deployment.

The fact that an event arrived later in wall-clock time does not by itself make
the event semantically newer or authorized to mutate current state.

#+LATEX: \newpage
*** Do not collapse dimensions into one revision integer

The framework must not collapse:

#+begin_example
valid time
recording/correction time
domain fact identity/version
causal relation
XTDB progression
actor knowledge frontier
command identity
execution identity
artifact compatibility identity
#+end_example

into one global monotonically increasing application revision.

A global revision number would erase distinctions that matter to correctness.

For example, XTDB may advance while Request R is unchanged.

Likewise, Request R may have meaningful valid-time history independent of when a
database transaction was recorded.

A browser may hold knowledge through frontier X17 while authority has progressed
to X23 without any relevant Request fact changing.

That is ordinary distributed execution, not necessarily staleness requiring
rejection.

*** Progression as provenance

When established by an *admitted authoritative acquisition*, a basis supplies
provenance evidence approximately of the form:

#+begin_quote
The acquired representation is justified by an authoritative read with the
stated subject, query/visibility and temporal interpretation at this admitted
basis or justified lower bound.
#+end_quote

The actor may subsequently choose that representation for command preparation.
A browser-carried =:observed-basis= field alone is instead a claim about its
starting context; it does not establish that the browser actually acquired,
installed, adopted, or used that representation. A protocol requiring evidence
of prior observation needs its separately validated issuance/receipt contract
as described in [[#req-observation-evidence][authoritative observation evidence]].
The distinction does not prohibit using a client-supplied basis as nonauthorizing
protocol context for stale-input handling.

It does not mean:

- the entire database is still at that frontier;
- every fact the command depends on is unchanged;
- the client owns authority over that frontier;
- the client can prove merely by sending the token that it really performed the
  observation;
- the framework should reject every command formed from an older frontier.

The authoritative-operations and progression part defines the exact command,
scope, and XTDB semantics, including
[[#req-progression-scope][scoped progression requirements]].

At the ontology level, authoritative progression is one provenance coordinate
among several and is not a universal domain revision.

*** Scoped progression decisions and requirement composition

A comparison result binds its exact operands, comparison context, and admitted
provider contract. Generic code can validate the representation and binding of
a witness such as "B2 advances B1"; that check does not prove the assertion is
true. The provider or another declared reasoning mechanism must establish its
meaning, with any backend assumptions retained.

An explicit comparison distinguishes equivalence, advancement, precedence,
known incomparability, and unavailable or invalid comparison evidence where the
contract supports them. Equality of opaque bytes is not provider-verified
equivalence across incompatible authority, incarnation, temporal interpretation,
or relevant scope. Failure to establish an ordering is not proof of
incomparability, and incomparable observations need not be individually invalid.
A comparison does not automatically prove that a read covers a particular
domain effect or that a selected query was complete; those are additional
observation and commit-correlation obligations.

Pending acquisition requirements compose as a conjunction of scoped
obligations. A conservative representation retains an orderless set of distinct
requirements and removes exact duplicates. Replacing it with one basis, taking
a numeric maximum, or discarding a requirement as subsumed needs an admitted
coverage/implication argument. No join or total order is assumed merely because
the provider can compare some pairs of bases.

An observation discharges a requirement only when its authority, subject,
projection/query scope, temporal meaning, principal context where relevant,
and progression satisfy that requirement. A later card read cannot by itself
discharge a board-membership acquisition requirement. Part IV owns the concrete
algebra and provider contract.

Bounds on retained requirements must preserve this meaning. Coalescing may
produce a conservative stronger reacquisition obligation when the contract
justifies it. Silently dropping debt to meet a memory bound is not permissible.
When no sound bounded representation is available, the runtime follows its
declared backpressure, retirement, or reacquisition policy.

** Roles, principals, actors, hosts, and authorities
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-semantic-identities
:END:

These concepts are distinct and must remain distinct throughout declaration,
projection, runtime execution, proof, diagnostics, and application assembly.

*** Role

A *role* is a static position in a choreography.

Examples:

#+begin_example
:helper
:request-authority
:observer
#+end_example

A role describes a position in the distributed protocol.

A role is not automatically:

- a user;
- a browser tab;
- a process;
- a web server;
- an authorization grant.

A principal or actor may instantiate a role only under the rules of the
particular operation and trusted runtime boundary.

*** Principal

A *principal* is the identity or entity whose authority is being exercised.

Examples:

#+begin_example
[:user user-id]
[:service :humanhelp]
#+end_example

A principal is a semantic/security identity.

A principal is not identical to a browser actor.

One principal may have:

- multiple browser tabs;
- multiple devices;
- multiple protocol executions;
- retries through multiple hosts.

Those actors may hold different knowledge while acting for the same principal.

*** Actor

An *actor* is a concrete participant carrying a local projected machine,
execution state, or knowledge state.

Examples:

#+begin_example
[:client client-id]
[:service-instance logical-service-actor-id]
#+end_example

An actor gives semantic identity to a participant beyond one physical callback
or request.

The exact runtime realization may be short-lived.

An actor identity does not require a permanently resident process.

*** Host

A *host* is the physical runtime that happens to execute a step.

Examples:

#+begin_example
[:web-node node-1]
[:web-node node-3]
#+end_example

Hosts are replaceable physical realizations.

A retry may run on another host without changing:

- semantic command;
- principal;
- authority;
- logical operation;
- relevant actor identity, where the protocol preserves it.

A host does not acquire semantic authority merely because it executes authority
code.

*** Authority

An *authority* is the semantic locus entitled to establish some class of
authoritative fact.

An authority is named by an authority identifier such as:

#+begin_example
[:authority :request]
[:authority :request request-id]
#+end_example

The identifier does not itself contain an authoritative fact, nor does it name
a particular database record or server process. It names a locus in the
application's semantic model. Application rules, together with Gesso's
execution rules, determine which facts and transitions that locus is entitled
to establish and under which premises.

For example:

#+begin_example
[:authority :request R]
#+end_example

may govern authoritative facts concerning Request =R=, such as its current
lifecycle state, ownership, or the incorporation of a command affecting it.
The concrete facts may be represented by one or several XTDB documents and may
depend on other authoritative observations, such as Membership or Organization
state. Those storage objects help realize the authority; they are not
themselves its semantic identity.

This distinction permits the physical representation to change without
changing what the application means by:

#+begin_example
[:authority :request R]
#+end_example

For example, the same authority could be realized using a different document
layout, a different database, or a different web node while continuing to
govern the same class of semantic facts.

Authority is therefore distinct from both *storage location* and *execution
location*.

A physical web node may temporarily realize an authoritative operation without
becoming the authority itself.

For a Request operation:

#+begin_example
semantic authority
    [:authority :request R]

first host
    [:web-node A]

retry host
    [:web-node C]
#+end_example

Node =A= and node =C= may each exercise the same semantic authority while
executing different attempts of the operation. Process-local identity or
process-local memory does not determine the authority.

The authority's logical identity can survive host replacement. The realization
must still establish continuity of authoritative state, command records, issuer
bindings, and applicable backend semantics. Merely reusing the authority name
after a database reset or migration does not establish that continuity. Old and
new progression values need an admitted comparison or migration relation before
they can be used together.

Consequently, any fact that must survive retries, failover, or migration
cannot derive its semantic force merely from having been remembered by the
same process. It must instead be recoverable from authoritative state, from
evidence validly issued on behalf of the semantic authority, or from another
mechanism whose semantics are explicitly admitted by the application and
Gesso.


*** Server is not a universal semantic identity

"Server" is normally an implementation location or a role category, not one
unique actor identity.

Multiple web nodes may realize the same logical authority while holding
different process-local state.

The design therefore avoids proofs or runtime assumptions that rely on:

#+begin_example
same server process
= same semantic authority
#+end_example

or:

#+begin_example
different server process
= different semantic authority
#+end_example

Neither equivalence is generally valid.

*** Relationship among role, principal, actor, host, and authority

A useful conceptual example is:

#+begin_example
role
    :helper

principal
    [:user U]

actor
    [:client C]

host of the browser-side actor
    user's browser/device

authoritative role
    :request-authority

semantic authority
    [:authority :request R]

host currently realizing authority
    [:web-node A]
#+end_example

A later retry may change the final line while leaving the semantic operation
otherwise intact.

Verification and diagnostics should identify which category an identity belongs
to instead of overloading one generic "id" field wherever practical.

Authentication establishes the principal binding. The operation's trusted
admission and model policy establish whether that principal may instantiate a
particular role for a particular subject. Actor and host identity provide
correlation or realization information; neither substitutes for those decisions.
Changing a browser tab, transport connection, or physical host must not silently
carry permissions into a different principal or authority scope.

An authentication or principal-context instance may also need identity distinct
from the principal itself. Logout, account switching, credential replacement,
or a declared revocation transition can invalidate pending work even when a
later session represents the same principal. The applicable contract states
which bindings survive and which must be re-established. No universal session
epoch or global counter is required.


A collaborative editor makes the categories easier to follow when several
change independently. Suppose Alice is permitted to edit document D. The
following is inspection data, not a credential accepted from the browser. The
fixture names U, D, C1, E19, and C77 denote bound identities of the stated kinds.

#+begin_src clojure
(def editor-session
  {:semantic/role :editor
   :principal [:user U]
   :principal-context :session/alice-1
   :actor [:client C1]
   :actor-host [:browser-context :alice-tab-1]
   :authority [:authority :document D]
   :operation :document/save
   :command C77
   :execution E19
   :transport-request-id :http/204
   :authority-host [:web-node :A]})

(def retry-session
  (assoc editor-session
    :transport-request-id :http/205
    :authority-host [:web-node :C]))

(= (select-keys editor-session
     [:principal :principal-context :actor :authority
      :operation :command :execution])
   (select-keys retry-session
     [:principal :principal-context :actor :authority
      :operation :command :execution]))
;; => true
#+end_src

The operation's retry contract preserves command C77, including its admitted
semantic arguments. The authority host and transport request change. The
browser actor's own host does not thereby become node C, and neither physical
location authenticates the principal.

Alice opens a second tab and forms a separate save intent:

#+begin_src clojure
(def second-tab
  (assoc retry-session
    :actor [:client C2]
    :actor-host [:browser-context :alice-tab-2]
    :command C88
    :execution E20
    :transport-request-id :http/206))

(= (:principal retry-session) (:principal second-tab))
;; => true
(= (:authority retry-session) (:authority second-tab))
;; => true
(= (:actor retry-session) (:actor second-tab))
;; => false
(= (:command retry-session) (:command second-tab))
;; => false
#+end_src

These maps isolate identity relationships; they do not implement the save
operation's argument binding or admission checks. The new intent has its own
command identity. If the second tab instead
reconciles C77, it may retain C77 only through that command's binding and
reconciliation contract. Copying a session map is not a command-transfer or
authorization protocol.

The authoritative server path authenticates each invocation independently of
the inspection fields:

#+begin_src clojure
(defn invoke-save!
  [{:keys [session] :as ctx}
   {:document/keys [id content] :gesso/keys [command-id]}]
  (let [principal (auth/current-principal! session)]
    (document-model/save!
      (assoc ctx :principal principal)
      {:document-id id
       :content content
       :command-id command-id})))
#+end_src

The admitted route binds this entry to the save operation. The public model
operation validates command binding and current disclosure permission for
replay, and current editing authority for new incorporation. It protects the
relevant state dependencies atomically with a new save and its command record.
An authorization check made earlier in a handler is insufficient if the relevant
permission can change before incorporation.

If the Organization revokes Alice's editing permission, a new save intent is
rejected under the current policy. An already incorporated C77 is handled by
its replay protocol: the recorded result is returned only if current disclosure
policy permits it. Revocation must not cause a successful old save to be
misreported as never having happened, nor must historical success bypass the
new disclosure decision. If outcome evidence is unavailable, the caller's
knowledge may remain unknown.

The principal, actors, and semantic authority can remain the same while the
authorization relation changes. Their continued existence does not imply
continued editing rights. Category-specific diagnostics identify which binding
or permission changed rather than reporting an unexplained generic id mismatch.

*** Semantic command identity

A semantic command identifies one logical intent to invoke an operation under
its bound semantic arguments and authority context. It is distinct from each
attempt to deliver or incorporate that intent.

It may survive:

- network retry;
- host replacement;
- a new protocol execution;
- another transport attempt;

where operation semantics permit that retry.

The [[#req-command-participant-uncertainty][command identity and uncertainty contract]]
in the authoritative-operations part defines the relevant retry and attribution
conditions. Possessing the same command identifier alone does not establish
that a retry is permitted or that the command already committed.

The authoritative binding covers the operation/interface compatibility context,
canonical semantic arguments, authenticated principal, and authority or tenant
scope required by the operation. A new business intent receives a new command
identity; retrying or reconciling an existing intent preserves its binding.
Changing bytes in an identity-bearing argument is not a harmless transport
retry unless the declared canonicalization treats the arguments as equivalent.

Command-record retention and identity reuse have a declared horizon. Forgetting
a record must not silently make an old command eligible for a second
incorporation under an unlimited deduplication claim. Expiry, tombstones,
namespace retirement, or another explicit protocol may bound that guarantee.
Absence of an outcome record is not, by itself, proof that no pending attempt
can commit.

*** Protocol execution identity

A protocol execution identifies one realization of a distributed trajectory.

Two executions may relate to the same semantic command without being the same
execution.

Their resources and callbacks must not be interchangeable merely because the
semantic command matches. A protocol execution can span several local steps,
transport requests, and backend attempts. Replacing or resuming it has a
declared ownership and compatibility contract; a new HTTP request alone does
not determine whether the execution is new.

*** Plan compatibility identity

A projected plan may remain in a browser across deployment.

Compatibility identity answers whether that suspended or retried projection is
safe to continue under the current server/runtime contract.

Compatibility does not necessarily require exact artifact equality. It must
nevertheless follow an explicit supported compatibility rule. A runtime must
not infer compatibility from matching names, approximate version ordering, or
the absence of a visible error.

*** Exact executable artifact identity

Diagnostic and proof correspondence may require a stronger identity than
runtime compatibility.

The exact executable artifact identity answers:

#+begin_quote
Does this diagnostic or proof metadata describe these exact emitted runtime
coordinates and transitions?
#+end_quote

A plan/protocol compatibility identity and an exact executable digest therefore serve
different purposes. Evidence tied to exact emitted coordinates must not be
attached to a merely compatible artifact as though exact correspondence held.
Conversely, a compatible continuation need not be rejected solely because
diagnostic-only material changed, if the declared compatibility contract permits
it and any exact correspondence claim remains accurate.

*** Admitted assembly generation and deployment identity

An admitted assembly generation identifies the immutable application realization
accepted for use under the construction contract in Part VII. Its bindings
include the relevant routes, prepared server operations, providers, rendering
rules, executable plans, and artifact correspondence.

Deployment identity identifies a physical rollout or placement, if the
implementation needs that distinction. One deployment may host more than one
admitted assembly generation during a supported transition. Moving an admitted
realization between hosts does not, by itself, change its semantic identity or
establish a new compatibility relation.

Neither identity substitutes for command, execution, or resource-instance
identity. Two concurrent executions using the same assembly generation still
have separate ownership. Replacing a resource inside one execution need not
change that execution's plan or the application's assembly generation.

*** Transport-request identity

A transport-request identity identifies one transport attempt. In browser/runtime
examples its canonical field name is =:transport-request-id=. A new retry attempt
has its own transport identity even when the command identity is retained under
the operation's retry contract. The execution-to-attempt relationship is declared
by the supported realization rather than inferred from shared field values.

HumanHelp's domain =:request-id= identifies a Request being acted upon. It does
not identify an HTTP request. Domain arguments and route parameters retain that
meaning; they must not be renamed as transport identities.

Event correlation identifies a candidate owner/attempt. Possessing a matching
transport-request identifier alone does not establish semantic admission,
current ownership, authorization, or command outcome.

*** Submission attempts and scoped capabilities

A submission attempt is one attempt by an authoritative realization to
incorporate a command. Several attempts may correspond to one command, and an
HTTP request may cause zero, one, or several attempts according to its declared
retry contract. An attempt's rejection does not necessarily reject the command
if another compatible attempt incorporated it.

A capability instance grants a scoped operation under an issuer, subject,
principal or delegation binding, applicability period, and use policy. A
one-shot writer capability can prevent repeated use of that grant without
preventing a fresh grant for the same command. Durable command deduplication,
capability consumption, and transport correlation are separate contracts.

Capabilities used by asynchronous work have explicit owners and lifetimes.
Transfer to a background execution must be admitted; merely retaining a
request-local value after its request ends does not extend its authority.

*** Logical target identity

A logical UI target may survive replacement of its physical DOM node.

For example, a Request card may remain the same logical fragment after HTMX
replaces the element representing it.

The runtime must not use physical-node identity as the sole semantic target
identity when representation replacement is expected. A target also has an
applicable actor, principal, projection, and ownership context. The same CSS id
after navigation or account switching does not imply the same semantic target.
Changing a query or projection meaning creates a new scope or requires an
explicit compatibility transition.

** Resource instance identity
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-resource-instance-identity
:END:

A replaceable physical or runtime resource must be distinguishable from prior
instances of the same logical resource when events from those prior instances
could still arrive.

For example:

| Event | Current instance for logical slot S | Permitted consequence |
|-------+-------------------------------------+-----------------------|
| Resource created | R1 | R1 may act within its admitted ownership |
| Resource replaced | R2 | R1's rights over the replacement are retired |
| Callback from R1 arrives | R2 | Reject mutation of R2-owned state through R1's retired ownership |

Resource instance identity answers a narrow question:

#+begin_quote
Does this event belong to the currently admitted instance of this replaceable
resource?
#+end_quote

A resource-instance generation counter, scoped epoch, opaque token, UUID,
object identity, or another suitable mechanism may represent resource-instance
identity. The representation must provide the properties required by the boundary;
additional ordering or structure is unnecessary unless the contract uses it.

A resource-instance generation counter is appropriate when the contract uses
monotonic ordering between successive instances within that resource scope.
A distinct opaque identity is sufficient when the only
required question is whether an event belongs to the current instance.

The distinction must remain valid for as long as callbacks from an earlier
instance can still reach the boundary. Reusing a token, resetting a counter, or
wrapping a resource-instance counter must not make a retired instance indistinguishable
from its replacement. A reset is safe only when a separate scope or lifecycle
boundary prevents old events from being admitted as current.

Resource instance identity must not be used as a compressed representation of
independent semantic facts.

An event may be inadmissible because it belongs to a retired resource instance,
but it may also be inadmissible because its command, execution, plan, artifact,
authoritative basis, ownership, principal, authorization context, or another
required semantic correspondence no longer matches the current operation.

Those distinctions remain separate even when several of them happen to change
at the same time.

For example:

| Coordinate | Before replacement | After replacement |
|------------+--------------------+-------------------|
| Continuity slot | S | S |
| Resource instance | R7 | R8 |
| Execution | X12 | X12 |
| Authoritative basis | B40 | B40 |

A late callback carrying R7 is rejected because that resource instance is
retired, even though the execution and basis still match.

In this case the execution and authoritative basis may still correspond. The
callback is rejected specifically because it belongs to the replaced physical
resource instance.

Conversely:

| Coordinate | Before authoritative advance | After authoritative advance |
|------------+------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| Continuity slot | S | S |
| Resource instance | R8 | R8 |
| Execution | X12 | X12 |
| Authoritative basis | B40 | B41 |

The provider justifies the basis relation. An advance of authoritative state
does not by itself replace the physical resource.

The resource instance has not changed, but an operation that requires basis B41
must not treat possession of R8 as evidence that B40 remains admissible.

This separation prevents a resource-instance counter, epoch, or opaque identity
from becoming a universal proxy for semantic freshness. A numerical comparison
is meaningful only within the declared counter scope and ordering contract;
identities from different resource or execution scopes cannot be ordered merely
because both happen to be integers.

a distinct boundary. Conceptually it can consume the correlated completion
only after the rest of the acquisition contract has also been established:

#+begin_src clojure
(defn admit-acquisition-completion
  [trusted-context runtime event]
  (let [{:keys [result] :as owner}
        (correlate-acquisition-owner runtime event)]
    (if (not= result :owner-correlated)
      owner

        {:trusted-context trusted-context
         :runtime runtime
         :event event

           trusted-context runtime event)}))))
#+end_src

The resolved contract determines the mandatory checks. These include the
expected acquisition plan, authorized principal context, source provenance,
observation scope, compatible progression, current resource owner, and permitted
installation right. Event fields and arbitrary callers cannot remove a check.
Owner correlation alone cannot make received HTML semantically admissible for
installation. Content escaping, active-content policy, and DOM effect safety
remain additional realization obligations; provenance is not sanitization.

The final state transition checks the latest owner and applicability, rather
than relying on a correlation result cached before an asynchronous gap. If
physical installation happens later, its effect carries the admitted owner
binding and the shell rechecks the required installation right at actuation.
An allowed installation is not automatically a completed installation. A
synchronous implementation may combine them under its correspondence contract;
otherwise completion or failure is admitted as a separate event.

Notice what the owner guard does *not* infer. It does not claim that B42 is
universally newer than every basis in the application. It does not prove that
the returned HTML is authorized for some other user. It does not establish that
the command which caused an invalidation succeeded. It establishes only that
this completion belongs to the currently selected resource instance and
execution.  The later admission boundary establishes the additional premises
required before downstream code may treat the completion as an admitted
authoritative acquisition result.

** Required correspondence checks are semantic gates
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-owner-relative-event-admission
:END:

At every runtime boundary, an event must satisfy the particular identity and
correspondence conditions required by that boundary.

Depending on the operation, those conditions may include:

- current resource instance identity;
- command identity;
- execution identity;
- plan or artifact identity;
- authoritative basis;
- ownership;
- authenticated principal;
- current authorization;
- continuity or provisional ownership state.

A boundary need not check every category. It must check the categories on which
its correctness actually depends.

These checks are not merely debug assertions. They determine whether a runtime
event is semantically admissible.

The design therefore does not require one universal generation value. It
requires independent semantic distinctions to remain independently
representable and requires each runtime event to satisfy the correspondence
conditions appropriate to the operation it is attempting to affect.

*** Admission follows the declared semantic owner

Every asynchronous event capable of influencing Gesso semantic state passes
the common semantic admission boundary. The boundary first resolves the event's
declared class and semantic owner, then applies the correspondences required
by that class.

Conceptually:

1. Resolve the declared event class and owner from the registered producer and
   admitted contract.
2. Check owner-specific correlation and current applicability.
3. Check that the proposed semantic transition is permitted.
4. Admit that transition, or classify the rejection and any recovery path.

The owner is not universally a command execution.

| <27> | <73> |
| Event class | Relevant owner and checks |
|-------------+---------------------------|
| Execution-private callback | Admitted execution, current resource instance, ownership, correlation, and permitted execution state |
| Shared fragment advisory | Declared subscription or fragment coordination scope, admitted source/correlation, current source instance where required, and valid advisory requirement |
| Authoritative acquisition completion | Admitted acquisition owner, request/resource correlation, returned observation scope and progression, and applicable installation rules |
| Continuity completion | Declared continuity owner or slot, current resource instance, and restoration rights still held for the target |

This table identifies distinctions rather than prescribing one public event
record or a closed enumeration of all future events. Each supported event class
must define its own required correspondence contract.

An idle board can therefore admit a legitimate shared advisory without an
unrelated command execution. A shared event must not be assigned to whichever
execution happens to be current. An execution-private callback must not become
shared merely because its execution was retired.

The declaration of an event as shared is part of the checked contract. An
untrusted field such as =:shared? true= cannot select weaker admission rules.
Shared admission permits only the declared shared transition; it does not
restore a retired execution's private ownership or authorize installation of
domain content carried by an advisory.

The browser-realization part defines the concrete normalization, transition,
and effect contracts implementing this boundary. All supported paths obey this
owner-relative rule; there is no alternate callback path that may mutate
semantic state while claiming that admission was enforced.

The event's class is resolved from its registered producer and admitted
contract, not selected by an untrusted =:event/class= field alone. Event payloads
may carry correlation values; the callback/resource registration supplies the
corresponding expected owner. Final applicability is checked against current
state. Checking identity early and mutating a replacement owner later would
leave a race.

Retirement removes the retired owner's semantic rights immediately, even if
physical cancellation or cleanup is incomplete. Late command-outcome evidence
may still be useful through a separately declared reconciliation path. Such a
path does not revive the old execution or authorize its stale presentation
effect.

These browser-runtime claims concern the admitted, cooperating implementation.
They are not a server-verifiable proof that an adversarial browser ran those
checks. The authoritative server boundary independently enforces the security
properties stated below.


** Trust boundaries and adversarial browser actors
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-trust-boundary
:END:

A browser is an untrusted participant.

This statement is stronger than saying browser input should be validated.

The user controls the machine and may modify:

#+begin_example
gesso-live.js
projected choreography data
local machine state
DOM
hidden fields
command envelopes
role names
principal ids
observed basis values
provenance labels
provisional/authoritative markers
available buttons
bound capabilities
diagnostic/proof sidecars
runtime correlation fields
#+end_example

Therefore no browser-originated value may confer trusted authority merely
because it has the expected representation.

*** What the browser may propose

A browser may propose values such as:

- a semantic operation;
- operation arguments;
- a semantic command identity;
- an observed-basis claim used as protocol context;
- correlation information whose validity is checked independently;
- provisional local state affecting only its own representation;
- a projected-plan identity whose compatibility is checked by trusted code;
- transport data required to correlate a request with a server-side operation.

Proposal is not authority.

*** What the browser may not establish by assertion

The browser may not establish by assertion:

- authenticated principal identity;
- entitlement to a choreography role;
- model authority;
- authorization to operate on a protected target;
- authoritative provenance;
- an authoritative fact;
- a privileged server-machine state;
- permission to read protected data;
- permission to subscribe to protected Live state;
- the truth of a proof certificate;
- that a stale execution is current;
- that a provisional projection is authoritative;
- that an observed-basis claim proves prior observation.

Trusted server code must derive or check those propositions through trusted
boundaries.

*** Trusted authoritative execution

For each protected authoritative operation, the supported trusted path must
establish the obligations relevant to the branch it actually executes:

1. Authenticate the concrete caller from trusted context and resolve the
   operation through the admitted application binding.
2. Validate the command envelope, identity binding, arguments, and compatible
   operation interface. Client fields do not supply privileged continuation
   state or current authority.
3. Handle a compatible recorded command under its current disclosure policy,
   without imposing a new-incorporation guard on historical success.
4. For new incorporation, acquire the required facts and atomically protect
   current authorization, relationships, guard dependencies, and domain effects
   together with the command-result record.
5. Account for concurrent compatible incorporation when interpreting a failed
   attempt, and preserve uncertainty when authoritative evidence is insufficient.
6. Produce only the permitted typed outcomes and scoped progression evidence,
   with disclosure and recovery rules appropriate to the current caller.

Preparation checks are protected or re-established at incorporation wherever
intervening changes could invalidate the decision. Browser freshness does not
supply that protection. Read-only operations and subscriptions follow their
own acquisition/disclosure contracts rather than manufacturing a write protocol.

The server must never treat a client-supplied machine continuation such as:

#+begin_src clojure
{:next-state :request-authority/commit-admin-operation}
#+end_src

as evidence that the client is entitled to reach that state.

Instead the browser requests a semantic operation, conceptually:

#+begin_src clojure
{:gesso/command-id C42
 :operation :request/claim
 :arguments {:request/id R}
 :observed-basis X17}
#+end_src

and trusted server semantics decide what operation and continuation are
permitted.

*** Security boundary

A malicious browser can always lie to itself.

It may render false information, suppress buttons, manufacture buttons, change
labels, or locally pretend an operation succeeded.

Preventing a user from corrupting their own local representation is not the
security target.

The security target is:

#+begin_quote
Compromising or replacing a browser projection may corrupt that browser's local
representation and protocol participation, but must not increase the authority
of its authenticated principal, disclose data unavailable to that principal, or
produce an authoritative model transition unavailable to that principal.
#+end_quote

This is a verification target, not merely an application coding convention.

*** Read and subscription authorization

Hiding content in a view is not access control.

Not rendering a button is not access control.

Not advertising a Live interest is not access control.

Every protected authoritative observation and subscription must be authorized
from trusted server context.

An opaque or signed token may protect integrity, correlation, or accidental
mutation.

Possession of such a token is not automatically authorization unless an
explicit capability design says that possession has that semantic meaning and
its applicability and validity conditions are satisfied.

The protected-observation and subscription contracts must state when
authorization is checked and what later permission changes imply for subsequent
disclosure. Initial subscription admission must not silently become an
indefinite permission grant. The protocol may use revalidation, bounded grants,
revocation, or another explicit policy consistent with the claimed protection.
No such policy can make an adversarial browser forget data already disclosed.
Invalidation metadata, counts, existence hints, and command-outcome responses
can themselves disclose protected information and need an appropriate scope.
A hidden card does not make an otherwise revealing subscription harmless.

*** Proof artifacts are untrusted in the browser

A proof sidecar or diagnostic certificate shipped to a browser cannot become a
security capability.

An adversarial browser may fabricate, modify, remove, or replace it.

Authoritative security therefore depends on trusted server-side state and
checked protocol boundaries, not the browser's possession of proof metadata.

This follows the general rule:

#+begin_example
evidence about why code was accepted
!=
runtime authority to perform operation
#+end_example

*** Trusted host code and accidental-misuse prevention

The adversarial-browser boundary must not be confused with a claim that Gesso
sandboxes arbitrary Clojure code executing inside a trusted application
process.

Code that deliberately obtains a lower-level transaction capability, database
handle, model implementation namespace, or another authority-bearing resource
may be able to bypass the supported Gesso/model path.  If such code can mutate
the same authoritative state directly, it belongs to the trusted computing
base for every guarantee that depends on those model invariants unless a
stronger isolation boundary prevents the bypass.

Within that trusted host-language boundary, Gesso is primarily concerned with
preventing *accidental semantic misuse* by cooperating application code.  The
normal path should make incorrect substitutions, missing derivations, stale
artifacts, undeclared authority paths, and accidental bypasses difficult to
express and easy to diagnose.

The supported paths have these obligations:

| Path | Required treatment |
|------+--------------------|
| Ordinary supported code, including opaque Clojure | Narrow semantic/model interfaces, checked construction, explicit premises, and admitted authoritative operations |
| Accidental mismatch | Early, specific rejection at the boundary that can establish the mismatch |
| Deliberate lower-level write | Explicit writer coverage or an environment assumption; reassess every dependent invariant and later guarantee affected by the write |

Checked managed construction is the ordinary path, including opaque Clojure
behind declared contracts. The assembly supplies scoped effect capabilities and
binds their realization to admitted operations. View and handler construction
does not ordinarily receive raw transaction authority simply to compute a value.
The whole-application contract specifies the actual enforcement and remaining
host-process assumptions.

A deliberate lower-level writer may affect every dependent invariant over the
state it changes, including guarantees used by later operations. Its impact is
not confined to the assurance of the single bypassing invocation. Initializers,
migrations, jobs, and external writers therefore participate in the declared
writer coverage or remain explicit environment assumptions.

These mechanisms are valuable even though a sufficiently determined trusted
application author can rewrite the program or deliberately acquire a lower-level
handle.  The target is not to make trusted code physically incapable of
cheating.  The target is to make the coherent path obvious, accidental misuse
hard, and deliberate escape from the claimed boundary conspicuous.

** Knowledge
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-knowledge-provenance
:END:

Knowledge is tracked with respect to actors or projected roles. In this
specification, a knowledge judgment records what an admitted execution is
justified in using under its premises. It is not an assertion that every
browser-held proposition is true, that an adversarial participant ran the
machine, or that a human understood the representation.

Knowing an input value and knowing a domain proposition about that value are
different judgments. A server may know that a browser submitted Request id R
without knowing that R exists, that its status is open, or that the caller may
act on it. Similarly, knowing that a client asserts F does not establish F.

A fact or value may become known through several distinct provenance paths.

At minimum the semantic model recognizes:

- =:input=;
- =:communicated=;
- =:authoritative=;
- =:derived=;
- =:asserted=.

These labels describe justification, not merely transport. Their appearance in
an incoming map does not establish the corresponding provenance. A knowledge
judgment includes the subject and scope of the fact and the premises on which
the permitted use depends; a provenance label alone is not the whole judgment.

The important verifier question is not:

#+begin_quote
Which packet delivered this map key?
#+end_quote

It is:

#+begin_quote
Before this actor performs behavior that depends on fact F, what justifies that
the actor knows F?
#+end_quote

*** A knowledge judgment has a subject, scope, and support

A semantic fact is more than a map key. Its judgment binds the actor or role,
the proposition and values, the subjects, applicable temporal/query context,
and the justification on which its permitted use depends. The surrounding
admitted contract may supply these bindings without repeating them in every
runtime value.

For example, an inspection view might show:

#+begin_src clojure
{:actor [:client C]
 :proposition [:request/status R :open]
 :context
 {:authority [:authority :request R]
  :projection :request/card-v1
  :arguments {:request/id R}
  :principal-context :session/helper-1
  :basis B
  :valid-at V}
 :support
 {:kind :authoritative
  :acquisition :acquisition/A17
  :premises #{:request/projection-contract
              :storage/qualified-read-semantics}}}
#+end_src

This describes the status at V under the authoritative read context B. It does
not assert perpetual openness or grant permission to claim. The map is
inspection data; admission must resolve its acquisition and premises rather
than trust caller-supplied labels.

A local key such as =:request/status= may index the fact only when the owning
context fixes which Request and observation it denotes. Reusing the key for
another subject must not merge their evidence. Equal values may have several
independent justifications, but a justification for R1 cannot strengthen R2's
claim merely because both values are =:open=.

Support is dependency-sensitive. A derived judgment retains the contracts,
assertions, and scoped facts used to justify it. Replacement or correction of a
current observation changes which dependent current claims are applicable;
it does not retroactively erase the event that an older value was observed.
A failed or withdrawn assumption makes dependent unconditional use unavailable,
while a conditional theorem may remain valid under its stated premise.

The implementation need not retain an unbounded runtime proof history. It may
keep sufficient bindings, compact evidence, or a reference to recheckable
sources. Eviction and retirement follow explicit scope/lifetime rules; loss of
evidence must not be interpreted as successful discharge.

*** Input knowledge

Input knowledge consists of the values and propositions supplied under the
operation's entry contract. The contract determines whether the actor knows
only the supplied value, knows that another participant asserted a proposition,
or may rely on the proposition itself under a named trusted premise.

Conceptually:

| Premise | Meaning |
|---------+---------|
| A receives an input under the admitted entry contract | The input's subject, type, and context are bound |
| The entry contract justifies the proposition being used | It distinguishes a supplied value, a reported assertion, and a trusted premise |
| Conclusion | A may use that scoped input knowledge with =:input= provenance and the contract's retained premises |

Input knowledge is not equivalent to arbitrary function arguments.

The operation contract must say which incoming facts are accepted as justified
entry knowledge and under what assumptions.

For example, a trusted server may supply an authenticated principal as input
knowledge to a server projection.

A browser cannot create the same trusted input merely by serializing an
identical principal id.

*** Communicated knowledge

Communication may establish knowledge constructively.

Conceptually:

| Premise | Meaning |
|---------+---------|
| A's send is justified under the message contract | The sent value and permitted semantic claims are established or explicitly assumed |
| B admits the matching receive | Sender, execution, payload binding, and required validation satisfy that contract |
| Conclusion | B gains the declared knowledge of X with =:communicated= provenance and the retained message premises |

An admitted choreography must justify the sender's knowledge of the value it
transmits. At an adversarial transport boundary, a message's receipt does not
prove that the sender satisfied that semantic premise; trusted validation or
the explicitly stated assumption must supply the justification being claimed.

The receiver gains only the values and semantic consequences declared by the
message contract. An authenticated sender can still communicate a mistaken
claim; sender authenticity and the truth of that claim are separate premises.
A delegated authoritative statement requires the declared issuer and validation
contract, not merely a familiar sender name.

Arbitrary extra map keys must not silently become choreography context.

Communication establishes the fact that the declared value was communicated in
that execution; it does not automatically establish that the value is
authoritative domain truth.

*** Authoritative knowledge

An actor may acquire knowledge by observing a representation justified by
authority.

Conceptually:

| Premise | Meaning |
|---------+---------|
| Authority justifies F | The proposition has a declared subject, time, and authority scope |
| B acquires an admitted projection establishing F | The read, disclosure, projection, and basis contracts hold |
| Conclusion | B may use F with =:authoritative= provenance at that observation context |

The physical representation may be:

- structured data;
- server-rendered HTML;
- another checked projection;
- an application-specific authoritative representation.

The representation format does not weaken the provenance if the trusted path
from authority to observation is valid.

*** Derived knowledge

A role may derive a proposition from facts it already knows through a declared
semantic rule. For a deterministic functional derivation, the justification
includes the exact input bindings and the function contract on which the
result depends.

| Required premise | Meaning |
|------------------+---------|
| Inputs available | The role has the required scoped values or propositions |
| Rule applicable | Its types, subjects, refinements, and other preconditions hold |
| Result justified | The supported evaluator/derivation establishes the result, or the admitted opaque contract remains an explicit premise |
| Dependencies retained | The conclusion preserves the assumptions and evidence scope used by the rule |

A dependency inventory is not itself a derivation. Recording that F depends on
X and Y, or accepting a caller-supplied result with the right shape, does not
establish that F follows from X and Y. A provenance constructor may record this
description, but a stronger checked judgment needs its own mechanism.

Ordinary Clojure remains available. If its functional behavior is opaque, the
semantic surface names the contract, implementation binding, and accepted
assertions. Input/output checks can establish instance-specific properties
without claiming universal correctness, purity, determinism, or termination.

An effectful or nondeterministic computation can establish knowledge of its
particular observed result through its declared effect/result boundary. It does
not become a deterministic derivation from inputs that omit the clock, random
choice, external read, or mutable state it used. Later reasoning may use that
observed result as an explicitly bound input.

The system must not infer equal results across actors from identical function
names or source text. Cross-actor derived agreement needs compatible semantic
inputs, the required deterministic contract, and correspondence of the
realizations to that contract. JVM/browser arithmetic, locale, time, and other
environmental semantics must be included where relevant.

Canonicalization and structural evidence checking must not acquire hidden
runtime effects by evaluating arbitrary application callbacks. Where execution
is part of an evidence mechanism, that execution has an explicit trusted
boundary, resource contract, and result scope.

Deriving a value from a client assertion does not make it authoritative. An
inference can strengthen a conclusion only when its rule and actual premises
justify that strengthening. Provenance labels are descriptions of support,
not a total ranking in which adding =:derived= upgrades every earlier premise.

*** Asserted knowledge

An explicitly accepted assertion supplies a premise that the verifier does
not derive. It is an ordinary supported mechanism for full-language and
external-system contracts, subject to the
[[#req-admission-assurance-policy][admission policy from Part I]].

Its identity binds the exact proposition, subject, parameters, scope, owner,
implementation or external boundary, and applicability conditions required by
the claim. Two assertions with the same friendly name but different subjects
or meanings must not merge. Accepting a premise is not discharging it.

An untrusted actor's assertion remains a claim by that actor unless a trusted
entry contract explicitly permits relying on its proposition. A browser cannot
register itself as the trusted owner of an authorization premise by sending an
=:asserted= tag or a familiar assertion id.

The assertion remains visible in every dependent explanation. It must not be
relabeled as a proved derivation, a successful authoritative observation, or a
runtime-enforced current premise merely because its result matches another
value. Contradiction, expiry, or loss of applicability propagates to dependent
uses according to their scope. Independent guarantees remain available when
they do not depend on the affected premise.

*** Knowledge use

Knowledge determines whether a role may, depending on the operation:

- transmit a value;
- consume a value;
- use a value as an FX input;
- correlate a message;
- choose a branch;
- rely on a discriminator when its projected behavior diverges;
- derive another value;
- construct provisional state;
- invoke an operation whose entry contract requires the fact;
- claim agreement with another role;
- establish a checked rule precondition.

A role must not consume facts it cannot justifiably know.

*** Knowledge is semantic, not incidental context

The presence of a key in a runtime map is not evidence of knowledge.

For example:

#+begin_src clojure
{:user/id U
 :request/status :open}
#+end_src

does not answer:

- who knows those values;
- why they know them;
- whether they are authoritative;
- at what basis they were observed;
- whether they may be transmitted;
- whether they may justify a branch;
- whether they are merely local provisional data.

The semantic machinery must not infer provenance from incidental map
membership.

*** Knowledge and privacy

Knowledge is scoped.

Correctness does not require every actor to learn every fact.

Privacy, authorization, role relevance, and protocol structure may deliberately
prevent knowledge propagation.

A proof that "eventually everybody knows everything" would be wrong for many
applications.

The useful property is:

#+begin_quote
Every admitted actor behavior has the scoped knowledge and premises its
contract requires, with the claimed degree of justification made explicit.
#+end_quote

Permission to disclose, retain, use, or forward a fact is separate from the
historical fact that it was acquired. Revocation cannot make an adversarial
recipient forget prior disclosure, but it can remove permission for a later
read, subscription, operation, or transmission. Knowledge alone is not a
capability.

*** Knowledge derivations as inspectable evidence

Where knowledge is machine checked, the checker should retain enough derivation
information to explain why a fact is known.

A boolean such as:

#+begin_src clojure
{:knows? true}
#+end_src

is insufficient as the long-term proof product.

The derivation should be able to expose leaves such as:

- input;
- communication;
- authoritative observation;
- deterministic derivation;
- explicit assertion.

These derivations belong to verifier/checker artifacts. Runtime state retains
the identities and premises actually needed for execution, not necessarily the
entire explanatory graph.

An observed adapter transition may supply a diagnostic account of a role-local
step, requested transport, provisional display, or authoritative installation.
It must be derived from the actual semantic transition rather than a fabricated
successor supplied by a caller. It is still not, by itself, evidence of a
physical network delivery, a database commit, or universal host correspondence.
The observation category determines which stronger claim could be supported.

** Authoritative observation as a knowledge path
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-authoritative-observation
:END:

Authoritative observation is a first-class semantic provenance path even when
it is not represented as a first-class portable machine opcode.

This distinction is important for ordinary web applications.

A HumanHelp browser may learn facts such as:

#+begin_example
Request R is done
Request R is claimed
Membership U is inactive
Organization O is closed
#+end_example

by rereading an authoritative representation after Live indicates that relevant
authority may have changed.

That knowledge is not weaker merely because it arrived as rendered HTML rather
than as a dedicated choreography participant message.

The authority remains in the model/database.

The HTML is one representation from which the admitted actor may acquire the
fact under the declared projection and normalization contract. Arbitrary DOM
text, an undeclared data attribute, or a locally fabricated status does not
become semantic knowledge by looking like the server's rendering.

*** Advisory invalidation versus authoritative observation

The normal causal relationship, when the application policy permits the
acquisition, is:

1. A model operation incorporates an authoritative mutation under its storage
   contract.
2. Live supplies an admitted, scoped advisory about potentially relevant change.
3. Application policy or user action supplies a permitted acquisition trigger.
4. An authorized read satisfies the applicable scope and progression requirements.
5. The actor acquires the justified observation. Adoption and installation follow
   their declared policy rather than being implied by notification receipt.

A Live invalidation means approximately:

#+begin_quote
Relevant authority may have changed; a sufficiently current authoritative
observation may be required for later behavior or offered for explicit adoption.
#+end_quote

It does not mean:

#+begin_quote
This particular domain fact is now true.
#+end_quote

Therefore invalidation is advisory knowledge about possible change, not
authoritative knowledge of the changed fact.

Receipt of the advisory does not by itself choose when the application adopts
new content. Acquisition may follow an explicit user action, a declared
automatic policy, or another admitted trigger. Any convergence claim that relies
on such a trigger must name it among its liveness premises.

*** Observation scope and selective incorporation

An authoritative observation establishes only the facts and coverage justified
by its acquisition contract. Its semantic context includes the authority,
subject or projection scope, relevant basis/progression, and applicable temporal
or query meaning. Filters, search terms, ordering and pagination where they
affect coverage, projection version, and principal-specific visibility may all
be part of that meaning. These need not all be repeated as fields when they are
unambiguously supplied by the admitted contract.

A later observation of one Request does not establish acquisition of all board
membership at the same database progression. An actor may hold individually
justified observations from different acquisitions without possessing a single
consistent snapshot of their union.

Consequently, installation of new authoritative content must preserve each
observation's justified scope. Claims spanning multiple observations require
the compatibility or consistent-acquisition premise appropriate to that claim.
The mere existence of separate authoritative observations is insufficient.

The authoritative-operations and progression part owns the detailed
[[#req-progression-scope][scope and progression contract]] and
[[#req-advisory-discharge][advisory discharge rules]]. Browser installation follows
[[#req-runtime-scoped-authoritative-installation][the scoped installation contract]].
HumanHelp's selective-adoption policy is a use of those generic contracts.

An observation contract distinguishes returned rows from coverage of the query.
A complete authorized board result may establish membership or absence within
that projection. A page, search subset, or single-card response does not
establish membership of the entire board. Ordering and cursor context matter
when they determine which portion was returned.

| Result | Permitted semantic conclusion |
|--------+------------------------------|
| Complete authorized scoped result | Returned facts and the stated coverage/absence conclusions at its read context |
| Partial or paged result | Facts in the returned scope; completeness only for explicitly established coverage |
| Authoritative not-found | Absence within the declared subject, visibility, temporal, and query contract |
| Denied or privacy-masked response | Only the permitted denial/opaque result; no stronger existence conclusion |
| Timeout, disconnect, or unavailable read | No successful observation of the missing result |

Two different values at the same raw basis are not automatically an authority
contradiction. First establish that the authority, subject, projection version,
query parameters, temporal context, visibility context, and deterministic
interpretation are the same. Different valid times or projections can produce
different correct answers at one database basis. If the complete meaning is
the same and incompatible facts remain, treat that as contradictory evidence;
do not resolve it by last-arrival-wins.

An incomparable observation can remain valid evidence in its own scope without
being eligible to replace the currently adopted projection. Replacement needs
the declared supersession, progression, or reconciliation relation. Equal
displayed values do not by themselves advance a knowledge or coverage frontier.

*** Acquisition, installation, and semantic adoption

Acquisition produces an observation under its declared source and read
contract. Installation applies a representation to a concrete target.
Adoption selects the observation or projection on which subsequent behavior
relies. The contract states when these transitions coincide and when they are
separate.

An authorized response may arrive without being installed because its owner
was retired, the target changed, or a newer compatible result was already
adopted. Its evidence may be usable through another declared admission path,
but it must not mutate a replacement owner merely because it is authoritative.

Scheduling an installation or emitting an allow-swap decision is not proof that
the representation was installed. A synchronous realization may establish both
within one supported boundary; an asynchronous realization needs a completion
or failure contract. The adopted frontier and advisory discharge must reflect
the transition they claim, including the actual permitted installation when
adoption requires it. Part VI owns the concrete shell/refinement relation.

An advisory received while an acquisition or installation is pending must be
evaluated against the resulting observation's actual coverage. It cannot be
discarded merely because it arrived before a callback or because the acquisition
was called Refresh.


*** Observed basis is protocol context, not proof of prior observation
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-observation-evidence
:END:

A naked browser-supplied =:observed-basis= value is a client claim about the
context from which the browser believes it is acting.  It is not, by itself,
proof that the browser actually received or incorporated an authoritative
observation at that basis.

For ordinary Gesso commands this is intentional.  The basis is useful protocol
context for consistency, reconciliation, provenance, stale-command handling,
and authoritative reacquisition even when the browser is adversarial.

For example, a browser may say:

#+begin_src clojure
{:request-id R
 :observed-basis B}
#+end_src

Here =R= is the domain Request identity, not a transport-request identity.
The server may safely use =B= to understand the command's claimed starting
context without treating =B= as permission:

| Validated comparison or context | Permitted treatment |
|---------------------------------+---------------------|
| B is compatible or sufficiently current for the declared use | Continue under the ordinary current model guards |
| B is stale or incompatible for that use | Follow the declared reconciliation, rejection, or reacquisition path |
| B is invalid, unsupported, or lacks required comparison evidence | Report or handle that condition explicitly; do not invent an ordering or treat B as authority |

Current authenticated principal binding, model authorization, authoritative
state, and transaction guards remain decisive. A client may lie about =B=
without thereby gaining authority.

The provider still validates the token's representation and applicable
authority/database context. A client cannot select a different backend, expand
query scope, or demand an unbounded wait by supplying a fabricated future basis.
Supported bounds and failure responses are part of the acquisition contract;
failure to satisfy a required basis is reported rather than silently dropped.

The trust requirement changes if an application gives *prior observation
itself* security significance.  Examples include:

- an operation is allowed only after the principal was previously issued a
  particular authoritative representation;
- a continuation is valid only when it follows a specific server-issued
  observation;
- an acknowledgement must refer to an exact previously issued semantic
  version;
- an application distinguishes "client claims to have seen X" from
  "the server can verify that it issued X to this principal/session."

In that case a forgeable client field is insufficient.  The application needs
an explicit server-verifiable mechanism such as:

- a server-issued capability;
- a MAC-authenticated observation receipt;
- trusted server-side session state;
- a signed server attestation;
- a nonce or continuation identifier bound to a prior response;
- another mechanism whose trust semantics are stated explicitly.

The framework must never silently promote:

#+begin_example
ClientClaim<ObservedBasis>
#+end_example

into:

#+begin_example
VerifiedObservationEvidence
#+end_example

merely because the value has the expected shape or happens to equal a real
basis.

A Message Authentication Code (MAC), for example HMAC-SHA-256, is one possible
way to let an adversarial browser carry integrity-protected server evidence,
under the named cryptographic, key-management, encoding, and verification
assumptions. This example specifies the trust relationship rather than a
complete cryptographic implementation. The server authenticates a canonical
payload using a secret key known only to trusted infrastructure:

#+begin_example
receipt-payload
    =
{:receipt/type :gesso/observation-issuance
 :receipt/version 1
 :issuer :humanhelp/observation-service
 :audience :humanhelp/request-flow
 :key-id K
 :principal [:user P]
 :authority [:authority :request R]
 :projection :request/public-row-v1
 :scope S
 :basis B
 :temporal-context T
 :semantic-digest D
 :purpose :prior-observation-issuance
 :issued-event :authorized-response-constructed
 :issued-at I
 :expires-at E}

mac
    =
HMAC(server-secret, canonical-encoding(receipt-payload))
#+end_example

The browser may read, store, copy, and return the payload and MAC. Under the
declared integrity assumptions it cannot feasibly change =P=, =R=, =B=,
=purpose=, or another authenticated field and produce accepted replacement
evidence. The MAC does not encrypt the payload; receipt contents must therefore
be suitable for disclosure to the recipient. Scope S and temporal context T
include the query, visibility, and provider/basis context required by the
projection contract; they are not inferred from the token's familiar shape.
The digest D identifies the canonical semantic observation material, not
arbitrary HTML bytes or the truth of the statement by itself.

On receipt, the server recomputes and verifies the MAC and then checks the
context in which the receipt is being used:

1. Parse the untrusted envelope under bounded, supported encoding rules.
2. Resolve the admitted receipt contract and its trusted verification key.
3. Verify the MAC over the exact canonical payload.
4. Check the required identity, scope, context, purpose, and lifecycle bindings.
5. Admit only the historical issuance proposition justified by those checks.

The verifier chooses the admitted receipt contract, trusted issuer/key mapping,
canonical encoding, and verification mechanism. Client-supplied fields do not
select an arbitrary algorithm or trusted issuer. Version, type, audience,
principal or session binding, authority, subject/scope, projection, purpose,
expiry, and semantic-material binding are checked as required by that contract.
Key rotation, clock interpretation, and revocation/expiry behavior belong to the
declared receipt lifecycle. Validation failure grants no stronger premise.

The MAC authenticates the statement represented by the payload; it does not
make an unjustified statement true.  If the server authenticates a bad or
overstated proposition, the MAC faithfully preserves that mistake.

In particular, a receipt normally proves something like:

#+begin_quote
Trusted server infrastructure issued an attestation for principal/session P
about the declared semantic observation of authority A at basis B, under the
receipt's stated issuance contract.
#+end_quote

The issuance contract must identify the event actually attested: for example,
construction of an authorized response or release of a response to a transport.
Issuance is not proof of successful delivery, browser installation, retention,
or human acknowledgement. Returning the receipt proves possession of the
authenticated bytes, not those stronger events by itself.

It does not by itself prove:

#+begin_quote
The browser installed the observation, or the human read, understood, or
consciously incorporated it.
#+end_quote

If human acknowledgement is semantically relevant, that is a stronger protocol
and needs its own explicit proposition and evidence.

Observation evidence must also remain distinct from an operation capability.
A valid authenticated receipt concerning an old authoritative observation may
remain historically valid even after current authority changes:

#+begin_example
valid observation receipt
!=
current authorization
#+end_example

For example, another helper may have claimed Request R after the receipt was
issued.  The receipt can still prove that an earlier observation was issued,
while current authoritative reread and model guards correctly reject a new
claim.

Gesso should therefore distinguish at least these concepts:

| Concept | Semantic meaning |
|---------+------------------|
| ObservedBasisClaim | Client-supplied, forgeable consistency or reconciliation context |
| AuthenticatedObservationReceipt | Server-authenticated evidence of the stated prior issuance event |
| CurrentAuthoritativeObservation | Trusted acquisition satisfying its scope and read contract; current relative to that acquisition |
| OperationCapability | Scoped permission under an explicit grant and its validity conditions |
| CurrentAuthorization | A current trusted decision permitting the concrete principal's operation under the authoritative policy |

None should implicitly coerce into another.  In particular, historical
observation evidence and possession of an operation capability do not silently
become current authorization unless the declared capability semantics explicitly
make possession sufficient and all of that capability's current validity
conditions are satisfied.

Gesso may emit compact authenticated observation receipts alongside
authoritative acquisitions when a supported protocol benefits from evidence of
prior issuance. Routine issuance is an implementation option, not a baseline
requirement justified solely by the cost of computing a MAC. Its key lifecycle,
payload disclosure, expiry, and replay contract must be explicit. The
authenticated object should be the *semantic observation* or a compact digest/identity of it, not
arbitrary HTTP response bytes.

The receipt contract must identify exactly what its semantic digest binds.
A digest of an untyped map without authority, projection, temporal, or query
meaning is not enough when those distinctions matter. Verifying a receipt
does not re-read the model or establish that the old facts remain current.

Even if such receipts become common, ordinary =:observed-basis= should remain a
distinct weaker protocol concept.  Requiring cryptographic evidence whenever a
client reports its starting basis would unnecessarily turn useful consistency
context into a security credential.

Replay semantics also remain explicit.  A MAC makes a token difficult to forge
or alter; it does not make the token single-use.  A reusable observation
receipt may legitimately be replayable because it attests to a historical
fact.  A one-shot capability must enforce its declared consumption or replay policy.
Expiry limits validity duration but does not by itself prevent reuse within
that duration. A nonce distinguishes issuances but does not by itself record
consumption. A consumed-token record or another trusted mechanism may enforce
single use; command identity and idempotency may instead enforce a permitted
duplicate-use outcome. Those are different contracts and must not be presented
as interchangeable guarantees.

The governing rule is:

#+begin_quote
When prior observation becomes a premise of authorization or another
security-sensitive conclusion, the mechanism establishing that premise must
cross an explicit server-verifiable trust boundary.  Client assertion remains
client assertion until that boundary is crossed.
#+end_quote

*** Observation need not imply a long-lived waiting opcode

Many authoritative observations form entry knowledge for a later short
choreography.

For example:

| Stage | Semantic consequence |
|-------+----------------------|
| Browser renders an admitted Request observation at X17 | That execution may finish with its declared adopted knowledge |
| A later Live advisory arrives | It records advisory information; it need not resume the finished execution |
| Policy or user action permits a new acquisition | An authorized read obtains an observation satisfying the new requirement, here at X24 |
| A new short choreography begins | Its entry contract binds the observation at X24 as justified input knowledge |

The portable choreography language therefore need not acquire a universal
=:observe= or durable wait operation merely to model ordinary web rereads.

If an application genuinely needs one semantic execution to suspend on an
authoritative condition and later resume, that facility should be designed
deliberately with explicit persistence, compatibility, ownership, and recovery
semantics.

It must not appear accidentally as a consequence of normal Live behavior.

*** Observation and representation independence

Authoritative observation is semantic.

Its correctness must not depend on one specific presentation format.

A browser may acquire justified knowledge from an authoritative HTML projection
when the server-rendered representation and its acquisition path establish the
required facts.

Another application may use structured data.

The important proof obligations concern:

- authority;
- authorization;
- basis/progression;
- projection semantics;
- correspondence between representation and declared fact.

They do not concern whether the bytes happened to be HTML or EDN unless the
representation mechanism itself introduces a relevant assumption.

** Agreement
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-semantic-agreement
:END:

Agreement is stronger than two actors having values with the same shape.

Agreement means the protocol can justify that the actors share the relevant
semantic value or fact under the declared relation, scope, and frontier.

This is a judgment about those actors' relevant observations or values at the
specified points. It does not automatically mean that either actor knows the
other actor received the value, that both know that both know it, or that they
continue to share the same current value after later changes. Protocols needing
acknowledgement or stronger knowledge about receipt must declare that additional
obligation.

If A communicates X to B:

The message contract justifies A's send, B admits the matching receive, and
the sent and received values are related by the contract's declared encoding
and semantic interpretation. Under those premises, the protocol may establish
=:communicated= agreement on X at those two events. It does not establish
acknowledgement, current-state equality after later changes, or domain truth
beyond what that message contract justifies.

Agreement can arise through multiple provenance paths.

At minimum:

- =:communicated=;
- =:authoritative=;
- =:derived=;
- =:asserted=.

*** Communicated agreement

Communication can establish agreement on the communicated value.

It does not imply agreement on every fact either actor knows.

Message contracts therefore matter.

Closed payloads make the agreement boundary explicit.

*** Authoritative agreement

Two actors may independently observe the same authoritative fact and obtain
justified agreement without receiving one identical network packet.

For example:

| Actor | Physical read path | Required semantic support |
|-------+--------------------+---------------------------|
| A | Web node 1 | An admitted observation supporting scoped proposition F |
| B | Web node 3 | An admitted observation supporting the same proposition F under the required compatibility relation |

The agreement judgment uses that compatibility evidence; matching host or
packet identities are unnecessary.

The hosts differ.

The packets differ.

The required agreement is on the scoped proposition being used, not necessarily
on an identical packet, exact read timestamp, or complete database snapshot.
The relation must establish that the observations support that same proposition
under compatible subject, temporal, projection, and authority contexts. Equal
progression tokens alone do not establish this; neither do equal status values
at unrelated times.

For example, two observations that a Request is open can support agreement on a
specified state/version or valid-time proposition when the authority contract
justifies the relation. They do not prove that the Request never became claimed
and reopened between those observations. Such a history claim needs its own
evidence. A generic basis advance is not evidence that every fact was unchanged.

*** Derived agreement

Agreement may propagate through a declared deterministic derivation when the
inputs and derivation justify doing so.

The system must not infer derived agreement merely because two actors happen to
run source code with the same function name.

The derivation relation must be part of the admitted semantic contract. Where
its realization is opaque, the conclusion retains the exact deterministic and
implementation assumptions used. A nondeterministic computation may support
agreement on an explicitly shared result, but independent invocations need not
produce that same result.

*** Asserted agreement

Application code may explicitly assert agreement that Gesso cannot derive.

Such assertions are named, scoped premises under the admission policy. They
remain visible in diagnostics and proof reports, including the particular
actors, proposition, relation, and frontier to which they apply.

They cannot silently suppress a mandatory failed correspondence check or be
introduced by an untrusted participant choosing an assertion id. An asserted
agreement does not become mechanically derived agreement because the two
displayed values happen to match.

*** Shape equality is not agreement

Two values can validate against the same Malli schema without being agreed
semantic facts.

For example:

#+begin_src clojure
{:request/status :open}
#+end_src

in two browser contexts does not establish:

- same Request identity;
- same authoritative basis;
- same provenance;
- same valid time;
- same semantic observation;
- same authority.

Malli answers shape questions.

Agreement answers semantic relationship questions.

*** Agreement is scoped

Actors need not agree on all application state.

Agreement may be scoped to:

- a Request;
- a fact;
- a command;
- an operation input;
- a branch discriminator;
- an authoritative frontier;
- another explicitly modeled semantic object.

Privacy and irrelevance are legitimate reasons not to establish agreement.

** Agreement frontiers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-agreement-frontiers
:END:

For evolving values, actors may agree only through a common frontier for the
relevant facts and relation. A frontier is not necessarily one globally ordered
position for all actor knowledge.

For a single modeled chain whose prefix observations are retained and justify
the claimed agreement, for example:

| Participant | Justified retained prefix of the modeled chain |
|-------------+------------------------------------------------|
| Actor A | F1, F2, F3, F4 |
| Actor B | F1, F2, F3 |
| Common prefix for this relation | F1, F2, F3 |

The example requires the stated prefix knowledge. Observing only the latest
snapshot at F4 does not automatically establish that the actor knows every
intermediate transition through F4. Snapshot agreement and agreement on an
event-history prefix are different relations.

The application universe may change continually.

Complete instantaneous agreement among all relevant actors may never occur.

That is not itself a correctness failure.

Correctness instead requires:

- recorded observations retain their historical meaning, while domain
  corrections and the applicability of their interpretations remain explicit;
- relevant divergence is explicit rather than silently erased;
- actors possess enough justified knowledge for the behavior they perform;
- where convergence is required, the protocol provides a valid path toward a
  newer common authoritative frontier;
- under quiescence and the choreography's stated liveness assumptions, required
  agreement debt can fall to zero.

*** Frontier is relation, not necessarily arithmetic

A frontier need not be represented by an integer.

The browser must not invent arithmetic ordering over opaque progression values
unless the authoritative system explicitly provides a supported ordering
operation.

The relevant semantic questions are relational:

- is this observation at least as strong as the requirement that triggered it?
- are two authoritative observations compatible for the fact whose agreement
  is being claimed?
- has this actor incorporated the authoritative change it was required to
  observe?
- may this local projection continue under the current compatibility contract?

The concrete XTDB and scope-coverage mechanisms are specified in the
authoritative-operations and progression part. An ordering relation must not be
assumed to provide a join, a greatest common frontier, or complete observation
coverage unless the supported contract actually establishes those properties.

*** Different actors may have different valid frontiers

For example:

| Participant | Justified scope in this example |
|-------------+----------------------------------|
| Authority | Modeled chain through F5 |
| Actor A | Required prefix observations through F5 |
| Actor B | Required prefix observations through F3 |
| Actor C | Only its privacy-permitted subset through F4 |

There may be no meaningful statement that all three actors share one universal
application frontier.

Agreement and knowledge are scoped to the relevant facts and authorities.

*** Advisory knowledge and adopted knowledge

An actor may learn that authority advanced without immediately adopting the new
authoritative projection.

This distinction is important for application behaviors such as a multi-user
board:

| Transition | Advisory state | Adopted projection |
|------------+----------------+--------------------|
| Relevant advisory received | Records the justified scoped reacquisition requirement | Existing projection remains selected |
| Explicit Refresh succeeds under the installation policy | Discharges only requirements covered by the adopted observation | Selects the newly admitted projection |

The advisory requirements and adopted authoritative observations are therefore
distinct pieces of actor state. The word frontier is shorthand for the scoped
knowledge relation; it does not require either state to be represented by one
maximum scalar token.

A correct application may intentionally preserve that distinction.


HumanHelp's board can represent that distinction explicitly instead of
overloading one current-revision slot. Suppose Alice creates Request R3 while
Bob's adopted board contains R1 and R2. The examples below are abbreviated
inspection and pure-transition fixtures; they are not browser assertions that
the server must trust.

The selected board projection is complete for its declared search/filter query,
not necessarily for every Request in the Organization. Each acquisition records
its concrete temporal context under the current-state read contract. X17, X18,
X19, and X20 are opaque basis labels; the admitted provider establishes the
relations used in this example.

#+begin_src clojure
(def board-scope
  {:organization-id O
   :projection :request/board-v1
   :query {:search "" :show-terminal? false :order :newest}
   :coverage-contract :complete-selected-query
   :time-policy :current-state})

(def bob-board
  {:actor [:client :bob-tab]
   :principal-context :session/bob-1
   :owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
   :projection board-scope
   :adopted
   {:requests [{:request/id R1 :request/status :open :request/title "R1"}
               {:request/id R2 :request/status :open :request/title "R2"}]
    :observation
    {:authority [:authority :request-board O]
     :scope board-scope
     :principal-context :session/bob-1
     :basis :X17
     :temporal-context T17}}
   :advisory {:requirements #{}}
   :acquisition nil})
#+end_src

For this example, the producing authority can attach a justified lower bound
X18 to the advisory generated by its committed change. The advisory does not
say that R3 is visible to Bob, and its issuance/disclosure follows the admitted
subscription contract.

#+begin_src clojure
(def create-advisory
  {:event/class :board-advisory
   :owner
   {:subscription/id :subscription/bob-board
    :source-instance/id :source/bob-board-1
    :scope board-scope}
   :requirement
   {:scope board-scope
    :authority [:authority :request-board O]
    :minimum-basis :X18}})

(def current-board-subscription
  {:board-subscription
   {:subscription/id :subscription/bob-board
    :source-instance/id :source/bob-board-1
    :scope board-scope
    :principal-context :session/bob-1
    :status :open}})

(defn record-advisory
  [board admitted-advisory]
  (let [requirement
        (advisory-admission/require-requirement-for!
          admitted-advisory
          {:scope (:projection board)
           :principal-context (:principal-context board)})]
    (update-in board [:advisory :requirements]
      requirements/conjoin requirement)))

(def admitted-create-advisory
  (admit-board-advisory
    admitted-board-source-context
    current-board-subscription
    create-advisory))

(def bob-after-advisory
  (record-advisory bob-board admitted-create-advisory))

(get-in bob-after-advisory [:adopted :requests])
;; => still R1 and R2

(get-in bob-after-advisory [:advisory :requirements])
;; => the scoped requirement for at least X18
#+end_src

=requirements/conjoin= retains the conjunction of obligations, conservatively
as an orderless set unless admitted implication evidence permits simplification.
It does not choose whichever token arrived last. The admission functions above
resolve registered source/owner contracts; calling a map admitted does not
supply that evidence. The local source context belongs to the cooperating
browser realization and is not a server credential.

A requirement saying only "later than the browser's X17" would not necessarily
cover the change at X18. The browser must not manufacture X18 from an advisory
with no such bound. A tokenless notification needs its separately declared
fresh-acquisition or source-fence protocol. Part IV defines how that protocol
establishes sufficient coverage.

Bob has learned that reacquisition may be needed or available for the scoped
board. He has not thereby learned R3's title, status, requester, or visibility.
The application may show a toast and Refresh affordance without replacing the
adopted rows.

The Refresh interaction separates a pure browser transition from the server
acquisition and the subsequent installation:

#+begin_src clojure
;; A2 is a fresh resource instance issued by the managed runtime.
(def refresh-start
  (board-runtime/step
    bob-after-advisory
    {:event/class :board/refresh-requested
     :trigger :user
     :resource-instance/id A2}))

;; Illustrative effect emitted by that pure step:
[:acquisition/request
 {:owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
  :resource-instance/id A2
  :principal-context :session/bob-1
  :scope board-scope
  :requirements
  (get-in bob-after-advisory [:advisory :requirements])}]

;; The host performs the authorized read outside the pure step. The trusted
;; server derives the real principal from authentication, not from this effect.
;; The returned observation is admitted under the request/response contract.
(def refresh-completion
  (board-runtime/step
    (:state refresh-start)
    {:event/class :acquisition-complete
     :owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
     :resource-instance/id A2
     :acquisition authorized-board-observation-X19}))

;; For an asynchronous realization this requests installation; it does not
;; yet assert that the new projection was physically installed and adopted.
(:effects refresh-completion)
;; => a scoped installation effect for the admitted observation

;; Another relevant advisory arrives before installation completes.
(def bob-before-install
  (record-advisory
    (:state refresh-completion)
    admitted-board-advisory-X20))

(def installed
  (board-runtime/step
    bob-before-install
    {:event/class :presentation/installed
     :owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
     :resource-instance/id A2
     :installation-result admitted-installation-result-X19}))

(def bob-after-refresh (:state installed))
;; => adopts the permitted projection at X19
;; => discharges the X18 requirement when its full scope is covered
;; => retains the X20 requirement and every unrelated uncovered requirement
#+end_src

This example's adoption policy permits installing the result that covers the
captured X18 requirement while retaining a newer X20 requirement. A policy that
requires all pending requirements to be satisfied before adoption must instead
defer that installation and reacquire; the runtime must not silently choose
between these policies.

The displayed =board-runtime/step= contract returns state and requested effects.
It is realized by the executable semantic core for this browser transition
contract. The effect shell binds callbacks to registered instances, checks
installation rights against the current owner, and reports actual completion
or failure. The final ownership
check and target mutation must share a declared serialization boundary; a
check followed by an unprotected asynchronous mutation leaves the same race.
An installation acknowledgement supplied as arbitrary client data is not
accepted as evidence
of this local execution. An adversarial browser can still falsify its own
display; the server's security does not depend on this local report.

The transition uses the latest state, including intervening advisories and
owner changes. Reusing the state captured when Refresh began could erase X20.
If another acquisition already adopted a stronger result, the query changed,
the account context changed, or A2 was retired, this completion cannot install
through the old ownership. A separate admitted path may retain independently
useful outcome or observation evidence.

A synchronous shell may combine installation and its successful semantic
completion when its correspondence contract justifies doing so. In either
realization, effects are not performed inside a retriable pure state update,
such as a =swap!= function. Only effects from the accepted state transition are
performed, under their declared identity and retry contract.

If Bob is authorized to see R3 and it belongs to the selected query, the new
board may contain it. If privacy or filtering excludes it, a complete authorized
acquisition can still discharge the covered board requirement without revealing
the hidden Request. It establishes the authorized projection, not all
Organization state.

Now consider agreement between Alice and Bob. They have separate admitted
observations. An agreement check states the proposition and relation it needs:

#+begin_src clojure
(agreement/check
  {:actors [[:client :alice-tab] [:client :bob-tab]]
   :proposition [:request/status R3 :open]
   :relation :compatible-authoritative-fact
   :observations [alice-observation bob-observation]})
#+end_src

The result can establish agreement only if those observations justify the same
scoped proposition under that relation. Equal =:open= values and larger-looking
basis labels are insufficient. The observations may have different physical
delivery paths and compatible rather than identical read contexts; the required
compatibility is evidence, not an inference from their labels.

Even established agreement that R3 is open does not establish agreement that it
belongs in Bob's authorized board. Alice may never have learned Bob's visibility
policy, query, or membership. That is a separate proposition with separate
premises. Nor does a shared observation of openness authorize either principal
to claim the Request now.

Privacy can make the shared proposition strictly smaller than either actor's
total knowledge:

#+begin_src clojure
(def alice-projection
  {:request/id R3
   :request/status :open
   :request/title "Need help moving a cabinet"
   :request/requester-phone "+1-555-0100"})

(def bob-projection
  {:request/id R3
   :request/status :open
   :request/title "Need help moving a cabinet"})
#+end_src

Given the appropriate admitted observations, they may agree on the shared
Request fact while Bob has no permitted knowledge of the private phone number.
The illustrative domain maps alone do not establish either provenance or
agreement. No convergence rule requires disclosure of the omitted field.

Bob can therefore retain board-adoption debt while holding newer justified
knowledge about an unrelated Request or account setting. The meaningful
question is agreement about which proposition, under which authority, scope,
relation, and observations.

*** Convergence does not require replay of every intermediate change

A browser offline from X17 through X900 need not replay every invalidation
between those points.

If the application semantics permit it, the actor may converge by acquiring a
current authoritative observation at X900 or later.

The correctness obligation is not "deliver every historical notification."

It is:

#+begin_quote
Provide a valid path by which the actor can acquire sufficiently current
authoritative knowledge for the behavior it needs to perform.
#+end_quote

That path must address the interval between establishing an interest and the
read/notification stream that covers it, as well as reconnect and source
replacement. Missing an invalidation before registration cannot be repaired by
assuming that all future invalidations will arrive. A supported contract may
use an acquisition/subscription fence, durable cursor or replay, gap detection,
or a declared periodic reacquisition trigger. The chosen mechanism and its
coverage must be explicit; this part does not require a particular broker or
multi-node routing topology.

Eventual convergence also needs an eventual trigger. Dependency availability
alone does not make a browser refresh if it never learns that its observation
may be incomplete. Where the product deliberately permits indefinite manual
deferral, the convergence claim remains conditional on the required user action
or another declared trigger.

*** Agreement debt

Where the choreography requires actors to converge, the difference between
their relevant justified knowledge may be understood as agreement debt.

The design does not require one universal numeric debt metric.

The useful property is qualitative:

- divergence is represented;
- required convergence paths exist;
- stale or provisional actors do not pretend agreement already exists;
- under stated liveness assumptions and quiescence, the required divergence can
  be eliminated.

The existence of a recovery path is distinct from its eventual execution.
Where adoption requires a user to activate Refresh, required eventual adoption
is conditional on that action or another permitted acquisition trigger. Where
the product permits indefinite deferral, remaining advisory information is not
by itself a violation of an unconditional convergence guarantee; such a
guarantee was never established for that deferred adoption.

*** Privacy constrains convergence

Agreement is always scoped by authorization and relevance.

The system must not satisfy a convergence property by leaking protected facts to
actors that are not entitled to observe them.

A correct convergence path may therefore involve:

- one actor learning the full authoritative fact;
- another actor learning only an authorized projection;
- both actors agreeing on a smaller shared semantic proposition.

For example:

#+begin_example
authority knows:
    full Request details + private requester metadata

helper A may know:
    Request is available for claim

observer B may know:
    Request count increased

A and B need not agree on private requester metadata.
#+end_example

*** Foundational invariant

The foundational agreement invariant is:

#+begin_quote
An admitted Gesso execution never treats mere co-presence, shape equality, physical
delivery, or coincidental local computation as semantic agreement.  Agreement
must be justified by a declared communication, compatible authoritative
observation, checked derivation, or explicit trusted assertion, and any claim
over evolving facts is relative to an explicitly justified temporal and
observation relation. Assertions remain premises, and a claimed common frontier
does not imply a universal order, complete history, or current authorization.
#+end_quote

* Part IV — Authoritative operations and progression
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-04-authoritative-operations-and-progression
:PART_REVISION: 04-20261107
:END:

This part defines the public model boundary, authoritative construction and
execution, command disposition, durable propagation, and scoped acquisition of
authoritative state. It connects the assurance classifications of Part I, the
identity and evidence model of Part II, and the operation contracts of Part III.
Browser coordination follows in Part VI; whole-application closure follows in
Part VII.

The central requirement is that the operation which an application admits, the
transition which it submits, and the outcome which it reports describe the same
semantic act. A well-typed declaration alone cannot establish that relationship.
Neither can a successful HTTP response, a copied certificate, a local callback
return, or an independently observed database state.

Ordinary full-language Clojure remains available under the
[[#req-full-language-construction][checked construction contract]]. Gesso proves
what its admitted semantics and evidence support, identifies what remains an
assertion or environmental premise, and preserves those distinctions through
execution. An opaque implementation is not silently treated as proved, and a
restricted proof mechanism does not define the limits of the application
language.

The declarations and API names below are illustrative specifications. They do
not claim that the exact displayed functions are already implemented. Inspection
maps explain admitted objects; constructing a similar map does not create an
execution capability or authoritative evidence. Example identifiers denote
separately bound fixture values unless a literal is supplied.

** Model integration
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-model-public-boundary
:END:

Application models own domain truth. HumanHelp supplies the running examples,
but Membership, Organization policy, and Request lifecycle rules remain
HumanHelp semantics rather than special cases in Gesso.

A model may organize its implementation as follows:

| Namespace | Responsibility |
|-----------+----------------|
| =request.domain= | Business predicates and pure transitions. |
| =request.schema= | Public value schemas and private persistence representations. |
| =request.graph= | Relationships and declared graph/query semantics. |
| =request.fx= | Acquisition, preparation, and authoritative effect realization. |
| =request.core= | Narrow public semantic interface. |
| =request.choreo= | Distributed programs consuming public model interfaces. |

The layout is conventional; the dependency rule is normative. Public =core=
operations may use their model's internals. Choreography consumes public =core=
interfaces and does not reach through them into private domain, graph, schema,
or FX implementation. A model's =core= does not facade its choreography.

For example:

#+begin_src clojure
(ns net.humanhelp.site.model.request.choreo
  (:require
   [gesso.choreo.core :as choreo]
   [net.humanhelp.site.model.request.core :as request]))

;; The authoritative participant invokes a public model operation.
;; trusted-context is supplied by that participant's admitted runtime.
(choreo/at :request-authority
  (request/claim! trusted-context claim-command))
#+end_src

This is not permission for the browser to supply =trusted-context=, nor for the
choreography to call =request.fx= directly.

*** Placement and semantic ownership

The placement test is whether a rule still matters when the model operation is
called synchronously from one JVM, without a browser or distributed protocol.
Claim eligibility, current authorization, valid state transitions, command
deduplication promised by the model, and atomicity normally pass that test.
Browser optimism, role participation, transport retries, projected execution
lifetime, and presentation settlement normally belong above the model.

Some information crosses this boundary because it has an independent model
meaning. A stable command ID can be a public model input because durable replay
requires it. An expected domain version can be a public precondition. Neither
requires the model to know a tab's execution ID or HTMX swap policy.

Choreography may consume a public business judgment; it must not reimplement a
slightly different Claim policy. A view may suppress a button as a convenience,
but that suppression does not establish permission to commit. Conversely,
business policy must not exist only inside choreography while =core= remains a
pass-through to unconstrained writes.

Cross-model calls use public semantic interfaces. When Request depends on
Membership and Organization, their models expose the necessary facts,
predicates, and composable authoritative obligations through those interfaces.
They need not expose private storage paths. A coordinating operation may then
protect the combined obligations in one transaction.

A cross-model public interface must make its *semantic commitment*
inspectable without disclosing the consulted model's private storage
or implementation. For a condition needed by another model's
authoritative transition, this includes its logical subject and
parameters, relevant trusted principal/authority, declared read
dependencies, and the point at which the condition must hold.
An admitted implementation may supply a data-level obligation,
a qualified guard constructor, or a trusted callback bound to that
public contract. Merely naming =:membership/active= in an operation
manifest does not establish that its actual guard is installed.

A successful preliminary Membership or Organization read is evidence
of that observation at its qualified basis, not automatically proof
that its predicate still holds when a later Request Claim commits.
Where Claim promises atomic enforcement of combined eligibility,
the authoritative operation must compose the relevant model-owned
conditions into the *actual protected decision* using qualified
transaction-current guards, assertions, or an equivalently justified
backend mechanism. A stability premise may replace such a guard only
when separately admitted for the precise interval and scope; an
unchecked preflight Boolean or caller-supplied `authorized?` cannot.
If the chosen backend cannot protect the promised combined condition,
report the missing enforcement/correspondence obligation or weaken
the *declared* atomic guarantee explicitly; do not silently accept
a race.

| Cross-model construction | Required judgment |
|--------------------------+-------------------|
| Request Claim composes Request state, current Membership eligibility, and Organization policy at one admitted authoritative incorporation boundary | Permit the operation under its declared backend and trusted-guard premises; keep the originating public rule owners distinct |
| A prior Membership read returned =:active=, but Membership was revoked before the Claim commit | The stale read alone cannot authorize incorporation; enforce the current guarded condition or retain the explicitly qualified stability premise |
| A browser or choreography sends ={:claim-allowed? true}= and the transaction uses that value in place of current model guards | Reject the attempted authority substitution, regardless of input shape |
| A coordinating operation uses admitted public constraints from two models without importing either model's private FX or XTDB schema | Permit composition where the declared atomic backend contract and cross-model dependencies are actually satisfied |
| The backend cannot enforce the promised joint invariant, but an assembly report infers safety from two separately successful reads | Do not certify the joint transition; report an open or failed physical obligation at the correct boundary |

The rule and its source owner remain the same in a single application
process or across several web nodes sharing one qualified logical
authority. Multi-node routing and propagation can change which host
learns about a change or when it can observe a sufficient basis;
they cannot introduce a second Claim policy or substitute a stale
host-local authorization cache for transaction-current enforcement.


A workflow spanning peer models should live at the level that owns the workflow.
An onboarding flow coordinating Invitation, Membership, Organization, and
external provisioning need not belong to Invitation merely because it starts
there. Semantic dependency also does not dictate a literal namespace load order.
Contracts may be represented separately to avoid circular implementation loads.

*** Public interfaces expose commitments, not implementation privilege

A public operation interface must expose enough information for a caller and
the assembly checker to establish:

- the semantic operation and interface version;
- input and outcome schemas, including closed semantic discriminants;
- required authority and the source of the trusted principal binding;
- semantic preconditions and the point at which they must hold;
- promised atomic effects and explicitly separate later effects;
- command binding, replay, disclosure, and uncertainty policies;
- authoritative observation or progression evidence supplied with outcomes;
- named assumptions and the assurance status of each claimed guarantee.

It must not make callers depend on private XTDB forms, FX scratch values, or
helper Vars. A synchronous REPL invocation remains meaningful when supplied an
admitted context and authorized principal; bypassing HTTP does not bypass the
model's contract.

Ordinary application code should receive scoped semantic capabilities rather
than a raw database mutation handle. Clojure namespace privacy, records, and
nominal wrappers do not sandbox a determined author in the same trusted process.
The assurance claim therefore states its trusted-code and writer boundaries.

A bypass writer can invalidate assumptions used by later supported operations,
not merely forfeit assurance for its own invocation. Coverage must account for
jobs, migrations, imports, administrative tools, other services, and direct
database access wherever their writes can affect a protected invariant. An
admission policy may accept a named external-writer premise; it must not report
that premise as established by observing only Gesso-issued calls.

*** Well-formed rules and appropriate policy are separate judgments

Where semantic types are known, malformed rules must fail admission. Comparing
a closed status enum as though it were a number cannot become a certified rule.
For ordinary Clojure predicates outside the analyzable vocabulary, the interface
records their types, contracts, implementation binding, and asserted meaning.
Merely invoking such a predicate does not prove its contract.

Neither type checking nor invariant preservation decides whether a business
policy is desirable. Gesso can establish that the declared Claim policy is
consistently enforced under stated premises. HumanHelp still chooses that
policy. The executable semantic core and any later proof kernel/checker have
different responsibilities, as established in Part I.

** Core, FX, and choreography

FX realizes the authoritative commitments of a public model operation. It may
acquire data, compute candidates, construct a protected transition, invoke a
backend, and translate evidence into a typed outcome. It must not invent a
second business semantics or a second distributed truth model.

One semantic operation can span a public =core= function, domain predicates, a
registered transition constructor, an FX pipeline, and a backend adapter. Those
are implementation components of the operation, not automatically separate
commands. Their contracts must agree on inputs, authority, permitted effects,
and outcomes.

The public model operation and the choreography invoking it are not required
to have identical callable signatures or host representations. A REPL,
scheduled job, installed HTTP handler, or another admitted Choreo realization
may invoke the same =:request/claim= meaning under its own authorized entry
contract. A shared keyword, copied interface map, route name, or successful
transport return is not evidence that the installed callable implements that
meaning. Application assembly binds the source-owned operation, selected
trusted executor, actual guard/write coverage, outcome interpretation, and
permitted disclosure to the same qualified semantic contract. Every path
claiming to implement the operation must satisfy its declared boundary;
passing through choreography is not a prerequisite for ordinary model use.

Conversely, a single command may involve several internal FX callbacks,
backend statements, and asynchronous publication attempts. Those do not
acquire independent authority to rewrite the business result or allocate a
second domain command identity merely because they execute separately.
A later notification worker has its own work/attempt identity and effect
contract, but its work remains correlated to the authoritative command
and retained publication intent under the selected protocol.

Choreography adds coordination obligations: which role calls the operation,
which participants learn its outcome, what they may provisionally display, and
how they recover when delivery is uncertain. Those obligations do not authorize
Choreo to manufacture model outcomes or bypass current model guards.

*** A concrete public Claim contract

For this example, Claim moves an eligible open Request to its production
=:claimed= state and creates the corresponding active *primary
RequestAssignment* for the authenticated helper. RequestAssignment owns
the helper relationship and its uniqueness/history semantics; a convenient
=:request/helper= field must not become a competing source of truth.
Collaborator assignments are not mistaken for a second primary helper.
The helper must have current active Membership in the Request's Organization,
and current Organization policy must permit the claim.

This worked operation explicitly selects a *durable work-recorded publication
profile*: successful new incorporation records the command result, the
required durable notification intent, and the protected Request plus primary
assignment transition together. Another operation may lawfully choose an
admitted best-effort profile, but it must publish a correspondingly weaker
contract rather than silently claiming work-recording or guaranteed delivery.
The durable intent is not evidence that a notifier received a message or
that an external effect completed.

The public value schema is small:

#+begin_src clojure
(def ClaimCommand
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:request-id uuid?]
   [:command-id uuid?]])

(def valid-claim-command? (m/validator ClaimCommand))

(defn claim! [trusted-context command]
  (when-not (valid-claim-command? command)
    (throw (ex-info "Invalid Claim input"
                    {:gesso/error :model/invalid-input
                     :operation :request/claim})))
  (model-runtime/invoke-admitted!
   trusted-context :request/claim command))
#+end_src

Validation is an unconditional boundary check, not an optional assertion that a
production configuration can disable. The example does not log the entire
command or trusted context in an error. A concrete application may use a typed
error return instead of an exception if its public interface declares that
choice.

=invoke-admitted!= resolves the exact registered operation and its capability
binding. It does not accept an arbitrary caller-provided guard/write map as an
equivalent operation. The browser's wire field =:gesso/command-id= may map to
the public model field =:command-id= through an admitted boundary mapping.
=:request-id= here denotes the HumanHelp domain Request; transport correlation
uses =:http-request-id= or =:transport-request-id=.

An inspection view of the contract might be:

#+begin_src clojure
{:operation :request/claim
 :interface-version 1
 :arguments ClaimCommand
 :principal-source :trusted-runtime-context
 :command-policy :claim-command-policy-v1
 :publication-profile :durable-work-recorded
 :new-incorporation
 {:requires [:request/argument-target-matches
             :request/open
             :request-assignment/no-active-primary
             :membership/principal-and-organization-match
             :membership/active
             :organization/claim-permitted]
  :establishes [:request/claimed
                :request-assignment/active-primary-recorded
                :request-assignment/primary-uniqueness-preserved
                :command/result-recorded
                :publication/intent-recorded]}
 :replay
 {:requires [:command/binding-compatible
             :command/current-disclosure-permitted]
  :effect :return-permitted-recorded-outcome}}
#+end_src

These keywords are schematic *inspection labels*, not a second registry of
facts or an executable guard-language API. The public RequestAssignment model
owns what =no-active-primary=, =active-primary-recorded= and
=primary-uniqueness-preserved= mean, including which other writers may
alter assignment state. The application derives or supplies their qualified
transaction-current realization through its admitted model/authority
interface; copying this map cannot establish that the operation, installed
handler, backend guard or durable worker exists.

A collection of reassuring labels is not a proof. In particular,
new-incorporation conditions do not apply unchanged to replay: after a
successful Claim the Request is claimed and has an active primary assignment,
but a compatible authorized repetition must still be able to recover the
historical recorded success. A later Unclaim or Reassign may change the current
Request and assignment without changing that historical command outcome.
Result disclosure still depends on current authorized policy.

| Public Claim construction | Required model/coordination judgment |
|---------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| An eligible authenticated helper claims an open, unassigned Request using a newly admitted command | The protected transaction establishes the =:claimed= state, exactly the permitted active primary RequestAssignment, recorded command outcome, and durable intent for the chosen profile |
| A different collaborator assignment already exists, but the primary slot is eligible and empty | Do not mistake collaborator presence for a conflicting primary; admit or reject according to the actual public RequestAssignment policy |
| A client supplies a matching =:request-id= and a forged helper identity in a transport wrapper | The trusted principal binding selects the actor; input shape or a matching route name cannot grant the claimed identity |
| A previous Claim committed and is replayed compatibly after the Request became claimed or was later reassigned | Resolve the prior command with permitted historical disclosure and without adding a second primary assignment or publication intent |
| An installed handler invokes a similarly named helper or FX callback that omits assignment uniqueness or actual durable work recording | Reject the stronger declared operation/assembly correspondence; accepting a map of obligations is not proof of enforcing them |
| A best-effort notice is selected by a separately admitted contract | Permit the weaker published guarantee; do not advertise =:publication/intent-recorded= or delivered receipt as a consequence of this weaker operation |

*** Model outcomes and invocation errors

The operation interface uses the following model outcomes:

| Outcome | Required meaning |
|---------+------------------|
| =:committed= | Qualified affirmative physical evidence attributes incorporation of the bound command to the reported admitted submission (or an equivalently justified invocation-specific mechanism). It does not mean that later propagation or presentation succeeded. |
| =:already-incorporated= | A compatible retained command record establishes that the command was incorporated; this resolution path returns the permitted historical result without another incorporation. The record alone does not attribute the winning physical attempt or prove incorporation predated an earlier ambiguous submission. |
| =:rejected= | The declared authoritative rejection/closure policy establishes that the bound command is terminally rejected within its promised scope; one failed preparation or physical attempt is insufficient. |
| =:commit-unknown= | Available evidence does not establish a permitted definitive command disposition. The command may or may not have incorporated, even when one particular physical attempt is known not to have committed. |

Outcome variants have closed discriminants and variant-specific payload schemas.
A successful variant carries command binding and result evidence; a rejection
carries its permitted reason and disposition evidence; an unknown variant
carries the admitted reconciliation reference and uncertainty classification.
Fields are not made optional merely to allow every variant through one loose
map schema.

A model outcome, a physical *submission-attempt* result, the historical
semantic command disposition, and a participant's *permitted knowledge* of
that disposition are different subjects. A positive, correlated backend
acknowledgment can justify an invocation-specific =:committed= result under
the qualified adapter contract. A later read of the durable command record
can establish incorporation of the command while leaving open which of two
attempts performed it; that read supports the compatible recovered-result
path, not invented evidence that the immediately preceding submission
succeeded. Physical submission receipt, transaction acknowledgment, actual
authoritative incorporation, result disclosure, Live delivery, and browser
installation are not one terminal event.

An invocation-specific =:committed= result requires qualified physical
evidence tying the *actual complete submitted effect*, its command binding,
trusted principal/authority, and affirmative outcome to the reported
submission or invocation. Co-observing a transaction key and retained command
record at one compatible pinned basis can establish narrower facts; it does
not by itself identify the attempt that caused the record. Abstract transition
proof does not supply that missing physical causality.

Conversely, a compatible retained command record can justify the historical
=:already-incorporated= recovery path without naming the winning attempt.
Qualified binding-scoped terminal closure, not a failed guard or absent row,
is required for stable =:rejected=. Otherwise a physically failed attempt,
timeout, incomplete result or unestablished backend premise leaves the command
unknown or supplies only a narrower typed attempt/invocation failure. These
classifications cannot be strengthened by exchanging report-shaped maps.

An authoritative readback must preserve its *own* observation scope, basis,
completeness and trusted origin. If a command record is not observed after
a timeout, absence at one insufficient or stale read is not evidence of
non-incorporation. If a command record *is* observed, matching the stable
ID alone is insufficient: its full bound operation, authority, principal,
canonical arguments and compatible interface must be checked before
returning a recorded result. When the present principal is no longer
authorized to learn it, disclosure can be unavailable despite known
incorporation. An observation of a Request's present =:claimed= state
cannot replace this bound command evidence.

Rejection has a similarly exact boundary. A failed authorization precheck
may prevent *this entry attempt* from proceeding; an explicit backend
assertion failure may establish that *this transaction attempt* did
not incorporate. Neither necessarily proves that a concurrent compatible
attempt did not or will not incorporate the semantic command. A terminal
=:rejected= outcome requires its promised authoritative exclusion/closure
evidence, or a specifically narrower declared rejection policy that
does not masquerade as permanent command closure. Otherwise use the
appropriate typed invocation failure, physical attempt result, or
unresolved command disposition. Do not manufacture a durable rejection
record from a transaction which definitely aborted without storing one.

Malformed inputs, unsupported interface versions, incompatible reuse, invalid
credentials, disclosure denial, and backend qualification failure require typed
handling. They are not all domain rejections. For example, an unauthorized
caller must not be able to create a terminal rejection record for somebody
else's command. A disclosure denial must not falsely say that the command was
never incorporated. Privacy policy may require an intentionally indistinguishable
unavailable response for several underlying conditions. Likewise, a caught
exception, a failed post-commit advisory, or a delivery timeout cannot be
reclassified as a domain rejection. A known incorporated command remains
incorporated despite failure in a separately owned publication/response
stage; if the physical outcome is genuinely indeterminate, preserve unknown
until qualified evidence resolves it.

The outcome recorded for replay is a historical operation result. A Request
projection freshly acquired alongside it has a different meaning: it describes
state at that acquisition's scope and basis. The API must distinguish these
values. It must not imply that a recorded successful Claim guarantees the
Request is still assigned to that helper now.

Presentation mapping is explicit:

| Model evidence | Presentation settlement |
|----------------+-------------------------|
| Permitted committed success | =:accepted= for the bound presentation operation. |
| Permitted already-incorporated success | The same accepted successor, without a new incorporation. |
| Authoritative command rejection | =:rejected= through the declared rejection branch. |
| Commit unknown | =:unknown= until admissible reconciliation evidence resolves it. |

A model may define successful results other than Claim success, and rejection
details may be undisclosable. Assembly must check the actual variant mapping,
disclosure requirements, and permitted presentation effects. A new command ID
is not an acceptable way to disguise an unresolved retry. Presentation
settlement additionally requires that the evidence be admissible to the
*current* bound execution and resource owner. A correct model outcome
cannot resurrect a retired browser execution, justify unrelated remote
adoption, or discharge a required physical installation that never happened.

*** An explicit invocation sequence

The following logical phases may be implemented by one FX pipeline or several
cooperating components. The semantics must survive either arrangement.

| Phase | Responsibility | What it does not establish by itself |
|-------+----------------+-------------------------------------|
| Admit | Validate inputs, operation/interface identity, trusted principal, and capability. | Permission to commit after mutable authorization changes. |
| Resolve or prepare | Recover a compatible prior result, or acquire data and compute a candidate. | A pre-read is not the atomic command-existence guard. |
| Incorporate | Protect semantic dependencies and atomically establish the domain transition, command record, and required durable intents. | A submitted request is not necessarily a committed transaction. |
| Propagate | Attempt publication or arrange durable recovery according to the operation contract. | Delivery success to all participants. |
| Respond | Return only the outcome and observation evidence the invocation can justify and disclose. | A browser has received, installed, or adopted the response. |

Replay does not blindly rerun notifications or other effects. A recorded durable
intent may remain unfinished and be resumed by its declared recovery owner, but
it retains its original work identity. A compatible replay is not a new reason
to create a second notification or charge.

Each phase may run on the same host or on a later compatible application
node. The semantic command binding, trusted model rules, durable disposition
and presentation/adoption policies do not change with host placement.
Cross-host reads still need qualified sufficient progression; neither
local cache agreement nor the fact that both hosts use the same logical
database automatically establishes this.

*** Outcome and invocation acceptance cases

| Case | Required judgment |
|------+-------------------|
| A fresh Claim receives a qualified affirmative transaction acknowledgment bound to the actual submitted attempt | Report justified =:committed= for that invocation; record durable notification debt separately from advisory/browser delivery |
| A Claim submission loses its acknowledgment, then a compatible command record is acquired authoritatively | Return the permitted recovered historical result; do not claim the readback identifies which attempt performed incorporation |
| Two concurrent physical attempts share one compatible command ID, and one has a confirmed assertion failure while the other incorporated | Preserve the unsuccessful *attempt* and the successful *semantic command* as separate facts; do not report terminal =:rejected= from the failed attempt |
| An input is malformed or the caller lacks an authenticated principal before any submission | Return the corresponding typed invocation failure without inventing a terminal command-rejection record |
| A terminal command rejection is promised, but an abort did not record a binding-scoped exclusion and another attempt may still commit | Do not report stable =:rejected= without a qualified closure/fence; preserve uncertainty or a narrower explicitly scoped failure |
| A known incorporation is followed by a failed Live publish or missing browser settlement | Preserve committed authoritative state and any outstanding propagation/adoption obligations; do not turn a post-commit failure into business rejection |
| An authorized read finds a compatible command record and a newer RequestAssignment state after later Reassign | Disclose the historical Claim result separately from the qualified current projection; do not assert that the previous primary helper still owns the Request |
| An old command record is found but the current principal lacks permission to disclose its outcome | Preserve the authoritative fact internally while returning only the permitted unavailable/indistinguishable result |
| A replacement browser execution receives valid evidence about an old command | Admit it only through its current authority, ownership, resource and presentation mapping; the retired execution does not regain installation rights |
| A host retries an unresolved command on another web node using the original immutable binding | Preserve the same resolve-or-incorporate rule, current backend/progression qualification and disclosed outcome contract; node placement does not create a new command |
| Concurrent same-binding attempts A1 and A2 target C42; A1 aborts while A2 commits, and a qualified read finds C42. | Admit the historical compatible recorded result; never infer A1 committed or that the command was terminally rejected by A1's abort. |
| A completed transaction key and command record are co-observed but the full-submission causal bridge is not established. | Preserve bounded physical evidence and historical recovery, but do not report invocation-specific =:committed= solely from coincidence. |
| A terminal rejection fence and a pending incorporation attempt contend under the qualified command namespace. | Only established binding-scoped exclusion permits =:rejected=; an isolated failed guard or absent-record read does not. |

** Atomic authoritative transitions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-atomic-authoritative-transition
:END:

An authoritative transition establishes the invariant-bearing effects of one
semantic command at a declared atomic boundary. Preparation may be extensive:
queries, calculations, validation, composition of model constraints, and
construction of proposed writes can occur before that boundary. They do not
become authoritative merely because preparation succeeded.

If preparation itself reserves stock, creates a lease, or persists an intent,
that change is a separate authoritative transition with its own valid state and
recovery obligations. It must not be hidden inside something described as a
pure pre-read.

For each transition, the contract identifies:

1. the authority and semantic state being changed;
2. the operation, command, canonical arguments, and trusted principal binding;
3. the conditions that must hold at incorporation;
4. the permitted state change and what must remain unchanged;
5. the command-result and durable-work effects included in the same boundary;
6. the backend mechanism protecting these commitments;
7. the evidence that distinguishes incorporation, rejection, and uncertainty.

The required protection concerns semantic dependencies, not automatically every
field read during preparation. An unused display name need not become a commit
guard. Conversely, absence, uniqueness, aggregate limits, and relationships may
be semantic dependencies even when no existing document contains the fact to
guard. Protecting only returned rows cannot establish that no matching row was
inserted concurrently.

Atomic writes alone are insufficient. A backend can atomically apply writes
derived from stale or incompatible facts. The qualification must establish that
the required conditions hold at the operation's declared incorporation point,
using an appropriate transaction isolation, conditional mutation, transaction
function, constraint, or equivalent qualified mechanism.

*** Semantic rules become implementation obligations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-authoritative-semantic-obligations
:END:

Every rule used to justify admission or a safety claim must have an explicit
path to its authoritative realization. The path identifies the semantic
predicate, bound values, mutable dependencies, protection mechanism, and
evidence status. It also identifies the frame: effects outside the declared
change are forbidden unless separately specified.

For Claim, the obligation set is:

| Semantic obligation | Binding or protection required |
|---------------------+--------------------------------|
| The requested Request is the acquired target. | Equality between the command's Request ID and the acquired authoritative identity, in the correct authority scope. |
| The Request is open and has no active primary RequestAssignment. | Protect the current lifecycle state and the absence of an active primary assignment for this Request in the correct Organization/authority scope; a nullable Request helper projection is not that relationship. |
| Membership belongs to this helper and Organization. | Principal, Membership, Request, and Organization associations protected together. |
| Membership is active. | Current eligibility protected at incorporation under the policy's temporal meaning. |
| Organization permits the claim. | The relevant policy and any inputs it depends on protected at incorporation. |
| Request state and primary assignment are the permitted domain change. | The helper is derived from the trusted principal binding; the transaction makes the Request =:claimed= and establishes exactly the model-permitted active primary RequestAssignment, preserving relationship history, collaborator distinctions and every affected document's frame. |
| The command is not incorporated twice. | Atomic command-binding compatibility and uniqueness within the declared command namespace. |
| Success can be recovered and propagated. | The compatible command result and the work intent required by this Claim example's selected durable work-recorded profile share the authoritative boundary with the Request and RequestAssignment effects; neither response delivery nor external notification is thereby established. |

Evidence may establish some of these obligations by construction, others by a
checked derivation, others by runtime checks, and others only under accepted
backend or opaque-code premises. The report must preserve those classifications.
The word /checked/ must identify what was checked; it must not silently include
all unexamined behavior beneath that check.

The correspondence is *bidirectional*. Every correctness-relevant source-owned
semantic rule has a traceable protection, construction, derivation or explicitly
accepted premise in the effective authoritative operation. Conversely, an FX,
model, backend or installed callback whose condition changes whether the
command is admitted, which domain effect is written, who may cause it, or what
protected result is disclosed must correspond to a declared rule or named
opaque/physical premise. An incidental query optimization, retry, logging
branch or diagnostic does not become business policy merely by existing.
An unreported extra policy guard that silently rejects valid declared business
operations is as much a correspondence problem as a missing required guard.
Gesso may impose a separate *declared* platform safety/admission policy;
it must not claim the narrower implementation is the complete realization
of a more permissive business contract without identifying that policy.

The active-primary absence and uniqueness rules deserve explicit care.
Checking a Request projection, an empty previously read assignment collection
or a single row's version does not necessarily prevent another writer from
inserting a primary assignment before incorporation. The actual backend
mechanism must protect the *predicate over the relevant set*, with its
qualified isolation, uniqueness/constraint, transaction assertion or other
justified serialization contract. A reference-model search result or
syntactically correct =ASSERT= is not, by itself, proof of that physical
concurrency property. If the selected backend, writer inventory or
precondition-coverage profile cannot establish it, retain the missing
correspondence or supported-premise obligation rather than certifying
primary uniqueness. A valid operation under a stronger backend/profile
is not rejected merely because a narrower proof mechanism cannot analyze it.

*** Canonical construction and physical submission

The default managed path derives a transition from the admitted operation's
canonical semantics and bound inputs. It does not ask application code to
independently maintain three descriptions of the same effect: a business rule,
a reassuring guard map, and an unconstrained transaction vector.

The derived or checked construction binds at least:

- the operation/interface and semantic-definition identities;
- the exact admitted implementation and relevant adapter/profile identities;
- typed argument, principal, authority, and target bindings;
- acquired input provenance and any required acquisition capabilities;
- protected predicates, permitted writes, and frame obligations;
- command-result and durable-work effects;
- the invocation and physical submission to which resulting evidence belongs.

The execution gate must account for the complete submitted transaction, not just
a selected domain fragment. Command ledger writes, outbox entries, creates,
updates, deletes, transaction options, and any effective backend-side hooks must
be included in the checked effect boundary or covered by an explicit accepted
premise. An extra write or a weakened guard cannot be ignored because the
original domain fragment still matches a certificate.

For the chosen durable Claim contract, the Request lifecycle change, new
primary RequestAssignment, binding-compatible command outcome, and retained
publication work must form *one protected authoritative effect*. A later
notification send is independently owned work, not a fifth in-transaction
provider delivery. Neither the number of public model interfaces used nor
the number of FX callbacks determines the number of transactions. An adapter
must expose enough of its effective guard/read and mutation footprint for
its selected assurance profile to judge the whole operation. An uninspected
transaction hook or extra writer is an explicit open premise or a rejection
under a closed coverage policy, not evidence silently erased from the frame.

An inspection report is not the executable capability. The runtime must prevent
ordinary callers from substituting a new transaction, changing a target,
replacing the executor, or attaching a report from an unrelated successful call.
The result must be linked to the actual issued invocation, submitted material,
and qualified backend return path. Structural equality with expected output is
not evidence of that provenance.

This judgment covers the **effective** submission after FX composition,
conditional branches, error handlers, backend option selection and installed
executor dispatch, not only an earlier approved plan fragment. A check performed
before a permitted dynamic branch must either cover every realizable effect
variant or be enforced again at the actual submission boundary. A normal
successful Claim path does not thereby credential a fallback handler that
emits a Request-only update, nor an unrelated worker entry that accepts a
caller-supplied transaction vector. For each declared variant, the checker
distinguishes legitimate model effects, additional effectful operations with
their own public contracts, and opaque implementation premises.

The **installed-path relationship** is separate from correctness of an
individual submitted vector. Part VII owns whole-application closure: the
served route or other real ingress must invoke the selected trusted executor
with the admitted operation/authority/command binding. Part IV's stronger
operation-family claim applies to that physical entry only under a checked
installed binding or explicit accepted premise; the existence of a similarly
named route, a valid standalone gateway or a copied report cannot establish it.

Different construction mechanisms can support different subsets. A proof for
one scalar document update does not cover nested values, nullable fields,
inserts, deletes, multi-document transitions, or arbitrary transaction programs
unless its proposition says so. Those operations remain available through
broader checked mechanisms or explicitly asserted contracts. They must not be
quietly forced into the narrow proof's vocabulary.


*** Atomic-construction acceptance and counterexamples

| Construction | Required semantic and evidence judgment |
|--------------+-----------------------------------------|
| An authorized Claim closes the current Request, Membership, Organization and active-primary predicates in a qualified atomic backend transition; it writes Request, RequestAssignment, command result and the required durable intent | Admit the operation under its declared enforcement and provider premises; distinguish actual atomic incorporation from later message delivery |
| A helper has a collaborator assignment but no active primary assignment, and the public model permits Claim | Admit the eligible primary assignment; collaborator presence cannot be converted into the absence or presence of the single primary owner by convenience-field logic |
| A candidate observes no active primary assignment, but another command inserts one before this Claim's unprotected write | Reject the claim of atomic uniqueness; require protection against the competing insertion, not merely a version check on the unchanged Request |
| Membership is revoked after a candidate read and before the actual transaction; the implementation submits only the prepared Request update | Reject the claimed current-eligibility enforcement; retain separately qualified stability only if the public contract explicitly permits it |
| An FX callback silently adds an otherwise legitimate-looking Organization eligibility condition absent from the declared product rule | Reject full declared-operation correspondence or expose the independent restriction as an authored rule/explicit admission policy; do not label the public business rule ill-typed merely because the implementation is stricter |
| A statement writes the Request and primary assignment, but the durable-work record promised by this selected profile is written only in a later unprotected effect | Reject the claimed work-recorded atomic boundary; a successful first commit does not establish durable publication intent |
| The effective submitted transaction contains an unapproved write or omits a required command/result guard, while a domain-only fragment still matches the checker | Reject the closed frame/guard coverage claim; a copied proof report or matching fragment cannot credential the full submission |
| An installed Claim route invokes the admitted trusted executor and its complete guarded Request/RequestAssignment/command/work submission | Admit only the scoped installed-route-to-executor claim under its physical/deployment premises; a replaced handler that calls a similarly named partial FX helper lacks that correspondence |
| A dynamic FX fallback submits a different command vector from the one checked before invocation | Recheck or derive the *effective* complete vector at the actual submission gate; a valid earlier branch does not confer admission on this alternative effect |

*** Representation and frame fidelity

The mapping between semantic fields and physical storage must be explicit and
checked for the supported representation. Distinct semantic fields must not
silently collide on one physical field. Required fields, system metadata, nested
paths, deletion, and field ownership need declared treatment.

Presence matters. A missing field, a field with =nil=, a field with =false=, and
an empty collection are not interchangeable unless the model explicitly makes
them equivalent. A proof that depends on a complete acquired entity cannot use
a partial projection or unqueried relationship whose absence is
interpreted as unclaimed. HumanHelp's primary helper relation belongs
to the public RequestAssignment model, not to a nullable Request
helper field.

For RequestAssignment, the *absence of an active primary assignment*
requires a scope-complete, authorized and provenance-qualified query over
the relevant relationship. An empty page, a missing field, an incomplete
query, a failed read, and a complete empty result are different. Even a
complete observed absence does not itself prevent a concurrent
insertion before incorporation. The selected backend must independently
protect the primary-absence/uniqueness predicate at the authoritative
transition under a qualified atomicity and writer-coverage contract.

Whole-document replacement must preserve the declared frame despite concurrent
changes. Comparing a selected subset of fields and then replacing an entire
stale document can overwrite unrelated updates. Valid solutions include a
qualified full-state guard, an appropriately maintained revision guard, or a
backend update whose semantics preserve untouched fields. The chosen solution
must actually support the claimed frame.

A revision field is sufficient only under a premise that every relevant writer
maintains it and that its comparison has the required semantics. An equal
before/after value does not prove that no intermediate change occurred. History
or non-repetition properties require stronger evidence than a state predicate;
ordinary state invariants do not universally require an ABA-prevention mechanism.

*** Capability lifetime and command lifetime

Execution capabilities are scoped to the admitted runtime and operation. Their
contract states whether they may be used once, reused, transferred to an
asynchronous worker, or retained beyond a request. One-shot consumption must be
atomic with respect to competing local users of that capability.

A one-shot capability prevents reuse of that capability instance. It does not
prevent another request from obtaining a different capability for the same
semantic command. Durable command deduplication still belongs to the command
protocol. Similarly, a thread ID, request path, or callback identity does not
replace the semantic command binding.

Retiring a request-local capability prevents future use according to its
contract; it does not roll back an already submitted transaction. Transferring
work to a durable worker requires an admitted transfer and a new appropriate
capability, not accidental retention of a web request's ambient authority.
The worker admission binds its retained work/intent identity, source-authorized
operation or continuation, current policy and permitted effect class. A worker
may be scheduled, retried, moved to another node or awakened by a provider
callback without owning an HTTP route; it still needs the admitted runtime
effect boundary and complete backend/writer-coverage classification. The fact
that the original request once held a legitimate capability does not credential
a replacement process, its installed worker handler or any additional
transaction that worker might submit.

A copied inspection report, structural admission handle, command record,
or caller-authored map is not a runtime mutation capability. The issued
capability must bind its qualified issuer, authorized actor/authority,
operation, allowed effects, target and resource lifecycle as required by
the runtime contract. A replacement web node may obtain a newly issued
capability while preserving the *same semantic command* and current
model policy; an in-process handle or retired request-local authority
is not made transferable by serialization.

*** A complete Claim preparation example

The following pure function illustrates *candidate computation* rather
than a required concrete Gesso API. It assumes that the principal, Request,
Membership and Organization have passed their public contracts, and a
public RequestAssignment acquisition supplied the separately validated
`assignment-observation`. This observation represents a complete, scoped
collection of active *primary* assignments for the exact Request; it does
not count collaborator assignments as primary owners. Its query meaning,
source provenance, disclosure, compatible basis and complete coverage
must already have been established independently by the model-owned
acquisition contract.

The illustrative `:observation/*` keys expose these obligations, but
their presence in arbitrary caller data does not prove them. The
function neither commits, authorizes a backend write nor resolves
durable command replay.

#+begin_src clojure
(defn claim-candidate-decision
  [command principal request membership organization assignment-observation]
  (let [active-primary (:observation/primary-assignments
                        assignment-observation)]
    (cond
      (not= (:request-id command) (:request/id request))
      {:candidate/status :invalid :reason :target-mismatch}

      (not (and (= :complete (:observation/coverage assignment-observation))
                (= (:request/id request)
                   (:observation/request-id assignment-observation))
                (vector? active-primary)))
      {:candidate/status :insufficient-observation
       :reason :primary-assignment-coverage-unestablished}

      (> (count active-primary) 1)
      {:candidate/status :invalid-observed-state
       :reason :multiple-active-primary-assignments}

      (not= :open (:request/status request))
      {:candidate/status :ineligible :reason :request-not-open}

      (seq active-primary)
      {:candidate/status :ineligible :reason :already-has-primary-assignment}

      (not= (:principal/id principal) (:membership/user membership))
      {:candidate/status :ineligible :reason :membership-principal-mismatch}

      (not= (:request/organization request)
            (:membership/organization membership)
            (:organization/id organization))
      {:candidate/status :ineligible :reason :organization-mismatch}

      (not= :active (:membership/status membership))
      {:candidate/status :ineligible :reason :inactive-membership}

      (not (true? (:organization/claims-enabled? organization)))
      {:candidate/status :ineligible :reason :claims-disabled}

      :else
      {:candidate/status :eligible
       :request-id (:request/id request)
       :helper-id (:principal/id principal)})))
#+end_src

This simplified policy is illustrative; a richer Organization predicate,
Location relationship or separately permitted manager-selected target-helper
variant has its own declared dependencies. Trusted authentication establishes
the actor, not current Membership or permission to select a different helper.

An =:insufficient-observation= result does not claim there is no helper;
=:invalid-observed-state= identifies inconsistent acquired data, not a
terminal command rejection. A complete collaborator-only collection is
not an active-primary assignment. A terminal Request with no primary
owner is not thereby reopened. Independently acquired model facts
must also satisfy the coherence relation required by the chosen
candidate policy; matching IDs alone is not a common snapshot.

The managed operation binds this candidate to canonical transition construction.
The authoritative realization protects the target, eligibility, associations,
absence/uniqueness of the active primary assignment, and full frame together
with the Request lifecycle change, created primary RequestAssignment, bound
command outcome and retained work required by this Claim's selected
durable publication profile. It must protect competing relationship
insertions, not merely compare the Request revision. A caller gains no
authority by returning ={:candidate/status :eligible}= or by inventing
={:observation/coverage :complete}=.

A candidate rejection from a pre-read is not automatically the final command
outcome. A concurrent attempt might already have incorporated the same command.
The command protocol below resolves that case before it reports authoritative
rejection. Conversely, a positive candidate must be revalidated or protected at
incorporation; it cannot survive arbitrary Membership or policy changes merely
because preparation succeeded.

| Interleaving | Required result |
|--------------+-----------------|
| Membership is deactivated after preparation, before incorporation. | The new Claim cannot incorporate under the old eligibility unless the declared policy expressly permits that temporal meaning. |
| Request moves to another Organization before incorporation. | The stale association cannot justify the assignment. |
| Another helper inserts an active primary RequestAssignment without updating the previously read Request field. | The protected current active-primary predicate prevents two new primary owners; a Request-only version check cannot establish it. |
| A primary-assignment query returns an empty partial page or was scoped to another Request. | Preserve insufficient acquisition/coverage; an empty page cannot justify an absent relationship. |
| A collaborator exists but there is no active primary assignment and the Request is open. | The collaborator is not primary ownership; a legitimate candidate remains possible under the declared model policy. |
| A Request is terminal but has no active primary assignment. | Lifecycle state independently prevents a new ordinary Claim; absence of primary ownership does not reopen terminal business state. |
| The same command already succeeded but its response was lost. | A compatible authorized repetition can recover the recorded success even though the Request is now claimed. |
| This physical attempt aborts while another attempt for the same command commits. | The command is incorporated; this attempt's abort is not command rejection. |

*** API boundaries do not force transaction boundaries

Models may expose composable constraints and effect constructors through public
interfaces, allowing one coordinator to establish a cross-model invariant in a
single atomic transition. Combining those components must check shared target
bindings, incompatible writes, frame overlap, and the conjunction of their
requirements. Concatenating independently prepared write lists is insufficient.

If the participating authorities cannot share the required atomic boundary, the
design must name valid intermediate states and a recovery protocol. A sequence
of individually atomic calls is not an atomic cross-model operation merely
because it appears in one FX vector or choreography step.

HumanHelp Claim's Request, RequestAssignment, command result and selected
durable publication intent share the protected authoritative transition,
including current Membership/Organization dependencies. The public models
expose semantic constraints without handing Choreo their private XTDB forms.
A backend that cannot establish this multi-entity boundary, including the
absence of competing primary assignments, must disclose the open physical
premise or reject the stronger claimed correspondence.

*** Representation and capability acceptance cases

| Positive or adverse construction | Required judgment |
|----------------------------------+-------------------|
| An authenticated eligible helper has a complete, trusted, scoped empty active-primary observation for an open Request | Candidate may be eligible; actual incorporation still needs protected current absence and the whole bound transaction |
| An empty assignment page is incomplete or filtered by a different visibility/query scope | Do not claim the negative relationship; return insufficient observation or use the declared recovery |
| A collaborator exists but is not the active primary owner | Do not reject a valid Claim candidate solely because the collaborator relationship exists |
| A Request projection lacks a helper field while a current active primary RequestAssignment exists | Public RequestAssignment meaning prevails; missing Request helper data cannot authorize Claim |
| Membership becomes inactive or another primary assignment is inserted after preparation | Recheck/protect mutable semantic dependencies in the atomic backend; candidate success confers no authority |
| A full-document replacement preserves checked fields but erases another writer's unrelated edit | Reject frame preservation without qualified full-state/revision/update semantics |
| An old request-local capability or a copied structural admission map is sent to another host | It grants no new production execution authority; issue appropriate fresh scoped capability while preserving command identity |
| Concurrent local users attempt to consume the same one-shot capability | At most one use is admitted for that capability instance; durable command deduplication remains separate |
| A retained work item is processed on another host after its original request capability retired | Admit only via a new authorized worker capability and the original bound work identity; never reuse the retired request authority |
| A background provider-result incorporation uses a source-bound worker entry and freshly authorized guarded local operation | Permit the separately identified worker/operation effect under its qualified owner and backend assumptions; reject a copied web-request capability or an unclassified bypass submit path |


** Command identity and authoritative disposition

A semantic command identifies one intended *semantic action occurrence* under
a declared command policy. It survives physical retry and authorized
reconciliation. It is distinct from the operation definition, any particular
server invocation, choreography execution, backend submission attempt and
transport exchange. One command can have several physical attempts without
becoming several admitted business actions.

| Identity | Meaning and lifetime |
|----------+----------------------|
| Operation identity | The public semantic operation, such as =:request/claim=. |
| Operation interface version | The input, result, and behavioral contract being invoked. |
| Semantic command ID | One bound intent whose disposition can be deduplicated and recovered. |
| Choreography execution ID | One distributed execution and its protocol-local resources. |
| Transport request ID | One HTTP exchange or other delivery attempt. |
| Backend submission-attempt ID | One physical attempt to submit or execute a transaction. |
| Capability instance ID | One issued execution or acquisition right. |
| Resource instance ID | One lifecycle instance of a managed resource or installation target. |
| Principal-context identity | The authenticated context relevant to authority and disclosure. |

Other identities, including deployment and projected-plan compatibility, may be
needed. They must be named for their purpose; a bare universal =generation=
coordinate must not stand in for them all.

A nominal identity wrapper or distinct wire tag is useful for preventing
accidental interchange of =:command-id=, =:execution-id= and
=:transport-request-id=. Its bytes do not authenticate an actor or prove a
durable command exists. A new transport exchange or compatible projected-plan
execution can carry the same command ID; it cannot reuse a retired execution's
resource/installation authority. Conversely, preserving an ID across a
deployment does not automatically make an incompatible old projected plan
safe to resume. Protocol continuation and stored command disposition are
separately admitted.

*** Command binding and canonical inputs

The admitted command boundary supplies or admits a stable =:gesso/command-id=
before the first possible send. A browser may carry an identifier, but choosing
its bytes confers no permission. The trusted boundary establishes the binding
and must preserve it through retransmission and reconciliation.

The command policy defines the namespace in which IDs are unique and the
immutable binding compared on repetition. That binding normally includes:

- authority/tenant and command namespace;
- semantic operation and compatible interface identity;
- canonical semantic arguments and their canonicalization version;
- the principal or explicitly declared delegated authority responsible for the
  intent;
- any domain preconditions that are part of the intent, such as an expected
  entity version.

Command compatibility is not equality of an arbitrary serialized map. Defaults,
omitted fields, numeric representation, ordered versus unordered collections,
and schema evolution require defined canonicalization. If a digest stands in
for canonical arguments, the contract identifies its algorithm and accepted
collision premise; a short convenience hash is not automatically an adequate
binding.

The public command namespace and the physical lookup/uniqueness key must
correspond. A model can choose globally unique IDs or IDs local to an
authority/tenant namespace, but two distinct admitted command identities
must not silently alias the same durable record. Conversely, a second
tenant or principal must not gain a foreign outcome by guessing an ID.
Record identity, full immutable binding, retained disposition and current
disclosure policy have distinct jobs: the record key locates a candidate;
it does not authenticate the caller or establish compatibility. A
canonicalization or interface upgrade must either preserve the admitted
old binding meaning through a declared compatibility relation or keep the
old command unresolved/unsupported with its own recovery; reinterpreting
a retained ID as a new intent is forbidden.

An observed read basis used only as a freshness hint need not be an immutable
semantic argument. An expected version that the user intentionally conditions
the command on is an argument. The interface must distinguish the two. Retry
must not silently turn a conditional command into an unconditional one.

Server-generated timestamps, random identifiers, and other choices also need
declared semantics. Values defining the user's intent or the identity of a
created entity must be stabilized or recoverable as the policy requires.
Transaction-local candidate calculations may be recomputed when permitted, but
their variation cannot change the command binding or invalidate replay.

*** Command identity is not an idempotency policy

Attaching a UUID does not provide deduplication. An operation must declare its
incorporation policy, storage boundary, compatible-repetition behavior,
incompatible-reuse handling, retention horizon, and recovery mechanism.

For Claim, the intended policy is one incorporation for a compatible command
binding. Repetition after incorporation returns a permitted recorded outcome.
Distinct command IDs can still conflict on the same Request; command
deduplication does not replace domain concurrency control.

A fresh Claim must protect *two independent exclusions*: the command
namespace cannot incorporate the same bound intent twice, and the Request
cannot acquire two concurrently active primary RequestAssignments.
Command uniqueness does not prove primary-assignment uniqueness; checking
only the Request's current revision or a node-local command cache does not
protect either property in all implementations. The Request, admitted
primary-assignment change, command record and required durable publication
intent of the selected Claim profile are one protected effect. Every actual
writer capable of violating a relied-upon rule must be included in the
qualified writer/enforcement coverage or treated as an explicit premise.

An operation that intentionally permits repeated independent effects must say
what constitutes a new semantic command. A new delivery attempt is not, by
itself, a new intent. If the product supports repeating an action, the admitted
entry path issues a fresh command for that deliberate action.

The command store need not contain an in-flight row for every implementation.
Its protocol must nevertheless distinguish evidence of incorporation, evidence
of terminal rejection or closure when promised, and the absence of a definitive
result. A transient local registry may assist scheduling; it cannot replace a
durable record where process failure recovery is promised.

The resolution boundary also needs a truthful *acquisition classification*.
A qualified, complete read finding no command record is an observation of
absence at its basis, not an exclusion of a concurrent or pending submission.
An unavailable, stale, unqualified, wrong-namespace or undisclosable
lookup is not such a read. It cannot safely authorize fresh incorporation
or be recast as a public =not-found= existence oracle. Existing-record
disclosure and new-command permission are distinct policy checks:
a compatible replay need not satisfy the now-false preconditions for
*new* incorporation, while any returned historical result still requires
current permission to learn it.

*** Atomic resolve-or-incorporate protocol

The following is a semantic algorithm, not a requirement to implement one giant
callback in the database:

1. Validate the admitted operation, interface, canonical arguments, authority
   scope, and trusted principal. Establish which command binding this caller is
   permitted to invoke or resolve.
2. If a retained command record is found, check binding compatibility and
   current permission to disclose the result. Return the permitted recorded
   disposition without applying the operation again. An incompatible record
   cannot be overwritten or treated as a new command.
3. If no definitive record is established, protect command uniqueness together
   with the conditions for new incorporation. An earlier non-transactional
   absence check is only a hint.
4. At the authoritative boundary, either establish the permitted domain
   transition, command result, and required durable intents together, or do not
   establish that transition. Conflicting concurrent attempts must not each
   incorporate the same command.
5. Interpret the backend result according to its qualified contract. A local
   return that means submitted or queued must not be translated into committed.
6. On contention, an abort, or an ambiguous return, resolve whether another
   attempt established a command disposition. Return only what the admitted
   evidence supports. If the result remains indeterminate, preserve unknown.

The implementation may use retries internally. Such retries retain the semantic
command and must not execute unrepeatable external effects inside a callback
that the backend may rerun.

The preliminary lookup in step 2 is an optimization, not an atomic
absence guarantee. A preliminary record read or candidate ineligibility
judgment can select an attempt's path, but does not settle command disposition
on its own. The protected incorporation/exclusion mechanism and qualified
backend outcome interpretation own that stronger judgment. Compatible replay
must resolve retained results without rerunning mutable eligibility guards
for *new* incorporation. Concurrent compatible invocations may both observe no
record and prepare candidate transitions. The qualified authoritative
mechanism must ensure that at most the policy-permitted incorporation wins
for the command namespace; a loser resolves the surviving record under
its own trusted disclosure conditions, rather than reporting terminal
business rejection solely because its physical transaction aborted.
An incompatible competing binding must not overwrite the winner's
record or enter the old command's authorized replay branch. A distinct
command ID cannot avoid the independent RequestAssignment concurrency
rules. A transaction that committed its domain effect without the
required command record is not a valid realization of this policy.

The same algorithm applies to one or several application nodes sharing
the qualified logical database. On another node, the command retains its
canonical identity and public semantics even if its local execution,
transport request and capability instances differ. A local registry,
cached negative lookup or lagging reader is never the authority for
deduplication; any replay or new submission must use a sufficient
authoritative acquisition/protected incorporation path. Cross-node
router placement and Live invalidation delivery do not define or
strengthen the command's business identity.

*** Command identity and incorporation acceptance cases

| Construction | Required judgment |
|--------------+-------------------|
| An eligible helper sends one Claim and the transaction protects Request, primary RequestAssignment, command result and selected durable work | Admit the new incorporation under qualified guards; the command record and model effects share the actual boundary |
| The same compatible command is retried after a lost response or on another web node | Resolve its retained binding and permitted historical result without constructing another domain or durable-work effect |
| Two same-binding attempts both read an absent command record before one commits | Only the protected incorporation wins; the other attempts compatible resolution and may not report its own abort as terminal command rejection |
| A concurrent attempt reuses one command ID with different target, principal, tenant or canonical arguments | Do not treat a collision as replay or create effects under the first binding; apply typed incompatible/undisclosable handling |
| Two tenants use IDs valid in their separately declared namespaces | Preserve both legitimate identities without physical record-key aliasing, while refusing cross-tenant disclosure |
| Two different command IDs attempt primary Claim for the same open Request | Command deduplication alone is insufficient; the protected RequestAssignment uniqueness/lifecycle policy determines the winner |
| A stale follower or node-local cache sees no command record despite an in-flight or incorporated transaction | Its absence is not definitive; require sufficient authoritative resolution and atomic uniqueness before any new incorporation |
| A successful Claim is replayed after its helper loses Membership or after a later Reassign | Do not reapply new-Claim eligibility guards; return only the permitted recorded outcome, distinct from current RequestAssignment state |
| An old record exists but the caller cannot disclose it or invokes an incompatible projected plan | Preserve command disposition without revealing protected data or reviving retired protocol resources; use the declared reconciliation/upgrade path |
| A canonicalizer upgrade changes argument meaning while an old command record remains retained | Require an explicit versioned compatibility judgment; neither silently recast an old binding as a new one nor fabricate a rejection |
| A command is durably recorded only after its domain write, or a direct writer bypasses protected uniqueness | Reject the stronger exactly-once/atomic correspondence or retain the identified writer premise; a successful test of one wrapper does not close the global writer set |


*** Rejection must name its scope

There are three distinct negative facts:

- preparation found a currently ineligible candidate;
- one physical transaction attempt definitely did not incorporate;
- the semantic command is authoritatively rejected under its declared policy.

Only the last justifies the model outcome =:rejected= as a definitive command
settlement. A successful competing attempt can coexist with either of the first
two facts.

If the operation promises a stable terminal rejection, its mechanism must close
the command against pending and future compatible incorporation. One solution
is an atomic terminal rejection record which contends with the incorporation
record; another is a qualified equivalent closure or fencing protocol. A mere
lookup that finds no record cannot provide this guarantee while a previously
submitted attempt could still commit.

Such closure needs a *linearization or exclusion contract*: the operation must
identify the authoritative command namespace/binding, the physical mechanism
by which an incorporation and a terminal rejection mutually exclude each
other, and the point at which the participant can justifiably learn closure.
A rejection fence that blocks only later entries at one web node does not
exclude an older pending submission at another node. Nor does an absent
record read at basis B necessarily exclude an already submitted transaction
that becomes visible at a later basis. If a model instead promises a weaker
*attempt-local* or *observed-at-basis* denial, it must use a distinct typed
outcome/diagnostic and not pretend to have closed the semantic command.

A permanently denied invocation may be invalid before any authoritative
submission—for example, an untrusted caller with no permission to originate
the command. That denial does not give the caller the authority to create
a durable terminal rejection for another principal's otherwise valid command.
A policy can intentionally settle a command as rejected, but the retained
binding and exclusion mechanism must establish that stronger fact under the
policy's specified lifetime.

A rejected database transaction generally cannot also be assumed to have
persisted its own rejection record. If closure requires a subsequent
transaction, that transaction has its own races and possible uncertainty. It
must resolve against any competing incorporation before the model reports a
definitive rejected command.

This does not require every backend failure or bad request to create a durable
terminal record. Interfaces may expose typed invocation failures or unresolved
attempt diagnostics without claiming terminal command rejection. An operation
with a different rejection lifetime must make that scope explicit and provide
a presentation mapping that does not falsely promise finality.

In particular, the rejection of a transaction because its RequestAssignment
or Membership guard failed is evidence about *that attempted submission*.
It need not be a retained command result. Another compatible attempt may
have incorporated the command, or the application may legitimately allow
a later new invocation under a different command ID after conditions change.
A confirmed failed attempt must not manufacture a terminal rejected
command row in the transaction that aborted, and an unknown return must
not be reclassified as an explicit assertion failure by the caller.

A fresh intentional action after rejection may use a new command ID. Automatic
retry after an unknown result must not do so merely to escape the unresolved
record. An unauthorized or malformed invocation cannot close another party's
otherwise valid command.

*** Retention, upgrades, and expiry

Deduplication guarantees have a declared horizon. Deleting a result while old
messages can still be accepted may permit reintegration of a previously
incorporated command. The design must choose a coherent policy: retain the
necessary record or tombstone, reject commands outside an admitted validity
window, retire the command namespace with a protected fence, or provide an
equivalent mechanism.

Result disclosure retention can differ from incorporation-prevention retention.
A compact tombstone may prevent duplicate effects without retaining sensitive
response data. In that case the public resolver reports only the information
the tombstone and disclosure policy justify; it does not fabricate the original
payload.

The retention policy must cover *delayed already-submitted attempts* as well
as new incoming retries. Expiring a result and deleting its prevention key
does not make an in-flight callback, durable queue entry, offline browser
command, or paused old worker harmless. To reuse or retire the namespace
safely, the authority needs an enforceable validity window, epoch/fence,
retained exclusion record, or an equivalently justified policy preventing
an old attempt from incorporating after its original evidence expires.
A wall-clock TTL checked only by a web handler is not sufficient if the
authoritative backend can still accept an older prepared transaction.

At expiry, the API must distinguish *cannot disclose the previous outcome*,
*cannot establish the previous disposition*, and *the old identity is
authoritatively barred from incorporation*. The latter may be known even
when the former outcome payload has been erased. When the command is still
unknown and old attempts are not safely excluded, a new ID is a new
semantic operation, not an innocuous retry or proof that the old operation
failed. Data-minimizing result erasure and durable duplicate prevention
must be reconciled without silently weakening either promise.

Interface upgrades must specify compatibility for retained commands. Changing
argument canonicalization or the meaning of a policy version cannot silently
rebind an old ID. A database restore, tenant migration, or authority fork must
also define which command namespace and records remain valid. Backend position
numbers alone do not preserve those identities.

A migration must preserve or explicitly retire the authoritative command
exclusion *and* the meaning of its bound operation, principal, authority
scope, canonicalization version, and result-disclosure rule. Merely copying
the latest domain Request and RequestAssignment documents does not copy
historical command disposition or outstanding publication work. A restored
database missing newer command records cannot advertise those commands
as safely absent unless its deployment/recovery protocol separately
fences the previously accepted attempts. An incompatible new
implementation may refuse a continuation, but it must not revive or
duplicate an old effect by calling the retained command a fresh one.

*** Command disposition and participant knowledge
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-command-participant-uncertainty
:END:

The command's authoritative disposition and a participant's knowledge are
different state components. The backend may have committed while the caller
still knows only that delivery failed. =:commit-unknown= describes this missing
knowledge; it does not assert a mysterious third kind of database commit.

Unknown must be preserved when the available evidence cannot distinguish
incorporation from non-incorporation. A timeout, disconnected SSE channel,
retired browser execution, failed response serialization, or exhausted retry
budget cannot turn that uncertainty into rejection.

Definitive knowledge is also subject to authority. A runtime may know that a
record exists while the current participant is not permitted to learn its
contents. The public interface must represent unavailable disclosure without
inventing a domain failure or leaking the existence of protected information.

The authoritative domain state, bound command disposition, and participant
knowledge/disclosure are separate coordinates. An allowed current-state
read may repair a Request card without proving which command produced it.
A permitted command record may prove historical Claim success after later
Reassign without proving who is primary *now*. A nondisclosable command
record remains an authoritative fact while the public participant receives
only its permitted unavailable response. A browser's retired execution
can neither reinterpret that unavailable result as rejected nor regain
rights to settle the old presentation target.

Knowledge must cite its evidence subject and scope: command binding and
namespace, authority and trusted read source, observation basis/coverage,
winning attempt if attribution is claimed, retention or closure policy,
and current disclosure entitlement. A response does not acquire a
stronger judgment because it moved through several HTTP nodes,
reconciliation callbacks or Live messages. Different hosts can have
different knowledge while sharing one logical authority; they may not
apply different business semantics for the command.

*** Rejection, retention, and knowledge acceptance cases

| Construction | Required judgment |
|--------------+-------------------|
| A terminal rejection transaction and a compatible incorporation contend under one admitted command exclusion/closure mechanism | At most the permitted terminal disposition wins; an authorized participant may report rejection only with qualified closure evidence |
| A preparation check finds Membership inactive, with no authoritative command attempt and no retained rejection | Report only the permitted typed precheck/invocation failure; it is not a durable semantic rejection of a command another eligible actor may execute |
| One XTDB assertion fails, but another compatible physical attempt records the bound Claim | Preserve this attempt's non-incorporation and the semantic command's recorded success separately |
| An explicit rejection record is written in a second transaction after the first transaction aborted | Require conflict/closure resolution against any competing incorporation; the aborted transaction itself did not write that record |
| A command result payload is erased but an admitted tombstone/fence still prevents the old ID from incorporating | Permit only a disclosure-limited, truthful resolution; neither fabricate the erased result nor allow a duplicate domain effect |
| A delayed offline or queued retry arrives after deletion of its record and before any enforceable expiry fence | Reject the claimed deduplication guarantee; record expiry alone cannot make the old attempt safe to execute |
| An authenticated, deliberately new command is admitted after an expired prior window that safely bars the old command | Permit the new intent under current domain policy; do not mistake the new command for reconciliation of the old |
| A database backup is restored without a command record for an already executed Claim while old attempts may still arrive | Do not infer authoritative non-incorporation from the restored absence; require restored exclusion, epoch fencing or declared reconciliation/recovery |
| An operation/interface upgrade changes canonical argument meaning while an old command remains unresolved | Enforce explicit compatibility or an unsupported/unknown recovery branch; do not silently reinterpret the old command |
| A caller may read the current Request but lacks permission to see the historical command record | Permit authorized current-state presentation only; do not reveal command existence/result or falsely reject the semantic command |
| A later web node has a stale negative lookup but another node has a qualified retained incorporation result | Treat read scope as an evidence difference, not different business outcomes; require qualified acquisition before claiming a definitive disposition |
| A retired browser execution receives late, valid command-specific success evidence | Preserve the reusable authorized knowledge through a current admission path but never revive the retired execution or install its stale target |


*** Worked example: Claim committed, response lost

Let =R= be the Request ID, not an HTTP request ID. Let =C42= be a stable
semantic command ID, not a choreography execution, transport attempt, or
database transaction ID. The domain argument and the wire command envelope
are distinct:

#+begin_src clojure
(def C42 #uuid "f1bf42ee-7965-4a53-905b-c59038f75e42")

(def claim-command
  {:request-id R
   :command-id C42})

;; Wire representation at the admitted browser boundary.
(def claim-wire-arguments
  {:request-id R
   :gesso/command-id C42})

;; Alice's authenticated identity is NOT a client-authoritative field.
;; The public operation admits/binds it from trusted runtime context.
#+end_src

Alice's original choreography execution is =E1=. The original HTTP delivery
is a distinct transport request =T1=, admitted at a server under Alice's
trusted principal. The selected Claim contract is the durable work-recorded
profile from section 04.02. Its one protected authoritative transition
moves Request R to =:claimed=, creates Alice's *active primary
RequestAssignment* (with primary uniqueness protected independently of
command uniqueness), records C42's complete compatible binding and success
result, and records the required durable publication intent. The transaction
incorporates. The HTTP response conveying this fact is then lost.

The following table uses an omniscient account of the example to distinguish
what *actually happened* from evidence available to each participant. Alice
has not yet learned that incorporation succeeded.

| Subject | Established fact or permitted knowledge |
|---------+-----------------------------------------|
| Authoritative command record | C42 incorporated under its bound principal, operation/interface, Request and canonical arguments; the recorded historical Claim result exists. |
| Current domain at the incorporation point | Request R became =:claimed= and its permitted active primary RequestAssignment identified Alice; later legitimate operations may change either state. |
| Durable publication work | The required work intent was recorded atomically; no notifier receipt, external delivery, browser acquisition or installation follows merely from work recording. |
| Physical transport T1 | Its response was lost after incorporation; T1's delivery failure did not undo the command. |
| Original browser execution E1 | It has no admissible success evidence; its command presentation must remain unknown until admitted resolution. |
| Current browser owner | It may be E1 or a successor with distinct resource-instance rights; no old callback may claim authority over a successor. |

An authorized current projection of Request R together with its active primary
RequestAssignment may establish that Alice is the currently assigned primary
helper at the read's actual basis and scope. It does *not* attribute that
relationship to C42. A different command could have produced the same state,
or C42 could have committed and then been followed by Unclaim, Take-over or
Reassign. A Request-only projection is not the source of primary helper truth.
The command resolver therefore uses retained *bound command-specific* outcome
evidence, not a pattern match on Request status or RequestAssignment.

Even a correctly disclosed current projection and a command record may be
read at different bases: their joint use must qualify any claimed temporal
ordering or causal relation. A stale or incomplete read that finds no command
record cannot prove non-incorporation. The operation may use one appropriately
qualified snapshot or a declared consistency/progression relation; mere proximity
of two reads, or their origin in a shared logical database, is not that relation.

The public resolution path is conceptually:

#+begin_src clojure
(request/resolve-claim!
 trusted-context
 {:request-id R :command-id C42})
#+end_src

This *schematic public operation*, not a claim that the named Clojure helper
already exists, resolves the full immutable binding required by the contract,
including authority/tenant namespace, operation/interface and canonicalization
compatibility, canonical arguments, principal or declared delegation. It uses
a qualified authoritative read, checks the current principal's permission to
request resolution and separately to disclose the historical result. The
request-supplied C42 bytes and =:request-id= are not credentials. Unauthorized
callers must not gain an existence oracle through differences in error wording,
timing-dependent disclosure, or returned binding details.

The resolver's receipt of a retained command record can establish the command's
incorporation without proving that T1, rather than a concurrent compatible
submission, caused it. Nor does a command's incorporation authorize the
current browser owner to install a stale projection. This is not a public
wrapper around unrestricted raw database lookup.

Resolution results distinguish:

| Resolution classification | Meaning |
|---------------------------+---------|
| Resolved incorporation | A compatible retained record justifies the permitted recorded result. |
| Resolved rejection | Qualified command-closure evidence justifies the permitted rejection. |
| Not established | This lookup did not establish a definitive disposition; pending incorporation or unavailable retained evidence has not been ruled out. |
| Unavailable | Acquisition or permitted disclosure could not supply a result. Privacy policy determines how much detail may be returned. |
| Incompatible | The admitted binding conflicts with the retained binding, when this distinction may be disclosed. |

These are resolver classifications, not additions to the canonical model outcome
set. The interface declares how each maps to an operation outcome or typed
invocation error. In particular, not established does not mean rejected.
A stable terminal rejection requires the closure/exclusion evidence of
04.07, not a failed assertion or lookup that merely found no record.
A command-record constructor that persists only incorporation results
does not, by its existence alone, establish durable terminal rejection,
expiry fencing, restored-command exclusion or an executable recovery owner.
Each stronger guarantee needs its own admitted contract and qualified
physical mechanism.

A retained tombstone may establish exclusion of duplicate incorporation
without retaining a disclosable result. A record missing because of
unqualified read, expiry, migration or restored-backup gap does not justify
fabricating the prior outcome or sending a new command under a fresh ID.

A compatible invocation of =request/claim!= can alternatively recover the same
record through its replay branch. It returns =:already-incorporated= and the
permitted success even though the Request is now claimed. It must not rerun the
new-incorporation guard first and reject solely on that changed state.

If another physical attempt finds a compatible, qualified retained success
after its own ambiguous submission, that proves command incorporation under
the record's admitted binding and read scope. It does not necessarily prove
which physical attempt incorporated it. Such evidence may justify the
historical =:already-incorporated= recovery result for an authorized resolver;
an invocation-specific =:committed= needs additional admitted attempt-to-
complete-submission causality. No such record can grant historical disclosure
to an unauthorized principal, and a present Request projection is not a
substitute command-existence oracle.

*** C42 recovery and presentation are independently admitted

The browser has two different useful recovery paths. One asks the public
command resolver about C42 under its current principal and binding. The
other reacquires a currently authorized Request/RequestAssignment
projection at an appropriate basis, to repair the displayed card or board.
The former resolves *historical command attribution* when permitted; the
latter acquires *current business state*. Neither substitutes for the other
when both properties are required. Local author feedback may be adopted
under its admitted command/presentation mapping. Unrelated remote board
changes remain subject to the application's selective-adoption policy;
receipt of an advisory cannot force their installation.

| Recovery observation or event | Required interpretation |
|------------------------------+-------------------------|
| Authorized C42 readback returns the exact compatible incorporation record | Disclose the historical Claim result to the permitted participant; do not construct/submit another Claim or durable work intent. |
| Authorized current Request/RequestAssignment read shows Alice still primary | Permit current-state presentation under its basis, resource-owner and adoption rules, without inferring that C42 was the cause. |
| Current projection shows Bob as primary after a legitimate later Reassign | Keep C42's historical success separate from Bob's current ownership; do not overwrite the current card with Alice's old result. |
| A scoped read reports no C42 record while an earlier T1 submission remains unresolved | Keep the command disposition unknown; absence does not prove a failed command or authorize a new C43 as its retry. |
| A server knows the command record exists but Alice's current authority forbids disclosure | Return only the permitted unavailable/indistinguishable result; neither leak the record nor claim business rejection. |
| A current reconciliation owner receives qualified C42 evidence after E1 retired | May settle surviving command knowledge via its admitted successor mapping; E1 cannot regain callbacks, overlay ownership or DOM installation rights. |
| A Late T1 response or E1 timeout arrives after replacement execution E2 owns the target | Suppress retired owner effects; do not erase a valid newer installation or falsely reject C42. |
| Durable notifier work is still pending after C42 resolution | Recover the original work identity through its declared worker; do not mint duplicate work because the browser requested resolution. |

The successor's admission is not automatic merely because it knows C42.
It must preserve exact operation/binding compatibility, current
principal/disclosure policy, its own resource instance and presentation
contract. Reconciliation evidence useful beyond E1 can be *transferred
as knowledge*, not as E1's retired mutation or installation authority.
This preserves [[#req-owner-relative-event-admission][owner-relative event admission]].

*** Reconciliation needs an owner, not only a lookup

A read-only resolver is useful but is not an executable recovery strategy by
itself. An admitted unknown branch identifies who requests reconciliation, how
the command reference and immutable binding survive local retirement, how
a successor proves its current permission and resource ownership, which
evidence sources are used, when retries are allowed, and what happens when
automatic recovery stops. A retained C42 reference without a current
principal, authority, canonical argument binding and compatibility contract
is insufficient for authorized reconciliation.

The recovery policy may include backoff, bounded automatic attempts, a durable
queue, user-triggered refresh, or operator handling. The safety claim must hold
when progress never occurs: no duplicate incorporation, invented rejection,
privacy leak or retired installation follows from an exhausted retry budget.
A liveness claim additionally names assumptions such as eventual backend
availability, retained command/exclusion evidence, correct authoritative
read visibility, scheduling fairness and continued authorization. A timeout
budget can end automatic work without establishing non-incorporation.

The selected recovery owner must be able to survive the loss of the resource
whose outcome is unknown when the application promises recovery beyond that
resource's lifetime. The specific choice—current browser successor, durable
server worker, operator intervention or another authorized owner—is a plan
decision. An implementation need not run a permanent poller for every
command. Whatever owner is chosen must retain or reacquire the required
identity, backoff/retry state and trust context through a declared mechanism;
a Clojure callback captured in a now-dead HTTP request is not a durable owner.

Static preflight need not predict dynamic conditions. It must nevertheless
require declared handling or recovery paths for conditions that can strand an
operation, including unknown commit, undisclosable replay, incompatible retained
interfaces, and unavailable authoritative acquisition.

Retiring E1 revokes E1's installation and settlement rights according to its
resource policy. It does not cancel C42 or erase its result. A later execution
E2 may reconcile C42 through an admitted participation path. Late evidence must
not revive E1 or install into a resource instance now owned by another
execution.

Cancelling a local request is similarly distinct from an authoritative
cancellation command. Whether an authoritative operation can be cancelled is a
model question with its own valid transitions. Once incorporated, an effect may
require a compensating operation rather than retrospective cancellation.

*** Lost-response acceptance cases

| Case | Required judgment |
|------+-------------------|
| C42 commits the complete Claim transition and durable work while T1's reply disappears | Preserve incorporated authoritative history and pending work; E1 reports unknown until its permitted resolution evidence arrives. |
| C42 readback is allowed and fully bound while Request R has since been reassigned | Disclose the historical success without falsifying current RequestAssignment or rerunning Claim. |
| A complete authorized Request/RequestAssignment projection shows Alice currently primary but has no command attribution | Permit current-state display under its adopted basis; do not conclude C42 incorporated solely from visible ownership. |
| Two compatible physical attempts overlap, one commits and both replies are lost | The retained C42 record can establish command incorporation without proving which attempt won; do not report a failed attempt as terminal command rejection. |
| The command record lookup is stale, out of retention, incomplete, or made on the wrong tenant | Preserve unknown/unavailable as qualified; do not turn absence into rejection or allow a new command to conceal C42. |
| A legitimate current owner E2 reconciles C42 after E1 retires | Permit explicitly mapped surviving knowledge, but deny retired E1 all installation and settlement rights. |
| A stale E1 callback fires after E2 installed a newer current projection | Reject the stale callback's effects without discarding independently valid command-record evidence. |
| Alice may view Request R but may no longer disclose C42's historical result | Keep the current-state and historical disclosure decisions separate; do not leak command existence or invent a rejection. |
| C42 is authoritatively resolved while its original durable notification work remains undelivered | Resume only the retained work through its proper worker; command reconciliation does not prove delivery or create replacement work. |
| A recovery owner's automatic attempts expire while command incorporation remains unproved | Terminate that scheduling attempt honestly with a declared unknown/manual/successor path; neither fabricate certainty nor violate safety. |
| Several web nodes share the same authority, but one read has not reached the necessary basis | Require qualified acquisition/progression for the command evidence; node placement and Live router delivery do not create different business outcomes. |


** External systems and valid intermediate states

One database transaction cannot generally atomically commit a database change
and an arbitrary external provider effect. The model must represent valid
intermediate states and durable recovery rather than describing a remote API
call as if it were part of the database's atomicity guarantee.

The durable-workflow boundary of [[#req-durable-workflow-boundary][Part III]]
applies: a process-local choreography execution is not automatically a durable
owner of unfinished business work.

*** Worked example: placing an order with payment authorization

Suppose the business policy permits an Order to become placed once the provider
has authorized the specified payment. Capture and settlement are separate
effects unless the operation explicitly includes them. This example must not
call an authorization receipt proof of a completed charge.

Money is an amount in a declared currency, with a supported precision and
rounding rule. A bare floating-point number or unqualified decimal is not the
complete payment argument. The immutable payment intent binds the Order,
merchant/provider account, operation type, amount, currency, instrument
reference, and relevant policy/interface versions.

The local public entry operation is conceptually:

#+begin_src clojure
(order/place-intent!
 trusted-context
 {:command-id order-command-id
  :cart-id cart-id
  :payment-instrument-ref instrument-ref})
#+end_src

The authoritative model validates ownership and eligibility of the Cart and
instrument reference, computes or validates the authoritative price, and
protects the inputs required by its pricing and inventory policy. It atomically
creates a valid pending Order, records the command result, and records durable
payment work. The pending Order is a committed domain state, not an optimistic
browser fiction.

For this workflow, invoking the payment provider is a separately admitted
physical effect *after* the immutable intent and durable work have committed.
A callback that the local transaction mechanism may re-evaluate or retry must
not make that external call. Neither a queued job nor a submitted HTTP request
is provider acceptance, and the durable work's owner must survive its original
worker, process, and browser execution.

The Order command's success means that this intent was established. If the UI
wants to wait for final placement, choreography must distinguish intent
acceptance from business completion. It must not display a final placed result
merely because =place-intent!= committed.

*** Provider request identity and evidence

The durable payment intent has its own stable identity. Provider requests use an
idempotency key derived or allocated under the provider's actual key scope and
retention rules. The binding includes the merchant/account, provider operation,
and immutable request meaning. A globally formatted string does not by itself
establish global uniqueness or deduplication at the provider.

The qualification contract states whether repeated requests with that key are
deduplicated, how conflicting payloads are handled, how long records remain,
and how an indeterminate request can be resolved. Provider idempotency is an
external premise; Gesso cannot manufacture it by storing a local key.

If the provider does not supply a sufficient deduplication or lookup contract,
the application must use a recovery strategy appropriate to that limitation.
An unknown effect may require manual reconciliation. Blindly sending a fresh
key after a timeout can create a second charge or authorization.

Provider evidence must be authenticated or otherwise admitted by a qualified
adapter and bound to the exact payment intent. Its interpretation checks the
merchant/account, provider operation, Order or intent reference, key, amount,
currency, and result status. Receiving an untrusted webhook map with a familiar
status string is insufficient. Signature verification alone also does not prove
that the signed event refers to this intent or establishes the required effect.

The adapter must distinguish authorization, capture, decline, pending status,
and an indeterminate transport result. A provider lookup that finds no record
does not establish non-occurrence while an earlier request may still be in
flight, unless the provider contract supplies that stronger guarantee.

If provider deduplication records may expire while local work is unresolved,
reusing the original key does not retain an expired deduplication guarantee.
Automatic submission must stop or follow an independently qualified safe
recovery protocol; allocating a fresh key cannot silently discharge the old
unknown effect.

*** Incorporating a provider result

A worker resolves or submits the provider operation under the durable intent.
It then invokes a public model result-incorporation operation with the admitted
provider evidence. That operation protects the payment intent and current Order
state, records the accepted receipt, makes the permitted domain transition, and
updates durable work in the same local atomic boundary.

This local result-incorporation operation is itself a new authoritative
transition, distinct from the original place-intent command and the remote
provider attempt. Its current guards, provider receipt identity, local command
binding where required, and work-state changes are protected together. If the
result changes an Order projection whose Live freshness is promised, it also
records the corresponding recoverable *publication impact* or qualifies an
equivalent discovery path. Completing the original payment-authorization work
does not establish that the new domain transition's observers were notified.
Conversely, a successfully delivered Live invalidation cannot establish that
the payment provider authorized, captured or settled money. Provider work and
publication work have separate identities, premises and completion criteria.

| Current local state and admitted evidence | Required treatment |
|------------------------------------------+--------------------|
| Payment pending; matching authorization succeeds. | Record authorization and advance according to the placement policy. |
| Payment pending; definitive matching decline. | Record the decline and move to the declared failure or alternative-payment state. |
| Payment pending; response lost or provider result unresolved. | Keep a truthful pending/unknown condition and retained recovery work. |
| Matching receipt already incorporated. | Return a compatible recorded result without applying the effect twice. |
| Receipt conflicts with immutable intent or prior evidence. | Reject or quarantine the evidence through a declared diagnostic/recovery path. |
| Cancellation requested; a late authorization succeeds. | Record the real external outcome and enter the declared void, compensation, or review path. |

*** Worked failure window: provider succeeded, local outcome unrecorded

In the preceding example, the following sequence is a permitted physical
history, not an atomic Order-placement transition:

1. The Order, bound payment intent, semantic command outcome, and payment-work
   obligation commit together. The Order is still =:payment-pending=.
2. A worker sends the admitted authorization request under the retained
   provider key. The provider authorizes the money, but the worker dies before
   the local result-incorporation operation commits; its HTTP reply may also
   disappear.
3. A successor recovers the same durable work and *first* reconciles the
   original provider intent by a qualified lookup or other admitted evidence.
   Verified authorization permits the guarded local incorporation; an
   inconclusive lookup leaves the intent unresolved, with its recovery owner
   intact. A different key is not an innocent retry.
4. A duplicate webhook or another worker's late result may race with this
   incorporation. Guarded, receipt-bound local transitions must prevent a
   second incompatible authoritative effect; they do not independently prove
   that the provider executed only one remote effect.

This history differs from a payment that never reached the provider and from
one whose local authorization receipt was already incorporated. The model and
its diagnostic result must not collapse the three cases merely because all
three can appear as a pending Order to a particular browser.

There is another distinct crash window *after* a qualified provider receipt
is incorporated locally but *before* the resulting Order-change invalidation
is delivered. Recovery here must not call the provider again to regenerate
the publication. The original provider attempt is already reconciled; the
new work is to deliver or reconcile the authoritative local Order change
under its own propagation contract. The application may avoid replaying
individual advisories when a qualified complete authoritative reread covers
the affected scope and frontier. A payment worker's completed flag and an
SSE emitter's successful enqueue are not interchangeable settlement evidence.

Workers may use leases or fencing to prevent stale local writes. Such mechanisms
do not retroactively stop a remote call already issued by an old worker. Late
evidence must still be handled according to the actual external effect and
current model state.

Cancellation therefore needs explicit states such as cancel requested, void
pending, refund pending, or review required when those distinctions matter. A
terminal cancelled label must not cause the system to discard evidence that
money was authorized or captured. Compensation is a new effect with its own
identity, uncertainty, and recovery obligations; it is not an atomic undo of
history.

*** Safety and progress for external work

The system can promise that no Order is reported placed without the required
admitted authorization evidence, under the provider and backend premises. It
cannot promise that every payment finishes merely because a worker exists.
Eventual resolution additionally depends on provider availability, usable
lookup/idempotency behavior, durable scheduling, and any required human action.

Required work must remain discoverable after process loss. Acknowledging a job
because an HTTP call was sent, rather than because the contracted work state
was durably incorporated, can lose the only recovery owner. Queue acknowledgment,
lease expiry, and model work completion require a declared relationship.
 
*** Paired external-effect acceptance obligations

| Admitted scenario | Adverse or unsupported counterpart | Required judgment |
|-------------------+------------------------------------+-------------------|
| The local pending intent and required work commit before the provider call. | A locally retryable transaction callback charges the provider, or the worker starts without durable intent. | Admit the durable boundary; reject the unsafe effect placement or retain a precisely named, unsupported premise. |
| A matching qualified provider authorization is incorporated under current Order guards. | An unsigned or mismatched webhook, wrong amount/currency, wrong merchant, or conflicting receipt claims success. | Admit the bound evidence; reject or quarantine the incompatible evidence without advancing the Order. |
| The provider authorized before the worker crashed; a retained-key lookup supplies conclusive evidence. | A lost response is treated as decline or automatically retried with a fresh provider key. | Reconcile the original intent; never manufacture rejection or a second authorization. |
| Compatible provider retries fall within its established key scope and retention contract. | Retention expires or deduplication/lookup semantics are unknown. | Use only qualified deduplication; otherwise retain unknown and the declared safe recovery or human-review path. |
| Duplicate compatible provider callbacks race to incorporate one result. | Conflicting, reordered, or late receipts target changed Order state. | Protect local incorporation and retain the real external history; local idempotence cannot imply remote exactly-once execution. |
| Cancellation and late provider success follow declared void/compensation policy. | Cancellation erases a real authorization or treats compensation as transaction rollback. | Admit the appropriate new transition and outstanding work; reject false historical erasure. |
| Durable work is discoverable after worker or node loss. | Queue acknowledgment precedes durable settlement and removes the only recovery owner. | Preserve replayable work or an equivalently qualified discovery guarantee. |
| Provider authorization is locally incorporated and its separate required Order-projection publication is recoverable. | The worker dies after local receipt incorporation, and replay incorrectly charges the provider again or treats a sent SSE frame as proof of payment. | Resume only the outstanding publication/acquisition obligation; keep provider-effect settlement distinct from Live delivery. |
| Resolution is conditional on provider availability and admitted reconciliation. | The system asserts unconditional eventual completion with no provider or human-progress premise. | State the liveness assumptions; unsupported completion is not a successful check. |
** FX value flow and lifecycle

FX should make value dependencies and effects inspectable while remaining usable
from ordinary Clojure. Pure calculations consume explicit values and return
explicit values. Effectful steps declare their inputs, outputs, failure classes,
and effect boundary. A context is a capability-bearing environment, not a
hidden bus into which every step writes arbitrary intermediate state.

*** Context and values have different roles

The context may contain admitted database/acquisition capabilities, a trusted
principal binding, clocks or randomness with declared semantics, tracing, and
scoped backend interfaces. Intermediate Request data, candidate decisions,
prepared transitions, and outcomes should normally flow as explicit arguments
and results.

This permits the system to explain which value justified a step and to detect
missing or mismatched inputs. It also reduces accidental use of stale ambient
data. The machine's original invocation argument and its subsequent state
outputs are distinct value categories. A later step must receive needed
invocation values explicitly, through a declared retained-value binding or
other admitted value path; it cannot assume that the ambient context silently
accumulated them. The same applies to graph or multistate resolvers.

A capability's presence in context does not extend its lifetime or widen
its authority. Authentication state captured at entry does not freeze mutable
authorization unless that temporal policy is explicitly part of the contract.
The trusted invocation boundary owns capability provenance: an untrusted input
map may not overwrite a principal, clock, database handle, or other privileged
context binding through an incidental map merge. Missing required capability
bindings known at construction must be rejected before invocation; effective
runtime authentication, availability, expiry and current policy still require
their appropriate dynamic checks.

Graph or multistate implementations obey the same rule. They may provide a
convenient evaluation strategy, but hidden mutable cells must not become an
untracked second model of operation state. Pure dependency nodes and effectful
nodes require distinct semantics, including recomputation and retry behavior.
Passing a client value through a trusted FX step does not upgrade it into an
authoritative fact. A query result passed to another step does not become
knowledge of a remote choreography participant until its admitted communication
or acquisition path actually establishes that knowledge.

*** Ordering and completion are explicit

An ordered effect description specifies what each step waits for, how values
flow, and what happens after failure. Merely placing effects in a vector is not
enough if the interpreter may run them concurrently or treat enqueue as
completion.

An illustrative descriptor could distinguish:

#+begin_src clojure
{:fx/type :sequence
 :steps [:admit-command
         :resolve-or-prepare
         :incorporate-authoritative-transition
         :construct-response]
 :failure-policy :phase-aware}
#+end_src

The registered step definitions supply types, branches, capability requirements,
and completion semantics. The map alone is not an executable operation. In
particular, an effect's vector-valued argument is not implicitly an ordered
list of effects. An effect-order edge only orders execution as specified; it
cannot itself establish transaction atomicity, durable completion, or remote
receipt. The public operation advertises typed inputs, outcomes, authority,
pre/postconditions, effect classes and failure semantics without requiring
Choreo to inspect its private FX state graph.
Independent publication work may proceed separately once durable intent exists;
it need not delay an otherwise justified commit response unless the public
contract explicitly promises that stronger acknowledgment.

Exceptions are interpreted by phase and evidence. A failure before submission,
an ambiguous failure during submission, a post-commit publication failure, and
a response-rendering failure are different events. A generic catch that turns
all four into =:rejected= violates the operation contract.

| Phase and available evidence | Permitted conclusion |
|------------------------------+----------------------|
| Preparation fails before any possible submission. | The attempt did not submit; this alone is not a durable terminal command disposition. |
| Submission or response is lost without conclusive incorporation evidence. | The command disposition remains unknown pending admitted reconciliation; no blind new-command retry. |
| Commit is established, but publication or dispatch fails. | Preserve committed domain and command truth, classify the delivery failure, and retain required recovery debt. |
| Commit is established, but rendering or reply delivery fails. | Preserve the command outcome; the participant may lack settlement knowledge and needs its permitted resolution path. |

A later compatible command replay, a delivery-work retry, and a browser's
fresh acquisition are different operations. Retrying notification must never
re-execute the original authoritative mutation merely to make an observer
current.

Every submitted effect path—including an exceptional fallback, deferred FX
continuation and worker retry—must be admitted for the **actual complete**
transaction and its trusted caller/worker authority. A registered step graph
does not establish which callbacks or transaction options were used by the
installed runtime. The selected checker can derive the effect before issuing
an executor, enforce a final gate immediately before backend submission, or
admit a qualified opaque implementation premise. It cannot claim full
transaction correspondence merely because the initial step order is valid.
Likewise, a worker's legitimate recovery of a publication item authorizes the
promised delivery effect, not an unrelated replay of the earlier domain write.

Retryable backend callbacks must not contain unprotected external effects.
Clock and random values used in semantic decisions need declared sampling and
retry semantics. A clock or randomness facility is an ambient capability; its
particular sampled value is a semantically relevant result whose origin and
reuse across retries must be explicit. Ordinary Clojure functions and macros
may implement these steps, but any opaque effect footprint remains an explicit
contract premise.

*** Post-commit delivery debt
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-post-commit-recovery
:END:

Once a command commits, failure to notify, render, or deliver does not uncommit
it. Required later work becomes explicit delivery or recovery debt. The
operation contract identifies what must be durable, who owns it, how it is
retried, and what counts as completion.

For a durable publication requirement, the domain transition and publication
intent must share an atomic boundary or an equivalently qualified discovery
mechanism. A commit followed by an unprotected best-effort publish can leave a
permanent gap if the process dies between those actions.

The intent binds the authoritative effect, relevant subject/query impact,
progression requirement, and stable delivery/work identity. It need not capture
every currently connected subscriber. A durable change log or outbox can feed
later routing and interest matching. The required impact must be derivable from
admitted source-owned change meaning and the selected application contract,
not solely from the subscribers known to one process at commit time. Matching
later-installed interests to that retained impact, or instead closing the
initial-read/subscription gap through a qualified acquisition fence, must
satisfy the promised awareness and recovery scope. This does not require
persisting a per-subscriber acknowledgment for every broadcast or retaining
every unrelated database change forever. Delivery semantics must state whether
duplicates are possible and how consumers recognize compatible repetition.

An in-memory list of local interests is not proof that all affected participants
will learn a change. If publication is conditioned on known interests, the
design must cover remote interests, subscription races, disconnects, and router
failover to the extent that its convergence claim includes them. Otherwise that
claim must be limited explicitly. The particular routing topology belongs to
the transport design; the no-lost-required-work obligation belongs here.

In particular, losing the process between authoritative commit and the first
in-memory publication must be covered whenever automatic notification is
promised. A durable change log, work intent, qualified reconciling acquisition,
or another declared mechanism may satisfy the relevant obligation; a live SSE
connection alone cannot. On subscription inception or reconnect the observer
must have an admitted way to acquire a sufficiently qualified state or recover
missed interests. If the only repair is user-initiated Refresh, the convergence
claim is explicitly conditional on that action.

Publication completion also has a scope: queued, durably recorded, accepted by a
broker, delivered to a connection, admitted by a participant, and adopted by a
projection are distinct milestones. The contract names the milestone it can
observe and retains downstream obligations where required. A work item may be
retired at a narrower milestone *only if* remaining promised obligations have
qualified independent owners, coverage or recovery. Replaying a publication
work item must not repeat the domain transaction whose impact it announces.
The same rule applies to a provider-result incorporation that creates new
publication impact: payment-work completion, local receipt incorporation and
Order-view dissemination are distinct settlements.

Recovery failure may leave debt visible and unresolved. It must not manufacture
success, discard the command record, or turn the committed domain operation
into a rejection. If an effect is intentionally best effort, its omission and
the resulting weaker guarantee must be part of the public contract.

** Authoritative basis and XTDB

An authoritative basis describes the database observation context justified by
the backend adapter. It is not a domain entity version, command ID, execution
ID, or universal time coordinate. A basis may advance because unrelated data
changed. Equal entity content at different bases does not make the bases equal,
and a changed basis does not by itself make a command's business preconditions
false.

XTDB integration should use XTDB-specific consistency mechanisms where they
provide useful guarantees. Gesso need not invent a backend-neutral global
revision system merely for speculative portability. It must describe the
selected mechanisms through a qualified adapter contract rather than inventing
generic arithmetic on opaque tokens or claiming guarantees from a dependency's
name alone.

The [[#req-backend-assumption-qualification][backend qualification contract]] of
Part I applies. A Gesso proof may be conditional on specific XTDB premises. It
need not prove the whole database implementation, but it must make visible
exactly which database behavior would invalidate the proof.

*** Basis provenance and the untrusted command boundary

An =AuthoritativeBasis= is established by an admitted authoritative read or
trusted runtime boundary. A =ProgressionRequirement= is a scoped obligation on
a later acquisition. An =ObservedBasisClaim= is what an untrusted participant
says it previously observed. They may carry identical XTDB token bytes, but
they are not interchangeable semantic capabilities or a demand for three
wrapper classes.

For example, a browser command may include =:observed-basis= to help request
consistent reads or explain what it believed. Parsing the token, comparing it
to a server-issued value, accepting its Malli shape, or trusting a client-added
=:authoritative= label does not prove that the browser received, installed, or
understood that observation. If this history matters, a separately admitted
receipt or trusted server-side record must establish the precise historical
proposition it supports; issuance still does not prove delivery, installation,
human acknowledgement, or current business authorization.

An untrusted basis claim may only influence a read through its validated
provider, authority/database incarnation, scope and permitted consistency
mode. Invalid or unsatisfiable claims fail under declared typed handling rather
than weakening the authoritative read. A command's current business guards are
determined by source-owned operation policy, not by whether its browser token
happens to match the most recent database frontier.

*** Backend assumptions are distinct obligations

The adapter/profile identifies the loaded backend version and relevant build,
configuration, transaction API, query API, value codec, temporal options, and
Gesso realization. A supported version range requires evidence of compatibility
under its declared policy; it is not an invitation to ignore version changes.

| Premise | Required meaning |
|---------+------------------|
| Atomic publication | The protected domain changes, command record, and required durable intents are established together or not established as that transition. |
| Dependency protection | The semantic predicates, including cross-document, absent-row, or aggregate conditions where used, hold at the declared incorporation boundary. |
| Representation and frame | Encodings, field presence, update semantics, and untouched data correspond to the admitted transition. |
| Command exclusion | Competing compatible attempts cannot each incorporate the same command under the declared namespace and retention policy. |
| Outcome interpretation | The qualified return or later evidence actually establishes the claimed committed, rejected, or unresolved status. |
| Read visibility | The read mechanism honors the declared progression and temporal requirement, and its observation evidence reports what was established. |
| Durability and recovery | Retained command and work evidence survives the failures included in the promise. |
| Writer coverage | Every writer capable of invalidating a dependent invariant follows the required discipline or is covered by an explicit external premise. |

These premises are not interchangeable. Atomic writes do not prove predicate
protection. A row count does not prove target or principal binding. A committed
transaction ID does not prove an exact immediate poststate unless the backend
contract supplies that relationship. A source-bound local return does not
prove all remote backend semantics. An external pinned pre-read plus a later
pinned post-read does not establish the transaction's internal snapshot or that
no unenumerated writer changed a protected dependency.

The adapter must specify the actual protected mechanism for each supported
operation class. It must not imply that arbitrary Clojure callbacks execute
inside every XTDB transaction, or that a particular SQL construction has a
stronger isolation meaning than its qualified contract establishes.

Writer coverage is an independent **deployment-scoped** premise, not a
conclusion from the number of registered model operations or successful local
gateway submissions. The covered physical inventory includes effective web
handlers, internal calls, imports, migration/repair entrypoints, scheduled
jobs, provider callbacks and any external writer able to change protected
state. Coverage is established by qualified installed binding and backend
access restrictions, by sufficiently supported independent inventory
evidence, or under an explicit accepted external-writer assumption. An
unknown inventory cannot be treated as an empty set. A non-route worker does
not need a fabricated HTTP handler, but its actual installed callback and
backend mutation rights belong to the same coverage argument.

A reported check applies to the exact active application/deployment, backend
profile, executor/adapter implementation and protected operation family.
Replacing a Ring/Biff handler after the checked installation, registering a
new raw writer or changing an effective backend hook invalidates the
dependent closure claim until its relationship is re-established. This need
not revoke unrelated established claims; it must not preserve a stale
application-wide certificate. Part VII owns the comprehensive installation
graph; Part X owns the stronger physical refinement proof. This XTDB premise
alone neither discovers hidden writers nor proves actual deployment adoption.

*** Qualification and conformance evidence

Qualification combines the declared backend contract, implementation binding,
checked Gesso-side correspondence, and targeted conformance evidence. Useful
tests include positive cases, deliberately weakened guards, altered targets,
extra submitted writes, representation edge cases, contention, and lost or
ambiguous returns.

Finite tests can falsify assumptions or detect incompatibilities; they cannot
prove that no current or future XTDB behavior violates a premise. Gesso must
therefore avoid promising that qualification will detect every possible backend
defect. Its defensible promise is that assumptions are explicit, evidence is
bound to the actual supported profile, relevant changes invalidate or trigger
requalification, and observed violations propagate to dependent assurance.

Qualification should target the behavior on which proof depends. A dependency
version string check is useful configuration evidence, but is not a semantic
conformance test. Likewise, a passing abstract transition model does not test
the actual adapter's transaction construction or backend interpretation.
Regression evidence must include the scope- and failure-sensitive boundary:
read-after-write progression, snapshot-token propagation, relevant guards
under contention, absence and aggregate dependencies, representation/field
preservation, valid-time selection, and unknown submission or readback outcomes.
A passing positive readback without paired mutated or competing-writer controls
is insufficient to certify the protected operation family.

If a required premise is violated or its support becomes unavailable, the
affected proof claim cannot remain unqualified. Admission policy may stop the
affected operation, accept a named weaker assurance class, or require operator
action. It must not silently keep the old certified label. In-flight commands
whose outcomes are uncertain still require resolution; qualification failure
does not establish their rejection.

*** Acquired prestate and actual transaction state

An acquired prestate can support preparation and construction checks. It is not
automatically the backend's internal prestate at the later incorporation point.
The protection mechanism must establish the needed relationship despite that
interval. If a prepared whole-document update can overwrite fields that were
not in the acquired predicate set, the declared frame and dependency protection
must cover those writes or explicitly narrow the proven operation class. A
version check on one document is not automatically a guard on absent rows,
related documents, aggregates, or unrelated writers.

The design does not universally require the backend to expose an internal
prestate token. A sufficient qualified guard, isolation contract, or conditional
transition may establish the required refinement without such a token. Where
that relationship is only a backend premise, it must remain named as such.

Reading before and after a concurrent transaction also does not necessarily
produce its exact state pair. Another writer may intervene. A read guaranteed
to include a commit can legitimately include later effects. That is useful
visibility evidence, but not an exact immediate poststate witness.

*** Commit evidence, visibility requirements, and observations

Submission acceptance, transaction completion, and query visibility may be
different backend milestones. The adapter must distinguish them. A log position
allocated to an aborted or merely submitted transaction must not be presented
as evidence that the domain command incorporated. A returned submission ID,
a completed transaction key, and a pinned read that observes a command record
are different evidence subjects; their association must be justified rather
than assumed from matching metadata.

An observation may establish that a bound command record exists, or that
an identified transaction completed, without proving that this invocation's
*complete submitted footprint* caused that record. The adapter must distinguish
(a) historical bound command incorporation, (b) identified transaction
completion, and (c) the physical association among the admitted invocation,
full effective submission and resulting record. (a) supports an authorized
compatible recovered-result branch; (c), under the declared backend completion
guarantee, is needed for attempt-attributed =:committed=. Without (c) the
narrower observations remain useful but must not be promoted into an
unqualified invocation outcome by a copied gateway report.

After a confirmed commit, the adapter may issue a progression requirement that
an authoritative read must satisfy to include the relevant effect. The read
path must enforce that requirement or return a typed unavailable/unsupported
result. It must not silently read from an older available snapshot and label it
current enough.

The actual observation can be later than the minimum requested basis. The
evidence must distinguish a minimum visibility guarantee from an exact observed
basis. It must not relabel the requested minimum as the exact snapshot used.
If the adapter can establish only a lower bound, the observation contract says
so and permits only conclusions justified by that bound.

Temporal interpretation matters separately. A transaction can be visible in
system time while a query at a different valid time returns a different domain
state. Authority/database incarnation, tenant, valid-time selection, system-time
selection, and relevant authorization context are part of scope compatibility.

A restore, fork, or migration may create an authority/database incarnation in
which old tokens have no comparable meaning. The adapter must establish an
explicit migration relation or reject comparison. Equal-looking numbers or
token bytes from different incarnations are not evidence of continuity.

*** Worked example: database advancement versus business conflict

At an admitted basis X17, an authorized browser observes Request R unclaimed
and constructs command C42. At X18 a different Membership changes; at X19
another Request changes. When C42 reaches authority at X23, that advancement
alone is not a reason to reject Claim. The source-owned Claim operation must
check R's current lifecycle, active primary RequestAssignment uniqueness,
membership/authorization dependencies and the command's retained identity
under its protected incorporation contract. If an intervening helper actually
claimed R, the relevant domain facts differ and the operation applies its
specified conflict or reconciliation policy. X17 and X23 are illustrative
opaque basis symbols, not integers on which the browser may calculate.

The same X17 bytes returned by the browser remain an =ObservedBasisClaim=;
they do not certify that its display was installed. A read satisfying an
admitted minimum X23 requirement may observe a later frontier and perhaps later
authoritative changes. It may justify visibility through X23 but cannot, solely
by that fact, prove C42's exact immediate poststate or its incorporation.

** Scope-aware progression and authoritative acquisition
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-progression-scope
:END:

A progression requirement asks for an observation of a particular meaning at a
sufficient authoritative frontier. It is not merely a token. A later Hotel
observation cannot satisfy a Flight obligation simply because the database
position appears newer.

An admitted acquisition declaration must make the following information
mechanically available, directly or through stable references:

- authority, tenant, database incarnation, and qualified provider;
- entity or query meaning, schema/projection version, and parameters;
- selection, filters, ordering, pagination, and coverage requirements;
- principal/visibility context and the policy for current disclosure;
- valid-time and system-time interpretation;
- the required progression relation and its evidence source;
- the acquisition owner and resource-instance correlation;
- the requested completion milestone: acquisition alone, or acquisition with
  permitted installation/adoption.

An acquisition result includes evidence of its actual scope and coverage, not
just the data. A familiar shape, a token supplied by the browser, or a tag saying
=:authoritative= is not enough to admit it. Admission follows the trusted
acquisition contract from Part II.

The evidence must relate the *executed* read to its effective query meaning,
including parameters after trusted validation, authorization/visibility
conditions, selected projection, actual returned coverage and read basis. A
declared route, correct result shape, or successful HTTP response is not by
itself proof that the installed handler ran the declared query or returned a
complete authorized result. A reused cached/singleflight projection needs its
own justified provenance, read-scope compatibility and current disclosure
permission; reuse does not grant a broader scope or a newer frontier.

*** Query meaning and completeness

Entity contents and aggregate membership are different scopes. A Request card
can establish facts about one Request without establishing the complete board.
A board acquisition must identify its authorized collection selection,
effective *server-side* predicates, projection, page/cursor interpretation,
and completeness claims. Where the server filters, searches, sorts or pages,
those inputs are part of the executed query's declared coverage.

Changing a server-side search or filter changes the query meaning. Sorting or
filtering an already adopted cohort in the browser changes only presentation;
it cannot narrow or broaden the authoritative membership acquisition, advance
its basis, or discharge collection-adoption debt. Neither kind of change
silently grants permission to adopt unrelated new rows. For HumanHelp's
selected board policy, Organization/Location and bounded cohort identify
authoritative collection scope; text search, display order, mine-first,
unclaimed-first and show-terminal are local presentation options, not server
query inputs. A future remote search or page acquisition needs its own
declared authoritative query and coverage contract.

An empty query result establishes absence only within the successfully evaluated
authorized scope and coverage. A partial response, timeout, inaccessible page,
or denied query must not become authoritative absence. Privacy policy may limit
which distinctions can be disclosed, but it cannot make a failed acquisition
evidence of a domain fact.

One page can satisfy a requirement for that page under a stable query/cursor
contract. It cannot satisfy a complete-board requirement merely by carrying a
sufficiently recent token. A set of separately acquired pages establishes a
complete common snapshot only when the paging contract supplies that stronger
relationship. In particular, page windows gathered under different query
parameters, visibility permissions, cursor generations or incompatible bases
cannot be combined into a complete result by counting rows. Changing sort
order may change page membership, even when it does not change the underlying
authorized relation. A valid empty page is not automatically an empty board.

*** Opaque progression and comparison judgments

The progression provider owns token interpretation. Generic Gesso code may
preserve opaque values and ask the admitted provider for supported relations;
it must not infer ordering from string comparison, integer-shaped encodings, or
HTTP arrival order.

Comparison distinguishes at least equivalent, advances, precedes, established
incomparable, and not established. Invalid or incompatible evidence is also a
typed failure. Unavailable comparison is not established incomparability, and
neither is equality.

For some providers, equality is the only available relation without additional
evidence. Equality of admitted tokens in a compatible context can be useful;
equality of untrusted bytes is still just equality of bytes. An advancement
witness must be admitted from a provider capable of establishing that relation
and bound to its endpoints, scope, incarnation, and relevant contract version.
Validating the witness map's shape alone does not establish its truth.

An unmet minimum, unavailable provider comparison, incompatible incarnation,
or unsupported temporal interpretation must not be reported as a satisfactory
read. The boundary may give a typed unavailable/unsupported verdict or use a
declared bounded retry/recovery policy; it must not silently drop the required
frontier, infer a relation from arrival order, or wait forever without a
contracted resource limit. A proof of progression between token values does
not itself prove the acquisition's query scope or authorization.

*** Worked example: travel observations with different scopes

Consider two requirements for one authorized travel-planning session:

| Requirement | Scope and coverage | Progression requirement |
|-------------+--------------------+-------------------------|
| F | Flight 17 availability, specified cabin and fare-class selection, projection v1, session visibility context, declared temporal policy. | Visibility through admitted flight requirement PF20. |
| H | Hotel 9 availability for check-in D1 and check-out D2, specified room/occupancy selection, projection v1, the same permitted context where applicable. | Visibility through admitted hotel requirement PH24. |

The symbols PF20 and PH24 are illustrative opaque values. Their numeric-looking
suffixes do not authorize arithmetic. Even if both are realized by one XTDB
database, scope remains part of the requirement.

Suppose the provider and acquisition boundary establish the following evidence:

| Observation | What it can establish |
|-------------+-----------------------|
| Full Flight 17 projection, correct cabin/fare selection, compatible time/context, admitted advancement through PF20. | Satisfies F. |
| Hotel 9 projection with advancement through PH24. | May satisfy H; it does not satisfy F. |
| Flight 17 seat count alone at a newer compatible basis. | Satisfies only an appropriately narrow seat-count requirement, not the full F projection. |
| Hotel 9 for different dates. | Does not satisfy H even if its basis is later. |
| Correct hotel query, first page only, when H requires complete offered-room coverage. | Does not satisfy H's completeness obligation. |
| Correct values with an unadmitted basis tag. | No authoritative satisfaction judgment follows from that tag. |
| Correct selection at incompatible valid time or visibility context. | Requires a declared relation; it is not silently interchangeable with the requested scope. |

F and H may be fulfilled by separate admitted acquisitions. That does not
establish that the combined travel display is one atomic database snapshot, or
that either offer remains reservable. Booking eligibility remains a current
authoritative model obligation at the booking boundary.

*** Worked example: HumanHelp board membership versus one Request card

Suppose an authorized HumanHelp board query at admitted basis P17 establishes
membership of the declared Location cohort, including active and terminal
Requests within its bounded authorized scope. Local search, sort and
show-terminal choices may hide or reorder the displayed members without
changing that acquisition. A later correlated authoritative operation
result or reread at P19 establishes a newer projection
for Request R, with current lifecycle and authorized active primary
RequestAssignment. The card may be displayed according to the declared local
adoption policy while the board still has its P17 membership coverage. The
actor cannot call this a complete board acquisition through P19; one card
does not prove inclusion, exclusion, ordering, or absence of all other Requests.

A later P19 full-board acquisition can justify new membership only if the
actual executed selection, visibility context, temporal interpretation,
completeness and current admission evidence match that requirement. Even then,
acquisition is not automatic visible adoption: an advisory Refresh policy may
require the current owner or user to approve replacing the board. Likewise,
a successful HTMX response whose target retired or whose swap was denied is
not evidence that the P19 board was installed. A retained P17 board need not
become false merely because some other scoped evidence advanced.

*** Requirement satisfaction and safe combination
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-progression-combination
:END:

The executable semantic core defines the following relations for each supported
scope and progression contract. The notation does not prescribe API names.

- =satisfies(O, R)=: admitted observation O establishes R's scope, coverage, and
  consistency requirements.
- =entails(R2, R1)=: every admitted observation satisfying R2 also satisfies R1.
- =combine(R1, R2)=: a representation retaining both requirements, or an explicit
  unsupported result where the requested representation cannot do so.
- =fulfills(E, B)=: the admitted evidence collection E fulfills every obligation
  in bundle B at each obligation's declared completion milestone.

The default conservative combination is conjunction: retain both obligations.
A provider may offer a sound compact representative or join. A successful
single-observation combination must satisfy the law:

#+begin_example
satisfies(O, combine(R1, R2))
  implies satisfies(O, R1) and satisfies(O, R2)
#+end_example

When no one observation can satisfy both, a bundle may allow separate
acquisitions. Bundle fulfillment does not imply a common snapshot. It also does
not erase the distinction between acquiring evidence and adopting it.

For a bundle B, =fulfills(E, B)= requires each currently outstanding obligation
to have a specifically admitted witness in E meeting its *own* query scope,
minimum frontier, authorized visibility and declared completion milestone. The
witnesses need not be one read, but no witness can be counted twice for
incompatible scopes merely because its token looks sufficiently new. An
unsupported compact join does not make either original obligation disappear;
the conservative bundle remains the safe alternative when it is representable.

Dropping R1 because R2 supersedes it requires established entailment, including
compatible scope and coverage. A later-looking token for another query is not
such evidence. Unknown comparison does not authorize dropping either obligation.
Entailment is a claim about *all admitted satisfying observations* under the
applicable provider and semantic versions, not a guess drawn from a few equal
payloads or previous request completions. Any proof or provider-qualified
subsumption rule must account for scope, authorization, query version,
valid/system-time selection, coverage and adoption milestones. Evidence that a
particular observation satisfied two requirements is not by itself a proof that
those requirements entail one another in every future state.

Normalization preserves semantic meaning and is deterministic where stable
identity depends on it, under the declared semantic/provider versions. If the
implementation coalesces duplicates, reorders, or regroups requirements, it must
establish the corresponding idempotence, commutativity, or associativity of
obligation meaning. These need not be claims of byte-identical representation.

A stronger combined requirement may preserve safety while making acquisition
impossible. A liveness claim must therefore establish that the strengthened
requirement is satisfiable by the admitted read path under its declared progress
premises. Soundness is not permission to promise eventual acquisition of an
impossible join. If no admitted read can satisfy the proposed common scope or
basis, Gesso must retain separately satisfiable obligations where supported or
report a typed unsupported configuration, rather than replacing achievable
requirements with an impossible one and continuing to promise progress.

*** Bounded representation and backpressure

One in-flight request does not imply bounded retained debt. A coordinator may
accumulate incomparable scopes or requirements while that request is pending.
Its bounds must distinguish request concurrency, obligation count, representation
size, and any finite abstraction used in model checking.

For a finite scope domain with sound subsumption, one representative per scope
may suffice. Otherwise the implementation may retain bundles, partition work,
apply backpressure, request an authorized broader acquisition, or report an
unsupported configuration. It must not silently discard obligations to make a
counter stay below a limit.

Broader acquisition requires permission and a coverage contract. It does not
automatically authorize broader visible adoption. A user who has not chosen to
refresh board membership must not acquire a new visible board merely because
the coordinator compacted its metadata.

Resource retirement may legitimately end obligations scoped solely to that
resource, under an explicit retirement policy. It must not erase durable
command uncertainty or work owned elsewhere. The reason for retiring an
obligation is part of its lifecycle semantics, not a claim that it was satisfied.

Backpressure must classify what is being bounded: physical in-flight requests,
queued wakeups, retained distinct requirements, serialized obligation size,
resource owners or the abstract states used in checking. A one-request limit
bounds only concurrency; infinitely many new incomparable query scopes can
still accumulate. A finite verification model must declare an abstraction and
why it preserves each checked safety or progress property. Queue eviction,
timeout, failed reads, cancelled swaps and owner replacement require explicit
retention, transfer, qualified retirement or an unsupported result; counters
reaching zero are not evidence of fulfillment.

*** Mixed observations do not imply an aggregate snapshot

A board may retain membership justified at P17 while one Request card is
updated through a later correlated operation result at P19. That can be a valid
application state under its adoption policy. It is not a complete board snapshot
at P19.

The representation must preserve enough provenance to support only the claims
actually established. It need not use a universal persisted fact graph, one
mutable object per fact, or a per-entity integer revision. It does need to
distinguish the scope of the later card observation from the older board
membership observation.

Replacing an adopted projection requires its declared supersession or
reconciliation relation. An incomparable observation may remain useful evidence
without being eligible to replace the current display. Equivalent bases also
do not make different projection schemas, principals, or query meanings
interchangeable.

*** Worked example: a later advisory during an in-flight Refresh

An authorized complete HumanHelp board starts a managed acquisition A1 with
outstanding obligation O1: acquire and adopt the current board query through
provider-admitted frontier P18. While A1 is in flight, another relevant advisory
adds O2 for the *same* query and adoption milestone, through P20.

A1's originally captured O1 is its request-start justification; it does not
freeze the set of outstanding obligations. On completion, the coordinator must
evaluate the *current* obligation set against the actual admitted observation
and its installation evidence. For the compatible board scope:

| Completion evidence from A1 | O1 | O2 |
|-----------------------------+----+----|
| Complete board acquired through P18 and actually installed. | Discharged. | Remains pending unless a separate admissible witness satisfies P20. |
| Complete board acquired through P21, with provider-established advancement through both P18 and P20, and actually installed. | Discharged. | Discharged. |
| Complete board through P21, but the current browser target retired before installation. | Remains for an acquire-and-adopt milestone. | Remains for an acquire-and-adopt milestone. |
| HTTP completion without admitted query, basis or coverage evidence. | Remains pending. | Remains pending. |

A =:acquire-only= obligation may be discharged by sufficient admitted
acquisition even when installation is denied; =:acquire-and-adopt= requires
actual permitted adoption. The names P18/P20/P21 are illustrative opaque
frontiers, not comparable integers. If provider evidence cannot establish
advancement, the coordinator retains the obligations despite numeric-looking
suffixes.

This example does not dictate one browser queue layout. It requires lossless
semantic accounting across the period in which new obligations arrive after
the physical request begins. A failed or cancelled A1 does not automatically
submit the original domain command again; any successor acquisition must
retain the necessary scope and requirements without weakening authority.

*** Progression flows through propagation

When an authoritative change carries a source-declared impact whose
freshness is promised to an eligible participant, the propagation contract
must preserve enough information for a resulting authorized reread to include
that change. Current connected interest is one possible routing input, not
necessarily the source of the impact obligation. An invalidation is an
advisory derived from a relevant authoritative change; not every database
write has to become a broadcast. The logical sequence is:

1. Qualified outcome evidence establishes the authoritative change.
2. Recoverable change/publication evidence carries an admitted progression
   requirement and affected scope information. A durable record, qualified
   change frontier, replayable source or equivalent reconciliation path can
   establish this under the declared delivery guarantee. The mechanism
   explains how the impact remains discoverable if no compatible interest was
   registered on the writer's local router at the instant of incorporation.
3. Routing delivers an advisory to a relevant participant under its transport
   and recovery contract.
4. The participant's acquisition requests a scope and progression sufficient
   to cover the relevant obligation.
5. The acquisition boundary admits only evidence that actually establishes the
   promised coverage and visibility.

The advisory remains advisory: it can establish that an acquisition is needed
without supplying the new authoritative contents. Preserving progression does
not require every stage to repeat the complete token; a protected reference to
the requirement is sufficient when it can be resolved under the contract.
The origin of a required notification and its recovery owner must survive the
failure windows covered by the application's promise. A process-local emit,
router enqueue, or SSE send is not an independently durable incorporation
record and does not prove delivery, participant receipt, authoritative reread
or installation. The system may safely redeliver advisory invalidations;
deduplicating or coalescing them must preserve outstanding scoped progression
obligations, not depend on recording per-recipient acknowledgements for each
broadcast.

A tokenless advisory needs an alternative qualified fence or acquisition rule
that prevents a satisfying reread from preceding the triggering change. Merely
reading something newer than the participant's old display may still miss that
change. If the transport cannot supply such a rule, the weaker guarantee must
be explicit. A router may broaden fanout or redeliver possible missed
invalidations without tracking exact delivery completion, but the resulting
read must still satisfy the affected scope and an admissible consistency fence;
redundant wakeups do not manufacture a trusted frontier.

*** Initial acquisition and subscription races

Initial content for a managed projection participates in the same scope,
authority, progression, and installation contract as refresh content. Being
server-rendered does not exempt it. The owner/resource instance and any initial
basis claim must be established through the admitted bootstrap path. A managed
empty target with neither qualifying initial projection nor a permitted first
acquisition is structurally incomplete even if its subscription can connect.
A server-side render with no qualified source/coverage evidence also cannot
assert that the browser actually installed the projected contents.

The relationship between initial read and subscription must avoid an uncovered
interval when the application promises eventual awareness of changes. Suitable
strategies include an admitted read/subscription fence, replay from a durable
cursor, buffered changes across registration, or another qualified recovery
mechanism. Each strategy states how it handles reconnect and owner replacement.
Interest registration at a single router is not proof that other writers or
routers knew about that interest in time. The protection must cover the actual
interest-discovery, publication, routing and acquisition boundaries, including
the uncertain handoff when a router or process fails. Single-node and multi-node
realizations preserve the same application-level guarantees; replication,
routing topology, health detection and selective redelivery are physical
strategies that must be checked against those guarantees.

A periodic authoritative refresh may provide a weaker eventual-awareness
strategy under explicit scheduling and availability assumptions. It does not
prove delivery of every invalidation. A router's successful local subscription
does not by itself prove that no relevant upstream change was lost before its
interest became visible. A new subscription can be justified without replaying
every earlier event if a post-registration authoritative acquisition is fenced
to include all relevant changes through a qualified frontier. That mechanism
can also cover router failover by conservatively triggering redundant
invalidations or acquisitions. It cannot infer a covering frontier merely
from the fact that all surviving routers report healthy or from a fresh
connection's existence.

*** Worked gap: read before interest registration

A browser acquires Request board Q at admitted frontier P17, then requests a
managed Live subscription. Before the subscription becomes effective for all
relevant writes, another authority transaction changes Q at P18. The initial
read excludes that change. If publication uses only the interests visible at
write time, and the subscription starts after the change without replay or a
new read fence, the browser can remain indefinitely at P17 despite a healthy
SSE connection. Neither a later keepalive nor successful router registration
repairs that history by itself. The failure exists even when all local
broadcast attempts reported success: the relevant interest was not yet
visible at the publisher's matching boundary. Recoverable source impact
plus qualified replay or a new subscribed-read fence can close the gap;
a durable per-recipient send ledger is one possible mechanism, not a
necessary semantic requirement.

An admitted implementation may instead register and buffer the interest before
the initial read, replay changes since a durable cursor, acquire a new qualified
frontier after registration, or periodically reconcile Q under explicit
fairness/availability premises. If registration or router failover loses its
continuity evidence, the participant must retain an unresolved acquisition
obligation until qualified recovery establishes Q at a covering frontier.
An implementation does not have to deliver every intermediate invalidation when
a fresh authorized acquisition suffices. But it cannot claim eventual awareness
without a path from this missed-change history to that acquisition.

*** Observed basis is not authority to commit

A browser-supplied observed basis can be useful as an untrusted input to
freshness handling. It does not prove that the browser acquired or adopted the
corresponding data, and it does not establish current authorization. Stronger
claims require admitted evidence bound to the relevant observation and actor.
Even a server-signed observation receipt proves only the proposition it
actually attests (for example issuance under a named scope and frontier), not
that the DOM installation completed or that the same principal remains
currently authorized.

The server must also bound and validate requested consistency behavior. A
malformed, foreign-incarnation, or unattainable token must not force unbounded
waiting or silently downgrade to an unconstrained read. A qualified server
frontier, known issued basis and merely client-echoed claim remain different
evidence sources even when their portable tokens have identical bytes.

Advancement alone does not invalidate a command. The model checks its actual
business preconditions. When the operation explicitly requires an expected
domain version, the server enforces that condition as part of command meaning;
when it does not, unrelated database advancement is not a reason to reject.

Dependency upgrades must not silently change token interpretation, read
visibility, temporal defaults, or command/result compatibility. The assembly
and qualification artifacts bind the relevant provider contract, and persisted
requirements need an explicit compatibility or migration policy.

** Advisory obligations and adoption
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-advisory-discharge
:END:

An advisory obligation records what a participant must still acquire or adopt
under the application's declared policy. It is scoped debt, not a Boolean
global dirty flag. Its meaning includes the required observation, coverage,
progression, owner/resource lifetime, and completion milestone. The owner and
*authoritative* query selection are part of the obligation's meaning: two
requirements with equivalent frontier tokens need not be coalescible across
distinct principals, server-side collection predicates, projections, or
adoption policies. HumanHelp's local text filter, local ordering and terminal
visibility toggle do not by themselves define a different acquired board
cohort; they remain current presentation state, not grounds to discard,
reissue or discharge authoritative board debt. A debt-free status is a claim
justified by admitted settlement evidence, not by a queue's emptiness.

Advisory knowledge and adopted authoritative knowledge remain separate. A
participant can know that newer relevant authority exists while deliberately
retaining its current visible board until the user chooses Refresh. A glow or
toast may represent that debt without adopting new membership.

The obligation representation must distinguish:

- an obligation to acquire admissible evidence;
- an obligation to acquire and semantically adopt it;
- any required physical installation that realizes that adoption;
- retirement or transfer of an obligation under an explicit lifecycle policy.

These distinctions need not create four public APIs or four universal stores.
They must be explicit wherever a completion claim depends on them.

*** Acquisition, permission, installation, and adoption

The following milestones cannot be collapsed:

| Milestone | Established fact |
|-----------+------------------|
| Acquisition completed | An admitted observation has arrived with the required source, scope, and correlation. |
| Installation permitted | Current owner, resource, scope, progression, and presentation policy allow this proposed installation. |
| Installation completed | The permitted physical effect actually completed under the realization contract. |
| Adoption recorded | The semantic state now treats the justified contents or membership as adopted, using the required completion evidence. |

For an acquire-only obligation, the first milestone may suffice. For an
acquire-and-adopt obligation whose realization includes a DOM update, merely
returning true from =can-install?= or emitting an HTMX allow-swap decision does
not suffice. Nor does HTTP success, an effect-issued marker, or an untrusted
client-reported completion prove that physical installation occurred. A
completed-installation observation is admitted only through its qualified
realization and exact effect/owner/candidate correlation. The semantic state
must then perform the declared adoption transition using *current* obligations
and authority/visibility policy; installation and adoption are not equivalent
merely because both happen in one ordinary browser callback.

The final installation-right check and the physical mutation must be serialized
against competing owner changes and relevant installations, or protected by an
equivalent mechanism. Checking a right, yielding, and later mutating an arbitrary
replacement target can install into the wrong resource instance. Permission is
scoped to the particular acquisition generation, authorized query, observation,
target instance and owner; it is not transferable to a newer DOM target or a
replacement Choreo execution. A late, duplicate or stale completion may supply
separately admitted evidence, but must not exercise the retired instance's
installation or debt-discharge rights.

The physical completion or failure is correlated to the admitted installation
effect, owner, target instance, and observation. Adoption and debt discharge use
that evidence under the current semantic state. A synchronous implementation
may establish these milestones together; an asynchronous shell needs explicit
completion/failure handling. The coordinator must not restore an obligation
snapshot captured before a newer advisory arrived. It settles each *current*
obligation only if admitted coverage and the required milestone are satisfied.
Conversely, if another admitted installation already fulfills that obligation,
a rejected late candidate does not justify retaining debt forever.

If a physical update can partially apply, the realization must define that case.
It may require repair, authoritative reacquisition, or a representation-unknown
state. It cannot report complete installation merely because the effect began.
Similarly, loss of a completion event requires a reconciliation path if the
physical representation may already have changed. A physical effect whose
completion is unknown cannot be classified as either definitely installed or
definitely absent without additional evidence. The recovery contract must say
whether it re-observes the actual target, replaces it from an admitted complete
projection, or transfers the unresolved obligation to an eligible owner. No
recovery route may silently promote speculative or incomplete DOM state into
authoritative adoption.

*** Worked boundary: permission before owner replacement

A1 acquires a complete authorized board projection at P18 and receives
permission to install it into target instance T1 owned by execution E1.
Before the physical mutation completes, E1 retires and a replacement E2
owns T2. A late =installation-completed= event for A1/T1 is *not* permission
to install into T2 or to declare E2's board adopted at P18. The old
obligation may be retired or transferred only by its declared lifecycle
policy; outstanding work for E2 is independent.

If A1's mutation already occurred but its completion event was lost, the
physical representation may be unknown. The system must reconcile or
reacquire before claiming an adopted P18 board. If a separate qualified
installation of P21 for the current owner has completed, that evidence can
satisfy compatible outstanding adoption obligations; the rejected P18
callback does not force a regression or perpetual pending debt.

*** Worked example: two advisories during one refresh

Let B denote one complete authorized HumanHelp board acquisition for the
selected Organization and Location, declared collection bounds, projection
version, visibility context and temporal policy. These authoritative query
properties are fixed for this example; local text search, sorting, priority
ordering and terminal visibility are *presentation* over the adopted cohort
and may change independently while a read is in flight. The board owner and
target are one admitted resource instance. The board has adopted an
observation at P17.

The provider establishes that an observation through P18 includes the change
announced by O1, an observation through P20 includes the change announced by O2,
and P21 covers both requirements in this compatible scope. It also establishes
the advancement relations used below. These are admitted provider judgments,
not arithmetic inferred from the names.

Advisory O1 creates an acquire-and-adopt obligation for B through P18. The user
chooses Refresh. That action grants one logical acquisition under HumanHelp's
declared scope and lifetime, not indefinite permission to fetch or install
later remote changes. The coordinator allocates a fresh acquisition resource A1
under the managed lifecycle and issues the read for O1's requirement. Its
identity is not merely a reusable request number; reuse would need a separate
admitted instance discriminator. The grant binds the current principal, board
owner, authorized Location/Organization, selection, acquisition scope,
progression requirements and permitted adoption transition.

While A1 is outstanding, advisory O2 creates a further obligation for B through
P20. The coordinator must retain O2. Completing A1 must not restore an old copy
of the obligation set captured before O2 arrived.

Assume the application permits adoption of a non-regressing partial advance
while stronger advisory debt remains visible. The sequence is:

| Event | Adopted board | Retained acquire-and-adopt debt | Required action |
|-------+---------------+--------------------------------+-----------------|
| Initial admitted state | P17 | None | No refresh required. |
| Receive O1 | P17 | O1 through P18 | Offer or schedule acquisition under the product policy. |
| Start A1 for O1 | P17 | O1 | Record current acquisition ownership and requirement. |
| Receive O2 | P17 | O1 and O2 through P20 | Retain O2 while A1 remains outstanding. |
| A1 completes with admitted B observation at P18 | P17 | O1 and O2 | Validate coverage; prepare a permitted installation. |
| Installation is allowed but has not completed | P17 | O1 and O2 | Keep adoption debt; permission is not completion. |
| Installation of P18 completes and is admitted | P18 | O2 | Adopt the covered advance and discharge O1 only. |
| Later permitted acquisition and installation covers P20 | At least the required frontier under provider evidence | None of these two obligations | Discharge O2 using its actual coverage and completion. |

If A1 instead returns an admitted B observation at P21 and its installation
successfully completes, that evidence can discharge both O1 and O2. The
coordinator checks the latest obligation set at completion, not only the set
that happened to start A1. The acquisition's actual evidence can be stronger
than its requested minimum.

If installation fails, neither acquire-and-adopt obligation is discharged by
that attempt. The acquired observation may remain available as evidence under
its lifetime and disclosure contract, but the visible board has not thereby
adopted it. An HTTP success response or HTMX swap permission is not completion;
any partial physical update or lost completion requires qualified inspection,
repair or reacquisition under the current owner.

If a different permitted path already adopted P21, the late P18 response must not
roll the board backward. The existing adopted P21 evidence may already satisfy
O1 and O2; that is a separate justification for discharge. Rejecting the late
installation does not mean the coordinator must keep debt that independently
admitted adoption evidence has already fulfilled.

An application may instead require an acquisition to satisfy all current board
debt before adopting anything. Under that policy, P18 is retained or discarded
as acquired evidence and the coordinator requests enough evidence for P20.
Either policy is valid when declared; the framework must not switch between
them accidentally according to response timing.

For the HumanHelp proving application, the selected policy permits the P18
non-regressing partial advance but keeps O2 visible. One user Refresh grants
one logical board acquisition and does *not* silently grant an automatic
successor read merely because O2 arrived in flight. A separate explicit
Refresh/Retry, or an independently declared bounded repair grant, is required
for the next physical read. Repeated user clicks while A1 is active can be
coalesced only under the specified bounded successor-intent policy; they do not
create an unbounded automatically draining queue. The generic browser adapter's
physical scheduling strategy must be qualified against this policy rather than
being treated as the policy itself.

*** The coordinator's semantic obligations

The example is implemented through admitted transitions, not direct mutation by
arbitrary transport callbacks. A useful transition decomposition is:

1. =advisory-received=: admit source and scope, then add or soundly combine debt.
2. =acquisition-requested=: validate or allocate the current acquisition grant,
   allocate the managed owner/resource, and bind the authorized query, requested
   scope, progression, grant budget and lifetime to a declared effect.
3. =acquisition-completed=: admit the effect completion and observation, then
   determine which requirements its evidence can cover.
4. =installation-requested=: propose only the permitted effect for the current
   target and owner; preserve obligations still awaiting adoption.
5. =installation-completed=: admit actual completion, update justified adoption,
   and discharge only obligations fulfilled in the current state.
6. =acquisition-failed= or =installation-failed=: retain unresolved obligations,
   release or retire resources according to policy, and select the declared
   recovery continuation.

These are semantic phases, not a mandatory source-file layout. Part VI specifies
their browser realization and the refinement between DOM effects and semantic
adoption. The permanent executable semantic core owns the transition meaning;
proof artifacts explain why the admitted realization preserves it.

Every completion checks current owner, resource-instance, acquisition-grant
and target correlation. A response for an obsolete server-side query
selection, principal context, Location, Organization, page/window, route or
target instance must not install merely because its database basis is recent.
Changing only HumanHelp's local search, sorting or terminal-visibility controls
does *not* make an otherwise compatible full-cohort acquisition obsolete. The
completed board may be installed under current ownership and rendered with
the *current* options; response-time display options must not overwrite those
newer local choices. A change to Organization, Location, declared window,
principal or authority context does change the acquisition scope and requires
an appropriately new owner/grant. Evidence may be usable through another
declared admission path, but cannot resurrect a retired execution's rights.

The obligation set is updated from current state. Removing O1 must not remove a
newer obligation with a superficially similar key, overwrite a concurrently
added scope, or clear a global dirty flag representing several independent
requirements. Sound coalescing may replace the representation, but must preserve
the combined meaning.

*** HumanHelp's selective adoption policy

HumanHelp can use these generic contracts to show the creator's own committed
Request immediately through a correlated admitted result, while other viewers
receive an advisory and explicitly refresh board membership. Remote creation
and remote lifecycle changes to already visible Requests both follow explicit
Refresh for ordinary board adoption. A user's own confirmed Create, Claim,
Unclaim, Reassign or other admitted operation may update only its declared
correlated Request-card or selective membership scope, provided the operation
and current trusted disclosure permit that result. The policy must state which
scopes a correlated operation result can update and how that update relates to
the existing board selection. An SSE advisory cannot carry authoritative
Request status, current helper ownership or permission to silently install it.

A card outcome can update an already adopted Request without proving a complete
new board query. The currently active primary RequestAssignment, not a cached
helper field or provisional card label, establishes authoritative Claim
ownership under the source-owned model. An operation that introduces or removes
a visible row needs an explicit membership-adoption rule. Filters, sorting,
retained selection, scroll continuity, and optimistic overlays must not cause
that scope distinction to disappear. A card conflict reread may correct that
one Request without adopting unrelated board membership or proving that an
outcome-unknown command incorporated.

The framework does not hard-code HumanHelp's particular choice. It provides
checked acquisition, ownership, progression, installation, and obligation
mechanisms from which the application declares that choice.

*** Worked HumanHelp sequence: remote R, local S, and an excluding filter

Bob has adopted authorized board cohort Q17 at P17. Alice creates remote
Request R at P18; Bob receives a valid advisory for the board but does not
adopt R. Bob then successfully creates Request S at P19. Bob's current
local search filter matches R but excludes S.

The correlated *authorized* Create result for S can selectively admit S
under Bob's current board-scope membership rule without importing R.
Because the current filter excludes S, the UI presents S in an accessible
"Your new Request" result area with an explicit way to reveal it in the
ordinary board. It does not silently clear Bob's search or introduce R.
Local search, sorting, show-terminal and other presentation changes operate
over the already adopted cohort; they cannot acquire remote R as a side
effect. The outstanding advisory for R remains visible.

Only Bob's subsequent explicit Refresh, followed by a complete authorized
board acquisition and qualified installation, can adopt R under this
policy. An observation with a later basis for S is not evidence that the
complete board was read at that basis, and its adoption does not discharge
R's scoped obligation. If Bob has an unrelated outcome-unknown command,
that command's attribution remains independently unresolved even after
a board reread shows its present Request status.

** Assurance obligations and acceptance cases

Part IV's assurance is a chain of bounded claims. The semantic transition must
express the intended rule; construction must bind it to the actual inputs and
implementation; the complete submission must realize the protected effect;
qualified backend behavior must justify the outcome; propagation and acquisition
must preserve scope and progression; installation must justify adoption.

A report uses Part I's separate claim dimensions: proposition, subject,
mechanism, premises, coverage, applicability, and status. It distinguishes
derivation or construction checking, runtime enforcement, testing, and explicit
assertion. Named environmental premises qualify a claim; conditionality is not
an additional proof mechanism. These descriptions are not interchangeable
assurance grades. A strong proof of an abstract fragment cannot conceal an
unexamined physical execution boundary. Status belongs to a precisely stated
proposition and subject: an established abstract invariant, a source-derived
conditional construction report, a trusted runtime enforcement requirement, a
bounded finite search and an observed physical outcome are different judgments.
Their agreement can support a stronger composed claim only through the declared
composition and correspondence rules. No copied report, familiar status tag or
passing test grants a certificate or application admission by itself.

*** Required invariant families

The supported construction and runtime must establish, or explicitly qualify,
at least the following property families:

1. *Binding fidelity*: operation, command, canonical arguments, principal,
   authority, target, and realization cannot be silently substituted between
   admission, submission, outcome, and reconciliation.
2. *Authoritative safety*: each incorporated transition satisfies its protected
   business conditions and declared frame under the backend/writer premises.
3. *Command consistency*: compatible repetition does not incorporate again;
   incompatible reuse cannot overwrite the old binding; terminal disposition is
   reported only with evidence of its declared scope.
4. *Truthful uncertainty*: absence of outcome evidence, timeout, and local
   retirement do not manufacture non-incorporation.
5. *Recoverable required work*: committed operations retain the declared
   command/result and propagation or external-work evidence across promised
   failure modes.
6. *Scoped observation*: acquired data supports only its admitted scope,
   coverage, temporal interpretation, visibility context, and progression.
7. *Justified adoption*: only current permitted installation/adoption paths
   advance adopted knowledge and discharge adoption debt.
8. *Evidence fidelity*: a classification, certificate, or passing test is not
   promoted into a stronger proposition than it establishes.

Each family needs a named source-owned operation or application subject, its
claimed boundary and the actual premises on which its status depends. An
uninspected writer inventory is unknown, not empty; a zero count of detected
violations is only about the inspected population. A missing required operation
is not a proof that the remaining operations preserve every invariant. Likewise,
an invariant true only because no valid transition is enabled is insufficient
when the application promises a usable operation. Independently declared
capability and positive reachability obligations prevent vacuous success.

Liveness claims are separate. They identify scheduling, delivery, backend,
provider, retention, authority, and human-participation assumptions. Safety must
not depend on pretending that those assumptions always produce timely success.

A structural mismatch is rejected at the owning admission boundary; a valid
construction with a modeled counterexample does not receive the claimed
invariant; an unestablished premise remains explicitly unestablished unless the
chosen policy permits a named conditional assertion. A heuristic suspicion about
otherwise admitted business policy is a review finding, not automatically a
proof failure. Assertions cannot substitute for a missing required declaration,
contradictory operation contract or fabricated physical evidence. The report
must preserve both unsupported and contradicted outcomes without silently
relabeling either as business rejection or environmental unavailability.

*** Executable conformance cases

The implementation must provide conformance cases for the supported mechanisms,
including negative cases, reachable positive controls, and relevant
interleavings. Each case names its original source and operation, the controlled
mutation or fault, expected checking boundary, affected proposition, oracle,
forbidden physical action, and permitted continuation. A positive case must pass
through the *same* boundary as its adverse variant; rejecting every input is
not conformance. The independent effect observer must establish when a
forbidden mutation, disclosure, installation or retry did or did not occur;
absence of a logged success message is not proof of absence. These cases
complement semantic derivations; passing them does not prove arbitrary backend
behavior.

Test fixtures must distinguish admitted evidence from illustrative maps. A
fixture that creates an advancement witness by changing a token field does not
test provider truth. A fixture that returns a successful predicate without
performing installation does not test adoption. A diagnostic resolver that
finds a retained record does not demonstrate a durable recovery driver.

A finite-state check reports the explored state/input universes, enabled
operations, examined interleavings, exhaustion or incompleteness and
proposition scope. Enumerating sample integers or principal IDs is not a
general theorem. Inductive preservation requires qualified initialization
and a step rule for every admitted operation under the declared assumptions;
neither finite success nor preservation alone proves physical execution
correspondence. Fault injection must distinguish an injected failure from
a malformed baseline and test the actual recovery or prohibition boundary.

*** Completion criteria for this part

An implementation satisfies this part for an admitted operation family when its
public interface, canonical semantics, construction, physical submission,
backend profile, outcome interpretation, command policy, propagation, and
acquisition/adoption paths form one inspectable contract. Supported mechanisms
must reject mismatches early and explain the exact unsatisfied obligation.

Unsupported proof cases do not disappear. They are either implemented through
another supported mechanism, admitted with explicit accepted assertions under
policy, or rejected with a usable explanation. Dynamic unavailability has an
explicit continuation. Success means the claimed semantic effect and evidence
were established, not merely that the application started without errors.

Completion is evaluated *per admitted operation family and assurance profile*.
A root application report identifies exact source/certificate identities and
versions, the enumerated operations and independent required capabilities, the
inspected physical route/writer/worker/browser boundaries, conditional premises,
uncovered or untrusted implementation bodies, admissible positive/negative cases,
and unresolved correspondence. Changes to backend behavior, trusted adapters,
installed handlers, asset bytes or relevant business policy invalidate only the
dependent claims under the declared dependency rules, but cannot retain a stale
root acceptance. One certified source fragment does not imply every deployed
entry point exercised it; genuine installation/adoption evidence must justify
that stronger conclusion. Operation-local effect proof, actual submitted
transaction correspondence, installed ingress/worker coverage, backend
writer completeness and response/adoption realization are separately
established edges. A valid physical operation under a narrower evidence
profile can retain that scoped claim while the broader deployed-application
claim remains unresolved; an undocumented bypass must not be silently
reclassified as a merely unsupported business rule.

An assertion cannot repair a missing operation binding, malformed schema,
unresolved capability, or contradictory effect declaration. It supplies a named
premise about otherwise well-formed behavior; structural admission obligations
still have to be discharged.

** Authoritative absence
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-authoritative-absence
:END:

An authorized observation may establish that a domain entity is absent
under a qualified entity lookup, or that a previously displayed entity
does not belong to a *particular covered projection*. These are positive
authoritative statements with specified provenance and scope. They are
not consequences of an empty HTML element, a missing response, local
filtering, a timeout, a missing DOM target or a rejected read.

The browser treats the following as distinct knowledge and physical
states, even when they produce similar-looking empty presentation:

| Observation or realization state | What it actually establishes |
|----------------------------------+------------------------------|
| Present | An admitted authoritative value or membership under its declared actor, query, field and temporal scope. |
| Authoritatively absent | A qualified negative entity or membership fact under an admitted coverage and progression contract. |
| Not yet acquired | No applicable source observation has established the requested presence or absence. |
| Acquisition failed or indeterminate | The attempted read did not establish the requested fact; earlier justified knowledge and outstanding recovery remain separately qualified. |
| Not disclosed or no longer authorized | The participant may not currently learn or retain protected content; the policy may deliberately hide the existence of an entity. |
| Physically not displayed | The actual current DOM/host does not show the entity, possibly because of filtering, retirement or failed rendering; no model absence follows. |

An authoritative absence may therefore be installed and adopted just
as an authoritative presence may. The *evidence* that the entity is
excluded and the *physical completion* of removing its representation
are separate propositions. Adoption of negative knowledge is not a
browser-only tombstone that will forever override future authorized
presence. It remains subject to the same source, projection,
progression, local continuity and ownership contracts as presence.

*** Absence requires actual covered authority, not an omitted row

The admitted source identifies the principal and Organization/tenant
disclosure context, applied query identity, server-side predicate and
arguments, entity or membership subject, projection/field coverage,
temporal and consistency interpretation, relevant authoritative
basis/progression, and the meaning of its negative result. A complete
membership observation can justify the omission of a member only
within the universe whose membership it actually closes.

A successful empty list is not automatically a complete query. A
paginated page, truncated or top-N result, projection missing the
membership field, filtered subset, cached partial response, redacted
record, tenant-limited view, or speculative client-side search result
does not certify exclusion from a larger universe. Even a complete
*authorized* query says nothing about entities that policy deliberately
does not disclose. A failed or denied read must not be converted into
"no Request exists" or expose protected existence through an
overprecise error.

An explicit trusted exclusion response may justify a narrower negative
membership fact without loading every card. It must bind the exact
entity, applied query/predicate and authorized context, provider
coverage/temporal semantics, and the exclusion reason it claims.
For example, "R17 is not in Bob's open-Requests board" need not
establish whether R17 still exists, was completed, was reassigned,
became inaccessible or was deleted. When the reason itself would
reveal protected state, only the permitted nondisclosing proposition
can be returned.

The provider's negative fact is admitted through a real acquisition
or qualified persisted observation. A client-written
=:provenance :authoritative= tag, a matching plan digest or a
structurally valid basis token cannot certify its truth. The generic
browser may preserve an opaque source/evidence reference and
scope; the trusted consistency/model boundary owns the provider
proof or expressly named premise. Absence need not allocate a
permanent tombstone for every theoretically missing entity: a
complete cohort observation or checked compact exclusion summary
can represent justified negatives within its declared retention.

The membership scope is distinct from the domain entity's existence
and from its fields. A Request can be excluded from an "open for
helper" board while remaining accessible in an authorized completed
Requests projection. An omitted optional field may reflect a
projection contract rather than a declaration that its domain
value is absent. These forms of negative knowledge have different
queries and cannot silently substitute for each other.

*** Worked example: R17 leaves Bob's open-Requests board

Bob has an admitted and physically adopted open-Requests board
observation B100 for applied query Qopen, Organization O and his
current authority context K. It includes Request R17. A subsequent
trusted operation changes R17's state so that it no longer matches
the predicate for Qopen. Depending on authorization, R17 might still
be present under an independently queried completed-Requests
projection Qdone; R17's absence from Qopen alone does not establish
its deletion or a different helper's Membership.

The next authorized acquisition is evaluated against these
possibilities:

| Candidate evidence | Valid conclusion about R17 |
|--------------------+----------------------------|
| An admitted complete Qopen membership observation B105 under K, with qualified progression and R17 excluded. | R17 is absent from *Qopen membership* at B105's stated provider interpretation. |
| An admitted explicit R17 exclusion witness for Qopen/K with the necessary coverage and temporal meaning. | R17 is excluded from that membership without claiming all other board members were acquired. |
| R17 omitted from page two, while Qopen covers multiple pages and no complete membership/exclusion witness exists. | No negative membership fact for R17 follows. |
| An admitted Qdone observation contains R17 as completed. | R17 is present in Qdone; Qopen exclusion still requires a qualified Qopen/predicate judgment. |
| Bob's permitted board changes from Qopen to Qdone or he toggles a local =show-terminal?= view option. | Distinguish a new applied server query from client presentation over an already justified cohort; neither action invents authoritative absence. |
| The server denies R17 disclosure after Bob's Membership changes. | Apply the required privacy/display revocation; do not assert that R17 was deleted or absent for all readers. |
| Qopen acquisition fails, is cancelled, or produces incompatible/indeterminate content. | Preserve the qualified previously adopted evidence where policy permits, plus stale/recovery debt; no newly proven absence. |

For the eligible complete B105 case the semantic path is:

#+begin_example
qualified source establishes R17 excluded from Qopen at B105
        |
confirm K/Qopen coverage and provider progression
        |
prepare current membership outcome :absent for R17
        |
issue current scoped removal/installation permit
        |
check exact board owner, target incarnation and representation
        |
perform permitted physical removal or checked no-op
        |
admit real matching physical postcondition
        |
record adopted absence for the covered membership
#+end_example

A prepared negative result is not yet physically adopted. The
permit binds the current runtime/assembly, actual Qopen coordinator,
B105 source and covered membership, current board/card instances,
target representation, mutation footprint and effect generation.
A stale prior request or an OOB/extension mutation cannot bypass
the permit by targeting the same HTML =id= attribute. If a
multi-target update removes a card but fails to install a new
count or status control, its actual per-target outcomes and
repair obligations remain distinct.

The physical postcondition must establish that the *current
owned representation* for R17's Qopen membership no longer
appears or grants obsolete managed actions. A generic "target
not found" may indicate an unmounted board, wrong owner,
selector mismatch or an already-correct current target. A
no-op removal can count as completed only if the qualified
host contract verifies the intended absence under the
current scope and owner. A generic =afterSwap= event does
not by itself establish that predicate.

Once a qualified absence is adopted, it retires obsolete
provisional/continuity restoration rights for the
corresponding presentation scope. A late stale callback
must not recreate the removed card from an old capture.
A current authorized later presence can legitimately
restore R17 to Qopen through its *new* qualified
observation and physical install. Absence does not
represent an irreversible global tombstone.

The local filter distinction is especially important:
Bob may hide an already acquired terminal Request
through =show-terminal?= or text-search without
starting a new read. Hiding the item is a local
presentation choice, not new authoritative
membership evidence. Re-showing it needs retained
still-justified authorized content and current
presentation ownership. If intervening authority
has excluded it or revoked its disclosure, the
local filter cannot resurrect stale markup or
grant a new subscription/operation binding.

*** Provisional presence and negative authority remain separate

Suppose E99 provisionally inserts Request R30 into a
local creator view while Create command C99 is
unresolved. A complete board acquisition omits
R30. The system must answer three *different*
questions before changing presentation:

1. Does an admitted source actually establish
   R30's exclusion from this exact applied
   query, actor/context and coverage horizon?
2. Does that negative evidence, its qualified
   progression and the selected presentation
   policy permit superseding E99's provisional
   presence in the current physical target?
3. Does a separately qualified command-result
   source establish whether C99 incorporated,
   was rejected, or remains =:commit-unknown=?

The acquisition may have occurred before C99's
possibly pending transaction incorporated, or
under a different query/actor scope. In that
case R30's omission is not automatically
rejection or proof of nonexistence. Even
sufficiently progressed *current board*
absence does not by itself establish C99's
historical attribution. A subsequent C99
incorporation, if still possible under the
model's temporal/deduplication contract,
requires its separately authorized
reconciliation and potential later read.

The application may keep a visibly labeled
pending overlay outside adopted membership,
remove the provisional card upon an admitted
negative-authority supersession, or use a
declared conflict-resolution/reacquisition
strategy. It must state the scope, owner,
command-outcome and temporal premises for
that choice; it cannot silently make an
unresolved Create a rejected command, or
misrepresent the overlay as an adopted
current Request.

If stronger authorized absence supersedes
E99's provisional representation, revoke
the old DOM/rollback/timer ownership before
physical cleanup. The C99 resolver
obligation survives under its proper owner
where promised. Removing an obsolete card
does not justify cancelling all C99 history,
repeating an unknown command with a fresh
identity, or treating a missing physical
target as a successful server operation.

A manager's later Reassign and the affected
RequestAssignment facts illustrate the same
distinction: a helper's card may no longer
belong in one projection while its
historical Claim was genuinely incorporated.
Entity/query membership, current assignment
and historical command disposition remain
separate trusted questions.

*** Absence participates in qualified progression and adoption

Presence and absence are both qualified
observation outcomes. The runtime compares
them using the source provider's declared
scope/coverage and progression relation,
not the lexical/numeric order of basis
labels, SSE arrival sequence or browser
request generation. Two distinct opaque
bases are not ordered merely because
one was fetched later.

For the *same* applicable membership scope,
a later provider-proved exclusion may
supersede an earlier present card; a
subsequent provider-proved presence may
supersede that absence. When basis values
are incomparable, the coordinator retains
the competing obligations or invokes
the declared read/merge/conflict policy.
A complete board membership from R
can coexist with an independently newer
creator-local card S only if the
qualified scope-aware merge preserves
both provenances and does not assert
a single atomic backend snapshot.

A scoped negative fact may satisfy an
advisory requirement only if its source
establishes the required query,
authority context, coverage, progression
and discharge stage. Acquisition of a
negative result does not clear
adoption-stage debt before the actual
current DOM removal/no-op postcondition
has been admitted. Failing to install
a negative membership result preserves
repair debt without falsifying the
underlying acquired fact.

Absence from an open-Requests board
does not discharge a complete-query
requirement for a different Organization,
a partial page or a card-specific
historical command resolution.
One explicit user Refresh continues
to obey its original read budget:
discovering an omission cannot
silently authorize another fetch
to "check whether it was really
deleted." If more evidence is
necessary, obtain a new permitted
read or enter an honest blocked/
unknown/reconciliation state.

Security and privacy revocation is
a different actuation contract.
A current principal losing access
may require immediate removal or
quarantine of protected presentation
even without evidence that the entity
is absent from the model. That
revocation does not permit a
new protected read and must not
reveal nondisclosable existence.
No browser-side cleanup can erase
data an adversarial client already
recorded; the trusted server
continues to enforce authorization
at every read, stream and write.

The initial baseline/subscription
and multi-node router gaps from
06.06–06.07 apply equally to
negative knowledge. Missing
invalidations must not leave a
newly excluded Request
permanently displayed under a
guarantee of current-state
convergence; the promised
replay/fence/reacquisition and
user-action assumptions must
actually support the claim.

*** Paired authoritative-absence acceptance histories

The finished design requires real eligible
trusted sources, complete coverage or qualified
exclusion and actual host completion. Negative
fixtures must reach the relevant absence,
scope or physical boundary rather than
pass solely by producing an empty element.

| Eligible positive history | Adverse or incomplete history | Required conclusion |
|---------------------------+-------------------------------+---------------------|
| Complete authorized Qopen B105 excludes previously present R17 and is provider-proved compatible with the installed membership. | R17 is merely omitted from one page of a paginated query or from a redacted subset. | Admit negative membership only under sufficient covered Qopen evidence; partial omission is unknown. |
| Trusted explicit R17/Qopen exclusion witness has narrower sufficient coverage. | Browser bytes copy =:provenance :authoritative= and a plausible basis without a qualified source. | The real narrow witness may establish exclusion; copied tags do not. |
| R17 is absent from Qopen while a qualified Qdone projection still contains it. | The browser infers global deletion or historical Claim failure from Qopen exclusion. | Preserve projection-relative membership, domain existence and command attribution as different facts. |
| A current authorized removal permit operates on the matching Qopen card and checks its physical postcondition. | A generic =afterSwap= or missing CSS selector is credited as proof the current card disappeared. | Only a qualified completed removal or valid current-target no-op establishes adopted absence. |
| New qualified B105 exclusion retires R17's old provisional/rollback rights; later provider-proved presence may reintroduce it. | A stale timer reinserts an old card, or an irreversible tombstone blocks new authoritative presence. | Prevent obsolete restoration while permitting later justified presence. |
| C99 Create remains unknown and R30 is omitted from a board read that predates possible incorporation. | Omission is declared terminal C99 rejection or permission for blind duplicate Create. | Distinguish temporal exclusion, provisional presentation and historical command result. |
| Bob hides a retained authorized R17 under a local text/sort/terminal filter and restores it while membership still justifies the content. | Local re-showing overrides a newer authoritative exclusion, query change or disclosure revocation. | Local visibility choices create no new server truth or stale presentation rights. |
| A complete authorized removal of one card and matching count update both physically finish under their distinct permits. | Card removal finishes, count/OOB update fails, yet the whole board is marked adopted. | Preserve per-target completion and repair debt; no imaginary atomic installation. |
| An acquired negative result satisfies O1 but installation fails and one-Refresh grant is exhausted. | The coordinator clears adoption-stage O1 or starts an ungranted second acquisition. | Keep acquired evidence, outstanding physical adoption debt and exact read budget separate. |
| Access is revoked and the honest client clears protected presentation under a current privacy policy. | The client displays "R17 deleted" or attempts a protected read without permission. | Revocation is not domain absence; enforce nondisclosure and trusted server authorization. |

These cases specify the required negative-knowledge
and physically verified removal contracts for every
profile claiming authoritative-absence adoption.

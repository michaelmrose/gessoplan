** Authoritative absence
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-authoritative-absence
:END:

An authoritative observation can establish that a particular entity or membership
is absent within a specified authorized scope. That is a positive evidence claim
about absence, not the lack of a successful response.

The browser distinguishes:

- an entity absent under a qualified entity lookup;
- an entity excluded from a particular query or collection membership;
- a field absent under the projection's field contract;
- information unavailable, undisclosable, or not covered by the observation;
- a missing physical DOM target.

These conditions need different continuations. A network error is not absence.
A permission denial need not disclose whether the entity exists. A card not found
in the DOM does not establish anything about the authoritative model.

*** Absence requires the appropriate coverage evidence

For absence inferred from omission, the observation must establish the closed
coverage needed for that inference. A paginated page, partial response, filtered
subset, or redacted projection cannot silently claim complete collection absence.
The membership question must also be within the query universe actually covered.

An explicit authoritative membership-exclusion result can provide narrower
sufficient evidence without reading a whole board. Its contract binds the entity,
query, context, progression, and exclusion meaning. The requirement is evidence
for the claimed absence, not a mandatory full-collection transport format.

The runtime preserves absence with its scope and admitted evidence just as it
preserves presence. It can represent this through a complete observation,
scoped exclusion record, or checked compact summary. It need not create a permanent
tombstone for every entity that could theoretically be missing.

*** Worked example: Request R17 leaves an open-request board

Alice's applied board query selects open Requests in Organization O. An earlier
admitted observation includes R17. Another operation changes R17 so it no longer
matches that query. A current acquisition can establish different facts:

| Observation | What the browser may conclude |
|-------------+-------------------------------|
| Complete admitted membership observation for the same query, with sufficient progression, excludes R17. | R17 is absent from this board membership at the observation's declared basis. |
| Admitted explicit exclusion for R17 under this query and context. | The covered membership is absent, without asserting complete board contents. |
| A different page of a paginated result omits R17. | No exclusion follows unless the paging/coverage contract establishes it. |
| A card lookup shows R17 in another domain state. | Board removal requires the admitted predicate/coverage relationship, not a guessed filter rule. |
| The user loses permission to view this board. | Apply the display-revocation policy; do not assert that R17 was deleted. |
| The request fails or returns incompatible data. | Preserve unavailable/unknown and the required recovery debt. |

A permitted removal is an installation effect. Its permit binds the affected
membership/target scope, current owner, and actual target instances. The runtime
records adopted absence only after the declared removal postcondition is
established.

If the target is already physically absent, an admitted no-op completion may be
valid when the removal contract can establish the required postcondition for the
same current owned scope. A generic target-not-found error cannot supply that
proof. A missing or replaced board root may instead require owner reconstruction
or full reacquisition.

The adopted absence is qualified by the observation's basis. It does not claim
that no concurrent future operation can reintroduce R17 into the board. Later
relevant changes follow the same advisory/acquisition contract as later presence
changes.

*** Provisional presence does not override authoritative coverage

Suppose execution E99 provisionally displays new Request R30 while creation
command C99 remains unresolved. A board acquisition omits R30. Several questions
remain distinct:

1. Does the observation establish exclusion from the relevant query, with the
   required coverage and temporal/progression meaning?
2. Does that evidence and the presentation policy authorize removing or replacing
   this provisional overlay now?
3. Does any evidence establish the authoritative disposition of C99?

An observation taken before a still-pending command incorporates may correctly
omit R30 while C99 remains unknown and may later commit. Even a sufficiently
current board observation need not establish attribution to, or rejection of,
C99. The application must not turn absence into a fabricated rejected command.

The presentation policy can retain a labeled pending overlay, remove it under a
declared supersession rule, or present it outside the authoritative membership
view. Its choice states the evidence/horizon it requires and keeps provisional
content visibly distinct from adopted authoritative membership.

If the overlay is removed, its stale rollback/continuity rights expire. The
command's surviving recovery obligation remains with its declared owner. Removing
one scoped presentation is not permission to retire all resources associated
with C99 or to discard its recovery record.

*** Absence participates in progression and convergence

An absence observation can satisfy an obligation only within its admitted scope
and required discharge stage. Entity deletion, query exclusion, authorization
loss, and a partial projection are not interchangeable.

A later compatible presence observation can legitimately replace earlier absence
when the provider's progression and model contracts permit it. The runtime must
not make absence an irreversible browser tombstone. It must prevent a stale
presence callback from resurrecting a removed card under revoked ownership.

Display revocation has a separate reason and postcondition when current policy
requires clearing sensitive content. It may remove presentation without claiming
that authoritative domain absence was established. Its future acquisitions and
reconciliation paths still enforce current authorization.

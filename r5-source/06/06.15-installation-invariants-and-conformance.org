** Browser realization invariants and conformance

*** Authoritative installation and resource invariants

Part VI closes the browser realization contract by stating what the
*actual admitted implementation* must preserve under every relevant
event, source, owner, target and failure class. An invariant about one
portable transition is not automatically an invariant of an external
HTMX callback, an opaque effect handler, XTDB transaction, third-party
DOM writer or installed deployment. Each stronger statement requires
the corresponding realization or independently accepted assumption.

The executable browser semantics owns current coordination, local
Choreo progression, abstract effect issuance and admission. The shell
interprets those effects through registered physical handlers and
normalizes actual host events back into the same semantic state owner.
The actual protected actions remain subject to their supported
physical gates. Trusted business operations and authoritative
observations remain separate model/backend concerns. Diagnostics
and assurance artifacts may explain this process, but do not decide
which transition production executes.

**** Installation and adoption invariants

For every admitted authoritative *presence or absence*
installation, the following propositions must be maintained
under the declared observation, host and authority contracts:

| Invariant family | Required property |
|------------------+-------------------|
| Source and disclosure | The observation is qualified for the actual principal/authority context, applied query, entity or collection, fields, temporal meaning, coverage and provider progression. |
| Exact representation | The prepared content and generated operation bindings correspond to that observation, the permitted render contract and relevant executable/protocol identity. |
| Permit and current target | The runtime issued a current permit for the exact effect, owner, physical target incarnation(s) and entire mutation footprint, including admitted OOB/secondary effects. |
| Last-actuation control | Each protected physical mutation remains permitted at the last controllable act; deferred or reentrant work cannot use a revoked permit to mutate a successor. |
| Physical result | Completion records an actual correlated target-specific postcondition; partial, failed, absent or unknown physical results are not promoted to full success. |
| Scoped adoption | Acquired, installed and adopted facts are recorded only for actually covered and completed scopes; an entity card does not certify a whole board cohort. |
| Obligation discharge | Only advisory, gap, acquisition, installation or recovery obligations whose separately declared satisfaction conditions now hold are discharged. |
| Nonregression | Older board R cannot silently erase a separately newer justified card S; an old provisional snapshot cannot overwrite newer authority. |
| Negative knowledge | A removal follows a complete covered membership observation or qualified exclusion, not a missing page, selector, redacted field or denied read. |
| Independent command truth | Neither browser installation nor local timeout changes a historical command's trusted incorporation/rejection evidence. |

An acquired fact can be valid while its installation is
pending or failed. A physical card can be correctly removed
while an unrelated counter/OOB target fails, leaving only
its own completed scope adopted. A current authorized
RequestAssignment observation can coexist with a previous
historically successful Claim and later manager Reassign;
the current card does not rewrite the earlier command
outcome. A checked no-op removal counts as physical
completion only if a current qualified postcondition
covers that exact representation and owner.

The provider's progression relation, not basis-string
arithmetic, determines whether an observation supersedes
previously adopted data. Different authorized cohorts
and independently installed cards may coexist only under
a declared scope-aware merge/reacquisition policy.
A mixed view is not evidence of a fictitious single
XTDB snapshot.

**** Resource, retirement and recovery invariants

Every live semantic resource is owned by one current
execution/coordinator/recovery scope or an explicit
shared-ownership class. Physical handles may remain
opaque, but the semantic issue ID, generation and
exact owner binding are established before host issue
and retained through callbacks. A late host handle
returned after retirement is cleaned up under its
retired issue; it cannot be installed into the
successor's live registry.

For an execution E that has *semantically retired*,
the central R4 property remains:

#+begin_quote
No subsequently delivered execution-private callback or
deferred effect may use E's revoked rights to advance
current semantic state, alter current protected presentation
or assume ownership of a successor's physical resource,
regardless of whether E's physical cancellation succeeded.
Separately admitted shared coordinator and historical
command-resolution work retains only its own rights.
#+end_quote

This property does not mean physical resource cleanup is
instantaneous, perfect or free. Failed aborts, retained
listeners, delayed timer callbacks, stale layout captures
and partial DOM effects are bounded/observable cleanup
and repair obligations. Physical cancellation is an
optimization and resource-management mechanism, while
semantic revocation plus last-actuation prevention
supplies the protected safety property.

A resource cleanup operation is idempotent for *its
issued resource instance*, not for whatever now
uses the same registry key, timer number, DOM selector
or CSS value. A newer owner writing the same
=min-height= value is not proof that the old owner
may restore the style. Where the host lacks an
adequate isolation primitive, the realization must
supply owner-aware arbitration, a genuine exclusive
lease, an admitted weak guarantee or an unsupported
profile rejection.

An unresolved command's lifetime can exceed the
execution that submitted it. Its immutable command
binding, trusted resolver, deduplication contract,
retention and disclosure rules must survive the
specified failure domain where recovery is promised.
The system cannot infer =:rejected= from timeout,
browser retirement or absent current card; it
cannot infer =:committed= from HTTP 200 or
a similar-looking current entity. Unknown
incorporation and currently acquired/adopted
model state remain separate.

The fragment coordinator has at most the
declared number of active logical cycles
and coalesced scheduling slots. That bound
does not cap unbounded incomparable
requirements, pending physical attempts,
stale handles or retained command history.
Every category has its own sound
compaction, retention, backpressure,
reset or explicit unavailable policy.

**** Event/source and runtime safety invariants

Every semantic event is normalized through its
actual registered source and admitted for its
declared owner class, current resource, issued
effect, generation, legal machine state and
duplicate/replay contract. A stale private
callback cannot relabel itself as shared.
A legitimate current SSE advisory may affect
a shared coordinator after the initiating
execution retires; the event must still
respect subscription interest and authority
scope.

A physical delivery ID consumed upon
source observation stays consumed if an
initially illegal receive later becomes
legal. Conversely an independently
qualified historical command resolver
result is not discarded just because
an old transport attempt used the same
semantic command ID. Transport H1,
command C42, effect/target incarnation,
subscription interest and trusted
model observation are not interchangeable
identity classes.

The runtime installs selected next
semantic state before interpreting
effects. Immediate nested callbacks
see current state, and all later
effects in the outer batch check their
own still-current physical permit.
An error or missing mandatory handler
fails at its actual boundary; neither
observer nor optional diagnostic is
permitted to fabricate a successful
semantic effect.

The supported HTMX/asset profile must
identify genuinely effective synchronous
gates for protected sends and
mutations. A handler that logs failure
after HTMX has already performed an
unauthorized swap is not conformance.
If no supported gate exists, choose
another qualified realization or
reject that managed combination.

*** Adversarial-browser authority boundary

The protected server is exposed to arbitrary client behavior.
An adversary may bypass the generated application, alter
=hx-*=  attributes and hidden inputs, forge plan/permit
maps, replay command IDs, invoke endpoints directly,
change its DOM, retain earlier received content, emit
syntactically valid request bytes or choose unexpected
ordering and multiplicity. A correct honest browser
adapter is an implementation-quality guarantee; it is
not a security boundary that the server may trust.

Under the named authentication, model, XTDB,
cryptographic and deployment premises, the trusted
boundary must enforce at least:

- **Principal derivation:** identify the authenticated
  actor using trusted credentials/session state, not
  browser-supplied role, Organization, Membership,
  permission or plan annotations.
- **Current guarded transitions:** validate typed
  operation/argument bindings and required current
  Request, active primary RequestAssignment,
  Membership and Organization facts at the
  transaction's authoritative conflict boundary.
  A pre-read is not an atomic guard.
- **Original-command binding:** qualify immutable
  command IDs, actor/operation/schema and canonical
  arguments, deduplication, compatible replay
  and allowed retention; a copied ID cannot
  confer command rights or mask changed inputs.
- **Read and Live disclosure:** authenticate every
  protected entity/collection read and subscription,
  with current scope, tenant and source/interest
  admission. Result readback may require *current*
  disclosure permission even for historically
  incorporated commands.
- **Provider evidence:** issue or qualify the
  command, observation, collection absence,
  scope and progression assertions actually
  justified by trusted model/backend operation.
- **Failure and privacy:** distinguish inaccessible
  from absent, and unknown from rejected, without
  leaking nondisclosable existence or claiming
  secrecy can be retroactively restored after
  an adversarial client has saved data.
- **Physical interpretation boundary:** client
  certificates, rendered affordances and
  DOM installation permits never replace
  trusted operation authorization, regardless
  of any browser-side assurance success.

The strong theorem, for its admitted server
transition relation, is that no arbitrary
syntactically representable client message
can cause an unauthorized domain transition,
protected disclosure or subscription merely
by inventing browser-side proof/correlation
metadata. The precise theorem must name
authentication, model authorization,
transaction atomicity, consistency,
request schema and output-disclosure
assumptions. A framework cannot certify
arbitrary opaque model side effects merely
by testing its HTTP dispatcher.

Client content safety and browser permissions
are separate: even an authenticated authoritative
observation may contain unsafe interpolated user
content. Escaping, script/extension handling,
response headers, OOB targets and active
HTML policy follow the qualified 06.03
rendering/transport contract. An adversarial
client may modify its own DOM, so the server
security argument does not assert that
hostile-client presentation stays honest.

An invalid endpoint check should not prevent
the *positive* authorized operation from
working. HumanHelp's independent acceptance
therefore exercises real eligible Create,
Claim, Unclaim, Done, Cancel and manager
Reassign as their actual production model
rules allow, plus negative competing-Claim,
revoked-Membership and cross-tenant attempts.
Rejecting all submissions, including
authorized ones, is not product conformance.

*** Required conformance scenarios

The finished conformance program combines portable
transition/property checks, application construction/
preflight checks, exact finite or stronger semantic
proofs where applicable, trace/source correspondence,
real browser/HTMX physical tests, trusted backend
operation tests, and multi-node/recovery tests for
supported profiles. Each layer states its actual
test subject, assumptions and nonclaims.

For each required safety property, the suite uses
an *eligible positive control* that demonstrably
reaches the tested physical/model boundary and a
nearby adverse case that reaches the same boundary
with the decisive premise changed. A test that
rejects every action because no handler was
installed, no authenticated user exists, or no
network response can be obtained is not a valid
positive conformance test.

| Test area | Eligible positive witness | Adverse/uncertain witness and required result |
|-----------+---------------------------+----------------------------------------------|
| Synchronous HTMX gate | An authorized bound request/swap executes once using a qualified cancellable profile. | Stale or unprojected request reaches the effective gate and is stopped *before* send/mutation; no silent raw fallback. |
| Main, OOB and response-driven effects | Authorized complete multi-target footprint installs with checked postconditions. | =hx-swap="none"=, retargeting, headers, script or unpermitted OOB effects are prevented or the unsupported profile is rejected. |
| Physical issue and callback reentrancy | An issued timer/local effect can complete synchronously, correlated to committed semantic state. | It retires before its host handle returns; the late handle is released and later outer-batch effects remain gated. |
| Wrong-target and partial DOM effects | Current B2/observation permit physically installs its prepared representation. | Stale B1 callback or failed second target cannot mutate B2 or claim a complete board. |
| Optional continuity and ownership | Valid focus/draft/height restoration runs for its current capture and target. | Old same-value CSS cleanup or delayed RAF cannot clobber a newer owner; optional failure does not annul canonical adoption. |
| Valid command and timeout | Eligible C42 Claim reaches trusted RequestAssignment write and correct bound result. | H1 timeout/lost response remains unknown without trusted rejection; H2 cannot duplicate or reinterpret C42. |
| Independent model authority | Current authorized Request, actor/Organization and active primary assignment guard succeeds. | Competing helper, revoked Membership, cross-tenant/client-forged tags fail at trusted guarded operation, not by hiding the button. |
| Current card versus command history | A historical successful Claim and newer Reassign both have qualified evidence; the newest authorized card installs. | Old optimistic settlement cannot restore prior helper or relabel the earlier Claim rejected. |
| Qualified negative membership | Complete Qopen or explicit exclusion proves R17 absent, and current target removal completes. | Partial page omission, unauthorized read or missing DOM selector never proves global deletion or adopted board absence. |
| One explicitly granted Refresh | Bob's current Q read occurs exactly once, installs covered O1/O2 state and retains separately newer S card. | Advisory alone or queued successor does not issue A2; uncovered, failed or incomparable debt remains. |
| Initial subscription and reconnect | Baseline/interest fence or qualified replay/current reacquisition covers missed W and establishes the declared continuity. | Stream silence, router redelivery lacking never-generated W or lifecycle wakeup alone cannot claim no gap. |
| Query, principal or plan change | Current Q2 and supported P8/asset/host combination admit new owner with authorized disclosure. | Old Q1/P7 callback or stale principal rights cannot install/disclose; unresolved command survives separately as promised. |
| Registration and resource cleanup | Current effect/interest registrations are exact and cleanup releases only their own physical instances. | Throwing abort or reused timer/selector retains bounded cleanup debt but cannot revive old semantic rights. |
| Duplicates, early messages and conflicting outcomes | A legal delivery is consumed once and an identical qualified repeat follows declared idempotence. | Early M1 cannot be replayed later; conflicting same-ID evidence produces a typed fault/reconciliation. |
| Authoritative acquisition and installation | Qualified provider response and real target postcondition advance only their actual scopes. | HTTP 200, copied basis witness or generic afterSwap cannot manufacture full-board adoption. |
| Actual trace and global refinement | Role-local, sender/delivery/global schedule and independently sourced physical/model witnesses align for the claimed concrete subject. | Candidate-only receive, synthetic send, omitted OOB mutation or ambiguous identity cannot pass a stronger global certificate. |
| Diagnostics and proof noninterference | Same canonical plan and inputs execute identically with a bounded observer present/absent. | Throwing/corrupt/overfull instrumentation cannot change branches, hide losses or grant admission. |
| Capacity and liveness | Under stated quiescence, grant, fairness and availability, an eligible user acquires and adopts the required scope. | Permanent denial, exhausted read budget or overflow does not justify unconditional convergence; resource loss gets explicit safe disposition. |
| Hostile client | An eligible authenticated operation succeeds with correct model result and authorized disclosure. | Arbitrary request bytes, forged certificates/permits and changed actor/argument claims cannot cause unauthorized effects. |
| Release and application closure | Every actual admitted route, browser asset, handler, worker, subscription and positive HumanHelp path is covered by the installed assembly/profile. | Unknown alternate renderer, extension writer, old service worker or unchecked route prevents the stronger whole-application claim. |

Coverage generation varies message order, request and
callback delay, nested completion, physical cancellation
failure, OOB partial mutation, owner/resource reuse,
read and write races, provider incomparability,
query/tenant transitions, duplicate delivery,
network partition and durable recovery expiry.
It must not assume a single convenient concrete
integer instance for an unbounded identity property.
No test fixture may simply return the *desired*
progression token, attest nonexistent physical
completion or self-report a trusted backend
result as its own evidence.

A good finite test suite can establish a range
of actual executions under a precise host
profile and fail on real mismatches. It cannot
turn tested traces into a quantified inductive
theorem. An exact finite model checker can
establish its enumerated subject and
assumptions, while a source/physical
correspondence check must additionally bind
those model events to the *real* installed
code and external transaction/host behavior.
A stronger claim requires an independent
proof or justified abstraction and the
identified trust premises.

Conformance evidence identifies the exact
loaded runtime/library/configuration,
application/deployment assembly, backend
provider, renderer and physical handlers,
observed source/extractor, relevant tests,
known failures, unsupported paths and
assumptions. Replacing a dependency or
enabling a new HTMX extension may invalidate
the profile: nominal version compatibility
alone does not qualify a newly exposed DOM
writer. Unsupported protected paths are
rejected or explicitly accepted only under
a weaker named guarantee, never quietly
included in a strong certificate.

*** Assembly obligations and the browser realization invariant

The application constructor must account for
the actual managed paths it emits and serves,
not just a sample Hiccup function or one
favorable demonstration route. A closed
application inspection relates:

| Assembly obligation | Required closure |
|---------------------+------------------|
| Semantic operations | Model-owned operation definitions, roles, typed arguments, current guards, authoritative transaction/readback and eligible user paths. |
| Executable projection | The exact Choreo plan/operation/observation mapping, installed interpreter, semantic preflight and known dynamic recovery branches. |
| HTTP and rendering | All relevant Ring/Biff/HTMX routes, response modes, alternate writers, templates, fragment roots, target/operation bindings and content safety policy. |
| Browser startup and assets | Actual entrypoints, compiled CLJS/JS modules, handler registrations, HTMX/extensions, service workers, cache/deployment compatibility and lifecycle gates. |
| Physical resources | Timer/transport/continuity/optimism handlers, source registrations, exact target/owner incarnation, last-actuation gate, partial effects and cleanup policy. |
| Live/coordinator | Interest/subscription lifecycle, baseline/gap fence, applied query/grant policy, obligation retention, progression and actual adopted installation. |
| Recovery | Incompatible plan retirement, surviving command intent/resolver, unknown-result handling, current disclosure, capacity and unavailable/manual alternatives. |
| Assurance subject | Invariants, exact finite model subject or stronger proof, actual source/executor/physical evidence, kernel/checker scope, known trust premises and deployment version binding. |

The construction pipeline may use Clojure's
full language and opaque application effects,
but it must require the admitted contract
for the effects and relationships that
its guarantees rely upon. It need not
statically predict future XTDB values,
network delivery, DOM disappearance,
which actor wins a competing Claim,
or whether a target becomes inaccessible.
It must, however, reject a missing
mandatory operation/effect realization,
inconsistent source or route binding,
known unsupported protected host gate,
invalid plan/protocol relation, or
dynamic condition with no declared
safe failure/recovery path when that
path is required by the selected guarantee.

Preflight and deployed runtime have
different jobs. Static admission
establishes the complete declared
construction, supported profile
and required recovery contracts.
Runtime admission checks the
current principal, context, owner,
source, applicable progression,
target and effect at the actual
boundary. Physical tests and
trusted provider witnesses establish
remaining observable facts where
the stated assurance requires them.
A successful static report alone
does not justify executing against
a changed runtime, or assert that
a browser's actual DOM changed.

No silent downgrade is admitted
for a protected managed affordance.
If the selected projection,
preflight, handler registration,
qualified HTMX hook or trusted
operation route is unavailable,
the application must fail at
the appropriate construction,
startup or runtime boundary,
not submit an unprotected
command while reporting that
the managed flow was enforced.
Ordinary explicitly declared
raw HTMX interactions remain
supported under their own
complete server authorization
and rendering contracts.

**** Unified Part VI invariant

#+begin_quote
For every admitted managed browser action, the actual
application has one selected executable semantic meaning
and a current qualified owner, source, plan, effect and
authority/observation scope. Its physical realization is
declared for the installed host profile, and every
protected act is prevented unless the applicable
current permit remains valid at its controllable
mutation/send boundary. All reported consequences
are attributed to the actual issued work and suitably
independent source/physical evidence. A request or
trusted command result is not by itself completed
DOM installation; acquired authority is adopted only
for its genuinely covered and physically completed
scope. Stale owner and provisional restoration rights
expire before best-effort cleanup, without cancelling
independently surviving command-resolution or Live
obligations. Failed, partial, absent and unknown
evidence remains distinct from trusted incorporation
or rejection, and neither opaque basis arithmetic nor
uncontrolled response effects may invent progression.
Current trusted authorization remains mandatory even
if an adversarial client ignores the browser entirely.
Logical concurrency, physical resources, obligation
retention and conditional liveness obey their own
declared bounds and environmental premises.
#+end_quote

This is a *normative invariant of the finished
supported realization*. Its applicability is
established through independently checked
obligations at the semantic, construction,
trusted model, physical host, subscription
and deployment layers.
A real deployment claim must report
which ones are discharged and which
remain conditional, unknown or
unsupported.

**** Whole-part reconciliation and independent acceptance remain distinct

Completing the fifteen numbered Part VI
sectional reviews is a necessary editorial
milestone, not automatically accepted
whole-part architecture or proof. The
next reconciliation explicitly checks:

- consistent identity and terminology
  for command C42, execution E42,
  physical H1/H2 attempts, effect,
  representation, target incarnation,
  coordinator, grant, authority
  context and deployed artifact;
- consistent transitions from source
  admission to physical last-actuation,
  qualified acquisition, installation,
  adoption and unmet debt;
- absence of contradictory HTMX
  control, OOB/response-effect,
  refresh/single-flight or
  shared-subscription claims;
- actual recovery/retirement
  ownership across 06.08–06.11,
  including durable command
  promises and resource bounds;
- R4 preservation coverage without
  accidentally restoring
  superseded illustrative APIs;
- Parts IV–V and VII–XII joins,
  especially HumanHelp positive
  product behavior, real installed
  application closure, provider
  correspondence and precise
  formal proof subjects;
- source/publication assembly,
  cross-part Org references,
  stable anchors, examples,
  trust-premise consistency,
  conformance matrix and
  explicitly unfinished
  implementation requirements.

An independent candidate-acceptance
review then evaluates the *whole*
reconciled Part VI, including whether
the specified abstractions are
compatible and sufficient for the
finished-system target. Only that
later decision should mark Part VI
accepted. It is not obtained merely
by counting reviewed files or
successfully concatenating them.

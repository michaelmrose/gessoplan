## Governing decision

Retain R5's exact finite-checking, typed-atom/propositional distinction,
small proof kernel, dedicated semantic checkers and explicit external
formal-bridge architecture. Extend the *normative* contract so “finite
checking passed” is impossible to interpret as universal checker
soundness, coverage of all real application writer paths, or production
XTDB/browser refinement. R4 already required honest assurance classes,
independently checked proof obligations, and fault containment of a
buggy elaborator; the more explicit R5.1 design makes these falsifiable.

The ordinary Gesso/HumanHelp author declares business rules,
operations and product requirements in Clojure/Gesso. Gesso is free
to use Recife, TLC, Quint, a local finite checker, or a separate
mathematical proof formalization behind the scenes. Users do not
maintain independently authored TLA+/Quint/Recife operation models
merely to enable framework correctness checks.

## R4 preservation and substantive refinements

1. **Evidence classes are not promotional badges.** Machine-checked
   model result, exact instantiated truth-table validity,
   source-bound conditional abstract proof, trusted external
   model assumption, runtime-enforced actor/read, installed
   artifact observation and product acceptance are different
   propositions. R4's proof-DAG vocabulary survives, while
   a single green “verified” label cannot substitute for the
   independent root application's required claims.

2. **An exact finite result identifies the finite subject.**
   Record the nominal state and input domains, initial-state
   predicate/list, all enabled operations and nondeterminism,
   state equality, declared property, checking strategy and
   completion status. A depth-limited trace is not exhaustive
   unless a separate completeness argument establishes the
   reachable-state bound. Dropping an out-of-domain successor
   cannot establish complete state-space closure.

3. **Full enum/Boolean coverage differs from finite representatives.**
   A closed enum domain can be represented completely, subject to
   its nominal type and source/version. A finite list of integers,
   strings, timestamps, user IDs, assignments or tenant scopes
   is ordinarily a selected sample, not the mathematical type's
   entire domain. Omitted nil/absent, alternative actors,
   competing assignments, late retries and multiple generations
   can change the theorem's actual applicability.

4. **A valid mathematical base may be vacuous.** An empty initializer
   class or universally disabled transitions may satisfy safety
   automatically. R4's positive acceptance spirit survives
   through separate inhabited-state witnesses, eligible
   operations and valid end-to-end application behaviors.
   A finite result over RequestStatus alone does not prove
   the independently promised primary RequestAssignment
   uniqueness requirement.

5. **Abstraction/refinement is a separate theorem.** A finite
   over-approximation can establish a property of a larger
   system only when a qualified relation covers concrete
   initial states and every relevant concrete transition
   and preserves the proposition. A finite sample
   under-approximation has no such implication by default.
   Symmetry, partial-order and stutter reductions need their
   own soundness conditions. A model counterexample may be
   spurious until a concrete trace/binding is shown.

6. **Exact Boolean truth tables have a specific quantifier.**
   An instantiated propositional tautology checked over
   all Boolean valuations is valid for every Boolean
   interpretation of its fixed atoms. This does not
   establish the universal soundness of every proof rule
   schema or correctness of the production JVM checker.
   It can rule out a particular unsupported derivation
   when a real Boolean countervaluation is found.

7. **Typed atoms may be correlated.** Distinct Boolean placeholders
   can be constrained by one Request binding. A valuation
   in which two typed predicates have logically inconsistent
   values is useful propositional falsification but is
   not automatically a reachable typed/app counterexample.
   The typed bridge checks nominal readers, nullability,
   shared source, bindings and actual simultaneous evaluation.
   A genuine typed counterexample needs a valid witness.

8. **Counterexamples are diagnostic artifacts, not just labels.**
   Record original source/operation identity, initial state,
   ordered enabled transitions, input values, exact failing
   property and replay result. Minimization may shrink
   the trace without changing what the trace witnesses.
   The diagnostic distinguishes model failure from
   physical exploit and from malformed source/incomplete checking.

9. **Safety, liveness, and fairness are distinct.** Exhaustively
   inspecting reachable states for a state invariant
   does not prove user Refresh, eventual outbox delivery,
   release of every physical target or a fair schedule.
   A finite temporal theorem must name its scheduling,
   cycle/fairness and temporal interpretation. Part VIII's
   advisory-only remote lifecycle policy explicitly
   permits nonadoption until authorized user Refresh.

10. **Independence is about implementation and semantics.**
    A captured reference function that recomputes its own
    output can detect replaced public Vars but not its
    own defect. Multiple wrappers sharing the parser,
    rule evaluator and operation generator have common
    assumptions. A separately written finite oracle,
    mathematical interpreter or model checker can
    challenge more of the trusted surface, but its
    shared semantics and scope must still be inventoried.

11. **Finite mutation testing is falsification, not meta-proof.**
    A test that detects an injected invalid proof rule
    is valuable evidence about that case. Passing
    thousands of source mutants or giving a module
    “soundness” in its name is not a theorem for all
    source programs. Universally sound proof-kernel
    inference needs a correct rule calculus and
    proof/checking-implementation correspondence,
    not a statistical score.

12. **Trusted base is per judgment.** Parser and decoder, typed
    state equality, source identity, abstract interpreter,
    proof kernel, certificate issuer, backend data
    acquisition and installed-browser facts have
    different trusted primitives. R4's desire for a
    small logical kernel does not make every
    dedicated state-model checker part of it.

13. **External formal evidence needs an explicit bridge.**
    A theorem checked by TLC/Recife/Quint/proof assistant
    must be tied to the exact formula, model semantics,
    configuration, checker/tool version, assumptions
    and original Gesso source/projection. Proof of a
    calculus alone does not prove a Clojure parser,
    implementation, installed Biff handler, XTDB
    transaction or browser/HTMX path.

14. **Coverage of required obligations precedes strong claims.**
    Every submitted derivation can be correct while
    the elaborator omits an original branch or
    required Reassign operation. The source/independent
    product coverage checker—not the finite-model
    oracle pretending to inspect external code—
    detects the omitted obligation. A matching
    proof and wrong generated executable is a
    separate lowering-correspondence defect.

15. **Incomplete result is neither success nor falsity.**
    State/time/depth/case budgets, absent external
    runner, unsupported operation, invalid type
    or timed-out formal checker have explicit
    failure categories. A counterexample to
    an actual accepted finite property differs
    from a solver failure or malformed proof term.

16. **Host portability needs both host runs.**
    A common `.cljc` file executed only on the
    JVM is not evidence of JVM/Node agreement.
    The same normalized source and results from
    both actual runtimes must be compared under
    declared integer, nil/absent, enum, keyword,
    Unicode and short-circuit codec premises.
    Finite agreement does not prove all
    platform operations.

17. **Certificates and reports never self-authorize.**
    Copyable finite-oracle reports, prior proof
    transcripts, source fingerprints and
    locally accepted certificate-shaped data
    do not create runtime rights. A certified
    construction rechecks required source,
    checker version, applicability and
    dependency premises at its trusted
    admission boundary.

18. **Non-vacuous positive and negative acceptance.**
    Genuine exhaustive small-model safety,
    valid Boolean tautology, correctly
    correlated typed atom, and a supported
    narrow conditional checker profile
    must pass, while faulty transitions,
    unsupported stronger claims and
    proof/coverage substitutions are
    rejected at their first correct
    boundary. An implementation that
    rejects every input cannot satisfy
    the acceptance corpus.

## Actual Gesso v1622 source correspondence

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/finite_model_oracle.clj`
  (v1309) implements a separate bounded, data-only
  guarded-replacement semantic oracle. It intentionally
  does not invoke production rule evaluation, proof
  kernel, transition checker, induction checker or
  certificate/admission paths. Its finite state/input
  vectors distinguish exact enum/Boolean coverage
  from sampled integer/string/keyword domains, represent
  optional absence with a distinct marker and check
  base and step preservation on enumerated typed
  instances. The returned object is a **copyable
  diagnostic**, not a certificate or universal
  invariant theorem.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/propositional_soundness.clj`
  (v1318) independently audits the original
  source-derived propositional derivation via
  finite Boolean truth tables without asking the
  production kernel whether its report was valid.
  It audits intermediate step entailments and
  premise-to-goal entailment. Its source explicitly
  excludes arbitrary typed-atom interpretation,
  unbounded rule-schema meta-soundness and
  physical authority.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/independent_transition_semantics.clj`
  (v1351) independently interprets abstract
  guarded pre/input/post states using typed
  presence/branch semantics without invoking
  the production transition checker. It
  validates one abstract transition witness;
  it does not prove completeness of the
  physical writer inventory or actual
  XTDB transaction correspondence.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/preservation_composition_soundness.clj`
  (v1359) provides a restricted independent
  conditional conjunction-composition schema.
  It requires every top-level invariant
  clause to be independently well typed
  and supported by an exact known rule
  family. It leaves nested/cross-clause
  optional-refinement cases open when
  the selected restricted calculus cannot
  justify them. It issues neither
  certification nor production authority.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/abstract_execution_induction.clj`
  (v1362) derives a conditional induction
  schema for a **finite enumerated
  initializer set** and closed
  **declared** abstract operation set.
  It can audit a finite abstract history,
  but physical initialization coverage,
  writer completeness and checker
  meta-soundness remain unproved.

- `test/gesso/assurance/proof/kernel_independent_soundness_test.clj`
  (v1424) defines independent local Boolean
  semantics and mutation cases across
  all seven current propositional rules.
  It describes itself as a bounded
  falsification gate, not proof of
  universal meta-soundness.

- `test/gesso/assurance/proof/recife_finite_correspondence_test.clj`
  (v1438) defines separate local guarded
  operations, the finite-model oracle
  and Recife/TLC tests over two
  one-shot processes with enum/Boolean
  domains. It expects a mutation to
  produce a TLC counterexample. That
  fixture does not exercise all
  HumanHelp operations, all writers
  or real distributed/XTDB behavior.
  Its tests were read, **not run**.

- `test/gesso/assurance/proof/jvm_cljs_semantics_test.cljc`
  contains a shared bounded JVM/Node
  probe/corpus including Boolean,
  safe integer, optional/nil,
  enum and negative transport
  cases. The file explicitly says
  running a test in only one host
  is insufficient to claim host
  parity. This editorial review
  did not execute either host.

These are meaningful concrete mechanisms supporting the
preferred design. There is no evidence from this inspection
that the complete compiler/proof-kernel/JVM/CLJS/XTDB/browser
correspondence chain or the meta-soundness of arbitrary
checker implementations has already been certified.

## Independent positive/adversarial matrix

1. Complete reachable-state search of a true two-operation
   finite enum/Boolean machine passes with a nonempty
   initial set, all enabled successors and an
   exact claimed safety invariant.
2. Mutating one guarded assignment causes a
   replayable property counterexample.
3. A valid finite model with a small case budget
   reports incomplete, not safe.
4. A trace-depth bound that misses later
   reachable states cannot report exact completion.
5. A successor outside the declared finite
   domain causes closure failure or a separately
   justified abstraction, never silent dropping.
6. An empty initial set is detected or reported
   as vacuously safe without satisfying
   product non-vacuity.
7. An unmodeled Reassign or second RequestAssignment
   entity cannot inherit a unique-primary proof
   from RequestStatus-only sample state.
8. A legitimate fixed-proposition tautology
   passes exhaustive Boolean valuations;
   one false valuation gives a
   propositional counterexample.
9. Two correlated typed atoms with an impossible
   opposing valuation do not produce a
   physical/typed counterexample.
10. A consistent typed binding makes the
    counterexample real for that declared
    typed rule interpretation.
11. A changed nominal enum, nil/absent encoding
    or mathematical integer profile invalidates
    a prior comparison or subject identity.
12. A separate oracle catches an intentionally
    unsound inference-rule or step substitution
    without relying on the kernel's own result.
13. Two wrappers calling one production
    checker do not count as two independent
    rule-soundness witnesses.
14. A valid conditional proof of a stated
    proposition retains unresolved
    checker-meta and XTDB correspondence
    assumptions.
15. A complete model checker with no actual
    observed committed backend transitions
    cannot certify all production writers
    or current Claim permission.
16. A finite Recife/TLC run with its exact
    selected two operations can pass
    its selected invariant; an inserted
    mutant must produce the relevant
    model counterexample.
17. Running the JVM shared corpus alone
    cannot establish Node/CLJS behavior;
    both independent process results
    are required for the selected
    portability regression claim.
18. A changed elaboration or omitted
    required proof obligation is caught
    by the corresponding source/coverage
    boundary, not credited to a finite
    oracle that never inspected it.
19. A copied green oracle report or
    self-proclaimed theorem cannot
    authorize an XTDB mutation or
    browser target installation.
20. A real valid finite model and
    admitted narrow-profile application
    remain usable when a stronger
    checker lacks formal meta-soundness;
    the requested stronger profile
    remains explicitly unachieved.

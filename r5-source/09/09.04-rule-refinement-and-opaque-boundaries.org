*** Path-sensitive presence and null refinement

The type judgment carries a reader-type environment and refinements for the
current expression path. Refinements refer to the same binding identity and
observation context; they cannot migrate to another entity or replacement read.

For sequential conjunction, later operands receive facts established when earlier
operands were true. For disjunction, later operands receive facts established
when earlier operands were false. Rule-level implication checks its consequent
under facts established by a true antecedent. Negation reverses the relevant
truth condition. At a control-flow join, retain only facts justified for every
incoming path unless an explicit rule establishes more.

For an optional nullable score, this order is safe:

#+begin_src clojure
{:readers #{:case/score}
 :expression
 [:and
  [:present [:read :case/score]]
  [:not= [:read :case/score] [:literal nil]]
  [:>= [:read :case/score] [:literal 10]]]}
#+end_src

The presence test does not prove non-nil. Moving the comparison before either
needed refinement is rejected. Replacing the presence test by ordinary truthiness
is incorrect for present false or nil values. The conformance corpus covers
absence, present nil, and each permitted scalar case separately.

*** Conservative normalization

Canonicalization preserves every distinction on which typing, evaluation, or
proof identity depends. The baseline may normalize map/set insertion order,
equivalent mathematical integer representations, and explicit default-false type
flags. It preserves operator and operand order, nominal enum names/domains,
optional versus nullable, and the distinction between absence and nil.

Do not sort, flatten, commute, fold, or rewrite short-circuit expressions merely
because their Boolean truth tables look equivalent. Such transformations can
change safe read order, refinement validity, error behavior, or source coverage.
A stronger normalization rule needs a specified preservation theorem or an
explicitly qualified trusted transformation with its actual assurance class.

Identity includes relevant language, type, evaluation, normalization, and encoding
versions. A fingerprint names a normalized interpretation; it is not a logical-
equivalence decision or a proof of its truth.

*** Opaque policy and relational extensions

Production policy may be richer than the baseline AST. A named Organization
policy function can be invoked outside the pure rule interpreter through an
admitted contract. Its Boolean result can enter the guard as a typed reader,
with provenance and dependency metadata binding that result to its actual inputs,
implementation, and protected evaluation context.

This does not make the policy true because a browser supplied =true=. It also
does not make the policy function pure or complete because its result is Boolean.
The operation's atomic dependency set includes every declared authoritative input
on which that policy result depends, or the function is recomputed at an equivalent
protected boundary. Hidden effects or unknown dependencies remain an explicit
implementation-contract risk accepted or rejected by the profile.

Relational schemas, quantified relationship constraints, finite family rules,
and richer transition constructors may use dedicated interpreters. Each registers
its syntax, typed environments, evaluation/transition meaning, obligations,
normalization, evidence class, and bridge to dependent judgments. A familiar
keyword such as =:relationship/valid= is not itself an inference rule.

** Profiles, diagnostics, and policy review

A certification profile is a *versioned admission policy* over exact
claim/proposition families, mandatory judgments, permitted evidence
mechanisms, open premise classes, implementation and installation
applicability, and positive acceptance requirements. It is enforced at
the real construction and relevant installation/effect boundaries. A
profile is neither a percent of the application's Clojure lines nor a
global toggle between “proved” and “unchecked.” The same operation may
have kernel-checked typing, finite-model evidence for one invariant,
a conditional abstract transition proof, a runtime guard obligation,
an opaque policy implementation premise and separately tested browser
behavior. None of those classifications silently absorbs the others.

The selected profile cannot disable the baseline semantic-route,
application-assembly, trusted-ingress and rendered-affordance closure
required by Part VII. When a stronger profile asks for unavailable
proof or physical correspondence, the result is a typed failure or
explicitly requested weaker product. The constructor does not
automatically downgrade a managed operation to an ordinary HTMX POST,
interpret a trusted assertion as checked derivation, or call a
timed-out verification successful.

*** Executable policy, claim-specific admission and applicability

An admitted profile specifies a stable profile identity/version and,
for each supported proposition category, at least:

- the original-source and required-obligation inventory owner,
  semantic/checker rules and accepted evidence class;
- which judgments are *mandatory* and where all of their cases,
  premises, dependencies and non-vacuity witnesses come from;
- which well-typed, precisely scoped opaque or environmental
  assumptions may remain open and which must be discharged;
- the required source, checker, compiler, model, host, provider,
  browser and writer correspondence, when the claim requires it;
- the applicable build, deployment, principal, resource and
  observation-generation boundaries, including when rechecking
  or requalification is required;
- how incompleteness, unsupported statements, resource exhaustion,
  contradictory premises and physical uncertainty affect issuance;
- any independently selected product capability, privacy, safety
  and progress gates that cannot be inferred from a proof catalog.

A baseline compositional profile checks closed source categories,
types and interpretation, source-derived complete obligation and
dependency closure, soundly bounded admitted derivations for
claims described as checked, exact original subject/proof identity,
and allowed premise accounting. It permits an *explicit* opaque
implementation or provider premise only for the specific
claim and scope its policy allows. Proof reports and supplied
claim descriptions are never accepted as issuer authority.

A stronger conditional-induction profile requires an inhabited
initial-condition argument where applicable, every invariant
base case and every operation/invariant preservation judgment
of the selected original catalog, the correct frame/guard
semantics, and precisely inherited open premises. A valid
but unsupported abstract effect requires a qualified dedicated
checker, supported refinement, or rejection of that requested
strong profile; neither eliding the operation nor calling its
preservation “trusted” yields an induction theorem. Coverage
of a declared operation catalog is not complete coverage of
physical non-HTTP writers or initializers.

A production-correspondence profile additionally requires the
specified trusted source-to-issued executor, actually installed
handler, writer/guard provenance, source-to-asset binding and
physical effect/observation evidence. It must say whether its
conclusion is an abstract model judgment, a bounded test
observation, one authenticated production occurrence or a
universal refinement under named simulation and external
premises. Merely selecting a profile with the word “production”
cannot authenticate a caller-supplied route inventory, ledger,
basis, browser stamp or purported transaction outcome.

The profile's interpretation, accepted external envelopes and
checking rules are themselves dependencies. New code, a changed
checker/kernel semantic version, withdrawn assumption
qualification, changed installed assets or changed policy
requirements invalidate *affected* consumer admission as
specified by 09.10–09.12. An old certificate cannot veto a
new policy or retain revoked source/physical applicability.
Immutable unrelated abstract proofs may be reused when their
actual claims and trusted dependencies remain valid.

A checked semantic source and its profile may be edited or
revised by application authority, but a consumer must use
the selected, currently admitted profile and the appropriate
application/deployment generation. Source-selected compatible
profiles must not be replaced by an untrusted request parameter
or report value. A disabled or omitted optional checking gate
is still different from a passed strict gate; the report must
make that distinction apparent to release reviewers.

*** First-blocker diagnostics with evidence and repair context

A rejected or incomplete construction produces a stable structured
diagnostic rather than only an exception string, unqualified green
badge or cascade of dozens of dependent errors. Each primary
diagnostic identifies the original subject, required proposition,
source span/path when available, selected checker/profile,
required versus supplied types/facts/bindings, attempted use
site and role, evidence status, first *actually enforcing*
boundary, dependency chain, and artifacts/effects not admitted.
Additional independent failures may be reported, but downstream
objects that were never constructed are consequences rather than
new defects. Lexical iteration order of an unordered map must not
randomize a first-blocker selection.

A diagnostic's *source authority* is as important as its code.
A copied report cannot establish that a check ran; an
unauthenticated physical snapshot cannot be reported as a
transaction-level witness. Source-derived diagnostics may
be copyable and useful without granting any proof certificate,
model-write permission or browser capability. Sensitive
counterexamples, principal/tenant facts and private policy
inputs must be redacted or represented by authorized
projections; a useful explanation must not become an
unintended disclosure channel.

The principal classification distinguishes:

| Category | Meaning and first relevant boundary |
|----------+-------------------------------------|
| Malformed syntax or type | Invalid reader, unknown source reference, nominal mismatch, illegal comparison, cardinality or unsupported data form at source/checker admission. |
| Refinement/presence unavailable | Reading an optional/nil/absent field or using a value before the required branch-local refinement holds. |
| Knowledge/provenance unavailable | The correct rule is used at a role/program point without justified source, disclosure, branch knowledge or relevant authoritative observation. |
| Authority/resource unavailable | Required trusted placement, current actor/resource rights or physical permit is absent at the enforcing boundary. |
| Proof/derivation invalid | Unknown rule, missing premise, bad inference/substitution, wrong subject, forged status or mismatched proof conclusion. |
| Required coverage missing | Original invariant/operation/branch/source dependency or independently required product capability is omitted from the claimed closure. |
| Dependency/version stale | Source semantic identity, checked interface, checker profile, callable, provider, artifact or installation scope no longer fits the relied-on claim. |
| Counterexample | The admitted model and explicitly checked assumptions permit a trace violating a declared invariant or property; the failing trace is a counterexample *at that scope*. |
| Unsupported judgment | Valid-looking construction asks for a checker/fragment, physical inference or guarantee unavailable under the selected profile. |
| Incomplete checking | Timeout, resource bound, cancelled or failed checker, unqualified observation, unavailable dependency or truncation makes the requested conclusion unestablished. |
| Profile/premise not admitted | The required assumption, checker category, release policy or product guarantee is disallowed even though its syntax may be well formed. |
| Advisory intent finding | A coherent checked policy appears suspicious or inconsistent with independently recorded business intent; the reviewer has not established a mechanical theorem or rejection. |

A failed writer/authorization check after the effect is *detected
post hoc*, not evidence of pre-effect prevention. A nominal proof
counterexample is not an authenticated deployed history. An
unsupported tool is not a failed proof of the intended statement,
and a reject-all application does not satisfy positive product
requirements just because no forbidden transition was observed.
The response reports those distinctions with the original
source, missing obligation and trust boundary.

Diagnostics should be actionable: for example, if
=:request/status= is a closed nominal enum, the expression
=(< request.status 10)= is malformed because no ordering
exists between =RequestStatus= and =Integer=. The report
names that operator, actual types, source rule, blocked
=VerifiedRule=, and downstream Claim/Choreo/plan products
that were *not* issued. It may recommend using an admitted
status-equality/membership predicate, but must not silently
rewrite the author's eligibility policy.

If =request.rules= was checked against Request schema S17
and S18 replaces =:claimed= with =:assigned=, the new
schema may be valid; *composition* rejects the old
dependent as stale. The diagnostic identifies both
interfaces and the relied-on changed guarantee rather
than calling S18 malformed or a copied cached
certificate current. Source and implementation changes
have distinct invalidation paths.

If an otherwise well-typed =approved?= predicate reads
private =request.private-review= in a helper-browser
branch before authoritative disclosure/knowledge, its
source typing remains established while its particular
distributed use is rejected. The report names the
role/state/required provenance, and proposes a
permitted server-derived public decision or correctly
scoped acquisition *if* its authority and privacy
preconditions can actually be met; it does not
suggest leaking the private field.

*** Mechanically invalid, formally counterexampled, product-wrong

The semantic checker proves/checks what the application
*actually declared*, under its recognized semantics.
It cannot infer the business owner's desired policy
from prose, an interface's friendly name or a
neighboring rule. In a typed age policy example:

#+begin_src clojure
;; Schematic authoring syntax; not an asserted existing API.
(rule adult? (> user.age 18))
#+end_src

This is a coherent rule when =user.age= is an
integer, even if product authority meant
“18 or older.” In contrast, comparing
=email= (an email/string nominal type) with
=18= using =< = is a semantic type error.
A source-level checker should reject the
type error and accept the well-typed rule
unless some *independently declared*
invariant or property is contradicted.

Where a well-formed rule allows a trace
violating an admitted property, the checker
can report a reproducible bounded/abstract
counterexample together with its model,
state, input, transition and assumed
scope. It must not infer physical XTDB
execution from a synthetic trace or invent
a new business rule in the process.

A reviewer might regard the well-typed
age rule, an asymmetrically permissive
Claim, a missing helper-role condition,
an unexpectedly reopening terminal
Request or a weaker disclosure rule
as suspicious. That is an *advisory
intent finding*, not an erroneous
proof-kernel rejection. Application
ownership determines whether the
policy changes. If accepted, the new
intent becomes a source-owned
explicit rule, invariant, operation
contract or product acceptance gate,
and normal source derivation/checking
runs again. It is never quietly
patched into a checker or browser
adapter to conceal a policy change.

Keep human-readable meaning, rationale,
security/lifecycle correctness notes,
examples and counterexamples adjacent
to the source semantic declarations,
and carry them through audit views
where feasible. These descriptions
are review evidence, not hidden
axioms or silently executable
authorization tests.

*** Semantic-policy inventory and escape ownership

Gesso compresses correctness-relevant
application decisions into inspectable
named rules, interfaces, operations,
invariants and explicitly scoped
implementation/host premises. The goal
is *semantic compression*, not
whole-program proof of arbitrary
Clojure. A condition belongs to the
assured surface when it changes a
claimed correctness decision, including
actor/target authorization,
capability, protected disclosure,
operation eligibility, lifecycle,
assignment/ownership, authoritative
outcome, protected data movement
or a claimed finality/non-regression
invariant. Incidental styling,
logging, collection formatting and
other implementation branching do
not automatically become business
rules merely because they use =if=.

For every *inspected* correctness-
relevant policy dependency, the
report identifies one of:

- a named admitted rule/judgment with
  original source and evidence;
- an imported checked semantic
  guarantee and its scoped premises;
- a runtime-enforced fact with
  actual trusted observation/effect
  boundary and instance scope;
- a registered opaque implementation
  contract with exact callable,
  dependencies and accepted profile;
- an unresolved detected semantic-
  policy escape that cannot yet
  support the requested strong
  correspondence claim.

The constructor derives coverage
from declared source, and checking
of generated/closed bounded code
may establish a complete policy
footprint for that *specified*
subset. An actual registered
execution may reveal an undeclared
guard, special case or writer.
When observable behavior influences
a claimed protected decision but
is not accounted for by admitted
rules or an explicit, allowed
opaque contract, the result is a
detected semantic-policy escape.
The report identifies its actual
inspection/observation boundary,
affected proposition, source and
needed repair. Moving that
dependency into a named model
rule, deriving it from an admitted
interface, binding it to a
qualified runtime condition or
explicitly accepting an opaque
implementation premise are
distinct choices with different
assurance strengths.

Ordinary arbitrary Clojure code
can contain closures, macros,
dynamic Vars, reflection,
third-party code and side effects
that a bounded analyzer cannot
enumerate. Silence cannot
establish the *absence* of
hidden policy within those
uninspected bodies. “Zero
detected escapes” must say
exactly which code, paths,
source versions and effects
were inspected; uninspected
code is =unknown=, not an
empty rule catalog, a negative
test result or a complete
host writer inventory. An
opaque contract can be
legitimate under a selected
conditional profile, but
naming it does not prove its
realization implements the
promised domain predicate.

The policy audit must not
maintain a competing source
of business rules. It derives
available views from the
admitted source/operation
and trusted inspection
reports, and clearly
marks where they fail
to expose the original
model's private implementation
or unknown physical entrypoints.
Discovering a potentially
relevant condition in host
code is an explicit review
question; the generic
framework must not silently
declare it part of the
product's intended policy.

*** Coverage metrics, review authority and promotion

The assurance report includes
scope, source/canonical subject,
dependency closure, relevant
evidence class, accepted open
premises, actual inspected
implementation subset, uninspected
bodies, known policy escapes,
first unresolved obligations,
required independent product
gates, deployment applicability
and any review findings. Each
required claim can carry multiple
evidence facts and applicability
conditions. The numerical counts
of checked, finite-checked,
runtime-enforced, trusted and
untested propositions are not
necessarily disjoint. No single
“percent of correctness proved”
can be obtained by adding them.

For example, a policy view might
show 173 registered rule identities,
80 kernel-checked propositions,
61 exact-finite-checked
propositions, 18 runtime-enforced
premises and 14 trusted/opaque
premises—numbers that may
overlap, are illustrative,
and make no claim about every
rule in the actual codebase.
It could simultaneously
report four uninspected
opaque model bodies, zero
*detected* escapes in a
closed checked subset and
three unresolved advisory
policy findings. This is
neither “zero unknown
business decisions” nor
a measured 100% proof
coverage. The report
always names the actual
subjects and provenance
behind its figures.

An advisory reviewer, including
an LLM or static heuristic,
can use this compressed
semantic view to flag
suspicious asymmetry,
contradictory rationale,
permissive guards, missing
actor constraints,
unreachable real capabilities
or untested privacy cases.
The reviewer must not
issue certificates, change
current state, discharge
assumptions, suppress valid
implementation paths or
rewrite model policy.
Application owners decide
whether a finding is a
requirement and authorize
the semantic source change;
a checked constructor
then derives and validates
the revised obligations.
This is a one-way
authority distinction:
suspicion -> policy
decision -> explicit
source contract ->
mechanical checking.

Product acceptance is
separate from semantic
admission. In HumanHelp,
the real Claim/Unclaim/
Reassign controls, valid
actor and target,
selective creator-local
Create, remote advisory
and one authorized
Refresh, real two-client
browser outcomes and
the separately selected
response interaction
have independent
positive gates in
Part VIII. A checker
that excludes every
admitted action can be
safe under a narrow
abstract model yet
cannot satisfy these
product requirements.
The root report must
show the actual status
of each gate; a missing
response-flow decision
is not solved by
renaming Claim.

*** Positive and adversarial acceptance cases

For the finished framework,
profile, diagnostics and
review behavior must be
tested at the actual
specified boundary with
paired valid and invalid
cases:

- a valid typed original
  Claim source and chosen
  conditional profile
  passes its mandatory
  checks while preserving
  open provider premises;
  a missing mandatory
  invariant/operation
  case cannot be relabelled
  trusted to pass;
- a real requested strict
  induction with all
  original invariants/
  operations passes;
  removing one while
  supplying a valid
  smaller proof DAG fails
  at source-derived
  coverage;
- a valid source uses a
  separately qualified
  opaque implementation;
  an unregistered
  implementation or
  silent “assume true”
  downgrade fails;
- exact source/checker/
  profile/assembly scope
  permits applicable
  cached proofs; changed
  kernel policy, host
  asset or live callable
  invalidates the
  affected claim;
- valid =RequestStatus=
  equality passes; enum
  numeric ordering and
  wrong nominal endpoint
  fail with correct
  typed source location;
- a well-typed
  =adult? (> age 18)=
  remains accepted absent
  an explicit policy
  contrary obligation;
  the equivalent
  =email < 18=
  expression fails
  source typing;
- a real well-typed
  rule that violates
  an independently
  declared invariant
  yields a model-bounded
  counterexample, not
  arbitrary business
  intent correction;
- a helper-browser
  branch with justified
  authorized knowledge
  passes while the same
  rule without private
  observation/provenance
  fails use-site
  admissibility;
- an old Claim certificate
  depending on schema S17
  fails composition under
  incompatible S18
  while independent
  unaffected claims
  can remain reusable;
- a truly checked
  source provides
  reproducible
  first-blocker
  diagnostics,
  whereas copied
  results, changed
  map iteration
  and spoofed statuses
  cannot alter
  admission or
  diagnostic
  attribution;
- a closed generated
  guard with complete
  declared read
  footprint may
  establish scoped
  zero unresolved
  detected escapes;
  an opaque arbitrary
  model cannot claim
  the absence of
  undiscovered
  policy decisions;
- a detected
  authoritative
  exception branch
  is either source-
  linked or
  reported as an
  escape; simply
  registering a
  whole body as
  trusted does
  not prove its
  inner policy;
- genuine
  authorized
  review promotion
  changes the
  source contract
  and triggers
  checking; an
  LLM comment
  alone cannot
  issue proof
  or rewrite
  application
  policy;
- a real
  eligible Claim/
  Reassign and
  scoped remote
  Refresh remain
  usable under
  selected
  product gates;
  rejecting every
  operation or
  silently adopting
  remote content
  cannot qualify;
- resource exhaustion,
  provider/host
  unavailability
  and unsupported
  proof tools yield
  typed incomplete/
  unsupported status
  instead of success
  or a fabricated
  counterexample.

The case oracle records
input provenance,
the required claim,
expected result,
first enforcing
source/checker/
assembly/physical
boundary and
independent valid
control before test.
The test corpus in
09.15 owns mutation
injection and coverage
execution. It must
distinguish an
actual physical
observation from
a merely source-
level report; no
copyable diagnostic
can become a runtime
authorization receipt.

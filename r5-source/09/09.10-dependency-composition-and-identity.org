** Dependency composition and semantic identity
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-certificate-composition
:END:

Compositional assurance is a judgment about a *consumer's required
propositions* and the actual certified interfaces and source dependencies
that justify them. An upstream artifact is not acceptable merely because
it has the expected kind, friendly name, cached object identity, valid
fingerprint, or an unrelated successful proof. The default composition
rule is exact checked semantic identity; controlled evolution requires
an independently checked, direction-sensitive compatibility argument.

Composition must preserve the distinction between an abstract semantic
contract, evidence that the contract is satisfied, the actual production
implementation, the installed application assembly, and the particular
runtime authority or resource instance. It must neither demand complete
rechecking of unaffected abstract contracts nor reuse an old physical
correspondence claim after the implementation changes.

*** Actual exported commitments and required closure

A certified semantic interface exposes only the claims downstream
consumers are permitted to rely upon: normalized subject and type
commitments, operations, outcome and branch contracts, authority/
knowledge requirements, checked guarantees, scoped open premises
and any declared relevant implementation correspondence. Private
implementation fields, diagnostic source spans and unrelated guarantees
do not silently enter the downstream proof contract.

For each requested downstream claim the constructor derives the
*required dependency manifest* from its original normalized source,
operation/Choreo declarations, checked judgment rules and selected
assurance profile. A graph supplied by an elaborator is candidate
evidence, not the authority that defines its own required edges.
Composition checks both:

- *Structural validity:* every node/reference has a recognized checked
  category and exact identity; roots and edge kinds match; required
  proof-justification edges are acyclic, reachable and well formed.
- *Required source closure:* every needed upstream proposition, producer,
  type/branch condition, premise, resource policy and operation case
  occurs in the admitted source-derived manifest, including the edges
  the elaborator or cache did not volunteer.

A graph can pass the first and fail the second. For conditional
induction the required closure includes the original operation catalog,
shared initialization, every declared invariant, every operation,
every base and preservation case, and their explicit open premises.
Removing the most difficult =Request/Unclaim= case while leaving a
self-consistent DAG is a failure of source-derived coverage, not a
successful proof of the smaller application. A checked proof about a
*deliberately smaller declared catalog* is legitimate only when
it names that reduced subject honestly.

This completeness is relative to the checked source universe. A
declared application catalog does not by itself prove every real
route, model writer, startup hook, dynamically selected handler,
background worker or installed browser source was discovered.
Part VII's assembly closure and the qualified physical writer/
host evidence in Parts IV, VI and X must supply that independent
coverage when a stronger installed-product claim depends on it.
Opaque Clojure bodies contribute declared effect/dependency contracts
and explicit conformance premises where mechanical discovery stops;
those premises are not automatically discharged by a valid graph.

*** Distinct edge kinds and legal cycles

The assurance structure includes dependency edges that say different
things: =requires-guarantee=, =uses-premise=, =justifies=,
=implements=, =realizes=, =installed-with= and observation/correlation
links. The selected *proof-justification projection* must be a
well-founded DAG with correct premise/claim dependencies. These
other edges cannot be silently counted as proof justifications.

Recursive rules, transition systems, subscription loops, retries
and self-sustaining Choreo protocols can be well formed while their
runtime graph contains cycles. An induction, fixed-point or
coinduction step may justify a recursive semantic construction only
under its admitted inference rule and premises. Two certificates
whose only grounds are each other's promised conclusions do not
become valid through ordinary graph cycle detection or by calling
the cycle a runtime loop.

At a branch join, a dependency on a role-local fact is valid only
with the exact knowledge and provenance required on all reachable
incoming paths, or through an explicitly checked conditional/
disjunctive qualification. A communicated fact, old snapshot,
opaque policy assertion and current authoritative observation are
not interchangeable merely because they share a field name.
Part III's distributed semantics and Part IX's 09.08 judgment
remain controlling at this use site.

*** Six identities answering different questions

| Identity | Subject and allowable reuse |
|----------+-----------------------------|
| Semantic interface identity | The normalized exported subject contract under named schema, nominal-type, interpretation and canonical-encoding semantics. |
| Claim identity | One exact proposition with subject, model/scope, parameters, context and its conditional-premise meaning. |
| Evidence/check identity | The actual derivation or checker result, proof rules, trusted checker version, profile, dependency closure and remaining premises. |
| Implementation binding | The specific callable, closure, generated executor, compiler/product, provider adapter or model writer whose conformance is claimed. |
| Application/installation identity | One coherent source-to-route-to-asset-to-host configuration, with its selected assembly/deployment generation. |
| Runtime instance identity | A particular command, role execution, actor/session, transport attempt, observation, physical target, lease or resource generation. |

These identifiers do not collapse into one digest. Two artifacts can
export the same abstract semantic interface and have different proof
evidence. An unchanged interface is not proof that a new implementation
conforms. The same installed application can serve different command
instances with different current authorization. A later state snapshot
is not the same observation or historical incorporation event as an
earlier command result.

Canonical identity is exact only relative to a versioned
normalization/encoding contract. Unordered maps/sets may normalize to
one term, while ordered vectors, optional versus missing values,
nominal identifiers, branch alternatives, closed versus extensible
variants, and integer semantics preserve their declared distinctions.
Where a normalization is claimed meaning-preserving, its own
source-to-term argument is checked or retained as a named premise.
Textual/diagnostic metadata is separate unless the proposition
explicitly refers to source location or provenance.

A digest is evidence of term identity under its encoding and
collision-resistance assumptions, not evidence of typing, truth,
implementation fidelity or currentness. When a claim requires
collision-free equality rather than assumed hash uniqueness,
admission compares canonical terms or uses an independently
qualified equality mechanism. Forged copied identity fields never
create an issuer-issued handle.

Implementation binding requires more than a source filename or
Var symbol. Captured closure state, late binding/dynamic Var
resolution, transitive library versions, build configuration,
provider options, generated bytes and actual selected handler can
change behavior without changing the public name. Strong
correspondence must bind the relevant realized dependency
footprint or retain an explicit, testable stability/conformance
premise where the host cannot prove that binding.

*** Dependency failure and transitive invalidation

Composition is consumer-relative. If a required upstream guarantee
is absent, or its semantic identity, premise scope, nominal type,
model observation, operation variant or trust domain differs, the
consumer's claim cannot be accepted through the old edge. The
failure propagates along *dependent* edges that rely upon the
changed commitment; independently established unrelated claims
need not be invalidated merely because they share a namespace.

A valid checker result with missing required source nodes is
insufficient. A complete graph with a changed producer body can
still support an abstract interface claim while losing production
realization evidence. A copied original certificate cannot keep
its old installation or resource rights alive after an
incompatible reload. Cache presence, prior successful tests or
an unchanged digest of a downstream file do not establish
current source closure.

For a concrete HumanHelp evolution, let Organization domain
O14 export =organization.claiming-enabled?= as a required
Boolean and let Claim rule C21 depend on it. Suppose O15
instead exports a closed =organization.claim-policy= enum:

#+begin_example
O14: claiming-enabled? : Boolean
  -> C21: Request Claim rule
  -> O31: Claim operation
  -> H12: Claim choreography
  -> P7: helper projection
  -> E5: browser executable plan

O15: claim-policy : {open, helpers-only, closed}
#+end_example

O15 may be a valid new product interface. It is not a valid
replacement for C21's *old required Boolean field*. The
composition of cached C21 against O15 must reject with the
missing or incompatible source commitment and a dependency path,
regardless of whether cached O31/H12/P7/E5 still have their
original bytes. On re-elaboration the old Claim rule must fail
its unresolved field access, rather than accidentally interpreting
=helpers-only= as unrestricted acceptance.

A repair changes the rule and issues a new semantic identity.
The repaired rule explicitly checks current Request status,
Membership and Organization association, trusted authenticated
principal/role evidence, and the enum branch: =open= permits
the policy conjunct, =helpers-only= requires the admitted
helper-role predicate, and =closed= rejects. The enum example
describes one policy conjunct; it is not a complete production
Claim authorization proof. Production writer and current-model
conformance remain distinct premises and actual runtime checks.

This illustrates that *coherent upstream evolution* and *stale
downstream composition* can coexist. The diagnostic should not
mislabel O15 as malformed or say that a changed policy field
necessarily makes every independent application artifact invalid.

*** Checked compatibility instead of optimistic substitution

Exact identity is the default. A proposed compatibility bridge
is a checked proposition:

#+begin_example
old dependent D requires interface Iold under premises Pold
new upstream U exports interface Inew under premises Pnew

bridge establishes, for D's actual use and scope:
  accepted guarantees(Inew, Pnew) entail required guarantees(Iold, Pold)
  without weakening required authority, knowledge, closed-world
  constraints, lifetime or physical implementation bindings
#+end_example

Compatibility is *directional* and *consumer-specific*, not an
equivalence relation inferred from field counts or a label like
“backward compatible.” A compatible upstream for one consumer
may be incompatible for another; transitive reuse requires a
checked composition of bridges, not an unchecked assumption
that each pair looked plausible.

The bridge must account for actual variance of typed inputs/
outputs, optional/null presence, field removal/renaming,
closed-enum exhaustiveness, newly possible errors/outcomes,
previously assumed impossibility, and temporal/authority
scope. Adding an enum variant can invalidate exhaustive
matching. Extending an operation from =:committed= and
=:rejected= to =:already-incorporated= and =:commit-unknown=
cannot preserve an old total-settlement Choreo that has no
unknown/recovery branch. Adding a field cannot repair an
earlier reliance on its absence or a confidentiality promise
that the new field makes observable.

The bridge must preserve the relied-on *premises*, including
issuer, principal/tenant, input scope and provider qualification,
or re-establish them through a separate valid premise-discharge
judgment (09.11). It may not silently upgrade =:asserted= to
=:derived=, an abstract model to a physical observation, a
past authenticated command result to current-state authority,
or a checked contract to its unverified new implementation.
Incompatibility is a typed rejection naming the first missing
judgment. Undecidable opaque equivalence is a separately
reported premise/unsupported-strong-profile outcome, not a
silent success.

*** Cache keys, impact analysis and safe reuse

The reuse key is claim-specific: normalized subject, exact
required interface/dependency identities, proof/checker rules,
profile, proposition and premise bindings, and any relevant
implementation, deployment or runtime applicability envelope.
An implementation may maintain separate abstract-contract,
proof-evidence, compiled-plan and installed-realization caches.
One cache's successful hit never promotes an entry into a
different assurance category.

A change propagates through the typed reverse-dependency
closure used by affected claims. Precise impact analysis can
allow an unaffected abstract rule theorem to survive a renderer
CSS edit while requiring requalification of the browser
artifact and physical installation claim affected by that
edit. Conversely, a changed closed enum or a strengthened
guard can force rechecking every consumer that relies on its
former meaning, even when the dependent source bytes are
unchanged. Source spans/diagnostics may need refresh even
when semantic proof remains reusable.

A cache miss may reconstruct and recertify; this is not a
failure of soundness. An old handle or successful cached
report is not authoritative for an altered source, changed
dependency graph, selected profile or installed executable.
Tests must exercise both direct composition and the *real
consumer's cache path*: a unit function that rejects stale
EDN is insufficient if production loading bypasses it.

| Change | Scope of required response |
|--------+----------------------------|
| Formatting/source locations only | Retain eligible semantic claims if canonical meaning is identical; refresh diagnostics/source provenance when relevant. |
| New irrelevant implementation detail | Abstract proofs may survive; dependent implementation/physical evidence requires its own affected-footprint determination. |
| Nominal type, required field, outcome, guard or assumption changed | Recheck every relying proposition and dependent judgment, or verify an explicit compatibility bridge. |
| Checker, kernel, normalization or policy version changed | Revalidate affected evidence under current admitted rules; old success alone is insufficient. |
| Changed opaque callable, provider/configuration or generated program | Re-establish applicable binding and correspondence or preserve an explicit bounded trusted premise. |
| Route/asset/host assembly generation replaced | Admit one coherent installation closure; reject mixed old/new certificates and plans. |
| Individual runtime resource or command instance retires | Remove that instance's effects/grants; static interface identity does not renew it. |

*** Fresh source, coherent deployment and temporal claims

A dependency graph can be internally consistent while entirely
obsolete. The consumer or responsible assembler must acquire its
*current* source, dependency generation, policy and selected
realizations from the trusted application/installation authority,
not from a caller's mutually matching old source plus old graph.
A source-issued handle proves only its named checked boundary
until this currentness and any required installed correspondence
are separately established.

For reload, construct and check a replacement closure before
atomic publication of the new admitted assembly identity.
New routes/handlers, browser plans/assets, model operation
contracts and authorization policy cannot be mixed with old
evidence merely because each component passed some check
at a different time. A failed candidate leaves the previous
compatible generation under its stated policy or prevents new
admission; it never creates partially certified service.
Per-invocation and per-effect authority remain current
physical/runtime obligations, not consequences of this
static source composition check.

Runtime continuity and durable command recovery are separate
from static graph reuse. A known Claim command C42 may retain
its historical durable outcome binding across a compatible
reconnect, but neither that command ID nor a reissued plan
stamp authorizes a new attempt, current readback or retired
browser lease. Under Parts IV–VI, the applicable historical
outcome, actor, session and current source/provider/plan
binding must be checked at the actual trusted boundary.

*** Positive and adversarial acceptance

The final system needs both genuine supported progress and
targeted rejection. At minimum, the test/report corpus covers:

- a complete original Claim source/induction DAG and successful
  consumer admission, versus a structurally valid pruned DAG;
- same-name new Organization policy O15 correctly rejecting stale
  C21 and all dependent old uses, followed by successful checked
  C22/new-operation/new-Choreo rebuilding;
- actual semantic equivalence after formatting or map-order
  changes being reusable, while an ordered-vector or nominal-
  type change is not silently equated;
- an irrelevant renderer style edit preserving rule-level proof
  reuse but requiring affected browser-asset correspondence;
- a closed-enum variant addition, missing-branch default,
  optional-field weakening, or altered operation outcome
  rejected before total-settlement/knowledge claims;
- a correctly scoped, checked asymmetric compatibility bridge
  accepted for one consumer, versus unjustified bridge reuse
  for another consumer, premise or implementation;
- injected extra proof node, omitted required edge, forged
  fingerprint and cycle of mutual proof justification rejected
  at their actual structural or source-derived boundary;
- copied proof report/handle and old source+matching old
  dependency graph rejected as current installed-app evidence;
- changed callable, provider, route or served asset detected
  for the corresponding implementation/installation claim;
- unrelated source change preserving reusable unaffected
  claims, while transitive changed dependencies are detected
  through the production cache and assembly path;
- positive eligible Claim and selective Create/Refresh
  continuing to satisfy independent product gates, with
  incorrect tenant/role or second unauthorised Refresh
  rejected by the real enforcement boundary.

The expected disposition and first responsible checker,
source-coverage, composition, construction, installation
or physical gate are recorded before exercising each
positive/adversarial case. Formal or structural checks are
not credited with observing actual browser/XTDB behavior
unless the required authenticated physical evidence exists.

** Semantic model, proof obligations, and conformance evidence

HumanHelp should make its useful semantic rules concrete enough to execute and
check. The production definition owns operation meaning, dependency declarations,
transition constructors, outcome interpretation, and explicit opaque premises.
The application composes that source into the supported relational construction
and executable semantic core; tests do not independently retype a convenient
model and then compare it with itself.

The independent product contract still supplies expected capabilities and
outcomes. Deriving model and executor from one source reduces drift, but cannot
prove that the source expresses the desired HumanHelp policy. Both source
consistency and policy adequacy need evidence.

*** Useful invariant and obligation families

| Family | Required meaning and important boundary |
|--------+-----------------------------------------|
| Entity and association validity | Requests, assignments, Organization/Location relationships, and referenced public identities obey the production model's declared schema and relationship rules. |
| Primary assignment uniqueness | There is at most one active primary assignment for a Request where that relation is defined; collaborator assignments are not falsely counted as additional primary owners. |
| Lifecycle/assignment correspondence | Production state and assignment history satisfy the selected lifecycle rules, including required closure on completion/cancellation. |
| Commit-time eligibility | A new command's actual protected prestate satisfies the actor, target-helper, Membership, Organization, and Request conditions used by its rule. |
| Command exclusion and replay | Compatible repeats do not reincorporate; incompatible binding reuse cannot obtain the old result or mutate under the old identity. |
| Observation and disclosure | Read evidence has its actual scope/basis and is disclosed only under the declared access policy. |
| Selective adoption | A local result or presentation operation cannot import unrelated remote membership or discharge unrelated adoption debt. |
| Resource and installation ownership | Retired executions, requests, targets, subscriptions, and continuity slots cannot perform protected successor effects. |

Commit-time eligibility is not automatically a permanent state invariant. If a
helper loses Membership after legitimately claiming a Request, the model must
state whether existing assignments remain, become unavailable, or require a
separate revocation transition. It is incorrect to prove “all current assignments
always have currently eligible helpers” while omitting Membership revocation from
the operation catalog or silently assuming it cannot occur.

The same distinction applies to Organization policy changes, user removal,
Location moves, and administration. They either preserve the invariant, perform
a valid repair transition, or are explicit external premises of a narrower claim.
A Live invalidation does not repair the authoritative invariant by itself.

*** Finite exploration and non-vacuity

Finite families include at least the following shapes, with the exact domains
and bounds recorded in the report:

- two eligible helpers competing for one Request;
- a requestor, an authorized manager, and a distinct replacement helper;
- multiple Organizations/Locations to exercise cross-scope rejection;
- active and inactive Membership and relevant policy alternatives;
- open, assigned, on-the-way, and terminal Request states;
- distinct commands, compatible repeats, and incompatible binding reuse;
- known commit, authoritative rejection, and participant uncertainty branches;
- one board with an unrelated remote change and a selective local result.

Not every shape must be one enormous Cartesian model. Separate bounded families
can discharge named obligations, provided their scopes and omissions remain
visible and their composition is justified where a stronger claim is made.

Admission checks declared rule exercise and required operation attempts/outcomes
rather than accepting a green invariant that no enabled operation ever challenged.
A rule that never matches a source state or an operation that is always disabled
is reported accordingly. Deleting Reassign from both source and model must still
fail the independent capability requirement.

Finite exploration establishes only the explored claim. Conditional induction
requires the stated initialization and transition-preservation argument. Abstract
successors must be bound to the issued implementation and actual effect gateway
before they support a physical correspondence claim.

*** Host functions and physical evidence

An opaque guard, query, constructor, or serializer can participate through its
explicit contract. Passing sample tests does not prove every possible host-language
execution satisfies that contract. The proof result retains the assumption and
the application policy identifies why it is accepted.

Runtime validation can establish concrete instance facts such as an output schema,
command binding, actual provider receipt, or required guard coverage when the
boundary exposes them. It cannot infer the behavior of an arbitrary callback from
its name or metadata. A supplied observation of physical execution also retains
the trust and coverage limits of the mechanism that captured it.

Comparison traces must bind operation, inputs, source/executor identity, admitted
predecessor state, actual submission/outcome evidence, and relevant successor
observation. A caller-supplied sequence is not automatically observed physical
chronology. A list of individually compatible transitions is not automatically
one adjacent execution history.

The application records which writer inventory was actually inspected, including
non-route ingress. “No unclassified entries in this supplied inventory” is useful
but weaker than “all physical writers are covered”. The evidence report must
retain that difference, including unknown inventory or unverified host adoption.

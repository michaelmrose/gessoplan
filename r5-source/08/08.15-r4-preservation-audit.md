## R4 preservation and improvements

1. **Preserve all eleven independent R4 completion gates.** Semantic-route denial, actual runtime preflight adoption, current generated browser artifact, genuine Claim, full six-operation lifecycle, rich board, independent multi-client adoption, tests consuming the production assembly, structural malformed fixtures, real browser product acceptance, and the selected Biff/Gesso substrate remain concrete non-vacuous conditions. They are not reduced to build/report checks.
2. **Keep R5's superior fourteen-gate separation.** The updated table makes explicit the currently critical divisions: protected model truth, single source-derived application, installed host/writers, actual shipped assets, real browser Claim, independent lifecycle positives, rich board, two-client adoption, one-use Refresh, recovery, malformed enforcement, semantic evidence, qualified substrate, and independent browser acceptance.
3. **Do not define product intent from the catalog of currently implemented operations.** R4 rightly rejects deleting a promised user feature and then claiming completion because all remaining declarations agree. This revision retains independent eligible positives for Create, Claim, Unclaim, Mark on the way, Complete, Cancel, Reassign, Refresh, details/search/options and the selected response flow.
4. **Preserve R4's central final invariant.** One production User/Membership/Organization/Request model boundary; one HumanHelp application assembly; no anonymous lifecycle POST downgrade; current guard and RequestAssignment semantics; correlated creator result; advisory-only remote board; one-use explicit Refresh; local-only options and continuity; real browser-generated commands; authoritative readback; and structural denial remain joined.
5. **Select the now-resolved R5.1 remote-lifecycle policy.** R4 correctly left already-visible remote lifecycle adoption undecided. R5.1 `08.06` explicitly chooses advisory-only remote Claim/Unclaim/Mark/Complete/Cancel/Reassign until separate Refresh. This is normative for HumanHelp and is not to be reopened as an unresolved product choice during final gate review. Local-result/affected-Request recovery and trusted access revocation are distinct exceptions, not auto-adoption.
6. **Leave the separately unresolved response-flow contract honest.** `08.07` `decision-humanhelp-response-flow` says the required response experience has not yet been independently selected. It cannot be fabricated as messaging or silently renamed Claim. Until the product chooses and tests the user-intent/output contract, rich-board/full product acceptance is blocked, even if all structural and lifecycle tests pass.
7. **Actual connected evidence is required, not ungrounded green labels.** R4's browser/XTDB and multi-client oracles remain separate from Gesso's source construction and conditional formal analysis. R5.1 now connects Claim, Create/selective adoption, Reassign and explicit Refresh with real identity, trusted outcome, physical installation and necessary disclosure/continuity evidence.
8. **Failure classification remains useful.** R4's four ownership categories—generic Gesso semantic/closure gap, HumanHelp app/assembly defect, HumanHelp model/business error, external/runtime qualification change—remain documented in 08.14; 08.15 does not reintroduce a second migration checklist or misassign every HumanHelp problem to Gesso.
9. **Recovery and liveness cannot be inferred from safety.** R4's disconnected/reconnect, stale callback and late response cases; R5 durable command/publication and explicit one-use Refresh; and R5.1 bounded advisory/resource debt have separate positive/fault obligations. A participant intentionally not refreshing may correctly retain an old board under the selected product semantics.
10. **A qualified weaker profile is not a silent downgrade.** R5.1 08.08 states a primary strong atomic publication-intent profile and explicitly allows separately chosen weaker user-Refresh/periodic-reconciliation deployments with limited guarantees. The final gates require the *selected* profile and independent product promise to agree; a weaker build cannot claim strong crash-to-publish recovery.

## Actual Gesso correspondence and limits

- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/biff.clj` requires the exact canonical application-handler module object in the Biff modules Var and a qualified module-ID start order beginning with it; it guards the installed `:biff.ring/handler` in its supported startup path. This is a meaningful host boundary but explicitly does not attest to arbitrary direct Biff startup, later handler replacement or pre-serialized HTML.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/rendered_request.clj` and `rendered_surface.clj` inspect supported canonical Hiccup HTMX/native-form requests against application/operation bindings. They cannot prove the behavior of arbitrary JavaScript, a physically different handler or content produced outside their guarded render profile.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc`, `shell.cljs`, `continuity.cljs` and optimistic modules provide bounded portable semantic effects, physical resource ownership and retirement logic; the presence of these mechanisms does not prove all real-browser/HTMX interleavings.
- `src/gesso/assurance/report.clj` describes a source-bound in-process conditional report, not a proof of original policy adequacy, a reusable execution credential, independently authenticated test results or deployed writer completeness.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_command_outcome_bridge.clj` links one process-local submit/readback and reported outcome under specific premises, explicitly without proving universal transaction causality, complete authentication, all writers, retry authority or browser/Live settlement.
- Gesso has finite and inductive formal checking and backend-conformance precursors, but the required full HumanHelp product and physical refinement/certification remains a separate join to satisfy.

## Actual HumanHelp correspondence and gaps

- `src/net/humanhelp/example/application_preflight.clj` is a production-derived Request Choreo → browser → route → server → publication → Live/acquisition construction. Its own documentation distinguishes this structural seam from actual dynamic rendered responses and native Biff hosting.
- `test/net/humanhelp/example/application_preflight_test.clj` checks the true production constructor, including controlled deleted operation/route/fragment cases. These do not by themselves prove installed host/asset correctness or independent full product intent.
- `test/net/humanhelp/example/chromium_acceptance.clj` contains a real Chromium acceptance scenario joining actual browser-generated lifecycle commands to Request model truth and reload for Claim/Unclaim/On-the-way/Complete/Cancel/Reassign. It is a substantial positive test definition, not evidence of pass status in this editorial revision or a complete two-client remote advisory and durable command/publication recovery suite.
- `test/net/humanhelp/site/model/request_claim_integration_test.clj` checks real Request/RequestAssignment XTDB Claim atomicity with public dependency seams; it does not establish all actual Membership/Organization backend interleavings or every external writer's exclusion.
- `src/net/humanhelp/example/live.clj` and `board.clj` use production Request source and XTDB Live progression. The current Live routing can automatically refresh some remote lifecycle updates; it does not yet establish 08.06's selected advisory-only policy for already-visible Requests.
- `src/net/humanhelp.clj` currently supplies a Biff module Var and start-component sequence that do not visibly meet the exact Gesso v1622 canonical application-handler module and qualified-ID ordering rules. This is a source-level integration mismatch, not a recorded result of running the actual server.
- The inspected source does not establish the selected durable transaction-coupled publication worker, fully qualified historical command recovery, or complete two-user Refresh/advisory evidence. These are open implementation obligations rather than reasons to weaken the timeless target.
- The response-flow *product decision* is also open, independently of the implementation: a full HumanHelp completion claim must not be reported as satisfied even if Gesso's underlying mechanism passes.

## Acceptance challenge cases retained

1. Real Claim generated from rendered control commits primary assignment and reloads to correct production state.
2. Each of six lifecycle operations has an eligible independent browser positive and meaningful negative; manager Reassign includes distinct actor/target.
3. Creating S locally does not admit unrelated remote R or clear its advisory debt; remote R becomes visible only after granted Refresh.
4. Remote lifecycle update to an already-adopted R remains advisory-only until permitted explicit Refresh/local affected-Request recovery.
5. A second concurrent Claim or policy-changing Reassign cannot produce two live primary assignments; actual XTDB guards are challenged.
6. An unknown C42 after lost delivery is not treated as rejected, and permitted exact replay does not execute another Claim.
7. A valid commit with later failed response/Live publish is not undone; chosen durable or explicitly weaker publication recovery is exercised.
8. One-use Refresh cannot grow into unbounded automatic collection queries or clear uncovered/future obligations after partial swap.
9. Stale E1/T1 and old A page cannot overwrite newer T2 or incompatible B generation; command recovery is independently authorized.
10. Removing Reassign from source and generated test subject fails the independent capability oracle even when finite/structural checks pass.
11. Wrong or missing Biff canonical handler, stale served artifact, anonymous semantic POST and forged principal fail at their distinct enforcement boundaries with valid paired controls.
12. Hidden raw XTDB writer or unqualified changed dependency leaves a physical-writer/refinement claim open rather than certified.
13. A product-selected response-flow journey completes real authorized input/output and negative/recovery tests; unselected response flow blocks full rich-board acceptance.
14. Actual installed two-client Chromium suite reports unexpected failures instead of suppressing console/HTTP errors and verifies both model and DOM settlement.
15. Conditional formal proof, source checks, tested runtime facts and physically connected evidence are each reported at their own scope rather than collapsed into a "verified application" label.

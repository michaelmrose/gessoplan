** Connected evidence and operational limits

At least the Claim, Create/selective-adoption, Reassign, and explicit-Refresh
journeys retain connected evidence of their actual boundaries. A report links:

- canonical application/source and assembly/deployment identities;
- prepared operation and rendered control, browser plan and actual asset identity;
- registered execution/resource instances and physical transport correlation;
- installed route and issued server realization;
- trusted actor/context and relevant provider contracts, with protected data redacted;
- authoritative command outcome, publication/recovery intent where applicable;
- scoped acquired observation, installation permission, physical completion,
  adopted evidence, and remaining obligations;
- claim classification, test/model scope, and unresolved or accepted premises.

The references come from the canonical construction and observed execution.
A second hand-maintained table of operation IDs is not an evidence chain. Nor
is a list of success-shaped maps without a trustworthy relationship to the
actual callables, transactions, and physical effects.

Absence of an unexpected error is useful but insufficient. The report also
establishes the expected command, authoritative transition, or visible adoption.
For a rejection case it establishes the absence of the prohibited effect at the
relevant boundary; it must not claim that a later output guard undid an earlier
valid commit.

Safety and progress are reported separately. Correct rejection of an old response
is a safety result. Eventual convergence requires the stated availability,
qualification, scheduling, retention, and user-action premises. Under explicit
Refresh policy, a user who never refreshes is allowed to retain an older board.
The application must still make the pending condition and recovery action clear.

*** Resource limits and cleanup

The profile bounds active acquisitions/installations, coalesced successor intent,
physical remnants after failed abort, pending effect resources, retained command
recovery references, local drafts, and projection size. It defines compaction or
backpressure for advisory debt without claiming that one scheduling slot bounds
the information in all unresolved obligations.

Cleanup revokes semantic rights before disposing physical resources. Timer,
subscription, and request cleanup is idempotent under duplicate completion and
partial failure. Removing a component cannot erase the only owner of an unresolved
command or publication intent.

The final application has no parallel demo store, alternate revision counter used
as authoritative progression, duplicate operation registry, anonymous lifecycle
fallback, or obsolete compatibility path that contradicts the admitted contract.
Source changes and removals remain traceable through repository history and the
relevant replacement rationale. This is a design requirement for a coherent
application, not a migration checklist embedded in the product specification.

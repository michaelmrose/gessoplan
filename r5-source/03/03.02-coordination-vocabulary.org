*** A concrete coordination vocabulary

The canonical global representation is a versioned graph of typed semantic
states. States have stable source identities for explanation, successors, and
the contracts their transitions use. Semantic keys and options are closed for
the selected representation version: an unknown opcode, misspelled required
field, unsupported option, or unresolved contract reference produces a specific
construction failure. An explicit nonsemantic metadata/extension area may be
retained, but its contents cannot affect execution or strengthen a claim without
a declared interpretation. A supported baseline has six node kinds:

| Global node | Meaning | Required contract |
|-------------+---------+-------------------|
| =:local= | Work at one role | Named implementation, placement, inputs, outputs, effects, failure and completion behavior |
| =:authoritative= | Result of a public authoritative operation at its trusted owner | Operation interface, caller/authority binding, result alternatives, and realization premises |
| =:branch= | Selection using an already established local value | Owning role, typed selector, exhaustive supported alternatives or an explicit unmatched-value failure |
| =:communicate= | One semantic transfer between roles | Sender, receiver, payload, correlation, disclosure, and channel contract |
| =:await= | A role admits an event from its environment | Registered source, event alternatives, owner, payload, and provenance obligations |
| =:return= | Declared global terminal disposition | Meaning of completion, remaining obligations, and any permitted handoff |

The corresponding role-local vocabulary includes =:send= and =:receive= for
the two sides of communication, alongside local work, authoritative work,
branching, environment waits, and local return. A participant message cannot
satisfy an environment wait merely because its event keyword matches. An
environment event likewise cannot impersonate a participant receive.

Selectors distinguish missing bindings, present nil, and false according to the
declared type. If nil is a supported member of that selector type, it needs an
explicit alternative; it must not be conflated with an absent value. A target
that cannot represent such a selector rejects the unsupported contract during
construction rather than interpreting the value through generic truthiness.

A local node is not permission to conceal a database write, outbound message,
protected disclosure, or required resource transition. Its interface exposes
effects that matter to the claimed behavior. A computation may be hidden in a
particular distributed trace only when the trace abstraction permits it; it
remains subject to local safety, resource, and implementation obligations.

An authoritative result is not synonymous with a committed write. It may report
rejection, a disclosed recorded outcome, or uncertainty under the operation's
contract. The result's category and payload determine what the next state may
conclude. Merely naming the state =:authoritative= establishes no runtime trust.

An acquisition can be realized as a declared effect followed by an environment
completion carrying an admitted authoritative observation. It need not be a new
portable observation opcode, and it must not be mistaken for a new model
mutation. The observation contract binds the authority, subject, query,
projection, principal context, basis, and temporal meaning required by Part II.

Higher-level constructs such as an operation invocation, authoritative
acquisition, retry, resource scope, or a source-language conditional elaborate
to these transitions or to an explicitly versioned extension with defined
semantics. An invocation at a browser role does not relocate the authoritative
operation into the browser: elaboration includes the declared request,
trusted-side operation, and outcome-delivery boundary. A macro or helper must
expose this expansion for inspection and checking.

For example, this small graph classifies an already acquired review input. It
does not itself perform an approval:

#+begin_src clojure
(def classify-review-graph
  {:name :expense/classify-review
   :initial :classify
   :states
   {:classify
    {:op :local
     :role :manager
     :action :expense/classify-route
     :requires #{:review/inputs}
     :outputs #{:review/path}
     :next :choose}
    :choose
    {:op :branch
     :role :manager
     :on :review/path
     :cases {:finance :finance-required
             :manager :manager-route}}
    :finance-required
    {:op :return :outcome :review/finance-required}
    :manager-route
    {:op :return :outcome :review/manager-route}}})
#+end_src

The operation entry binds =:review/inputs= to the manager's admitted observation.
The action interface promises an output of type =[:enum :finance :manager]=
under its typed policy or retained opaque contract. The branch consumes that
output; it does not compute the policy or invent the selector. If the action
can fail or yield a third alternative, its actual interface and graph must
expose that path or the admitted outer failure contract must handle it. The
constructor cannot accept a partial success-only graph as a totality proof.

The sample spells out input/output sets to show the semantic representation.
Authoring helpers derive them from inspectable expressions and operation
interfaces where possible. An opaque action's declared boundary remains an
explicit commitment. Source states such as =:classify= may become compact
runtime locators; that encoding does not change these semantics.

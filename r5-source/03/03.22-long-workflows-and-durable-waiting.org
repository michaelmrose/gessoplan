** Long logical workflows and short runtime executions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-durable-workflow-boundary
:END:

A choreography may describe a logical workflow that spans human-scale time.

That does not imply that one Aleph node should retain a suspended server-side
machine in process memory for hours or days.

For example, a business flow may conceptually involve:

An approval workflow requests approval, waits under its domain policy, and
continues when an admitted decision establishes the next condition.

A robust HumanHelp realization may instead be:

| Period | Short execution and durable connection |
|--------+----------------------------------------|
| Request submission | E1 records pending approval and settles or hands off its own command |
| Human-scale delay | Pending authority remains discoverable under the model's contract |
| Approver action | E2 observes current premises and records a permitted decision |
| Later requester action | A fresh observation supplies entry knowledge for E3 if further work is needed |

The durable wait lives in model state.

Subject to the authoritative store's declared durability and recovery
assumptions, the durable workflow fact can survive:

- web-node failure;
- deployment;
- load-balancer rerouting;
- client disconnect;
- browser suspension;
- long offline periods.

The ordinary Gesso choreography contract does not promise durable persistence
of arbitrary suspended server-local executions.

If a future application genuinely needs durable workflow-machine continuations,
that should be an explicit opt-in capability with its own persistence, ownership,
versioning, and recovery semantics.

It must not silently become a requirement of ordinary choreography.

*** Durable waiting belongs in authoritative state by default

Human-scale waiting is normally represented as durable domain state rather than
a suspended in-memory choreography execution.

This has several consequences.

First, subject to those persistence and compatibility assumptions, the durable
workflow meaning can survive:

- deployment;
- process loss;
- browser suspension;
- load-balancer rerouting;
- reconnect;
- long periods without any participant online.

Second, later actors can acquire the relevant facts by authoritative
observation rather than requiring the original process to resume.

Third, the framework avoids making one runtime's memory an accidental source of
business truth.

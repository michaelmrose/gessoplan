*** Durable model state is the normal rendezvous point

For long workflows, authoritative state provides a stable rendezvous point
between actors who were never concurrently connected.

A later actor may know what happened because authority establishes:

#+begin_example
:approval/pending
:approval/approved
:request/claimed
:request/done
#+end_example

not because it receives a packet from the actor who caused that state.

This follows [[#req-authoritative-observation][Part II's authoritative observation
model]]: observation is a first-class knowledge path.

Durable pending state is not itself a progress guarantee. The application must
provide the declared opportunity for an eligible actor to discover and act on
it, or state the environmental premises on which progress depends. Where loss
of a required participant can strand the workflow, the runtime-condition and
recovery requirements from Part I apply. Any lease, reassignment, cancellation,
or other successor policy remains model-owned.

*** Deployment compatibility remains explicit

A durable business fact may outlive the executable plan that originally
produced it.

That is normal.

What cannot be assumed is that a suspended *runtime execution* from an older
deployment remains compatible forever.

The later stale-plan/recovery design therefore treats durable model truth and
runtime-plan compatibility as separate dimensions.

A deployment may invalidate continuation of an old projection while leaving the
authoritative business state entirely valid. If a deployment also changes the
meaning or representation of persisted domain state, the model needs its own
compatible interpretation or migration. Restarting a current browser plan does
not perform that domain migration by implication.

# R5.1 Part III — 03.19 Durable Request Publication and Selective Adoption

**R4 baseline:** main Part III, the original HumanHelp Request publication
and board-advisory worked example within “Projection preserves necessary
communication but does not worship messages.”
**R5 candidate:** the independent “Durable Request publication and
selective adoption” subsection.
**Disposition:** focused design preservation review. The normative Org
source describes completed Gesso, not an implementation checkpoint.

## R4 requirements preserved

- The authoritative Request model owns durable creation and result truth.
- A remote viewer need not receive the creator's complete new Request
  through direct participant communication; scoped advisory plus qualified
  authoritative reacquisition is a legitimate alternative.
- An advisory must pass current subscription owner, registered source
  instance, provenance, valid requirement/scope and permitted transition
  before changing browser state. A matching subscription identifier alone
  cannot certify it.
- Advisories carry an authorized requirement, not arbitrary Request
  contents or visibility privileges.
- Refresh checks the latest state, principal, scope, acquisition owner,
  authoritative progression and installed target; a stale read cannot
  overwrite newer adopted state or erase concurrent advisory requirements.
- Pure state updates do not run physical effects; retriable state-update
  functions must not duplicate external work.
- Other viewers may keep old boards and offer explicit Refresh rather
  than automatically adopting every relevant new Request.

## Superior R5 and Part II/IV design retained

- R5 distinguishes accepted, rejected and unknown command outcomes, with
  stable identity, command-specific creator acquisition and recovery.
- Required publication arises at incorporation or through qualified
  recoverable handoff; committed operations remain committed if
  subsequent delivery fails.
- Typed scoped advisories preserve producer-established progression
  obligations and do not manufacture basis bounds from viewer state.
- Initial registration/reconnect and missed invalidations need a
  qualified discovery/fence/delivery contract.
- Pure browser transitions, effects, installation permissions, physical
  installation, and semantic adoption are separate when necessary.
- The X18/X19/X20 example distinguishes partial permitted adoption
  from the alternative all-current-requirements policy and retains
  newer independent obligation debt.
- An accepted partial card update does not prove complete board
  membership, sorting, filtering or privacy coverage.

## Focused improvements in 03.19

1. Preserve immediate creator presentation from an admitted correlated
   result where its content and membership scope justify it; a separate
   full-board reread is not mandated in every successful create.
2. Restate required publication as atomic intent or qualified durable
   rediscovery, rather than unprotected best-effort browser signaling.
3. Restore R4's explicit source/owner/scope/provenance admission contract
   before recording a shared advisory, including protection against
   retired subscription instances and attacker-chosen event contracts.
4. Make read/subscription registration races and tokenless advisory fences
   explicit; reconnect and missed-event repair are part of the guarantee.
5. Distinguish acquire-only debt from installation/adoption debt and
   require checked physical completion under current target ownership.
6. Retain visibility and query-scope correctness when a fully covered
   authorized board legitimately omits a hidden or filtered Request.
7. Make X19-versus-X20 partial/all-current adoption choices explicit
   and independent of asynchronous arrival order.
8. Add twelve adversarial cases spanning publication loss, admission,
   privacy, command uncertainty, ownership, adoption and duplicate effects.

## Gesso implementation correspondence (audit only)

- `gesso.live.browser.adapter` is a pure normalized transition/effects
  boundary and tracks execution/resource generations, current fragment
  requests, stale callbacks and conservative queued advisory requirements.
  It does not authenticate server principals or itself prove physical DOM
  installation or an application-specific board policy.
- `gesso.live.fragment` protects render/caching work but explicitly
  delegates subscriptions, routes, streams and broader browser lifecycle.
  Its fragment keys must account for user, scope, parameters and any
  relevant authoritative progression.
- `gesso.live.browser.preflight` statically checks a runtime assembly
  manifest, not execution of JavaScript or installed DOM effects.
- Parts II and IV already define the X18/X19/X20 and publication-debt
  semantics; this section now aligns its short worked example with them.
- These observations provide implementation lessons, not an argument
  for weakening the completed design or claiming its present certification.

# R5.1 Part III — 03.13 Shape Validation and Closed Value Boundaries

**R4 baseline:** Part III's “Shape validation is necessary but not semantic
certification” and “Closed value boundaries are the default.”
**R5.1 candidate:** source section 03.13.
**Disposition:** focused semantic preservation review; canonical Part III
describes the completed framework, while this audit records the reasons
for design choices and implementation correspondence.

## R4 normative obligations preserved

- Malli/value-shape success is not authentication, authorization, provenance,
  knowledge, global choreography correctness or a formal theorem.
- Semantic claims belong to their actual trusted construction and proof
  boundaries even if carried by ordinary host-language maps or records.
- A shape-valid `:type :verified` discriminator is not permission to
  assert a stronger category.
- Semantic payloads are closed by default for commands, participant messages,
  provisional/canonical envelopes, settlements, projections and FX effects.
- Explicitly open physical data cannot become semantic knowledge or operation
  inputs unless a corresponding semantic contract and admission exist.
- The decoder keeps semantic fields distinct from permitted transport
  metadata rather than merging unknown fields into a trusted context.

## Superior R5 treatment retained

- Schema checks are evidence about their admitted validator and custom
  predicate contracts, not universally executable mathematical proofs.
- Formal reasoning over custom predicates needs a supported semantics or
  honestly recorded assumptions, including effect, exception and portability
  behavior when applicable.
- The explanation of value shape, semantic type/refinement, knowledge,
  authority and choreography as separate judgments is retained rather
  than assuming a schema-checking library proves every layer.

## Changes in 03.13

1. Make the correctness layers independent and composable, rather than a
   mandatory runtime sequence or a false implication chain.
2. Describe trusted semantic category admission without requiring special
   physical wrappers for all values. Copying a tagged map does not confer
   issuer, subject, scope or consumer-specific rights.
3. Specify schema closedness against the effective admitted semantic
   contract, including optional, nested, variant and extension semantics.
4. Clarify that open physical envelopes can carry adapter data without
   creating role knowledge, semantic branch decisions, authority, correlation,
   or machine-history entries. Semantic promotion requires a new admitted
   interface, not opportunistic reuse of previously ignored fields.
5. Require unambiguous decoded semantic bindings: conflicting duplicate
   fields, aliases and coercions cannot silently choose authority-relevant
   values. Transport metadata may still have a separate policy.
6. Add nine negative cases for forged trust tags, copied envelopes,
   closed extras, open transport hints, undeclared extension reuse,
   decoder alias collision, attacker-selected schema, unsupported custom
   predicate proof and cross-scope cache reuse.

## Implementation lessons (not part of the timeless specification)

- `gesso.choreo.type` defines a Malli shape vocabulary and explicitly
  disclaims authentication, authorization, role knowledge and proof.
- `gesso.choreo.core` preserves optional/required/correlation fields
  and makes `:open-payload?` an explicit exception.
- `gesso.choreo.machine` enforces projected required and undeclared
  message keys, validates sender knowledge of every transmitted declared
  field, and excludes physical-only extras from semantic history.
- `gesso.choreo.knowledge/establish-communicated` establishes only
  declared communicated fields as role-local knowledge.
- These code observations are implementation context, not proof that
  all physical decoders, caches, external inputs and application routes
  already meet the complete design.

## Cross-section and cross-part obligations

- 03.12: effective schema registry identity, predicate contracts and
  actual runtime portability of validators.
- 03.14: physical boundary validation, diagnostic redaction and schema
  compatibility under deployment changes.
- Parts II, IV, VI, VII, IX and X: trust admission, authoritative
  observations, real installed decoder/route coverage, physical artifact
  correspondence and sound checker claims.
- Later Part III projection must preserve required semantic communication
  while neither discarding necessary knowledge paths nor manufacturing
  knowledge from transport metadata.

The original `req-value-schema-boundaries` anchor is unchanged; 03.13
adds no new second-level heading or competing source of semantic truth.
No R4 normative requirement in this section is intentionally removed.

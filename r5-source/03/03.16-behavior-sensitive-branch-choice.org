*** Behavior-sensitive choice is the central branch rule

A role needs evidence sufficient to select its continuation when its observable
local behavior depends on a choice. The required knowledge must exist before
the first differing behavior, not merely become available eventually.

This avoids both extremes:

- projecting too little and leaving a role unable to decide its next behavior;
- overcommunicating every global discriminator to every mentioned role.

The analysis should compare continuations semantically rather than merely by
source syntax.

If two global branches project to equivalent local behavior for role R, R need
not learn the branch solely because R appears somewhere inside both branches.

If they diverge, the projector/verifier must establish a valid knowledge path
before the divergence.


A focused expense example separates private choice from public behavior.
Suppose an accepted manager review has one of two recorded results:

| Manager result | Notifier behavior | Finance behavior in an explicitly participating short session |
|----------------+-------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Awaiting Finance | Notify that a manager review was recorded | Receive review-required, acquire current permitted facts, and offer the Finance action |
| Approved by manager | The same notification with the same declared payload | Receive not-required and terminate local participation |

In this variant the notifier's message is deliberately neutral: it says that a
manager review was recorded for this Expense and admitted review session,
not which operation or result it produced or what the Expense's current status
is. The neutral payload uses the common session identity; it does not require
the two branches to create identical command identities. Its effect and result-handling
contract are the same on both successful branches. The notifier does not need
the private threshold or the route selector. If the application changes the
notification text or destination by branch, that difference requires a justified
input, typically an explicitly communicated permitted outcome.

The two notification paths must also have compatible causal prerequisites.
Here each notice follows an authoritative result establishing a recorded review
of the same Expense under the declared message contract. Merely finding equal
payload shapes in two branches would not establish that meaning.

Finance is different when it is already a participant in the short session.
The global contract must distinguish both continuations, for example through a
declared disposition message:

#+begin_src clojure
{:message/id :expense/finance-disposition
 :from :manager
 :to :finance
 :required #{:expense/id :review/command-id :review/disposition}
 :correlation #{:expense/id :review/command-id}
 :disposition-type [:enum :review-required :not-required :review-unavailable]}
#+end_src

The projected receive can branch on an admitted =:review/disposition=. A
=:review-required= message justifies entering a current observation/action
path; it does not itself authorize Finance approval. A =:not-required= message
permits local return without claiming that Finance acted. =:review-unavailable=
closes this session's participation when the manager's command could not produce
a usable result. That last label does not assert the command never committed;
unknown command resolution remains with its declared recovery owner.

These are additional messages deliberately chosen by this session protocol.
They must not be inserted by the projector merely to rescue an otherwise
unrealizable declaration. Their delivery, timeout, and session-retirement paths
must also be defined. A role that simply waits for =:review-required= forever
cannot claim completion of both branches; absence of a message is not knowledge
that Finance was unnecessary.

The invalid alternative is to give Finance a branch on =:needs-finance?= simply
because the projector knows the manager's selector. Finance has neither that
selector nor its private policy inputs. The projector must report the missing
knowledge rather than copy either into Finance's context.

The normal durable alternative avoids this participating short session. The
manager commits =:awaiting-finance=, and a later Finance actor starts a new
execution from an authorized queue observation. Selection from that queue is
an explicit user/application input, not automatic approval of every visible
Expense. The chosen operation rechecks current eligibility at incorporation.

That alternative establishes a new workflow entry condition; it is not proof
that a particular old command or branch occurred unless the observation also
establishes that command-specific link. Projection may use it only where the
global contract declares that continuation semantics.

Behavior-sensitive projection therefore controls both how much knowledge is
distributed and when it must exist. Equivalent local continuations can share a
plan; different continuations require evidence before the first difference,
including a difference between waiting, performing an effect, and terminating.

**** Supported equivalence and conservative rejection

Behavior-sensitive checking must define the local equivalence relation used by
its supported language subset. The observables include whichever messages,
effects, authoritative invocations, resource actions, and terminal or failure
outcomes the semantic contract distinguishes. Relevant ordering and timing
constraints must not disappear merely because the final values happen to match.

Permitted internal steps may be abstracted only under an explicit semantic
relation. The checker is not required to decide equivalence of arbitrary
Clojure functions or unrestricted programs.

A concrete conservative baseline compares normalized continuation graphs up to
renaming of diagnostic state identities and local binding names. It preserves
operation/action contract identities, bound inputs and results, message
destinations and payload meanings, observable effects, owner scopes, and
terminal/failure alternatives. Ignoring source coordinates does not ignore
causal dependencies or substitute one implementation contract for another.
Cycles require a checked graph relation rather than recursive unfolding without
a termination bound. Stronger equivalence rules must identify their mechanism
and premises; equality of final values or of function names is not such a rule.

A receiver may merge remote alternatives when admitted incoming messages
distinguish the alternatives before their different behavior. If alternatives
share a sender, event, channel, and correlation scope, their admitted payload
contracts must be disjoint or an explicit discriminator must select them.
Optional fields alone commonly overlap. A supported checker rejects a known
ambiguity and reports an unresolved obligation when opaque schema predicates
prevent establishing disjointness. It must not choose the first matching branch
by map or registration order.

When the supported analysis cannot establish equivalence or a sufficient
knowledge path, it must reject or report an unresolved obligation at the
appropriate admission boundary. It must not silently assume equivalence,
disclose a private discriminator, or introduce an undeclared message to make
projection succeed. An explicit supported communication path or visible trusted
assumption may be authored where permitted by the relevant contract. An asserted
equivalence remains a premise of the resulting projection claim; it cannot
silently create missing input data, discharge a mandatory failed owner check,
or authorize private disclosure.

**** Trace preservation and progress are separate obligations

Excluding forbidden local traces is a safety claim. It does not by itself prove
that a projected program can perform every required interaction, avoid deadlock,
or eventually complete. A projection that admits no useful behavior must not
satisfy the required operation merely because its empty behavior is safe.

Each claimed projection result must state the global/local behavior relation
and the entry and environmental premises under which it applies. Required
realizability, terminal behavior, and progress obligations must be discharged
with the appropriate evidence. Liveness remains conditional on its named
delivery, availability, fairness, or application-action assumptions.

These obligations supplement, rather than replace, the independent application
capability acceptance defined in Part I.

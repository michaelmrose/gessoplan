*** Availability is not authorization

An operation may be present in a model or choreography registry without being
currently executable by a particular principal.

Likewise, a prepared or bound operation may be rendered as an affordance without
becoming durable authorization.

These distinctions must remain explicit:

| Established fact | What it does not establish by itself |
|------------------+-------------------------------------|
| Operation exists | Its use is valid at this choreography state |
| Use is structurally valid at this state | The current principal is authorized now |
| View renders an affordance | The browser has authority over protected state |

The trusted side re-establishes dynamic authorization at execution and protects
the relevant model premises at the authoritative transition boundary where
intervening changes matter. A rendered affordance, a successful earlier check,
and a valid operation contract do not reserve future eligibility.

This separation lets Gesso reason strongly about operation identity and
distributed protocol structure without pretending to statically prove current
business facts that can change between render and commit. A valid source-level
operation interface also does not attest that its named handler, current
authenticated principal, or transactional guard was actually installed and
enforced. An admission diagnostic must identify whether it checked
choreography structure, current model authorization, or the physical join;
an unavailable independent premise is not a successful authorization.

*** Malformed operation type versus wrong business policy

The unified design distinguishes structural/type malformedness from product
correctness.

If a declaration says that =:request/status= is an enum and a rule attempts:

#+begin_src clojure
(< (:request/status request) 10)
#+end_src

the expression is malformed relative to its semantic types and should be
rejected.

If the application states:

#+begin_src clojure
(= (:request/status request) :open)
#+end_src

when product policy actually intended another valid status test, the expression
may be perfectly well formed while encoding the wrong policy.

Gesso should make the former difficult or impossible to certify *within the
supported typed rule language*. It cannot infer the latter merely from syntax.
The two Clojure-shaped examples illustrate the semantic distinction;
they do not imply that arbitrary host-language Clojure expressions are
automatically translated into a closed proof language. The admitted typed
rule language has explicit reader types and operators, so a numeric
ordering comparison against a nominal enum fails admission under
the corresponding type discipline. Arbitrary Clojure policy functions instead retain
an opaque typed/effect contract unless their relevant source is elaborated
into an inspected language or checked by another admitted mechanism.

This distinction becomes more explicit in the later compositional-proof part,
where typed business-rule expressions and certified semantic interfaces are
defined in detail.  It is already a language-design constraint here because
Choreo must not confuse "type checks" with "is the policy humans intended."

*** A valid rule may still be inadmissible at a distributed program point

Intrinsic rule validity is not enough.

For example:

#+begin_example
rule:
    (= (:request/private-review request) :approved)

intrinsic type:
    Boolean
    VALID

browser use before browser knows :request/private-review:
    INVALID AT THIS PROGRAM POINT
#+end_example

A distributed use of a rule therefore creates obligations beyond ordinary type
checking, including:

- the role knows every required fact;
- required provenance is available;
- trust or authority requirements are satisfied;
- optional values have been refined before use;
- authoritative-basis requirements are met where the rule depends on
  authoritative observation.

This is one of the central reasons Choreo needs a semantic type/knowledge layer
richer than ordinary function typing. A global value store's containing a
binding does not make it definitely available to the chosen role on every
incoming path. Nor do a claimed authoritative basis and a correctly typed
provenance record prove the observation was actually acquired or permits this
use. A role may depend on an optional value only after its applicable presence
refinement. A program point may therefore be rejected even when its predicate
is intrinsically well typed and its runtime inputs would happen to be present
in one favorable execution.

*** Operation declarations should remain inspectable data

The ordinary authoring surface may eventually include macros or helpers, but the
semantic representation after elaboration should remain explicit enough to:

- inspect;
- normalize;
- verify;
- project;
- fingerprint semantically;
- explain on failure;
- compare against dependent declarations.

Convenient syntax must not create an opaque second semantics.

Normalization must preserve the information needed to identify the owning
declaration and explain failed obligations without making source coordinates
part of semantic identity when they are diagnostic-only. The compiler or
elaborator must not synthesize an undeclared trusted assertion simply to make a
knowledge, authority, or continuation obligation pass.

The desired direction is:

Author-facing syntax elaborates into explicit normalized semantic data. That
same subject supports verification, projection, diagnostics, and certification;
none may silently interpret a different policy hidden in the syntax layer.
The checked normalization retains the owning source and the identity of the
semantic contract, even if locations and pretty source snippets remain
diagnostic-only rather than contributing to semantic identity. A generated
plan, copied checker report, or friendly syntax cannot independently become
an authoritative second definition of a business rule.

*** Operation-admission diagnostic classes

| Classification | Evidence and response |
|----------------+-----------------------|
| =:invalid= | A contradiction is established in the admitted syntax, type, value-flow, role knowledge, or stated interface; reject at the earliest competent boundary |
| =:unsupported= | The construct is outside the checker’s supported language or bounded capacity; do not report a successful check or silently discard it |
| =:open-premise= | A needed model/authority, external Clojure, runtime producer, or physical deployment correspondence was not established; preserve the premise and the owning boundary |
| =:runtime-required= | A declared dynamic fact such as authenticated eligibility, current transaction state, or resource ownership must be established when the relevant action occurs |
| =:runtime-rejected= | An actual guarded operation was rejected by its trusted boundary; do not reinterpret this as a static type failure |
| =:indeterminate= | Delivery or outcome evidence is insufficient; retain command-specific uncertainty and a permitted recovery path instead of inventing rejection |
| =:policy-mismatch= | Product policy is allegedly wrong despite type-correct syntax; require a source-owned rule, product acceptance criterion, or counterexample rather than pretending the type checker knows intent |

These names describe required diagnostic distinctions; an API may
expose them using an equivalent typed representation rather than
these literal enum keywords. A useful report identifies
the declaration and semantic subject, the role and program point where
relevant, required versus available knowledge/provenance, the supported
checker property, the failing or open premise, and the correct next
construction, runtime, or product-acceptance boundary.

*** Admission counterexamples

| Apparent success | Required outcome |
|------------------+------------------|
| A nominal status enum is passed to a numeric comparison in the admitted typed-rule AST | Reject the malformed type use, without claiming arbitrary Clojure compilation was analyzed |
| A well-typed predicate tests =:open= but product policy intended another permitted status | Type checking may succeed; the policy-intent discrepancy needs independent rule/acceptance evidence |
| A browser branch reads a private fact known only to the server | Reject that role-local use; global value availability does not grant browser knowledge |
| An optional receive field was present on one path but omitted on another | Require path-specific presence refinement before unconditional use |
| An input claims an authoritative basis with the expected schema | Do not upgrade a claimed token to a trusted acquired observation on shape alone |
| A valid choreography has a declared but physically uninstalled required command handler | Report the missing implementation/assembly premise, not successful physical admission |
| A command times out after being sent | Preserve the indeterminate outcome and reconciliation contract; do not classify timeout as a rejected business rule |

A failed supported check, an unsupported check, an open external obligation,
and a genuine runtime rejection must remain separate. None can be laundered
into success through a structural fingerprint, a synthesized assertion, or an
exception path that swallows a checker limitation.

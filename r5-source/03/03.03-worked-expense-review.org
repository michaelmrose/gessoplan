*** A worked expense review

Consider a manager reviewing a previously submitted Expense. This short
choreography starts with a submitted Expense selected from an authorized queue.
The employee's earlier submission and Finance's possible later approval are
separate short executions connected by durable state. This keeps an unused
Finance role from waiting forever in a protocol that never needed its action.

The application owns the domain schemas and public operations:

#+begin_src clojure
(def Expense
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:expense/id uuid?]
   [:expense/employee-id uuid?]
   [:expense/department keyword?]
   [:expense/amount Money]
   [:expense/status
    [:enum :draft :submitted :awaiting-finance :approved :rejected]]])

(def ApprovalPolicy
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:policy/department keyword?]
   [:policy/finance-required-at Money]])

(def finance-route-rule
  {:rule/id :expense/requires-finance
   :inputs {:expense Expense :policy ApprovalPolicy}
   :preconditions
   [[:= [:expense/department :expense] [:policy/department :policy]]
    [:same-currency [:expense/amount :expense]
                    [:policy/finance-required-at :policy]]]
   :returns :boolean
   :expression
   [:money/>= [:expense/amount :expense]
               [:policy/finance-required-at :policy]]})
#+end_src

=Money= and its operations use the explicit money profile from Part I. The rule
does not compare unspecified host decimals or mix currencies. Its policy is
deliberately simple: amounts at or above the threshold require Finance; smaller
amounts may take the manager path, subject to all other model guards. A real
application may supply a richer opaque Clojure policy under an explicit contract.

The review acquisition returns Expense and Policy under one compatible read
context. It binds the Expense to the selected id and the Policy to that Expense's
department. Knowing an id does not establish any of these facts. The manager
principal and permission to read come from trusted acquisition context.
Compatibility of independently acquired values must be established by the
read/authority contract; merely putting them in the same return map does not
prove they were read from one coherent basis.

A domain may also impose a manager approval ceiling. This simplified
Finance-threshold example does not silently assert that a manager has
unlimited approval authority: if the real domain applies a manager ceiling,
that rule belongs to the admitted public model operation and its
transaction-current guards (and, where used for routing, to the declared
review policy). It must not survive merely as an unused schema field or
an informal browser-side assumption.

The operation invocation contracts are:

| Operation | New-incorporation requirement | Successful recorded effect |
|-----------+--------------------------------+----------------------------|
| =:expense/approve-as-manager= | Current manager permission, submitted Expense, applicable Policy, and manager-route eligibility | Expense becomes approved |
| =:expense/mark-awaiting-finance= | Current manager permission, submitted Expense, applicable Policy, and Finance-route eligibility | Expense becomes awaiting Finance |

Each operation atomically protects its current dependencies, domain changes,
and command-result record under Part IV. The preparation read determines which
operation to try; it does not reserve eligibility or authorize incorporation.
A compatible replay follows the recorded-outcome and current disclosure rules
without reapplying a new-incorporation status guard. The authoritative
incorporation's notification contract is chosen independently of the
subsequent caller-visible send. If the business operation requires a
*durable review notification*, C2 incorporation also establishes the
relevant stable publication/work intent at that atomic boundary, or the
operation supplies an equivalently qualified durable discovery path.
A manager browser's later result-bearing send may prompt that work but
cannot be the only recoverable record when the browser may disappear
between commit and send. If the notice is deliberately best effort,
the operation does not manufacture mandatory delivery debt; its
accepted command and terminal claims explicitly retain that weaker
notification guarantee.

Here is the choreography's control structure. The invocation and acquisition
rows are authoring constructs whose elaboration exposes the boundaries described
above; the table does not introduce additional hidden primitive semantics.

| State | Owner and action | Successors |
|-------+------------------+------------|
| Acquire review inputs | Manager requests the admitted Expense/Policy observation | Success: classify; denied/not-found: close the session without notice; temporary failure: declared retry or close without notice |
| Classify | Manager applies the typed Finance-route rule to the bound observation | Finance required: invoke escalation; otherwise: invoke manager approval |
| Invoke escalation | Trusted authority processes the bound escalation command | Settle the command-specific model result |
| Invoke manager approval | Trusted authority processes the bound approval command | Settle the command-specific model result |
| Settle | Manager admits the disclosed result for the actual command | Accepted: publish review notice; rejected: handle rejection and close without notice; unknown: reconcile or hand off recovery, then close this session without notice if needed |
| Publish review notice | Manager emits the permitted historical review result toward the notifier | Manager's sending boundary may finish under its declared handoff; semantic receiver consumption, physical notification and global terminal remain separate |
| Close without notice | Manager emits the declared no-notification disposition | Notifier must admit that closure message or follow the declared missing-delivery/retirement path; command recovery remains with its owner |
| Notify or close | Notifier receives the correlated disposition | Review notice: perform the declared effect; no-notification: return without that effect; delivery failure follows the session recovery contract |

**** Admitted elaboration of the review protocol

The table is an authoring view. It does not justify the manager learning a
value merely because the authoritative operation appears earlier in
the *global* graph. Checked elaboration retains the following boundaries
and source-to-plan evidence:

| Authored step | Admitted semantic/role-local obligation |
|---------------+----------------------------------------|
| Manager acquires Expense and Policy | The manager's declared acquisition effect and admitted =:await= observation establish scoped, coherent input facts and a suitable authority basis, or the declared denied/retry/close path; a readable id or HTTP success does not establish those facts |
| Manager classifies the route | A =:local= action or supported typed expression establishes the manager-known selector; a =:branch= checks both routes and any declared failure/unmatched path without exposing the selector to the notifier |
| Manager submits C2 to Expense authority | The bound command request reaches the declared trusted operation interface under the chosen transport/participant contract; if the source declares a participant message, its actual =:communicate= occurrence is retained, not replaced with a fabricated =:local= step |
| Expense authority settles C2 | The `=:authoritative=` semantic operation occurs at the trusted authority role with authenticated caller, immutable command binding, current guards, and an admitted committed/rejected/unknown-or-reconciliation result; a browser's completion or transport acceptance is not that occurrence |
| Manager acquires C2's result | A declared authority-to-manager =:communicate= with admitted =:receive=, or a genuinely authored trusted =:await= outcome observation, establishes the manager's command-specific knowledge; copying the authority's global result into manager context is forbidden |
| Manager selects notification or closure | A role-local =:branch= uses the actually admitted outcome. Recorded success uses the result-bearing notifier contract; rejection/unavailable cases use the declared no-notification/recovery paths, without treating unknown incorporation as definite failure |
| Notifier receives the selected disposition | The chosen admitted =:communicate= establishes the notifier's permitted fields at actual receiver consumption; unmatched or overlapping variants, false correlation, and absent delivery are not accepted as another branch |
| Notifier performs the declared notification effect | The effect has a declared semantic footprint and a separately evidenced physical issue/outcome, with stable work identity and allowed retry/duplicate handling. A hidden =:local= calculation cannot conceal a notification whose completion the contract claims |
| The review session finishes | Local =:return= / completion may occur at different times for manager and notifier; the claimed global =:return= requires the actual semantic receiver occurrence, declared notification-effect disposition or a separately admitted durable handoff/retirement under the selected terminal contract |

This is an *obligation mapping*, not a claim that the rows are literally
nine global opcodes or that every deployment must use direct
manager-to-authority messages. Different transport and observation
choices may be valid only under their exact authored contracts and
a checked refinement relation. The compact executable plans and
their source/proof mappings must preserve causal order, role
knowledge, observable operation identity, failure alternatives,
and all terminal obligations. The notation does not assume that a
physical provider callback is an authoritative database decision.

The model outcome and the presentation settlement are distinct:

| Model evidence | Permitted continuation |
|----------------+------------------------|
| =:committed= with recorded successful approval/escalation | Accepted continuation for that exact command |
| =:already-incorporated= with a permitted recorded success | The same accepted continuation, without another incorporation |
| =:rejected= with an authoritative command rejection | Rejection handling; no accepted-success notice |
| =:commit-unknown= or indeterminate result delivery | Unknown handling until command-specific authoritative evidence resolves it |

If a model retains other terminal outcomes, the disclosed recorded disposition
has its explicit mapping. A transport failure after a successful commit does
not turn that commit into a rejection. An unrelated Expense now being approved
does not establish that this command approved it.

This worked review chooses a *result-bearing notifier protocol*: the
recorded message deliberately discloses the permitted result of the bound
manager command to the notifier. This is a different disclosure contract from
the *neutral notifier protocol* in the later behavior-sensitive example.
The outward notification text can be identical under both manager outcomes,
but an internal recipient of =:review/result= still learns which result
was reported. Matching text on screen is not a proof that the notifier
cannot distinguish the branches.

The message contains only the declared, disclosure-permitted review
information. It does not contain the manager's private threshold or entire
observation. The issuer or admitting trusted boundary justifies the command
outcome before that result is disclosed. A small inspection view of
this *result-bearing* contract is:

#+begin_src clojure
{:message/id :expense/review-disposition
 :from :manager
 :to :notifier
 :required #{:review/session-id :review/disposition}
 :correlation #{:review/session-id}
 :variants
 {:recorded
  {:required #{:expense/id :gesso/command-id :review/result}
   :result-type [:enum :approved :awaiting-finance]
   :source-evidence :admitted-command-outcome}
  :no-notification
  {:required #{}}}
 :delivery-contract :expense/review-notification}
#+end_src

The =:variants= entry in this map is *higher-level contract inspection data*,
not a self-executing global =:communicate= operation or a second independent
message language. Checked elaboration turns the alternatives into actual
admissible communications and disjoint projected receive alternatives,
for example with separately identified events or an admitted discriminating
closed payload contract. Each resulting communication retains its required,
optional, correlation, sender/receiver, and disclosure contracts. The
constructor must reject unresolved overlaps rather than letting message
arrival or an optional field arbitrarily choose a continuation.
Role-local knowledge-flow checks alone are not proof of the
application-level tagged result's meaning or trusted source.

The admitted review session supplies its correlation identity at entry.
The declared =:review/disposition= discriminator must be checked as
=:recorded= or =:no-notification=, with the recorded variant's
=:review/result= bound to its closed approved/awaiting-Finance
alternative and exact command-result evidence. Checked elaboration
makes the common and variant-specific obligations explicit:

| Admitted variant | Closed semantic fields and discriminator | Required justification |
|------------------+------------------------------------------+------------------------|
| =:recorded= | Common =:review/session-id= and literal =:review/disposition :recorded=, plus =:expense/id=, =:gesso/command-id= and =:review/result= | Trusted command-specific successful incorporation or permitted recorded replay; source, subject, command and result must agree |
| =:no-notification= | Common =:review/session-id= and literal =:review/disposition :no-notification=; no command result or failed-read private subject | Admitted session and source with the declared closure/recovery reason; no result, non-incorporation or domain absence is inferred |

The variants' schemas, source/recipient admission, correlation and
permitted disclosure are independent obligations; the =:variants=
inspection map alone does not prove them. They may elaborate into
distinct identified communication events or a common event with an
admitted disjoint tagged-payload contract. A receiver may not select
a variant by absent optional fields, message order, or a convenient
fallback match. The receiver's actual consumed variant alone
establishes its declared knowledge. Only the recorded variant
carries a command outcome. The no-notification variant
terminates participation without claiming the Expense is absent, the command
failed, or no review was ever recorded. It does not carry a failed read's
private subject. Both variants have closed payload contracts and declared
recipient permission; timing and participation metadata also follow the
declared disclosure policy. A required participant is not left waiting merely because
an earlier acquisition or operation failed; unavailable delivery follows the
session's explicit timeout/retirement or recovery contract.

This is contract inspection data, not a credential. The trusted notification
boundary must validate the origin and permitted command outcome if an
adversarial manager browser transports the notice. A manager-role label or
plausible result string cannot establish that a review occurred. If the service
instead derives the notice from an authoritative outbox, that is the declared
realization and causal path; projection must not invent it silently.

The delivery contract also decides what failure means. The following
milestones have *different* completion evidence:

| Review/notification milestone | What it establishes, and what remains open |
|-------------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| C2 incorporated | The bound Expense operation and command outcome are authoritative; if required, a protected durable notification intent also exists, but delivery does not follow |
| Publication work recorded | The model owns a stable scoped work/intent identity and recovery obligation; neither a particular notifier's receive nor a provider-side effect has occurred by implication |
| Manager local =:send= completed | The manager's declared local emission succeeded under the send contract; the notifier may still know nothing |
| Notifier admitted =:receive= | The particular global semantic =:communicate= occurrence is realized and declared communicated facts are learned; no outside notification is proved |
| Physical notification issued or accepted | The registered notifier/provider completed the *named* physical stage under its correlation and failure contract; an accepted dispatch is not proof a human saw the notice |
| Notification effect fulfilled, failed or handed off | Only the separately declared completion, failure, retry, retirement or durable continuation evidence discharges the corresponding effect obligation; a terminal claim names the actual milestone it asserts |

**** Notification realization profiles and recovery ownership

The worked table above depicts a *manager-mediated* review session. It
does not silently define the physical topology of every durable notice.
The application selects and admits its actual realization, including
the producer of each semantic message, recovery owner, and terminal claim:

| Authored notification profile | Permitted behavior after C2; required proof boundary |
|-------------------------------+------------------------------------------------------|
| Manager-mediated, best effort | After an admitted result, the manager may send the correlated disposition. Loss of that send is permitted only under the authored attempt, timeout or retirement contract. A required waiting notifier still needs its declared closure or recovery; neither global receive nor actual delivery is inferred |
| Model-owned durable publication | C2 creates or qualifies stable discoverable work. A separately admitted worker/notifier execution acquires that work under trusted origin, scope and disclosure rules and produces its *own* declared message/effect steps; the vanished manager's projected =:send= is not manufactured |
| Recoverable manager-mediated delivery | If the authored global protocol requires the manager-to-notifier occurrence, durable state alone cannot substitute for it. An admitted continuation, delegated sender with a checked role/issuer transfer, or explicit protocol alternative must preserve or replace that dependency under a stated refinement relation |

A worker may continue the *business notification obligation* after the
manager's browser retires without continuing the manager's old runtime
execution. A model-derived outbox trigger is not by itself a peer
=:communicate=, a consumed =:receive=, or the old manager's actuation.
If the worker's protocol is a new short execution, its entry knowledge,
current authority, consumed work identity and effect ownership must be
established there. If a checked alternative replaces the manager send,
the specification and projected plans expose that alternative rather
than silently rewriting a manager-origin message as worker-origin.

The result-bearing disclosure contract is retained under each profile:
an authority- or worker-origin notice may disclose an admitted command
outcome only through its own trusted command binding and current
recipient policy. The best-effort profile cannot interpret a failed or
omitted post-commit send as an authoritative =:no-notification= result
unless that exact closure path is authored and admitted. In particular,
silence does not turn accepted C2 into a rejected C2.

A durable worker and an optional browser hint may coexist, but the
contract must relate their publication identities, independently
admitted occurrences and deduplication effects. A repeated hint is not
a second business command, and an outbox retry is not proof the provider
failed the earlier attempt. A global terminal for the manager session
can promise C2's incorporation and recorded work independently of
the later notification execution. A terminal promising actual external
fulfillment still needs that later effect's qualified evidence and
assumptions; a promise of eventual fulfillment additionally names
the permitted discovery, availability, provider and fairness premises.

The stable delivery/work identity is not interchangeable with C2's
semantic command identity, a transport attempt, or the root identity of
one participant message. A compatible command replay does not create
new required work. A retried delivery may create a new physical attempt
without authorizing an additional externally significant notification;
the provider and receiver contracts govern deduplication, reconciliation,
and any legitimately repeated effect. An exactly-once physical effect
claim requires evidence strong enough for the chosen failure model;
a durable outbox by itself does not establish it.

An accepted
=:send= completion establishes that the sender discharged its *local sending*
boundary, not that the global =:communicate= occurred at the recipient or
that the notification effect executed. The declared semantic communication
is realized at admitted receiver consumption; if that cannot happen
before local retirement, the global terminal path requires a distinct,
explicitly admitted handoff or failure/timeout continuation with its
own business meaning. Neither a missing receive nor a sender's local
=:return= silently chooses =:no-notification=. For a required durable
notification, the model transaction or another justified handoff must retain
recoverable notification work. A direct browser send that can disappear after
commit cannot establish eventual notification. For a best-effort notice, loss
may be allowed but the assurance claim must say so. The normal global
terminal for such a protocol may promise an *attempt* or admitted
retirement, not guaranteed delivery. A durable-handoff terminal may
promise that recoverable work exists, not that the future effect
already happened; that stronger future-delivery claim remains subject
to explicit scheduler, provider, and fairness premises. If the declared
terminal promises actual physical fulfillment, the proof must also
include the qualified effect-completion evidence and the physical
observation/refinement contract, not only a coarse distributed trace.
Replaying an accepted command result must not resend an externally
significant notification unless its own deduplication or repeat
policy permits it.

*** Worked-review counterexamples and correspondence duties

| Case | Required response |
|------+-------------------|
| Manager's Policy/Expense observation is inconsistent or no longer eligible by incorporation | Reject or reacquire under the declared command path; do not publish an uncommitted success notice |
| A manager approval ceiling applies but is absent from the chosen operation's guarded policy | The operation contract is incomplete; do not count browser routing as enforcement |
| A recorded and no-notification disposition use the same event, and the receiver selects by whether optional result fields happen to be present | Reject ambiguous or overlapping variants unless the required literal discriminator and schema are checked at admission |
| Manager completes a transport send of the no-notification variant and the compiler records that the notifier returned without admitting it | Reject premature receiver/global completion; a sent closure message is not a received closure event |
| A retry discloses a permitted recorded successful command outcome | Do not incorporate again; notification replay still follows its *own* deduplication and disclosure rules |
| A browser fabricates =:recorded= with a plausible Expense id or command id | Trusted-side notifier admission must establish the actual bound outcome or use an authoritative outbox |
| A result-bearing message discloses =:review/result= to the notifier while a proof calls the notifier route-ignorant because its displayed notice is neutral | Reject the confidentiality claim or use a separately authored neutral message contract with justified coarse outcome evidence |
| The global graph contains the authority's C2 result and the manager settles on it without a receive, trusted observation or other admitted knowledge path | Reject the manager's result-dependent continuation; a globally recorded authority output does not establish manager knowledge |
| A manager-side HTTP 200 response is lowered into =:authoritative= at the manager rather than at Expense authority | Reject the trust/owner substitution; transport success is not the trusted operation or its verified command outcome |
| The manager finishes locally after sending a notifier message, and the compiler reports global success while the required notifier receive/effect remains unresolved | Distinguish local completion from global terminal correspondence; preserve required delivery/effect or an expressly admitted recovery handoff |
| Two result variants can match the same sender/event/channel and payload | Reject ambiguous projection or require a checked discriminator, not arrival-order selection |
| An accepted model commit is followed by notification delivery failure | Retain delivery/recovery debt if notification is durable; do not retroactively reject the command |
| C2 commits and a required notification is lost because the browser closes before sending, with no durable intent or discovery | Reject the claimed required-delivery construction; the authoritative incorporation must establish qualified recoverable work independently of the browser |
| A notifier admits the review message and the global terminal is reported as a delivered user notification before the provider effect completes | Reject the stronger effect claim; semantic receipt is not physical fulfillment |
| A notification worker retries after an unknown provider response using a new unbound work identity | Preserve/reconcile original delivery intent and its duplicate policy; a second attempt is not permission to double-notify |
| The manager chooses an approval path without notifying Finance | Finance's later participation requires its own admitted queue observation and guards, not an absent earlier message |

These are example-level acceptance and correspondence obligations, not
by themselves a certification of compiler, application, or notifier
realization. An all-participant choreography remains permissible *when*
its declared delivery and branch-knowledge obligations can be met;
this short review intentionally uses a later Finance execution
rather than requiring an idle Finance participant to wait indefinitely.

Projection produces complementary plans, not separately authored workflows:

| Projection | Required behavior and knowledge |
|------------+---------------------------------|
| Manager | Knows selected Expense id; acquires scoped inputs; derives the route; invokes the selected operation; interprets its command result; transfers a permitted notice or closes participation without notice |
| Expense authority | Admits each actual invocation, authenticates the caller, applies replay or atomic new-incorporation semantics, and discloses the permitted result |
| Notifier | Receives the declared disposition; performs the notification effect with its own outcome handling or returns without that effect |

Under this result-bearing contract the notifier acquires the disclosed
=:review/result= when the recorded variant is admitted, whether or not
its physical notification text varies with that result. This contract
cannot be used to prove that the notifier is ignorant of the manager's
route. It does not require disclosure of the private threshold itself.
An application requiring a route-neutral notifier instead authors the
separate neutral contract discussed under behavior-sensitive choice;
the projector cannot erase a required result-bearing message merely
because the visible notification would look the same.

Finance's later queue observation establishes entry knowledge for a new
execution; no unused role in this short choreography has to infer that
a missing message means it may terminate.

A role's local return means that role has no remaining work under its contract.
It is not proof that every other role completed, every message was delivered,
or the whole business workflow finished. The global completion judgment and
any durable recovery handoff retain those distinctions.

*** A worked expense review

Consider a manager reviewing a previously submitted Expense. This short
choreography starts with a submitted Expense selected from an authorized queue.
The employee's earlier submission and Finance's possible later approval are
separate short executions connected by durable state. This keeps an unused
Finance role from waiting forever in a protocol that never needed its action.

The application owns the domain schemas and public operations:

#+begin_src clojure
(def Expense
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:expense/id uuid?]
   [:expense/employee-id uuid?]
   [:expense/department keyword?]
   [:expense/amount Money]
   [:expense/status
    [:enum :draft :submitted :awaiting-finance :approved :rejected]]])

(def ApprovalPolicy
  [:map {:closed true}
   [:policy/department keyword?]
   [:policy/finance-required-at Money]])

(def finance-route-rule
  {:rule/id :expense/requires-finance
   :inputs {:expense Expense :policy ApprovalPolicy}
   :preconditions
   [[:= [:expense/department :expense] [:policy/department :policy]]
    [:same-currency [:expense/amount :expense]
                    [:policy/finance-required-at :policy]]]
   :returns :boolean
   :expression
   [:money/>= [:expense/amount :expense]
               [:policy/finance-required-at :policy]]})
#+end_src

=Money= and its operations use the explicit money profile from Part I. The rule
does not compare unspecified host decimals or mix currencies. Its policy is
deliberately simple: amounts at or above the threshold require Finance; smaller
amounts may take the manager path, subject to all other model guards. A real
application may supply a richer opaque Clojure policy under an explicit contract.

The review acquisition returns Expense and Policy under one compatible read
context. It binds the Expense to the selected id and the Policy to that Expense's
department. Knowing an id does not establish any of these facts. The manager
principal and permission to read come from trusted acquisition context.
Compatibility of independently acquired values must be established by the
read/authority contract; merely putting them in the same return map does not
prove they were read from one coherent basis.

A domain may also impose a manager approval ceiling. This simplified
Finance-threshold example does not silently assert that a manager has
unlimited approval authority: if the real domain applies a manager ceiling,
that rule belongs to the admitted public model operation and its
transaction-current guards (and, where used for routing, to the declared
review policy). It must not survive merely as an unused schema field or
an informal browser-side assumption.

The operation invocation contracts are:

| Operation | New-incorporation requirement | Successful recorded effect |
|-----------+--------------------------------+----------------------------|
| =:expense/approve-as-manager= | Current manager permission, submitted Expense, applicable Policy, and manager-route eligibility | Expense becomes approved |
| =:expense/mark-awaiting-finance= | Current manager permission, submitted Expense, applicable Policy, and Finance-route eligibility | Expense becomes awaiting Finance |

Each operation atomically protects its current dependencies, domain changes,
and command-result record under Part IV. The preparation read determines which
operation to try; it does not reserve eligibility or authorize incorporation.
A compatible replay follows the recorded-outcome and current disclosure rules
without reapplying a new-incorporation status guard.

Here is the choreography's control structure. The invocation and acquisition
rows are authoring constructs whose elaboration exposes the boundaries described
above; the table does not introduce additional hidden primitive semantics.

| State | Owner and action | Successors |
|-------+------------------+------------|
| Acquire review inputs | Manager requests the admitted Expense/Policy observation | Success: classify; denied/not-found: close the session without notice; temporary failure: declared retry or close without notice |
| Classify | Manager applies the typed Finance-route rule to the bound observation | Finance required: invoke escalation; otherwise: invoke manager approval |
| Invoke escalation | Trusted authority processes the bound escalation command | Settle the command-specific model result |
| Invoke manager approval | Trusted authority processes the bound approval command | Settle the command-specific model result |
| Settle | Manager admits the disclosed result for the actual command | Accepted: publish review notice; rejected: handle rejection and close without notice; unknown: reconcile or hand off recovery, then close this session without notice if needed |
| Publish review notice | Manager communicates the permitted historical review result to the notifier | On admitted handoff, manager's local work may finish |
| Close without notice | Manager sends the declared no-notification disposition | Notifier returns without a notification effect; any command recovery remains with its declared owner |
| Notify or close | Notifier receives the correlated disposition | Review notice: perform the declared effect; no-notification: return without that effect; delivery failure follows the session recovery contract |

The model outcome and the presentation settlement are distinct:

| Model evidence | Permitted continuation |
|----------------+------------------------|
| =:committed= with recorded successful approval/escalation | Accepted continuation for that exact command |
| =:already-incorporated= with a permitted recorded success | The same accepted continuation, without another incorporation |
| =:rejected= with an authoritative command rejection | Rejection handling; no accepted-success notice |
| =:commit-unknown= or indeterminate result delivery | Unknown handling until command-specific authoritative evidence resolves it |

If a model retains other terminal outcomes, the disclosed recorded disposition
has its explicit mapping. A transport failure after a successful commit does
not turn that commit into a rejection. An unrelated Expense now being approved
does not establish that this command approved it.

This worked review chooses a *result-bearing notifier protocol*: the
recorded message deliberately discloses the permitted result of the bound
manager command to the notifier. This is a different disclosure contract from
the *neutral notifier protocol* in the later behavior-sensitive example.
The outward notification text can be identical under both manager outcomes,
but an internal recipient of =:review/result= still learns which result
was reported. Matching text on screen is not a proof that the notifier
cannot distinguish the branches.

The message contains only the declared, disclosure-permitted review
information. It does not contain the manager's private threshold or entire
observation. The issuer or admitting trusted boundary justifies the command
outcome before that result is disclosed. A small inspection view of
this *result-bearing* contract is:

#+begin_src clojure
{:message/id :expense/review-disposition
 :from :manager
 :to :notifier
 :required #{:review/session-id :review/disposition}
 :correlation #{:review/session-id}
 :variants
 {:recorded
  {:required #{:expense/id :gesso/command-id :review/result}
   :result-type [:enum :approved :awaiting-finance]
   :source-evidence :admitted-command-outcome}
  :no-notification
  {:required #{}}}
 :delivery-contract :expense/review-notification}
#+end_src

The =:variants= entry in this map is *higher-level contract inspection data*,
not a self-executing global =:communicate= operation or a second independent
message language. Checked elaboration turns the alternatives into actual
admissible communications and disjoint projected receive alternatives,
for example with separately identified events or an admitted discriminating
closed payload contract. Each resulting communication retains its required,
optional, correlation, sender/receiver, and disclosure contracts. The
constructor must reject unresolved overlaps rather than letting message
arrival or an optional field arbitrarily choose a continuation.
Role-local knowledge-flow checks alone are not proof of the
application-level tagged result's meaning or trusted source.

The admitted review session supplies its correlation identity at entry.
The declared =:review/disposition= discriminator must be checked as
=:recorded= or =:no-notification=, with the recorded variant's
=:review/result= bound to its closed approved/awaiting-Finance
alternative and exact command-result evidence. The variants' schemas,
correlation and source admission are independent obligations; the
=:variants= map alone does not prove them. Only
the recorded variant carries a command outcome. The no-notification variant
terminates participation without claiming the Expense is absent, the command
failed, or no review was ever recorded. It does not carry a failed read's
private subject. Both variants have closed payload contracts and declared
recipient permission; timing and participation metadata also follow the
declared disclosure policy. A required participant is not left waiting merely because
an earlier acquisition or operation failed; unavailable delivery follows the
session's explicit timeout/retirement or recovery contract.

This is contract inspection data, not a credential. The trusted notification
boundary must validate the origin and permitted command outcome if an
adversarial manager browser transports the notice. A manager-role label or
plausible result string cannot establish that a review occurred. If the service
instead derives the notice from an authoritative outbox, that is the declared
realization and causal path; projection must not invent it silently.

The delivery contract also decides what failure means. For a required durable
notification, the model transaction or another justified handoff must retain
recoverable notification work. A direct browser send that can disappear after
commit cannot establish eventual notification. For a best-effort notice, loss
may be allowed but the assurance claim must say so. Replaying an accepted
command result must not resend an externally significant notification unless
its own deduplication or repeat policy permits it.

*** Worked-review counterexamples and correspondence duties

| Case | Required response |
|------+-------------------|
| Manager's Policy/Expense observation is inconsistent or no longer eligible by incorporation | Reject or reacquire under the declared command path; do not publish an uncommitted success notice |
| A manager approval ceiling applies but is absent from the chosen operation's guarded policy | The operation contract is incomplete; do not count browser routing as enforcement |
| A retry discloses a permitted recorded successful command outcome | Do not incorporate again; notification replay still follows its *own* deduplication and disclosure rules |
| A browser fabricates =:recorded= with a plausible Expense id or command id | Trusted-side notifier admission must establish the actual bound outcome or use an authoritative outbox |
| A result-bearing message discloses =:review/result= to the notifier while a proof calls the notifier route-ignorant because its displayed notice is neutral | Reject the confidentiality claim or use a separately authored neutral message contract with justified coarse outcome evidence |
| Two result variants can match the same sender/event/channel and payload | Reject ambiguous projection or require a checked discriminator, not arrival-order selection |
| An accepted model commit is followed by notification delivery failure | Retain delivery/recovery debt if notification is durable; do not retroactively reject the command |
| The manager chooses an approval path without notifying Finance | Finance's later participation requires its own admitted queue observation and guards, not an absent earlier message |

These are example-level acceptance and correspondence obligations, not
by themselves a certification of compiler, application, or notifier
realization. An all-participant choreography remains permissible *when*
its declared delivery and branch-knowledge obligations can be met;
this short review intentionally uses a later Finance execution
rather than requiring an idle Finance participant to wait indefinitely.

Projection produces complementary plans, not separately authored workflows:

| Projection | Required behavior and knowledge |
|------------+---------------------------------|
| Manager | Knows selected Expense id; acquires scoped inputs; derives the route; invokes the selected operation; interprets its command result; transfers a permitted notice or closes participation without notice |
| Expense authority | Admits each actual invocation, authenticates the caller, applies replay or atomic new-incorporation semantics, and discloses the permitted result |
| Notifier | Receives the declared disposition; performs the notification effect with its own outcome handling or returns without that effect |

Under this result-bearing contract the notifier acquires the disclosed
=:review/result= when the recorded variant is admitted, whether or not
its physical notification text varies with that result. This contract
cannot be used to prove that the notifier is ignorant of the manager's
route. It does not require disclosure of the private threshold itself.
An application requiring a route-neutral notifier instead authors the
separate neutral contract discussed under behavior-sensitive choice;
the projector cannot erase a required result-bearing message merely
because the visible notification would look the same.

Finance's later queue observation establishes entry knowledge for a new
execution; no unused role in this short choreography has to infer that
a missing message means it may terminate.

A role's local return means that role has no remaining work under its contract.
It is not proof that every other role completed, every message was delivered,
or the whole business workflow finished. The global completion judgment and
any durable recovery handoff retain those distinctions.

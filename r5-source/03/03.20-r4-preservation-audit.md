# R5.1 Part III — 03.20 Ephemeral Typing Reports

**R4 baseline:** `main` Part III's embedded collaborative typing
example under “Projection preserves necessary communication but does
not worship messages.”
**R5 candidate:** modular section 03.20, “Ephemeral typing reports.”
**Disposition:** focused R4 preservation and completed-design improvement
review; not a claim that current physical browser transports are certified.

## R4 normative requirements retained

- Typing is an admitted recent *intent report*, not evidence that a
  human is in fact still typing.
- The adversarial payload is closed and contains only document
  identity and typing intent; sender principal/actor identity comes
  from an authenticated, currently registered channel.
- The sender must have permitted document editing/typing access,
  and each recipient's subscription must independently authorize
  receipt of the indicator.
- The peer indicator is scoped by document and admitted actor, and
  the display's timer is a managed owner-relative resource.
- A later =true= can replace an earlier timer; stale expiry must not
  hide the replacement. A =false= hides only the admitted actor's
  indicator.
- Required durable Request invalidation and best-effort ephemeral
  typing have different loss, recovery and acquisition semantics.
- Message shape validation is not authority, provenance, or sender
  authentication.

## Superior R5 treatment retained

- The R5 schema and contract sketches replace R4's larger, partly
  illustrative `defprojected` program while preserving its behavior.
- A trusted issuer's per-source ordering, receiver high-water mark
  and explicit source-instance succession prevent older/duplicate
  reports from resurrecting the display.
- Revocation and recipient disclosure are live contracts, not
  permanent grants from original channel registration.
- Three seconds refers to elapsed local time after admitted receipt,
  not a guarantee about actual typing or punctual wall-clock timers.
- Distinct domain, actor and principal identities must not collapse
  merely because their carriers have the same representation.

## Focused changes in 03.20

1. Clarify source and recipient admission as separate checks, with
   authenticated principal, actor, source instance and ordering evidence
   issued or validated by the trusted boundary rather than wire fields.
2. State that ordering sequence is trusted envelope metadata, not a
   previously missing `TypingPayload` wire field; best-effort transport
   can still reorder/drop reports.
3. Require monotone ordering *within* a registered stream and an
   admitted source-succession rule across reconnections, not numeric
   comparison across unrelated epochs.
4. Retain the high-water mark through =false= and timer expiry until
   admitted retirement has fenced late deliveries.
5. Make timer allocation, replacement, retirement, deadline and
   current owner admission explicit; callbacks cannot mutate state
   or DOM outside the pure transition/effect boundary.
6. Preserve actual presentation semantics under browser suspension,
   lost reports, authorization revocation and identity replacement.
7. Add ten negative cases for spoofed sender fields, unauthorized
   recipients, reordered reports, retired source epochs, replay,
   stale timers, suspension, direct callback bypass, false database
   replay and claims of live human activity.

## Implementation observations (audit only)

- `gesso.live.browser.adapter` has normalized state/effect
  transitions, execution-owned timer generations, stale callback
  checks and owner lifecycle handling. This does not automatically
  certify the described document-specific indicator contract.
- `gesso.choreo.machine` distinguishes declared semantic
  communications and role-local knowledge from untrusted physical
  data, and does not execute host timer or DOM effects itself.
- `gesso.choreo.knowledge` distinguishes communicated provenance
  from independently authoritative facts.
- The older R4 worked example used conceptual constructors and
  callbacks; retaining them verbatim would misleadingly imply those
  exact APIs or a proof of current integration.

## Cross-section and cross-part joins

- 03.17–03.19: receipt admission, message occurrence identity,
  open/closed payloads, durable Request publication versus
  best-effort transient coordination.
- 03.21: executable owner-relative resource guards and
  normalized effect completion.
- Parts II and VI: principal/actor/source categories, browser
  transition ownership, actual DOM effect timing and timer
  resource lifetimes.
- Parts VII–X: assembly closure, adversarial physical
  correspondence, scoped proof and assumption evidence.
- Application acceptance: an indicator is a permitted, recent
  report, not authoritative editor activity and not a durable
  business fact.

No meaningful R4 normative behavior has been removed. The
canonical text remains a timeless requirement of finished Gesso.

**** Durable Request publication and selective adoption

For a newly created Request, the authoritative result belongs to the public
Request model. The creator learns the permitted command outcome and reacquires
the declared card or board projection. Other clients need not receive the whole
Request from the creator's execution.

#+begin_src clojure
{:operation/id :request/create
 :authority :request
 :caller-role :customer
 :command-protocol :stable-bound-command
 :accepted-continuation
 {:creator-acquisition :request/created-card
  :remote-coordination :request/board-advisory}
 :rejected-continuation :request/create-rejected
 :unknown-continuation :request/reconcile-create}
#+end_src

This is an inspection view of imported contracts. The admitted create
operation supplies the authorized caller and command-specific created Request
identity. An arbitrary new row found by a query does not establish which Request
this command created.

Remote coordination is triggered from the authoritative incorporation or a
recoverable publication handoff under its declared contract. Required
invalidation cannot depend solely on the creator's browser receiving a response
and deciding to notify peers. Publication failure after commit leaves a
committed command plus delivery debt; it is not a rejected create command.
Part IV owns the atomicity or recoverability of that handoff.

An advisory identifies a permitted scope and justified acquisition requirement.
Its disclosure does not reveal a hidden Request by implication. A lower bound
must be supplied by the admitted producer or a declared fence protocol, not
invented from the receiver's old basis. Subscriptions and reacquisition cover
initial registration, reconnect, and missed-notification gaps.

The browser uses the pure transition and effect-shell separation from Part II:

#+begin_src clojure
(def after-advisory
  (board-runtime/step current-board admitted-board-advisory))

(def refresh-start
  (board-runtime/step
    (:state after-advisory)
    admitted-user-refresh-event))

;; The shell executes the emitted acquisition effect. It retains its
;; registered owner, concrete instance, scope, and principal-context binding.
(:effects refresh-start)

;; The completion is admitted against the latest board state, not the state
;; captured before the request. A later installation completion may be needed.
(board-runtime/step latest-board admitted-acquisition-completion)
#+end_src

The server derives the caller from its actual authentication context. The
browser effect cannot grant a principal identity by carrying a field. The
returned observation is bound to the full query, projection, visibility,
temporal context, authority, and basis required by the acquisition contract.

Acquisition, permitted installation, actual installation, and semantic adoption
are separate where the realization makes them separate. A pure state update
does not itself swap a DOM node. Adoption discharges only requirements that the
installed/adopted observation actually covers, preserving later or unrelated
requirements and respecting the current owner. Physical effects run outside
retriable state-update functions. The worked X18/X19/X20 race in Part II fixes
the intended interpretation.

Bob may therefore retain the old board while showing a Refresh affordance.
The notification does not establish the new Request's title or visibility;
authoritative reacquisition does. Eventual adoption remains conditional on the
declared automatic or user trigger, not merely on the database being available.

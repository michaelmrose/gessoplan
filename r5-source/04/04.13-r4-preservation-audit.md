**Disposition:** thirteenth substantively reviewed candidate section.

## R4 requirements preserved

R4 Part IV's `Requirement satisfaction and safe combination`,
`Finite representations and boundedness claims` and
`Mixed authoritative observations do not imply one aggregate snapshot`
provide the semantic baseline. Required properties include:

- `satisfies(O,R)`: an admitted observation covers the exact scope,
  completeness, progression and relevant declared milestone.
- `entails(R2,R1)`: all satisfying observations for R2 satisfy R1;
  an established relation, not token ordering or observed coincidence.
- `combine(R1,R2)`: conjunction of requirements, with optional compact
  representations only when sound; it need not produce a universal join.
- Dropping an obligation requires admitted entailment. Idempotence,
  commutativity and associativity of normalization/coalescing refer to
  semantic obligation meaning rather than incidental vector order.
- A stronger single-observation requirement cannot be promised eventually
  if no authorized acquisition can satisfy it.
- One in-flight request does not bound incomparable outstanding scopes,
  serialized queue bytes, or the finite formal state model.
- A broader authorized acquisition does not itself grant visible adoption.
  Retirement may end owner-scoped obligations, but not durable command
  uncertainty or other owners' work.
- Board membership at P17 and one newer Request card at P19 do not imply
  complete board membership observed at P19.

R4's extended O1/O2 acquired-board example explains that A1 can start
with O1 requiring P18, then receive O2 requiring P20 during the read.
An admitted P18 completion can discharge O1 but not O2. A genuinely
admitted P21 completion may discharge both if its scope and milestones
are satisfied. An uninstalled response does not satisfy an acquire-and-adopt
requirement even if the read itself completed. This behavior is preserved
as a concise evidence table rather than restoring R4's illustrative
`complete-acquisition` as an assumed Gesso public API.

## R5 architecture preserved

R5 provides a broader `fulfills(E,B)` relation for bundles of evidence
and obligations at their respective milestones. It already separates
single-observation combination from multi-acquisition bundle fulfillment,
sound canonicalization from representation equality, safety from liveness,
admission of broader read scope from permitted browser adoption, and
mixed-scope observations from invented common snapshots.

The revision preserves the conservative design: provider-specific joins
are optimizations; a bundle or explicit unsupported result remains valid.
It does not prescribe one mutable browser queue, persisted fact graph,
universal total ordering, or mandatory one-representative-per-scope layout.

## Focused normative edits

1. Require each obligation in a bundle to have admitted scope/coverage/
   frontier/milestone evidence, without silently overcrediting one read.
2. Make entailment a universal qualified claim rather than inference from
   coincident payloads or a finite example trace.
3. Reject impossible combined-read liveness claims; preserve separately
   satisfiable obligations or report unsupported configuration.
4. Distinguish concurrent request bounds, retained obligations, total
   representation size, resource owners and the soundness of a finite
   verification abstraction.
5. Restore R4's A1/O1/P18, O2/P20 and stronger P21 example, including
   explicit acquisition-only versus acquire-and-adopt settlement.
6. Add ten paired admitted/adverse cases covering cross-scope soundness,
   qualified entailment, joint satisfaction, liveness, in-flight obligations,
   adopted installation, queue bounds, formal abstraction, owner retirement
   and mixed authoritative observations.

## Current Gesso application-assurance v1622 correspondence

Inspected `gesso.live.progression`: `compose` treats requirements as
sets of opaque bases and merges by union, eliminating only exact
duplicates. `satisfied-by?` checks equality or caller-supplied
direct progression witnesses; the truth of a witness must originate
at a qualified provider. `gesso.live.consistency.xtdb` supplies
XTDB-specific basis comparisons and may choose the greatest
compatible same-database basis at its trusted adapter boundary.

Inspected `gesso.live.browser.adapter`: one logical fragment has at
most one active refresh. The state contains an independent
`:queued-refresh?` flag and `:queued-requirements` set so even
a tokenless advisory can survive an in-flight request. A previously
attached exact requirement need not manufacture a redundant successor.
The implementation's refresh completion/callback and swap transitions
are pure browser coordinator machinery; they do **not** independently
prove an executing server query's authorization, complete returned
coverage, truthful advancement witness, or actual browser DOM
installation at the declared global policy milestone.

Inspected `gesso.live.acquisition-preflight`: managed fragment
acquisition paths are derived from the compiled Live graph and trusted
physical route declarations. The preflight itself does not inspect
arbitrary installed Ring handler bodies or provide universal
scope-complete query/refinement evidence.

No browser, XTDB, model checker, full suite, or real application
acceptance tests were executed during this document revision.
The code's observed one-in-flight and queued-set structures are
not a proof of bounded arbitrary outstanding scope obligations,
the `fulfills` relation for all application policies, or universal
correctness of an installed presentation.

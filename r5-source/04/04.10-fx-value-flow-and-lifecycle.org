** FX value flow and lifecycle

FX should make value dependencies and effects inspectable while remaining usable
from ordinary Clojure. Pure calculations consume explicit values and return
explicit values. Effectful steps declare their inputs, outputs, failure classes,
and effect boundary. A context is a capability-bearing environment, not a
hidden bus into which every step writes arbitrary intermediate state.

*** Context and values have different roles

The context may contain admitted database/acquisition capabilities, a trusted
principal binding, clocks or randomness with declared semantics, tracing, and
scoped backend interfaces. Intermediate Request data, candidate decisions,
prepared transitions, and outcomes should normally flow as explicit arguments
and results.

This permits the system to explain which value justified a step and to detect
missing or mismatched inputs. It also reduces accidental use of stale ambient
data. A capability's presence in context does not extend its lifetime or widen
its authority. Authentication state captured at entry does not freeze mutable
authorization unless that temporal policy is explicitly part of the contract.

Graph or multistate implementations obey the same rule. They may provide a
convenient evaluation strategy, but hidden mutable cells must not become an
untracked second model of operation state. Pure dependency nodes and effectful
nodes require distinct semantics, including recomputation and retry behavior.

*** Ordering and completion are explicit

An ordered effect description specifies what each step waits for, how values
flow, and what happens after failure. Merely placing effects in a vector is not
enough if the interpreter may run them concurrently or treat enqueue as
completion.

An illustrative descriptor could distinguish:

#+begin_src clojure
{:fx/type :sequence
 :steps [:admit-command
         :resolve-or-prepare
         :incorporate-authoritative-transition
         :construct-response]
 :failure-policy :phase-aware}
#+end_src

The registered step definitions supply types, branches, capability requirements,
and completion semantics. The map alone is not an executable operation.
Independent publication work may proceed separately once durable intent exists;
it need not delay an otherwise justified commit response unless the public
contract explicitly promises that stronger acknowledgment.

Exceptions are interpreted by phase and evidence. A failure before submission,
an ambiguous failure during submission, a post-commit publication failure, and
a response-rendering failure are different events. A generic catch that turns
all four into =:rejected= violates the operation contract.

Retryable backend callbacks must not contain unprotected external effects.
Clock and random values used in semantic decisions need declared sampling and
retry semantics. Ordinary Clojure functions and macros may implement these
steps, but any opaque effect footprint remains an explicit contract premise.

*** Post-commit delivery debt
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-post-commit-recovery
:END:

Once a command commits, failure to notify, render, or deliver does not uncommit
it. Required later work becomes explicit delivery or recovery debt. The
operation contract identifies what must be durable, who owns it, how it is
retried, and what counts as completion.

For a durable publication requirement, the domain transition and publication
intent must share an atomic boundary or an equivalently qualified discovery
mechanism. A commit followed by an unprotected best-effort publish can leave a
permanent gap if the process dies between those actions.

The intent binds the authoritative effect, relevant subject/query impact,
progression requirement, and stable delivery/work identity. It need not capture
every currently connected subscriber. A durable change log or outbox can feed
later routing and interest matching. Delivery semantics must state whether
duplicates are possible and how consumers recognize compatible repetition.

An in-memory list of local interests is not proof that all affected participants
will learn a change. If publication is conditioned on known interests, the
design must cover remote interests, subscription races, disconnects, and router
failover to the extent that its convergence claim includes them. Otherwise that
claim must be limited explicitly. The particular routing topology belongs to
the transport design; the no-lost-required-work obligation belongs here.

Publication completion also has a scope: queued, durably recorded, accepted by a
broker, delivered to a connection, admitted by a participant, and adopted by a
projection are distinct milestones. The contract names the milestone it can
observe and retains downstream obligations where required.

Recovery failure may leave debt visible and unresolved. It must not manufacture
success, discard the command record, or turn the committed domain operation
into a rejection. If an effect is intentionally best effort, its omission and
the resulting weaker guarantee must be part of the public contract.

# R5.1 Part IV — 04.12 R4 preservation and Gesso correspondence

**Section:** `r5-source/04/04.12-scope-aware-progression.org`  
**Disposition:** twelfth substantively reviewed candidate section.
This reviews the intended completed-system contract. It is not evidence

Part IV and the twelve-part plan remain editorial drafts.

## R4 preservation

R4's `Scope-aware progression and authoritative acquisition` distinguishes
database progression from the actual read scope it constrains. An
authoritative basis does not make every entity current, prove complete
aggregate membership, or imply that an unrelated query was acquired.

R4's Flight 17 and Hotel 9 example presents concrete counterexamples:
a newer Hotel read cannot discharge a Flight seat map obligation;
one more recent seat is not the complete seat map; a matching full
Flight read may satisfy the Flight requirement only under the selected
provider's actual progression relation. The revised R5 travel table
preserves those semantic distinctions, without copying R4's schematic
`satisfies?` function as a universal executable Gesso API.

R4 also requires:
- explicit entity/query/authority scope and positive evidence of coverage;
- full board membership distinct from an individual Request card;
- source-owned selection, authorization, search, filtering and
  effective temporal interpretation;
- page/partial and empty-result distinctions; failure and denial do not
  prove absence, even if revealing the distinction is forbidden publicly;
- scope-preserving requirement satisfaction, combination and entailment;
- consistency and acquisition not being the same as installed/adopted
  browser presentation;
- independent observations not silently becoming a common aggregate
  snapshot.

The combination algebra and bounds are owned in the next R5.1 source
unit 04.13, with mixed observations joined there as well. This review
does not duplicate their normative definitions.

## Stronger R5 design retained

R5's acquisition declaration includes authority/tenant/incarnation,
query meaning, schema and projection version, parameters, selection,
page/cursor coverage, current principal and visibility context,
valid/system-time policy, progression, resource owner and the
required completion milestone.

It also distinguishes provider-admitted equivalent/advances/precedes,
established incomparable, not-established and invalid/unsupported
comparison; witness shape alone is not a truthful relation. The
Flight/Hotel example retains its advantages as an independent
cross-scope check, and the new HumanHelp board/card example preserves
the source's selective Refresh semantics without claiming a universal
current board snapshot.

## Focused normative changes

1. Require evidence tying actual *executed* read meaning to effective
   parameters, authorization, returned coverage and basis. A route
   declaration or successful HTTP response cannot prove that on its own.
2. Make cache and singleflight reuse preserve original scope, trusted
   provenance, read basis and current disclosure permission.
3. Strengthen pagination: mixed query/cursor/visibility/basis windows
   cannot be counted into a complete board without a qualified contract;
   a valid empty page is not a proven empty complete result.
4. Require typed handling of unmet progress, unsupported comparison,
   incompatible incarnation or temporal scope, without silent weakening.
5. Restore R4's HumanHelp board/card counterexample with actual
   RequestAssignment ownership and selective visible adoption.
6. Add nine paired positive/adverse acceptance families spanning
   executed query, coverage, absence, pagination, advancement witnesses,
   temporal/tenant guards, cached reuse, mixed observations and
   installed versus merely completed responses.

## Current Gesso application-assurance v1622 correspondence

Inspected `gesso.live.progression`: its portable requirement contains
an orderless set of opaque bases, composing by set union. Its
`satisfied-by?` checks exact equality or directly supplied shaped
advance witnesses; witness truth remains a trusted adapter premise.
This does not itself establish query-selection completeness,
authorization or a general heterogeneous-scope coverage algebra.

Inspected `gesso.live.consistency.xtdb`: `read-query-opts` applies
the required XTDB progression last so ordinary options cannot
silently weaken it; the XTDB-specific adapter owns trusted
comparison of supported basis values. That does not prove an arbitrary
application's query, returned coverage, read-time authorization or
valid-time selection is exactly the declared one.

Inspected `gesso.live.acquisition-preflight`: required managed
fragment acquisitions are derived from the compiled Live graph and
trusted route declarations. Its own contract admits that a route
capability does **not** inspect arbitrary Ring/Biff handler bodies.
This is valid assembly-relative structural evidence, not a
certification of the actual deployed query or complete returned page.

Inspected `gesso.live.fragment`: cache/singleflight keys can carry
scope, user, params and progression; `strict-fragment-key` requires
scope and user dimensions. Its documentation places responsibility
for including all render-affecting dimensions on the caller.
The presence of a key does not establish current permission,
scope-complete query execution, or that cached HTML was installed.

Inspected `gesso.live.browser.adapter` and
`gesso.live.browser.observation`: the pure adapter treats bases
as opaque and distinguishes HTMX swap decisions from physical
`:authoritative/installed` observations. These provide relevant
transition machinery but do not independently verify server query
execution or a customer's authority to adopt an arbitrary result.

Source contracts were inspected, not runtime-tested. No full browser
gate, route-host inspection, XTDB query-coverage experiment or formal
cross-scope proof was executed for this editorial revision.

** Scope-aware progression and authoritative acquisition
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-progression-scope
:END:

A progression requirement asks for an observation of a particular meaning at a
sufficient authoritative frontier. It is not merely a token. A later Hotel
observation cannot satisfy a Flight obligation simply because the database
position appears newer.

An admitted acquisition declaration must make the following information
mechanically available, directly or through stable references:

- authority, tenant, database incarnation, and qualified provider;
- entity or query meaning, schema/projection version, and parameters;
- selection, filters, ordering, pagination, and coverage requirements;
- principal/visibility context and the policy for current disclosure;
- valid-time and system-time interpretation;
- the required progression relation and its evidence source;
- the acquisition owner and resource-instance correlation;
- the requested completion milestone: acquisition alone, or acquisition with
  permitted installation/adoption.

An acquisition result includes evidence of its actual scope and coverage, not
just the data. A familiar shape, a token supplied by the browser, or a tag saying
=:authoritative= is not enough to admit it. Admission follows the trusted
acquisition contract from Part II.

*** Query meaning and completeness

Entity contents and aggregate membership are different scopes. A Request card
can establish facts about one Request without establishing the complete board.
A board acquisition must identify its authorized selection, search terms,
filters, sort order, page/cursor interpretation, and completeness claims.

Changing a server-side search or filter changes the query meaning. Sorting a
locally adopted set changes presentation order, not knowledge of authoritative
membership. Neither operation silently grants permission to adopt unrelated
new rows.

An empty query result establishes absence only within the successfully evaluated
authorized scope and coverage. A partial response, timeout, inaccessible page,
or denied query must not become authoritative absence. Privacy policy may limit
which distinctions can be disclosed, but it cannot make a failed acquisition
evidence of a domain fact.

One page can satisfy a requirement for that page under a stable query/cursor
contract. It cannot satisfy a complete-board requirement merely by carrying a
sufficiently recent token. A set of separately acquired pages establishes a
complete common snapshot only when the paging contract supplies that stronger
relationship.

*** Opaque progression and comparison judgments

The progression provider owns token interpretation. Generic Gesso code may
preserve opaque values and ask the admitted provider for supported relations;
it must not infer ordering from string comparison, integer-shaped encodings, or
HTTP arrival order.

Comparison distinguishes at least equivalent, advances, precedes, established
incomparable, and not established. Invalid or incompatible evidence is also a
typed failure. Unavailable comparison is not established incomparability, and
neither is equality.

For some providers, equality is the only available relation without additional
evidence. Equality of admitted tokens in a compatible context can be useful;
equality of untrusted bytes is still just equality of bytes. An advancement
witness must be admitted from a provider capable of establishing that relation
and bound to its endpoints, scope, incarnation, and relevant contract version.
Validating the witness map's shape alone does not establish its truth.

*** Worked example: travel observations with different scopes

Consider two requirements for one authorized travel-planning session:

| Requirement | Scope and coverage | Progression requirement |
|-------------+--------------------+-------------------------|
| F | Flight 17 availability, specified cabin and fare-class selection, projection v1, session visibility context, declared temporal policy. | Visibility through admitted flight requirement PF20. |
| H | Hotel 9 availability for check-in D1 and check-out D2, specified room/occupancy selection, projection v1, the same permitted context where applicable. | Visibility through admitted hotel requirement PH24. |

The symbols PF20 and PH24 are illustrative opaque values. Their numeric-looking
suffixes do not authorize arithmetic. Even if both are realized by one XTDB
database, scope remains part of the requirement.

Suppose the provider and acquisition boundary establish the following evidence:

| Observation | What it can establish |
|-------------+-----------------------|
| Full Flight 17 projection, correct cabin/fare selection, compatible time/context, admitted advancement through PF20. | Satisfies F. |
| Hotel 9 projection with advancement through PH24. | May satisfy H; it does not satisfy F. |
| Flight 17 seat count alone at a newer compatible basis. | Satisfies only an appropriately narrow seat-count requirement, not the full F projection. |
| Hotel 9 for different dates. | Does not satisfy H even if its basis is later. |
| Correct hotel query, first page only, when H requires complete offered-room coverage. | Does not satisfy H's completeness obligation. |
| Correct values with an unadmitted basis tag. | No authoritative satisfaction judgment follows from that tag. |
| Correct selection at incompatible valid time or visibility context. | Requires a declared relation; it is not silently interchangeable with the requested scope. |

F and H may be fulfilled by separate admitted acquisitions. That does not
establish that the combined travel display is one atomic database snapshot, or
that either offer remains reservable. Booking eligibility remains a current
authoritative model obligation at the booking boundary.

** Assurance obligations and acceptance cases

Part IV's assurance is a chain of bounded claims. The semantic transition must
express the intended rule; construction must bind it to the actual inputs and
implementation; the complete submission must realize the protected effect;
qualified backend behavior must justify the outcome; propagation and acquisition
must preserve scope and progression; installation must justify adoption.

A report uses Part I's separate claim dimensions: proposition, subject,
mechanism, premises, coverage, applicability, and status. It distinguishes
derivation or construction checking, runtime enforcement, testing, and explicit
assertion. Named environmental premises qualify a claim; conditionality is not
an additional proof mechanism. These descriptions are not interchangeable
assurance grades. A strong proof of an abstract fragment cannot conceal an
unexamined physical execution boundary.

*** Required invariant families

The supported construction and runtime must establish, or explicitly qualify,
at least the following property families:

1. *Binding fidelity*: operation, command, canonical arguments, principal,
   authority, target, and realization cannot be silently substituted between
   admission, submission, outcome, and reconciliation.
2. *Authoritative safety*: each incorporated transition satisfies its protected
   business conditions and declared frame under the backend/writer premises.
3. *Command consistency*: compatible repetition does not incorporate again;
   incompatible reuse cannot overwrite the old binding; terminal disposition is
   reported only with evidence of its declared scope.
4. *Truthful uncertainty*: absence of outcome evidence, timeout, and local
   retirement do not manufacture non-incorporation.
5. *Recoverable required work*: committed operations retain the declared
   command/result and propagation or external-work evidence across promised
   failure modes.
6. *Scoped observation*: acquired data supports only its admitted scope,
   coverage, temporal interpretation, visibility context, and progression.
7. *Justified adoption*: only current permitted installation/adoption paths
   advance adopted knowledge and discharge adoption debt.
8. *Evidence fidelity*: a classification, certificate, or passing test is not
   promoted into a stronger proposition than it establishes.

Liveness claims are separate. They identify scheduling, delivery, backend,
provider, retention, authority, and human-participation assumptions. Safety must
not depend on pretending that those assumptions always produce timely success.

*** Executable conformance cases

The implementation must provide conformance cases for the supported mechanisms,
including negative cases and relevant interleavings. These cases complement
semantic derivations; passing them does not prove arbitrary backend behavior.

| Area | Case | Required observation |
|------+-------+----------------------|
| Target binding | Command names R1; acquisition or submitted mutation targets R2. | Admission or execution rejects the mismatch; no claimed R1 transition is established from R2's state. |
| Claim eligibility | Request is open but already has a helper. | New Claim cannot incorporate under the unclaimed policy. |
| Relationship | Membership belongs to another principal or Organization. | New Claim is not authorized by that Membership. |
| Concurrent authorization | Membership, Organization association, or relevant policy changes after preparation. | Incorporation obeys the declared current-condition policy. |
| Same-command concurrency | Two compatible attempts race. | At most one incorporation; the other resolves or remains unknown rather than inventing command rejection. |
| Different-command concurrency | Two helpers claim the same unclaimed Request. | Only transitions allowed by protected current state incorporate. |
| Replay | Original Claim succeeded, response was lost, Request is now claimed. | Compatible permitted replay returns the recorded outcome without a second effect. |
| Incompatible reuse | Same ID, changed canonical arguments or authority binding. | No overwrite or new incorporation under the old binding; disclosure follows policy. |
| Terminal rejection | A pending attempt races with rejection closure. | The protocol cannot settle rejection while permitting that command to incorporate later. |
| Unknown outcome | Submission may have committed; local response is lost. | Unknown remains until admitted evidence resolves it. |
| Disclosure | A former caller no longer has permission to read a recorded result. | No unauthorized result disclosure and no invented domain rejection. |
| Atomic footprint | Domain update succeeds but ledger/outbox write is removed or fails. | The promised combined transition cannot be reported established. |
| Submission fidelity | A checked fragment is followed by an extra write, changed option, altered target, or weakened guard. | The actual complete submission fails the relevant admission/correspondence check. |
| Return provenance | A copied success report or unrelated callback return is substituted. | It does not establish this invocation's outcome. |
| Frame | Unrelated fields change concurrently before a whole-document replacement. | The frame is preserved or the guarded transition fails. |
| Representation | Missing, nil, false, nested values, and field-name collisions are exercised. | Unsupported cases reject explicitly; supported cases preserve declared semantics. |
| Predicate protection | A row is inserted that defeats an absence, uniqueness, or aggregate condition. | The operation cannot incorporate using an inadequately protected pre-read. |
| Writer coverage | An external writer bypasses a required revision or invariant discipline. | The dependent premise is identified; local writer observation is not reported as universal coverage. |
| Capability lifetime | One capability is raced, reused, or retained beyond its admitted lifetime. | Its use policy is enforced independently of command deduplication. |
| Opaque code | A full-language callback uses an asserted effect contract. | Supported execution remains possible, with the assertion retained in the assurance report. |
| External intent | Provider evidence changes account, operation, amount, currency, or intent identity. | It cannot authorize the expected payment transition. |
| External uncertainty | Provider request times out and later succeeds. | No blind new-key retry; the effect remains recoverable under the provider contract. |
| Cancellation race | Authorization arrives after cancellation was requested. | The actual effect enters the declared void, compensation, or review path. |
| Scope | A newer Hotel or single-card observation is offered for a Flight or complete-board obligation. | The unrelated or narrower scope does not discharge the obligation. |
| Temporal context | Token bytes match but incarnation, valid time, query, or visibility context differs. | Compatibility is established explicitly or the substitution is rejected. |
| Acquisition failure | An empty-looking result actually represents failure, denial, or partial coverage. | No authoritative absence or completeness claim is created. |
| Adoption | Installation is allowed, then fails or targets a retired owner. | Acquire-and-adopt debt is not discharged by permission alone. |
| New advisory | O2 arrives while an acquisition for O1 is pending. | Completion preserves O2 unless its actual evidence and adoption cover O2. |
| Late response | P18 completes after compatible P21 was adopted. | No regression; independently fulfilled debt need not be retained. |
| Subscription race | A relevant change occurs between initial read and effective subscription. | The declared fence, replay, or recovery mechanism covers the gap, or the weaker guarantee is explicit. |
| Retention | A stale retry arrives after result eviction. | The namespace/horizon/tombstone policy prevents an unjustified duplicate effect. |
| Dependency change | Backend or adapter behavior changes beneath a retained proof claim. | Relevant qualification is invalidated or re-established; old evidence is not silently reused. |

The semantic cases should be derived from the same canonical definitions used
for construction wherever practical. Independent adversarial and mutation cases
then challenge the correspondence boundary rather than merely restating the
implementation. Backend integration cases exercise the actual qualified adapter
and its relevant transaction/read paths.

Test fixtures must distinguish admitted evidence from illustrative maps. A
fixture that creates an advancement witness by changing a token field does not
test provider truth. A fixture that returns a successful predicate without
performing installation does not test adoption. A diagnostic resolver that
finds a retained record does not demonstrate a durable recovery driver.

*** Completion criteria for this part

An implementation satisfies this part for an admitted operation family when its
public interface, canonical semantics, construction, physical submission,
backend profile, outcome interpretation, command policy, propagation, and
acquisition/adoption paths form one inspectable contract. Supported mechanisms
must reject mismatches early and explain the exact unsatisfied obligation.

Unsupported proof cases do not disappear. They are either implemented through
another supported mechanism, admitted with explicit accepted assertions under
policy, or rejected with a usable explanation. Dynamic unavailability has an
explicit continuation. Success means the claimed semantic effect and evidence
were established, not merely that the application started without errors.

An assertion cannot repair a missing operation binding, malformed schema,
unresolved capability, or contradictory effect declaration. It supplies a named
premise about otherwise well-formed behavior; structural admission obligations
still have to be discharged.

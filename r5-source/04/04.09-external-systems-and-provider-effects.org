** External systems and valid intermediate states

One database transaction cannot generally atomically commit a database change
and an arbitrary external provider effect. The model must represent valid
intermediate states and durable recovery rather than describing a remote API
call as if it were part of the database's atomicity guarantee.

The durable-workflow boundary of [[#req-durable-workflow-boundary][Part III]]
applies: a process-local choreography execution is not automatically a durable
owner of unfinished business work.

*** Worked example: placing an order with payment authorization

Suppose the business policy permits an Order to become placed once the provider
has authorized the specified payment. Capture and settlement are separate
effects unless the operation explicitly includes them. This example must not
call an authorization receipt proof of a completed charge.

Money is an amount in a declared currency, with a supported precision and
rounding rule. A bare floating-point number or unqualified decimal is not the
complete payment argument. The immutable payment intent binds the Order,
merchant/provider account, operation type, amount, currency, instrument
reference, and relevant policy/interface versions.

The local public entry operation is conceptually:

#+begin_src clojure
(order/place-intent!
 trusted-context
 {:command-id order-command-id
  :cart-id cart-id
  :payment-instrument-ref instrument-ref})
#+end_src

The authoritative model validates ownership and eligibility of the Cart and
instrument reference, computes or validates the authoritative price, and
protects the inputs required by its pricing and inventory policy. It atomically
creates a valid pending Order, records the command result, and records durable
payment work. The pending Order is a committed domain state, not an optimistic
browser fiction.

For this workflow, invoking the payment provider is a separately admitted
physical effect *after* the immutable intent and durable work have committed.
A callback that the local transaction mechanism may re-evaluate or retry must
not make that external call. Neither a queued job nor a submitted HTTP request
is provider acceptance, and the durable work's owner must survive its original
worker, process, and browser execution.

The Order command's success means that this intent was established. If the UI
wants to wait for final placement, choreography must distinguish intent
acceptance from business completion. It must not display a final placed result
merely because =place-intent!= committed.

*** Provider request identity and evidence

The durable payment intent has its own stable identity. Provider requests use an
idempotency key derived or allocated under the provider's actual key scope and
retention rules. The binding includes the merchant/account, provider operation,
and immutable request meaning. A globally formatted string does not by itself
establish global uniqueness or deduplication at the provider.

The qualification contract states whether repeated requests with that key are
deduplicated, how conflicting payloads are handled, how long records remain,
and how an indeterminate request can be resolved. Provider idempotency is an
external premise; Gesso cannot manufacture it by storing a local key.

If the provider does not supply a sufficient deduplication or lookup contract,
the application must use a recovery strategy appropriate to that limitation.
An unknown effect may require manual reconciliation. Blindly sending a fresh
key after a timeout can create a second charge or authorization.

Provider evidence must be authenticated or otherwise admitted by a qualified
adapter and bound to the exact payment intent. Its interpretation checks the
merchant/account, provider operation, Order or intent reference, key, amount,
currency, and result status. Receiving an untrusted webhook map with a familiar
status string is insufficient. Signature verification alone also does not prove
that the signed event refers to this intent or establishes the required effect.

The adapter must distinguish authorization, capture, decline, pending status,
and an indeterminate transport result. A provider lookup that finds no record
does not establish non-occurrence while an earlier request may still be in
flight, unless the provider contract supplies that stronger guarantee.

If provider deduplication records may expire while local work is unresolved,
reusing the original key does not retain an expired deduplication guarantee.
Automatic submission must stop or follow an independently qualified safe
recovery protocol; allocating a fresh key cannot silently discharge the old
unknown effect.

*** Incorporating a provider result

A worker resolves or submits the provider operation under the durable intent.
It then invokes a public model result-incorporation operation with the admitted
provider evidence. That operation protects the payment intent and current Order
state, records the accepted receipt, makes the permitted domain transition, and
updates durable work in the same local atomic boundary.

| Current local state and admitted evidence | Required treatment |
|------------------------------------------+--------------------|
| Payment pending; matching authorization succeeds. | Record authorization and advance according to the placement policy. |
| Payment pending; definitive matching decline. | Record the decline and move to the declared failure or alternative-payment state. |
| Payment pending; response lost or provider result unresolved. | Keep a truthful pending/unknown condition and retained recovery work. |
| Matching receipt already incorporated. | Return a compatible recorded result without applying the effect twice. |
| Receipt conflicts with immutable intent or prior evidence. | Reject or quarantine the evidence through a declared diagnostic/recovery path. |
| Cancellation requested; a late authorization succeeds. | Record the real external outcome and enter the declared void, compensation, or review path. |

*** Worked failure window: provider succeeded, local outcome unrecorded

In the preceding example, the following sequence is a permitted physical
history, not an atomic Order-placement transition:

1. The Order, bound payment intent, semantic command outcome, and payment-work
   obligation commit together. The Order is still =:payment-pending=.
2. A worker sends the admitted authorization request under the retained
   provider key. The provider authorizes the money, but the worker dies before
   the local result-incorporation operation commits; its HTTP reply may also
   disappear.
3. A successor recovers the same durable work and *first* reconciles the
   original provider intent by a qualified lookup or other admitted evidence.
   Verified authorization permits the guarded local incorporation; an
   inconclusive lookup leaves the intent unresolved, with its recovery owner
   intact. A different key is not an innocent retry.
4. A duplicate webhook or another worker's late result may race with this
   incorporation. Guarded, receipt-bound local transitions must prevent a
   second incompatible authoritative effect; they do not independently prove
   that the provider executed only one remote effect.

This history differs from a payment that never reached the provider and from
one whose local authorization receipt was already incorporated. The model and
its diagnostic result must not collapse the three cases merely because all
three can appear as a pending Order to a particular browser.

Workers may use leases or fencing to prevent stale local writes. Such mechanisms
do not retroactively stop a remote call already issued by an old worker. Late
evidence must still be handled according to the actual external effect and
current model state.

Cancellation therefore needs explicit states such as cancel requested, void
pending, refund pending, or review required when those distinctions matter. A
terminal cancelled label must not cause the system to discard evidence that
money was authorized or captured. Compensation is a new effect with its own
identity, uncertainty, and recovery obligations; it is not an atomic undo of
history.

*** Safety and progress for external work

The system can promise that no Order is reported placed without the required
admitted authorization evidence, under the provider and backend premises. It
cannot promise that every payment finishes merely because a worker exists.
Eventual resolution additionally depends on provider availability, usable
lookup/idempotency behavior, durable scheduling, and any required human action.

Required work must remain discoverable after process loss. Acknowledging a job
because an HTTP call was sent, rather than because the contracted work state
was durably incorporated, can lose the only recovery owner. Queue acknowledgment,
lease expiry, and model work completion require a declared relationship.
 
*** Paired external-effect acceptance obligations

| Admitted scenario | Adverse or unsupported counterpart | Required judgment |
|-------------------+------------------------------------+-------------------|
| The local pending intent and required work commit before the provider call. | A locally retryable transaction callback charges the provider, or the worker starts without durable intent. | Admit the durable boundary; reject the unsafe effect placement or retain a precisely named, unsupported premise. |
| A matching qualified provider authorization is incorporated under current Order guards. | An unsigned or mismatched webhook, wrong amount/currency, wrong merchant, or conflicting receipt claims success. | Admit the bound evidence; reject or quarantine the incompatible evidence without advancing the Order. |
| The provider authorized before the worker crashed; a retained-key lookup supplies conclusive evidence. | A lost response is treated as decline or automatically retried with a fresh provider key. | Reconcile the original intent; never manufacture rejection or a second authorization. |
| Compatible provider retries fall within its established key scope and retention contract. | Retention expires or deduplication/lookup semantics are unknown. | Use only qualified deduplication; otherwise retain unknown and the declared safe recovery or human-review path. |
| Duplicate compatible provider callbacks race to incorporate one result. | Conflicting, reordered, or late receipts target changed Order state. | Protect local incorporation and retain the real external history; local idempotence cannot imply remote exactly-once execution. |
| Cancellation and late provider success follow declared void/compensation policy. | Cancellation erases a real authorization or treats compensation as transaction rollback. | Admit the appropriate new transition and outstanding work; reject false historical erasure. |
| Durable work is discoverable after worker or node loss. | Queue acknowledgment precedes durable settlement and removes the only recovery owner. | Preserve replayable work or an equivalently qualified discovery guarantee. |
| Resolution is conditional on provider availability and admitted reconciliation. | The system asserts unconditional eventual completion with no provider or human-progress premise. | State the liveness assumptions; unsupported completion is not a successful check. |

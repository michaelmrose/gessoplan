# R5.1 Part IV — 04.14 R4 preservation and Gesso correspondence

**Section:** `r5-source/04/04.14-propagation-acquisition-and-basis.org`  
**Disposition:** fourteenth substantively reviewed candidate section.
This is an editorial examination of the finished-system specification
and its implementation assumptions, not a runtime qualification or
whole-Part-IV acceptance.

## R4 obligations preserved

R4 Part IV, particularly `Progression must flow through authoritative
propagation`, `Initial acquisition is part of a managed authoritative
projection`, `Observed basis remains a claim unless stronger evidence
is verified`, `Advancement alone does not make a command invalid`,
and `Dependency upgrades may not silently weaken consistency`, requires:

- Committed change identity and a qualified progression fence surviving
  through required invalidation and scoped reread, rather than treating
  invalidation as a snapshot of the new business contents.
- Initial knowledge or a first permitted acquisition path for a managed
  authoritative projection. SSE connectivity alone is insufficient.
- Strict separation of a browser's ObservedBasisClaim from a trusted
  AuthoritativeBasis, authenticated historical issuance receipt,
  operation capability and current authorization.
- Typed, resource-bounded handling for malformed, incompatible,
  unsupported or unsatisfiable requested consistency.
- No generic stale-database-position rejection of an otherwise
  business-valid operation.
- Backend and HTTP/Biff/XTDB regression evidence for snapshot/token,
  required-progress read, commit, publication, reread and failure
  semantics across dependency upgrades.

R4's `Advisory knowledge and authoritative knowledge remain separate`
and detailed O1/O2 in-flight discharge sequence were already
preserved by 04.13, while 04.15 owns the adoption/debt milestone
contract. This review does not duplicate those mechanisms or
copy R4's illustrative functions into required production APIs.

## Stronger R5 architecture retained

R5 describes a direct propagation chain from qualified authoritative
outcome, recoverable publication obligation, advisory delivery, reread
and admitted scope-covered visibility. It supports token-bearing and
tokenless wakeups, provider-specific fences, initial read/subscription
handshake alternatives, explicit reconnect/owner replacement,
conditional periodic refresh, and typed backend migration.

The specification does not prescribe a particular durable queue,
router topology, two-router replication rule, per-recipient broadcast
acknowledgement ledger or implementation language. Single-node and
multi-node realizations preserve the same application semantics;
redundant safe invalidations may be redelivered as an optimization of
recovery, not as a substitute for scope/basis correctness.

## Focused normative edits

1. Permit a durable record or equally qualified recovery/acquisition
   source for required change publication rather than prescribing one
   universal outbox. Name the process-loss window and recovery owner.
2. Separate durable change debt, local enqueue, SSE send, participant
   receipt, authorized reread and installed/adopted observation.
3. Clarify that tokenless advisories still need a provider-qualified
   fence or a declared weaker guarantee; redundant redelivery is safe.
4. Explicitly reject managed targets with neither admitted initial
   projection nor a first acquisition path.
5. Cover interest-discovery, read/subscription and router-failover gaps
   with a compact P17-read/P18-change/late-interest counterexample.
6. Separate signed historical observation issuance from installation
   and current authorization; retain bounded, typed consistency claims.
7. Add nine positive/adverse cases for publication, token provenance,
   tokenless fences, bootstrap, missed subscriptions, router failover,
   reconnect, observation receipts and token failure classification.

## Current Gesso application-assurance v1622 correspondence

Inspected `gesso.live.invalidation`: it expands app-declared primary
changes and carries authoritative progression metadata into expanded
invalidations. It does not itself inspect DB writes, maintain durable
publication work, or establish cross-node interest completeness.

Inspected `gesso.live.core` and `gesso.live.transport.sse`: the Live
facade orchestrates invalidation, hot broadcast, client filtering and
SSE framing. The SSE transport does not establish durable replay,
authoritative read scope or DOM installation.

Inspected `gesso.live.acquisition-preflight`: a managed path relates
the compiled Live graph to declared fragment GET and stream routes.
Its own docs state that the checker does not inspect arbitrary
installed Ring/Biff/Reitit handler bodies, so its successful assembly
does not prove physical routing, initial gap closure or cross-node
delivery.

Inspected `gesso.live.consistency.xtdb`: `bind-request-frontier`
derives a trusted latest-completed XTDB frontier, composes the minimum
with existing requirements and refuses an incompatible stale binding.
This is valuable request-local read consistency machinery, not a
universal interest registration fence, multi-node subscription proof,
or a trusted browser-reported prior installation.

Inspected `gesso.live.browser.adapter`: current fragment refresh
state distinguishes active and independently queued requirements,
with owner/generation checks, but that alone cannot prove no change
was lost before interest registration or across a dead router.
Physical delivery, subscribed-interest history, recovery and
actual installation require further source-bound evidence.

No Live runtime suite, multi-node failure test, simulated gap replay,
XTDB test, formal model checker or browser installation test was
executed for this document revision.

## Cross-part joins and future proof obligations

- **Parts I–II:** trust provenance, scoped knowledge, valid basis and
  source-owned authorization.
- **Part III:** interest-safe communication and role-local knowledge
  admitted only through declared sources.
- **Part IV 04.11–04.13:** trusted backend basis, exact acquisition
  coverage, conservative pending obligation retention.
- **Part IV 04.15–04.17:** installation/adoption milestones,
  selective Refresh and assurance evidence.
- **Parts V–VI:** actual browser ownership, initial bootstrap,
  physical swap completion and reconnect lifecycle.
- **Part VII:** deployed writer, query, route, listener, worker,
  interest-discovery and publication closure.
- **Part VIII:** HumanHelp newly created Request visibility and
  per-participant selective adoption.
- **Parts IX–X:** proof of gap-free or explicitly weaker progress
  under declared failure/fairness premises and physical refinement.
- **Parts XI–XII:** paired positive/negative and injected-failure
  acceptance checks for initial registration and host failure.

This audit does not claim a working multi-node router, universal
exactly-once notification, or all-client automatic convergence.

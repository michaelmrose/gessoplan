## R4 requirements preserved

R4 Part IV's `Advisory obligations are scoped and discharged by coverage`
and `Advisory knowledge and authoritative knowledge remain separate`
require:
- A Live advisory can establish that newer relevant authority exists
  without itself establishing the changed business contents or visible
  adopted state.
- Debt is scoped to the actual query and required progression; a recent
  Request card does not discharge complete board membership.
- An admitted read discharges only requirements whose actual source,
  scope, coverage and frontier it satisfies. HTTP completion, an
  echoed minimum token or a refreshed-looking label is not sufficient.
- An O2 advisory that arrives during an O1 acquisition survives
  completion unless the actual admitted observation satisfies both.
- A read satisfying acquire-only debt does not thereby fulfill
  acquire-and-adopt debt; failed or denied installation leaves the
  latter unresolved.
- An older late response cannot replace a compatible newer adopted
  view merely because it finished later. Retirement/reconciliation
  must preserve independent obligations and current target rights.
- HumanHelp may show advisory glow/toast without silently adopting
  newly authorized aggregate membership before the user's Refresh.

The fuller P17/P18/P20/P21 O1/O2 timeline is already covered in 04.13
and 04.16. This section focuses on the completion/adoption boundary,
not duplicating the coordinator's entire worked example.

## Stronger R5 architecture retained

R5 introduces four distinct milestones: admitted acquisition,
installation permission, physical completion and semantic adoption.
It requires current owner and target-instance rights at the final
mutation boundary and supports equivalent serialization against races.
It explicitly handles partial physical mutation, missing completion,
unknown presentation state and separately admitted recovery.

This is a semantic distinction, not a mandate to introduce four public
APIs, a universal DOM receipt, one installer implementation or a new
storage layer. One synchronous trusted boundary may discharge several
milestones when its actual guarantees justify doing so.

## Focused normative changes

1. Prevent cross-principal/query/owner debt merging solely on basis
   equality or a global dirty flag.
2. Require qualified installation-completion evidence rather than
   counting HTTP success or an allow-swap as completion; require
   a current-state adoption transition for its declared scope.
3. Strengthen owner/target/request/candidate correlation, including
   stale/duplicate callbacks and replacement executions.
4. Settle the current obligation set after installation; do not
   erase O2 using a captured pre-O2 snapshot, or retain already
   fulfilled O1 debt without justification.
5. Make partial DOM changes and lost callbacks first-class unknown
   physical outcomes requiring declared reconciliation/reacquisition.
6. Add the E1/T1 -> E2/T2 worked replacement example.
7. Add ten paired valid/adverse acceptance families involving debt,
   acquisition-only, swap permission, completion authenticity,
   newer advisory, monotonicity, retirement, partial failure,
   lost completion and HumanHelp selective adoption.

## Current Gesso application-assurance v1622 correspondence

Inspected `gesso.live.browser.adapter`: managed fragment
before-swap admission checks current request identity and a
provider-witnessed monotonicity rule. It emits
`:htmx/allow-swap` without advancing the authoritative
installed-basis state. A later correlated `:htmx/after-swap`
transition advances the pure adapter's installed frontier and
emits `:authoritative/installed`. This is the important
implemented distinction between permission and observed completion.

Inspected `gesso.live.browser.core`: HTMX `beforeSwap` and
`afterSwap` are normalized from exact active-request records and
dispatched through the shell. The after-swap event is a trusted
physical-boundary assumption to the pure adapter; adapter transition
alone does not independently verify the DOM tree or rule out
arbitrary external mutations of it.

Inspected `gesso.live.browser.observation`: it classifies emitted
`:authoritative/installed` separately from provisional
presentation and HTMX decisions. That does not itself prove the
source-bound browser host performed every physical effect under
all target-replacement and missing-callback races.

Inspected `test/gesso/live/browser/adapter_test.cljc`: it contains
cases requiring after-swap to establish the installed frontier and
rejecting backward installation. These test sources were inspected,
not executed. They do not independently establish a complete
application-specific debt/adoption semantics for arbitrary HumanHelp
board query scope.

The completed-system contract therefore requires further physical
installation correspondence, complete owner/target evidence, and
qualified recovery for partially applied/lost-completion effects.
That is distinct from existing adapter-level recognition.

## Independent editorial conclusion

Keep R5's material improvement over the original chronology: the
checker/kernel and public semantics are developed before complete
source-derived cross-namespace composition. But R4's ambitious
certificate hierarchy survives as *real exported obligations* across
schema, domain, relationship, rules, operation, Choreo, projection,
executable plan and whole application, not as a requirement that
every component use a certificate-shaped map or compatible namespace
name. An independently generated original-source requirement manifest
must prevent pruning inconvenient dependencies; a closed submitted
DAG is not sufficient.

Crucially, the issuer may check a derivation only over an exact
original abstract subject. Its checked proposition and open premises
do not automatically establish source completeness, independent
checker soundness, actual installed writers, current actor permission
or full HumanHelp business correctness. Conditional induction over
all declared operations is distinct from ensuring the declarative
catalog covers real Unclaim/Reassign/administrative mutations.
Discharging P's premise Q needs an independently accepted proof of
exact Q in the right subject/time/authority/provider context;
a copied report or another claim's green status never suffices.

The *real Claim pilot* has three genuinely separate conclusions:
abstract semantic checking of the original public Request and
RequestAssignment relationships, qualified production binding
of that exact source to installed host/XTDB/browser behavior,
and independent product acceptance of a live eligible Claim
control. One success cannot substitute for the other two.
Full product completion remains separate, with independently
required Create and all six lifecycle interactions, Response
selection, search/Refresh/recovery and fourteen Part VIII gates.

Stage 14 is an executable adversarial specification, not one
percentage of rejected mutants. A valid positive baseline,
one injected change, predeclared first gate and result class,
independent observation of forbidden issuance/effects and
the test instrument's actual scope are essential. A successful
failure at a downstream substitute gate is not evidence
the intended before-effect guard worked. Neither a controlled
fault detected too late nor an absent log line constitutes
proof that a prohibited physical effect was prevented.
Wrong-but-well-formed policy is accepted or reviewed by its
product owner; heuristic suspicion cannot become a kernel
verdict.

## Gesso v1622 actual source correspondence

This review inspected concrete source/test namespaces on the pinned
`application-assurance` branch. They establish meaningful but bounded
precursors to the finished specification:

- `src/gesso/assurance/dependency.clj`: strict structural graph of
  admitted subject identities, expected fingerprints, typed edges,
  root/reachability and DAG coherence. Its documentation explicitly
  says this is *structural only*, not independent proof of required
  original-source dependency completeness or installed route/writer
  coverage.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/obligation.clj`: generates
  *unadmitted* initial/invariant-preservation obligations over an
  original typed guarded transition. Initial-state existence,
  completeness of the invariant set, projection coverage and checker/
  physical soundness remain explicit open assumptions.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/operation_set.clj`: checks a closed
  *declared* original operation catalog and shared invariant/initial
  source. It does not discover all actual physical writers or
  independently establish source-completeness.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/certificate.clj`: source-relative
  checker-backed, process-local handles for propositional,
  guarded-transition and conditional-induction proof kinds.
  Requires current graph/source rechecks and exact open premise
  accounting; it does not prove independent kernel metatheory,
  current runtime authorization or XTDB/browser refinement.
- `src/gesso/assurance/report.clj`: source-bound explanatory root
  output with caller-reported observations. Its implementation
  explicitly refuses to treat reports as issuer or authentic
  test-runner certificates; root green fields cannot establish
  deployed Biff/XTDB correspondence.
- `src/gesso/assurance/diagnostic.clj`: stable first-blocker
  diagnostic output derived from original sources, explicitly
  *not* an admission gate; observed correspondence and finite
  model results remain scoped and cannot be promoted to proof
  of physical production correctness.
- `test/gesso/assurance/proof/certificate_test.clj`:
  adversarial regressions include independently checked
  expected reason families and paired positives.
  These actual test sources are narrower than the required
  generic whole-application mutation corpus; their presence
  is not a statement that the tests ran in this review.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight.clj` (previous
  source comparison in Parts VII/12.06) supplies scoped
  application assembly and canonical rendered-surface
  admission, but neither actual deployed handler adoption
  nor all physical writer discovery follows automatically.
- `src/gesso/model/command_resolution.clj` (12.04/12.07
  audit baseline) constructs an exact compatible original
  command/result record under trusted plan/disclosure
  assumptions; it does not prove the HumanHelp production
  Claim path through the actual browser and XTDB writer.

These source names and their stated limitations are concrete.
No tool run in this editorial pass executed the JVM tests, real
XTDB transactions, browser journeys, model checker, external
formal proof or independent cross-host faults. The existence
of synthetic proof examples does not establish the real
Request/RequestAssignment compositional pilot.

** Cross-stage acceptance invariants

Gates A–H apply from the *first boundary at which their subjects are
constructed, interpreted, installed or used*. They are not a final
ceremonial release checklist, nor a fresh, competing policy layer.
The owning Parts and original source define their full semantics.
Each gate names: the original admitted operation or requirement,
the boundary that can first know/enforce it, the permitted positive
behavior, an independently falsifying neighbor, the actual checker
or physical observation, and any residual premise. Completing
a generated graph does not prove that its original obligation
denominator, deployed writer or independent product inventory
was complete.

Gates A–H therefore connect the permanent executable semantic
core to its selected implementation, runtime and evidence
boundaries. Gates I–P additionally govern admission by
representation, product completeness, outcome knowledge,
adoption rights, proof/evidence scope, source audit, formal
transfer and human intent review; the two groups must be
jointly satisfied for a release profile that requires them.

*** Gate A -- No silent semantic downgrade

A public semantic operation retains its original model-owned
command, required inputs, role/authority, typed results, recovery
and effect contract through view construction, Choreo projection,
generated plan, route, browser event and trusted invocation.
A missing or incompatible binding is rejected at the first
knowable boundary or the affordance is explicitly unavailable.
It may not become an unmarked ordinary HTTP/HTMX request,
anonymous route, browser-only imitation or direct backend
mutation simply because the managed metadata is absent.

The source-derived application and generated artifact must
distinguish deliberately ordinary, separately declared HTMX
from a semantic operation whose managed origin was stripped.
A valid ordinary form remains permitted under its own
narrower guarantee, but cannot claim a managed operation's
assurance or authorization. An explicitly unavailable
managed affordance may be structurally sound while still
failing an independent positive product gate requiring
an actual usable Claim/Reassign action.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* a genuine enabled authorized
Claim reaches the matching trusted route and model;
removing its =:choreo/op= or plan/route binding does
not preserve a working anonymous Claim. Check the
actual generated/rendered surface and installed route,
not only a local "unavailable" report.

*** Gate B -- One connected semantic subject

Runtime, tests, source proof/finite-check subjects,
global Choreo, role-local projection, executable plans,
issuer results, generated assets and their actual
installed consumers derive from *one original admitted
semantic meaning*, or have a specifically checked,
direction-sensitive representation/refinement relation.
The subject need not have identical data layouts in
every representation. It must not change business
guards, scope, actors, command identity or allowed
outcomes without preserving a justified relation.

The permanent executable semantic core is not
replaced by a proof kernel or one-off test reference
machine. A seven-rule propositional checker, guarded
transition checker, independent formal backend,
browser adapter and physical XTDB writer own
different propositions. Nonsemantic sidecars
may explain their source correspondence but
must not change runtime execution or issue
permission. A valid abstract Claim theorem
is useful under its premises without
proving that the deployed handler performs
the same guarded transaction.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* original
Request/RequestAssignment Claim with current
Membership runs through the supported plan;
a proof-only model using invented
=:request/helper-id= or omitting Reassign
is refused as a full-subject correspondence
even if its reduced model checks internally.

*** Gate C -- Source ownership stays single

Public model/core interfaces own Request,
RequestAssignment, Membership, Organization
and other domain business semantics. Choreo
owns participant interaction and justified
knowledge; application construction
joins the public semantics to actual plans,
routes, effects and protected acquisition.
Proof obligations, operation summaries,
policy audit, code generation, diagnostics
and tests derive from the *same* original
declarations rather than maintaining
plausibly synchronized business copies.
The browser and router are not alternative
owners of Claim or remote read permission.

Ordinary application Clojure may express
genuinely complex domain policies. Where
a correctness-relevant host function
cannot be checked in the supported closed
rule language, its original owner supplies
the typed/effect/dependency contract,
selected trusted boundary and an honestly
open conformance premise. An empty
source-audit catalog, a smaller proof DAG
or an opaque implementation body cannot
be relabeled complete mechanical
inspection. HumanHelp-specific rules
may occur in the HumanHelp product model,
not be hard-coded into generic Gesso.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* a changed
Organization Claim policy changes its
source-owned affected judgments and
dependent execution/assurance; a
hand-maintained "helper eligible"
predicate in the browser/prover/router
that disagrees with the model is rejected
or recorded as a source-conformance gap,
not accepted because both names match.

*** Gate D -- Authority remains at the trusted boundary

A browser-supplied role, principal,
request/tenant identifier, plan, certificate,
basis, correlation ID, DOM field or
=already-authorized= flag grants *no*
protected authority. Trusted ingress
binds the authenticated actor and exact
operation/target; a real authoritative
model/read boundary checks current
Organization/Membership, scope,
disclosure and operation policy at its
applicable use. Each protected write
must guard changing conditions at
incorporation, and each protected read,
subscription or historical command
resolution must independently establish
current permitted disclosure.

A source-checked proof is not a runtime
authorization token. A legal HTML
affordance is not protection against
a manually constructed hostile request.
An old accepted membership observation
does not continue authorizing operations
after a relevant revocation. An opaque
provider progression witness may
justify the *scope of an observation*
when its authority was independently
qualified, but cannot manufacture
the actor's right to perform a read
or adopt a remote collection.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* authenticated
eligible Alice Claims R using the
current trusted model; a forged
client helper role or revoked
Membership fails at the protected
model/transaction boundary even
when the previously served UI
still displays Claim. Test
the direct hostile route, not
only the disappearing button.

*** Gate E -- Exact artifact and implementation binding

Where a release or proof claim depends
on exact correspondence, preserve the
subject and immutable interpretation
through source, issued plan/callable,
generated manifest, emitted bytes,
served bytes, actual client-loaded
bootstrap, selected handler, live
provider and installed generation.
A matching title, timestamp, route
name, local receipt, copied hash or
source similarity is not an
authenticated production observation.

A build receipt may bind the approved
manifest to locally emitted JavaScript
bytes; it does not prove a server
served those bytes, a browser loaded
them, or a later substituted Biff
handler used the source-checked route.
Actual installation/activation and
user-facing entrypoint evidence
remain separate. Exact byte identity
and checked semantic compatibility
are different relations; A-to-B
old/new compatibility does not
imply B-to-A. Changed implementation
invalidates affected physical
applicability without necessarily
invalidating an unaffected
mathematical theorem.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* a
qualified browser build loads its
actual current plan and the
server invokes the matching
checked handler; a stale CDN
asset, forged manifest, post-
preflight Ring replacement or
changed XTDB adapter cannot
reuse a green candidate report.

*** Gate F -- Dynamic premises remain dynamic

Static preflight rejects *structurally
known* missing, incompatible or
unsupported realization and requires
the actual declared handling/recovery
path for a relevant dynamic outcome,
as owned by
[[#req-preflight-dynamic-handling-coverage]].
It cannot guarantee future
Membership, Request state, network
delivery, scheduler fairness,
transaction prestate, principal
revocation or resource lifetime.
Declared handling means a real
source-derived continuation with
owner, trigger, required knowledge,
permitted effects and terminal or
explicitly unresolved outcome;
a boolean =:recovery-exists?=
or copied success report is not
structural coverage.

The trusted invocation, guarded
incorporation, live acquisition,
browser last-effect mutation
and historical command resolver
enforce actual current facts at
their respective boundaries.
An aborted HTTP attempt after
possible C42 submission does not
prove C42 rejected; missing
history or unknown outcome must
retain its uncertainty. A
lost Live advisory must not
make manual authorized
acquisition impossible; an
advisory alone grants no
automatic user Refresh.
Safety can hold when optional
liveness/fairness assumptions
do not.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* current
eligible Claim and its admitted
unknown-result resolution work;
a fake recovery branch hides
missing issuer/read authority,
or preflight claims that
the future provider succeeds.
Reject the missing structural
contract early; after an
actual possible submission,
retain precisely scoped
=:commit-unknown= rather than
invented rejection.

*** Gate G -- Real physical integration remains required

Portable semantic/projection checks,
finite exploration, source inspection
and proof checking cannot by themselves
establish actual Ring/Biff handler
adoption, XTDB guarded transaction,
transaction-internal prestate,
complete physical writers, current
principal, real Chromium/HTMX
request/swap/OOB installation or
cross-host fault recovery. A
release or completion claim
records exactly which *physical*
subjects, versions, sessions,
entrypoints, bytes, witnesses
and fault windows were exercised,
with untested or trusted premises
retained.

A required check that is failed,
skipped, missing, stale,
unavailable, unsupported or
incomplete does not pass.
A successful *negative* test
must reach the intended real
enforcing boundary and
independently observe that the
forbidden write/disclosure/DOM
mutation did not occur within
the stated observation scope.
A different earlier exception
or a post-write diagnostic
cannot count as prevention.
Every applicable negative
has a nonvacuous eligible
positive through the
same installed path. If
the requested assurance
requires an independent
external proof rather than
bounded test evidence,
a physical test cannot
substitute for that proof.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* real
authenticated Claim succeeds,
survives reload and is
visible through a permitted
installed browser; competing
physical Claim, missing FX
clock, stale served artifact
and actual OOB partial swap
have distinct appropriately
typed results. A successful
mocked model-only browser
fixture cannot certify the
installed production path.

*** Gate H -- Reject contradictions when they become knowable

A contradiction is refused at
the *first boundary with
sufficient information and
legitimate authority* to
recognize and enforce it.
A malformed typed operation,
unrealizable Choreo knowledge,
missing route/FX capability,
incompatible plan/asset or
required recovery contract
should fail construction or
pre-installation checks,
before the associated
managed effect is exposed.
A malformed rendered
Hiccup/HTMX affordance
fails at the supported
real pre-serialization
boundary; an invalid
invocation fails at
trusted ingress; a stale
or unauthorized guarded
transaction fails before
incorporation; an expired
browser callback fails
before its physical DOM
mutation.

A static checker must not
pretend to know future
permissions, race outcome,
physical network state or
target incarnation. Nor
may a late diagnostic
describe itself as
preventing an already
incorporated command or
previously disclosed
response. A failure
after an authoritative
commit may block HTML
installation, create
publication/recovery debt
or leave client knowledge
unknown, but cannot
retroactively turn the
committed model
transition into rejected
truth. The observed first
blocker must not be a
cascading =:missing-plan=
error when the original
cause was an earlier
invalid rule or lost
permission.

*Positive/adverse oracle:* one
genuine valid Claim reaches
the appropriate physical
model/DOM boundaries;
missing =:biff.fx/now=
is detected before the
required invocation can
issue a protected effect,
revoked Membership is
rejected at current
incorporation, an invalid
render blocks actual
response delivery, and
a retired callback is
stopped at its last
mutating effect.
If the discovered bug is
only detectable after an
effect, report late
detection and surviving
uncertainty rather than
a false prevention claim.

The gates apply to every
supported entrypoint and
effect path within the
selected guarantee. A
narrow declared legacy
HTMX path may remain
valid, but cannot inherit
stronger managed proof,
installation, policy or
runtime claims without
the corresponding checks.
Their report preserves
which original source,
installed generation,
profile, eligible
positive, adverse case,
actual observer and
open obligations justify
each successful gate.

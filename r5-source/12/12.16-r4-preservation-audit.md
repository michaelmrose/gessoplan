## Main finding

The previous R5 closing section was
a good compressed conceptual summary.
Its weakness, relative to the complete
R4 final principle and now-reviewed
R5.1 Parts I–XI, was omission of
several decisive *qualifications*
that distinguish Gesso from a generic
model/typed-RPC framework.

The revised final keeps all four
R5 headings and avoids a second
business logic specification.
It restores the R4 affirmative
distinctions: knowledge from multiple
admitted sources rather than simply
communication; declarative HTMX
ownership of its lifecycle rather
than mandatory replacement/custom
extension; a narrow pure browser
adapter and physically qualified
effect shell; actual trusted actor
and protected XTDB transactions;
Clojure and XTDB as deliberate
implementation choices rather than
speculative universal portability;
immediate effect right retirement
rather than cleanup-based permission;
a source-owned inspectable policy
surface with honest opacity; and
the strict separation of formal
proof, finite checking and
physical testing.

The reviewed implementation
stages strengthened these R4
ideas substantially: independent
complete capability and writer
denominators; RequestAssignment
rather than fictitious scalar
ownership; C42 historical identity
after lost response; provider-opaque
and source-scoped authority bases;
explicit one-use HumanHelp remote
Refresh; durable outbox *without*
assuming the router already knows
all future interests; initial
registration fence/replay/gap
recovery; four different release
decisions; and independent S0–S4
soundness/production transfer.
The final invariant adopts those
stronger accepted contracts while
keeping it clear that they are
normative design requirements, not
claims that current Gesso has
already implemented or proved
all of them.

The restored final quotation
provides one accessible top-level
philosophy, without replacing
the precise earlier contracts or
inventing an additional
certificate hierarchy. The
section closes with independently
usable application correctness,
honest incomplete premises and
nonvacuous positive evidence
rather than a simple
“everything is formally proved”
slogan.

## Concrete Gesso v1622 source correspondence

This audit compared *actual* code at the pinned
`application-assurance` source baseline.
These names support the design's
implementation trajectory but do not
constitute a framework-completeness
certificate or a runtime test.

- `src/gesso/model/core.clj`
  compiles Malli entity descriptors,
  repeated Graph lookups/projection
  and simple single-document Biff FX.
  Its own source explicitly leaves
  complex Graph relationships and
  complex domain FX operations to
  public model/application code;
  real HumanHelp Claim is not
  proved by generic generated
  single-document plumbing.
- `src/gesso/choreo/project.cljc`
  projects one verified global
  choreography into an admitted
  role-local plan using a small
  actual operation/communication/
  await/branch vocabulary.
  This is meaningful one-machine
  structure; actual browser/
  backend refinement is not
  automatic proof from a
  projected plan name.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc`
  implements a pure
  Clojure/ClojureScript
  =AdapterState × NormalizedEvent →
  AdapterState × AbstractEffects=
  machine, with no DOM,
  EventSource, timers, XHR
  or actual HTMX host APIs
  inside that pure boundary.
  Its own contract assigns
  physical effects to the
  imperative browser shell,
  so source alone does not
  attest actual browser
  installation/adoption.
- `src/gesso/live/progression.cljc`
  treats source positions as
  opaque orderless bases,
  with trusted provider evidence
  needed for comparisons.
  It checks the wire shape,
  not its external truth;
  a newer-looking string
  cannot grant Refresh
  coverage or permission.
- `src/gesso/live/core.clj`
  is a thin orchestration
  facade over invalidation,
  SSE, per-client flow,
  fragment protection,
  consistency and
  optimistic state.
  This does not by
  itself implement the
  strong distributed
  outbox, router
  interest-fencing
  or reliable multi-host
  guarantee described
  normatively.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight.clj`
  constructs source-bound
  operation/route/browser/
  publication/acquisition
  preflight and canonical
  dynamic Hiccup
  enforcement, but expressly
  cannot prove that arbitrary
  deployed servers adopted
  the checked Biff handler,
  that all external writers
  were found, or that
  pre-serialized alternate
  Ring responses are checked.
- `src/gesso/assurance/report.clj`
  builds an original-source-
  linked conditional
  evidence ledger,
  not an issuer,
  authenticated test
  runner, production
  deployment certificate
  or independent
  metatheorem. Its
  own =:proved=
  wording is
  explicitly limited
  to a checked
  conditional
  abstract derivation.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/physical_writer_inventory.clj`
  (source inspected in
  prior 12.15 audit)
  supplies a bounded
  host-observed
  diagnostic of
  potentially protected
  XTDB writer paths,
  explicitly not
  proving full host/
  external writer
  enumeration or
  universal physical
  correspondence.

The source evidence
does not establish
actual fully qualified

HTMX paths,

No JVM, browser,

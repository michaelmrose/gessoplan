** Worked Claim path: one meaning through the stages

Follow *one* HumanHelp Claim source through construction, actual execution,
recovery and increasingly strong evidence. This is an acceptance contract and
illustrative failure history, not a collection of eighteen differently
implemented Claim applications and not an invented Gesso API. R4's sketches
of calls named =admit=, =issue=, =prepare= or =check= were conceptual
checking boundaries, not guarantees that those exact callable functions
exist. The description here names the source, proposition, authority and
physical observation that each real supported implementation must establish.

Assume an authenticated, currently eligible helper *Alice* claims an
existing open Request R from its permitted Organization. The original
authoritative model owns Request, Membership, Organization and the separate
RequestAssignment relationship. The active primary assignment is **not**
a persisted =:request/helper-id= invented for a convenient demonstration.
*Bob* is another authenticated user with a separately authorized board
and the selected remote-advisory-only presentation policy. Alice's
Claim intent is logical command *C42*; it may have multiple actual HTTP/
server/transaction attempts. Its operation, request target, arguments and
original principal binding must remain immutable and compatible through
recovery. The names are narrative identifiers, not prescribed string,
UUID or keyword encodings.

*** One original operation, several identities and evidence subjects

The static public Claim declaration records the original subject,
canonical arguments, business policy/guards, expected input readers,
protected relationships, guarded effects, results, permitted disclosure
and recovery. It has **no particular actor, command ID, execution ID,
transport attempt, source read basis or browser resource** yet.
Its generated Choreo and view attach an affordance to this same model
operation without becoming a second implementation of Claim.

The following coordinates remain distinct throughout the trace:

| Coordinate | What it identifies | What it cannot grant or prove |
|------------+--------------------+------------------------------|
| Public =:request/claim= operation and model source | Original business transition, authoritatively owned policy and versioned dependencies | Alice's current Membership or one transaction's outcome |
| Request R and RequestAssignment aggregate | Target and protected one-primary/relationship history | That C42 was incorporated merely because R is now claimed |
| Authenticated Alice and target Organization | Actual trusted principal/relationship for one permissible disclosure or effect | Eternal authority after session, Membership or Organization changes |
| Logical C42 and canonical binding | Exactly one intended model command across compatible attempts | Authentication, commit, result disclosure or safe new submission by name alone |
| HTTP request/transport attempt and host A or C | A particular delivery, connection or serving process | Identity of C42, all other attempt results or domain truth |
| Execution and one-shot physical submission | One admitted attempt, actual plan, provider and bounded effect permission | Universal transaction-internal prestate or durable command history from a local receipt |
| Browser plan, target/resource instance and effect generation | Exact installed and currently permitted local behavior | A new command, server authorization or right to mutate a recycled DOM target |
| Assembly, browser artifact and release generation | Checked source-to-plan/route/build/host interpretation | Actual listener adoption, loaded bytes or old/new compatibility without physical qualification |
| Provider basis/qualified read and original command record | Scoped authoritative observation or C42-specific historical evidence, respectively | An orderable integer frontier or equality of current card and old command outcome |
| Proof, issuer, test and physical observation identities | Evidence of exactly their checked propositions and environments | An arbitrary stronger theorem, deployment credential or product completion |

The browser may transmit C42, a route target and protocol data;
an actor label, client-provided basis, certificate-shaped map or
displayed Claim button is never trusted identity or authority.
Trusted server admission reconstructs the authenticated principal,
the selected original operation, canonical target, clock, required
current model/Graph inputs, permitted disclosure, execution context
and applicable handler/provider profile. Structural admission
still cannot promise that Alice's Membership or R's open status
will remain true at actual incorporation.

*** Stage-by-stage construction remains one trace

The table names the minimum meaningful *same-source*
construction/evidence join. Later stages strengthen the
scope instead of reimplementing the business transition.

| Stage | Same Claim subject and added work | Exact boundary or open premise |
|-------+-----------------------------------+--------------------------------|
| 1 — semantic ownership | Original HumanHelp Request/RequestAssignment model, Alice/R/Organization policy and independently required product duties | Public model owns semantics; original source adequacy and physical realization remain separate |
| 2 — typed rules | Source-derived supported guard/read/nominal/absence interpretation and explicitly opaque Clojure policy dependencies | Correct typing is not current actor authorization; unknown/unsupported expressions are not true |
| 3 — small proof mechanisms | Original Claim abstract guarded transition, initial/invariant obligations and eligible proof candidate | Seven-rule or induction checking is conditional on exact catalog, premises and checker soundness |
| 4 — qualified host | Actual selected Biff/Ring/FX/XTDB invocation, lifecycle, clock/principal and trusted effect context | Startup order, real installed handler, protected writers and unsafe early initializer effects require qualification |
| 5 — authoritative operation | Static Claim, immutable C42 binding, resolve-before-new-incorporation and current business guard/effect specification | Server pre-read does not prove transaction-current Membership, cardinality or old command history |
| 6 — physical mediation | Complete guarded multi-record transaction, command-result and selected durable notice intent through actual writer | Internal prestate, presence/frame, uniqueness, ABA, real contention and all relevant writer paths remain physical duties |
| 7 — global Choreo | Alice/authority roles, required knowledge, authoritative action, causal result/recovery, projected role-local plans | Browser does not know global truth or gain server authority from a role label |
| 8 — browser realization | Pure adapter, generated plan, exact target/effect rights, real HTMX/DOM install and optional optimism | Provisional display, HTTP 200 and before-swap are not authoritative adoption or physical completion |
| 9 — Live/adoption | Source-derived change/debt, selected durable dispatch, qualified read basis and explicit user-granted remote acquisition | Advisory delivery and router interest are neither protected read permission nor Bob's board adoption |
| 10 — application closure | Original source-to-operation/plan/route/model/handler/asset/acquisition graph and first physical preflight | Green assembly is not actual deployment or an independent full product/writer inventory |
| 11 — release | Candidate, installation, activation, compatibility and evidence/currentness of the exact served generation | Old asset, old report or A-to-B compatibility does not automatically cover B-to-A or current effects |
| 12 — HumanHelp product | Actual enabled Claim, all other lifecycle operations, rich board, Response selection, selective Refresh and product gates | A successful Claim slice is not full HumanHelp completion |
| 13 — compositional pilot | Source-complete Claim obligations and three separate semantic, installed and real-browser conclusions | Reduced operation/proof DAG, copied certificate or real card cannot establish omitted obligations |
| 14 — mutation | Positive Claim plus competing, revoked, lost-response, stale-writer, stale-browser and false-evidence mutations | Defect must be refused at first qualified enforcing boundary before forbidden effect, with independent observation |
| 15 — explanation | Source-relative first blocker, open physical premise and actual/illustrative counterexample provenance | An explanation or advisory product-intent suggestion is neither proof issuance nor a policy edit |
| 16 — independent kernel | Formal S0/S1 seven-rule soundness under named axioms, proof tool and assumptions | Does not establish actual JVM checker S2 or typed original model atoms S3 |
| 17 — formal transfer | S2/S3 and S4 issuance/source-to-physical links, concrete-to-abstract execution refinement when selected | A theorem, finite model and matching physical-looking readback are not interchangeable |
| 18 — multi-node | Same C42 and model truth across A/C hosts, retained outbox, interest fencing/failover and Bob's selected one-use Refresh | A host replacement, duplicate advisory or new current card is not command attribution, publication receipt or automatic adoption |

The operation is not “proved” just because a stage's table row
exists. Each strengthening is attributable to an actually executed
checker, approved source, issued handle, qualified external assumption,
installed physical boundary or observed result. Claim completion and
the independently required Part VIII capabilities have different
denominators. In particular, real manager Reassign can later affect
the same aggregate and must appear in a claimed full invariant
operation catalog even if this specific trace follows Alice's self-Claim.
The independently selected Response interaction at
[[#decision-humanhelp-response-flow]] remains an open product
requirement until the owning application makes and qualifies it.

*** Trusted incorporation: one command, complete guards and writes

A genuinely eligible positive path needs a rendered and enabled Claim
control bound to the admitted semantic operation and installed plan;
the generated browser sends C42 to the actual effective route.
The serving handler qualifies its original model/FX inputs and
authenticated Alice, binds R's target independently of client
role claims and checks a compatible historical command record
*before* treating C42 as a new incorporation attempt.

For a genuinely new C42, the actual atomic guard protects R's
open/eligible status, current Alice Membership/Organization
association, absence or permitted history/cardinality of active
primary RequestAssignment, the correct immutable command
binding/exclusion, and all relevant source-owned policy reads.
A selected strong profile atomically applies the real Request
lifecycle mutation, creates or updates the appropriate **separate**
assignment/history, retains the C42 canonical result and records
required durable publication intent in the same suitable
authoritative boundary. Optional after-commit dispatch is a
separate effect and cannot turn an incorporated Claim into
a rejected command.

The selected XTDB transaction must actually implement the full
guard/effect/frame, including presence and previously unseen
fields, uniqueness, revision or history-sensitive ABA exclusions
when required, and other relevant writers. The actual statement
or immutable lowered transaction, exact provider, authentic
submission and qualified transaction-internal prestate relation
are different pieces of evidence. An external pre-read, a
syntactically valid ASSERT/PUT, a process-local one-shot return
or a matching later Request card does not independently prove
all four. If a required physical witness or all-writer
enforcement is unavailable, record the precise open provider
premise or fail a profile that mandates proof.

A successful incorporation must support an eligible positive
physical readback after appropriate provider progression.
A competing valid helper's attempt, revoked Membership,
association change, same-ID incompatible binding, missing
primary guard, duplicate assignment or stale snapshot must
receive its intended typed rejection/unknown outcome; a
runner that only proves such mutants fail while no real
Claim works has not demonstrated the operation.

*** Failure schedule C42: authoritative truth and actor knowledge diverge

Assume the selected strong profile has an atomic domain/command/
outbox record and the requisite shared authority. The following
is a *prospective controlled execution schedule*, not a report
that the current HumanHelp app or multi-host runtime has run it.

| Step | Physical or user event | Established fact and outstanding obligation |
|------+------------------------+---------------------------------------------|
| 0 | Alice has the permitted R affordance and creates C42 before its first possible send | Original logical intent/binding exists; no commit or enduring actor privilege follows |
| 1 | Host A admits an attempt; C42's new-command guarded transaction actually incorporates | Actual authority now holds the protected result under backend qualification; command/outbox persistence is in the selected atomic profile |
| 2 | A's response is lost after possible physical submission | Alice's client may be =:commit-unknown=, regardless of the already committed backend fact |
| 3 | A dies before dispatching the durable notice and her old browser plan/resource retires | C42 and publication debt survive independently; obsolete effect/DOM rights do not |
| 4 | Host C receives a permitted recovery/resolution for *the same bound C42* | Current authenticated disclosure and canonical historical record determine =:already-incorporated= or permitted result; never a second Claim |
| 5 | Replacement dispatcher claims/retries original notice intent; a router redelivers an advisory | Duplicate Live signals are idempotent and do not duplicate domain writes or user grants |
| 6 | Bob's router learns or restores his scoped interest; he may receive the advisory late, twice, or not at all | A fence, replay, gap/reacquisition or named weaker contract owns lost-interest recovery; notice is not an authoritative board read |
| 7 | Bob is shown an allowed pending indicator and explicitly selects Refresh | One scoped acquisition grant exists under Bob's currently permitted principal and applied board query |
| 8 | An authorized provider read returns a qualified basis/coverage result and the actual browser finishes all required HTMX/OOB installation | Only this actually covered and installed authorized cohort can discharge applicable debt; insufficient/incomparable progression or missing target remains open |
| 9 | A subsequent Reassign/Unclaim occurs or Bob's Membership/visibility changes | Current card/permissions may change, but neither proves nor retroactively cancels C42's original historical incorporation |

At step 4, **successful compatible historical replay is not new
incorporation**. A new Claim guard may now reject because R is
already claimed; the resolver must first check the retained exact
binding and independently authorize disclosure of the old result.
If C42's record is absent, unreadable, expired, not yet visible
at a qualified basis or outside current disclosure, a new current
card cannot manufacture attribution; the status remains unknown
until appropriately established or excluded by the selected
protocol. An attempt on A failing or aborting does not prove
a concurrent attempt on C never incorporated. A different C43
is a different intended command, not a safe replay of C42.

At steps 5–8, Live invalidation is *advisory* for Bob, including
remote lifecycle changes to already-visible cards. An eligible
manual Refresh must remain possible even when the advisory is
lost entirely. The current user grant covers one logical
acquisition, not one new acquisition for each arriving notice
or one per network retry. Provider scope/basis and actual
physical OOB/DOM completion establish which debt it covered.
A newer-looking token or HTTP 200 does not create provider
entailment; an advisory arriving mid-Refresh cannot silently
mint a successor read. Creator-local Create and narrowed
own-command recovery are separately declared exceptions,
not automatic Bob-board replacement.

*** Alternate schedules expose the necessary distinctions

A complete acceptance corpus also includes these nearby cases:

- **No incorporation:** Alice's Membership is revoked, R is
  no longer eligible or another primary Claim wins *at the
  actual guarded transaction*. The corresponding new command
  rejects with the typed actual reason; a rejected fresh
  command is not an inferred outcome for earlier unknown C42.
- **Potential incorporation without available history:** an
  HTTP timeout or host crash leaves command record access
  unavailable. Preserve =:commit-unknown= or the precisely
  permitted unresolved result; do not treat “no record
  seen” as exclusion without a qualified fence.
- **Same command, multiple attempts:** attempt A1 aborts
  under contention while A2 incorporates C42; the
  command outcome is not the Boolean result of A1.
  Incompatible actor, arguments, operation or scope
  reuse under C42 is rejected rather than replayed.
- **Bad browser continuation:** a delayed callback from
  Alice's retired target or an old release tries to
  write a new DOM incarnation; last-effect ownership
  prevents it even if an earlier scheduling check passed.
- **Publication/interest gap:** commit persists, dispatcher
  fails, backup router carries an incomplete coarse filter,
  or Bob registers interest after notice generation.
  The admitted recovery path must not certify false
  freshness from silence, nor disclose protected
  details to a merely *possibly* interested recipient.
- **Proof and installation substitutions:** a conditional
  abstract Claim theorem is supplied with an omitted
  Reassign operation; a checked artifact receipt is
  offered for bytes another host served; or a valid
  local wrapper is bypassed by deployed routing.
  Each requires source/inventory or actual physical
  rejection before claiming full certification.

The test oracle records its *original requirement*, injected
change, eligible positive, first legitimate rejection point,
actual observed forbidden physical effects and applicable
limitations. Seeing a failure afterward or reading a
copied report is not the same as prevention or independent
physical conformance.

*** Certification uses the *same* source, not a proof-only Claim

The source-owned Claim policy, original complete relevant
operation and invariant catalog, supported closed typed rules,
guarded relation, Choreo, projected plan and application
assembly are the source subjects of Part IX's
[[#req-humanhelp-certification-pilot]]. The proof checker
and dependency issuer establish only their own propositions
and exact open premises; source dependency completeness
cannot be inferred from an applicant-provided DAG.
The original model still owns opaque Organization policy
and real multi-record RequestAssignment meaning.

The pilot keeps **three distinct outcomes**:

1. Semantic source checking and conditional abstract proof:
   verified derivations/finite assertions over the selected
   original operation catalog, with correct nonvacuous
   initial/Claim examples, declared premises and bounds.
2. Production binding: current issued operation and
   active trusted Biff/Ring route, guarded XTDB writer,
   authenticated principal, command result, selected
   outbox/Live and built/served/loaded browser artifact
   match the admitted source under appropriate independently
   checked or explicitly still-open physical correspondence.
3. Real product behavior: an actual enabled Claim in
   supported Chromium/HTMX, authenticated Request and
   Membership, guarded persistence, reload, permitted
   settlement and controlled failure/recovery occur
   through the real production-backed model/installed UI.

A small propositional kernel proof may establish S0/S1
over its seven inference rules; it neither establishes S2
actual Clojure checker correspondence, S3 typed policy
atom interpretation, nor S4 source-to-installed physical
transfer. Formal admission soundness and concrete
execution safety simulation are separate propositions.
A changed provider, verifier, compiler, asset or host can
invalidate physical *applicability* while leaving an
unmodified abstract theorem sound. An early finite
example, a successful model-only fixture or a browser
test by itself never closes the missing intermediate edge.

Evidence labels must identify original source, current
subject, checker and actual proof/run status, issuer,
premises, provider/host/asset qualification, observation
window and selected release profile. A copyable
certificate-shaped map, named theorem, fingerprint
or locally matching readback cannot be substituted
for an independently checked issuer or physical fact.
The four release decisions and separate operational,
compositional, external-formal and multi-node *completion*
milestones retain their Part XI definitions; a narrow
Claim proof pilot cannot silently finish the independent
HumanHelp product inventory.

*** Source changes preserve the claim and invalidate its dependents

A legitimate change in HumanHelp's Claim eligibility or
Organization policy is made through its original public
model/requirement owner. The dependent rule, operation,
Choreo knowledge/projection, plan, application closure,
relevant proof and issuance, currently served browser
bytes, runtime guard and selected real-user/fault
evidence must be rechecked when their semantics or
qualified implementation dependencies change.
Unrelated Search evidence and an unaffected abstract
theorem may be reused only after checking their
actual dependency/interpretation scopes.

A new XTDB adapter, added direct writer, Biff handler,
host version, browser build or router topology need not
change the original mathematical Claim meaning, but
does require the relevant physical/writer/asset/fault
requalification. A copied old receipt or cached result
cannot mint a new current release admission. The
corrective action belongs at the first owning source
or physical boundary, not by teaching the browser,
router or proof generator a second Claim policy.

Across all eighteen stages this is **one continually
strengthened Claim**: the model supplies truth and
policy; Choreo supplies justified interaction and
role knowledge; the browser owns only provisional
and adopted presentation; the physical writer owns
authoritative incorporation; command history owns
C42-specific attribution; Live owns advisory
publication/recovery; checkers own their exact
abstract propositions; release admission owns
current installed applicability. Every stronger
claim needs evidence at its own actual boundary,
and every missing original HumanHelp capability
remains visible outside the successful Claim slice.

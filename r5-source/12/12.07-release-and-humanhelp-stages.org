*** Stage 11 -- Release integrity, reuse, and bounded compatibility

Implement the four subject-specific release decisions of
[[#req-release-evidence]] and the directional compatibility
contracts of [[#req-bounded-release-compatibility]] for the
application's independently chosen intent and assurance profile.
Admission, installation, activation and completion have different
subjects and required observations; they are not four states of
one trusted Boolean or four independent completion milestones.
This stage integrates obligations already active at the first
managed deployment; delaying Stage 11 work does not permit
the first installed application to expose unchecked handlers,
stale browser assets or unauthorized protected effects.

*** Bind the original release intent before generating results

The original application, product and deployment requirement
denominators, selected evidence policy, source/operation versions,
target hosts, protected writer universe and requested milestone
are fixed before the associated checks run. A generator must not
derive the complete requirement set exclusively from the
operations, gates or tests that happen to succeed. A mandatory
unselected product decision, unsupported physical writer or
unexamined Clojure policy stays an explicit blocker or conditional
premise for the *relevant* strong claim; it cannot disappear into
a smaller closed report. A deliberately narrower operational
release remains legitimate when its stated profile permits it.
Its status must not impersonate a stronger requested profile.

Deliver:

- an immutable candidate identity binding original source, public
  rules, required capability inventory, exact operation/plan
  and route graph, artifact/manifest/issuer inputs, source
  dependencies, evidence policy and selected environment;
- four separately admitted and reported judgments: candidate
  construction/admission, actual host installation, current
  activation/exposure and named completion milestone;
- exact gate coverage and closed result dispositions, distinguishing
  proven or checked, passed runtime/physical test, conditional,
  trusted premise, unsupported, missing, inconclusive, failed,
  inapplicable and excluded *according to the selected Part XI
  contract*, without laundering a premise through an aggregate
  green status;
- dependency-cone invalidation and selective reuse of unaffected
  abstract theorems, while separately requalifying changed
  adapters, issuer dependencies, installed handlers, providers,
  direct writers, browser assets and current authorization;
- robust cache deserialization, original-source rechecking and
  fresh issuance wherever an in-process proof handle or
  qualification cannot safely be transferred as raw bytes;
- actual generated/staged/served and client-loaded artifact
  correspondence, including expected bootstrap, codec,
  transport, role and executable-plan identities;
- coherent compare-and-activate, bounded old/new overlap,
  failure fencing, writer/reader capability withdrawal,
  private installation cleanup and host-level observation
  of the serving generation;
- source-derived continuation and retirement contracts for
  old clients, requests, streams, deferred effects, workers,
  migrations, persistent data and surviving command-recovery
  ownership, with explicit compatibility and retention limits.

*** Check directional overlap, not adjacent version labels

Four release coordinates must not be misrepresented as four
version identities: source-derived semantic identity, installed
physical realization and client artifact/continuation identity
are three separately qualified subjects; the *fourth
coordinate is a directional compatibility relation* for an
origin, target, operation and actual overlap condition.
A/A, A/B, B/A and B/B are checked when reachable in the
selected routing and deployment topology. Equality of
labels, route names, nearby Git revisions or plan digests
neither proves cross-direction compatibility nor establishes
current read/write permission. One old browser may reach
a new server while a new browser reaches an old server,
and their decoders, payloads and state transitions may
differ.

Before a protected read, authoritative command or DOM
installation, the active gateway verifies the applicable
origin/target relation and current principal/scope/
resource rights. Missing or unsupported compatibility
must refuse the effect or use an independently admitted,
non-mutating recovery path; it cannot silently downgrade
to an anonymous HTMX route, ignore changed command fields,
replace another client's adopted board or run an obsolete
unsafe writer. Retain only the old assets, decoders, command
records and other resources needed within their declared
horizons; retention is not indefinite authorization.

A pending old command C42 may have incorporated before
the old execution retired and its response was lost.
The target release may supply a new *authorized historical
resolver* for the same immutable command ID and binding
without admitting the retired mutation implementation,
reconstructing the old browser execution, or assuming
current Request state attributes C42. If retention,
authorization or evidence is insufficient, preserve
unknown status and the selected recovery or explicit
terminal-exclusion policy. Current-state acquisition,
command-specific settlement and browser continuity have
different owners.

*** Activate one safe generation and retain honest failure debt

Installation validates the exact private host resources,
Biff/Ring handlers, trusted writers, provider configurations,
worker processes and asset-serving paths. Activation then
checks the current predecessor and exposed traffic before
permitting the successor to serve. A failed candidate
cannot borrow an old green report or leave new privileged
callbacks alive by relying on best-effort cleanup; revoke
their active rights before retiring physical resources.
A predecessor may remain active only while its own
physical, semantic and security premises still hold.
Reactivation or rollback is a *new checked transition*:
neither a code rollback nor a browser swap reverses
a durable Request/assignment/command history.

A selected production run must test useful valid admissions,
installations, activations and unchanged-evidence reuse,
not only mutant rejection. Adverse cases include omitted
mandatory gate, forged report/issuer, stale cache, changed
source/premise, suppressed check, changed XTDB adapter,
wrong served or loaded JS, post-check handler substitution,
concurrent activation, stale external writer, unsupported
A-to-B/B-to-A continuation, partial cleanup and C42 lost
response across retirement. The earliest enforcing
boundary rejects, retains conditionality or preserves
a recoverable unknown; pass-by-skipping is never success.

An accepted operational release does not automatically
prove compositional certification, independent external
formal soundness or multi-node production. Those four
completion milestones and their separate evidence subjects
remain as defined in Part XI, not as a linear sequence
of required construction stages.

*** Stage 12 -- Complete HumanHelp proving application

Complete the *independently selected* Part VIII product
capability set through the real production public model,
admitted Gesso application, actual installed Biff/XTDB/
HTMX/CLJS paths and observable browser behavior.
The early Claim vertical slice is a useful architecture
test; it is not a replacement for Create, all other
lifecycle operations, selected Response interaction,
search/details/continuity, recovery and multi-user
adoption. Product acceptance is not a consequence of a
structurally closed generated registry. HumanHelp must
remain a proving *application*, not a special case baked
into the framework's generic runtime or proof kernel.

*** Preserve the original product denominator and authority

The protected model and its public core APIs own
Request, RequestAssignment, Organization, Membership,
User and Location semantics. Actual sign-in produces
separate authenticated principals and current read/
write/disclosure scopes; a browser session label or
helper name cannot confer those permissions. Each
eligible operation must cross a real rendered control,
bound command, trusted server operation, multi-record
guarded effect where needed, authoritative result and
physical browser adoption/settlement path. One-primary
assignment, current helper eligibility and
Organization policy apply at incorporation.

The six independently required lifecycle capabilities
are *Claim, Unclaim, Mark on the way, Complete, Cancel
and Reassign*. Reassign is not ordinary self-Claim:
an authorized manager must choose an actual distinct
eligible target helper, and the guarded transaction
must preserve primary-assignment history and current
authority. Create, authentication, Refresh, search/
options, details/continuity, response handling and
fault recovery have their own positive product gates.
Conditional ineligibility is legitimate, but a
permanently disabled control or unimplemented handler
cannot satisfy a required reachable positive case.

The Response interaction remains an independently
owned, *unselected* HumanHelp product contract at
[[#decision-humanhelp-response-flow]]. The framework
must not invent a default Response workflow just to
turn a progress report green. A selected design will
specify its actor, lifecycle relation, command/response
semantics, rendering, disclosure, completion and
positive/adverse tests. Until that choice is made
and qualified, the full operational HumanHelp
completion claim stays open, even if an independent
narrow Claim assurance pilot succeeds.

*** Use the adopted board cohort and selected user policy

Creator-local Create success may show the creator's
new authorized Request promptly under the declared
scoped exception, even when it falls outside the
currently applied board filter; its membership and
projection effect must be explicitly defined rather
than changing a global query invisibly. The same
exception does not grant acquisition of unrelated
remote content or erase pending advisory debt.

For any other actor's remote Create *or lifecycle
change*, including changes to an already-visible
Request, the ordinary selected HumanHelp policy
remains *advisory until explicit Refresh*. The
advisory may expose only the permitted pending
indicator/status, not protected changed Request
contents or automatic collection membership. A
user-created grant permits one scoped board
acquisition under the current principal, Location/
Organization and applied-query context. Its
authorized observation must establish the covered
cohort, relevant progression and physically complete
adoption before discharging debt. A new notice during
Refresh may be covered by that same justified result,
but cannot create an ungranted successor request.
User-selected reauthorization, rejected/unknown
own-command affected-card recovery and trusted loss
of disclosure rights are distinct explicitly scoped
paths. Reconnect, plan change, delivery success or
server push alone does not grant remote adoption.

Search, ordering and presentation options operate on
the *already adopted, currently permitted cohort*.
They may support newest/oldest, mine-first,
unclaimed-first and terminal visibility without
a covert read of newer remote membership. Disclosed
title/details/location and permitted search labels
form the search surface; undisclosed personal data
cannot become a side-channel through search matching.
The JVM/CLJS normalization, option semantics and
current viewer projection must be compatible under
their declared profiles. A locally filtered or hidden
row is not an authoritative absence.

Details, active selection, focus, editing drafts and
scroll continuity are compatible browser-local state,
not durable domain facts or a license to restore
obsolete provisional DOM. A stale Claim control can
remain visible under remote-advisory policy, but a
click must still face current Request and Membership
guards; the resulting rejected or uncertain command
has an honest affected-card recovery path without
automatically importing unrelated collection rows.

*** Prove full product behavior at actual enforcing boundaries

Each of the **fourteen independent Part VIII completion
gates** must have its own original requirement,
meaningful eligible positive, applicable rejection/
concurrency/fault cases, actual observed result
and declared source, build, host, backend, browser,
actor/scope and evidence provenance. They cover
production model authority, one admitted application
source, installed host/writers, actual client assets,
real Claim, all six lifecycle actions, rich-board
behavior, multi-client selective adoption, explicit
Refresh, fault/recovery, real runtime enforcement,
semantic/assurance evidence, physical substrate
qualification and independent real-browser acceptance.
A green browser fixture, passed model check or
deployed handler by itself cannot substitute for
another gate.

Acceptance uses actual Chromium/Playwright sessions
and rendered controls, not invented transport fields
injected by a test; real current production models
and backend truth, not parallel sample rules;
persisted readback after reload, not only optimistic
DOM; multiple genuinely distinct users and
current Organization/Membership policy; and
deterministic contention/failure fixtures with
precisely attributed outcomes. Negative tests
must fail for the intended reason and unexpected
console/page/request errors must still fail the
test. An HTTP 200, disabled button, uninstalled
browser artifact, fabricated stored state or
unimplemented Response cannot count as an
eligible successful product capability.

Deliberate adverse scenarios include competing
Claim and Reassign, revoked Membership between
read and commit, lost C42 result then later
lifecycle change, malformed route/manifest,
Create during another client's pending remote
debt, one-use Refresh with a later advisory,
lost SSE/registration fence, incompatible
old assets, OOB partial installation, unknown
outcome, failed continuity, changed reader
permission and unauthorized disclosure.
A dropped advisory must have an independently
permitted current-state acquisition path:
reconnect alone is neither the only trigger
nor a user grant. The result distinguishes
actual domain truth, authorized observation,
installed presentation and unresolved command
attribution.

The genuine product can finish its operational
milestone while independently stronger
compositional, external-formal or multi-node
milestones remain open if they were not
required by its selected release intent.
Conversely, no degree of formal Claim analysis
can declare the unfinished product complete.
Completion requires all original Part VIII
requirements actually discharged, with
remaining conditional or unqualified physical
claims reported exactly and never hidden by
a narrower generated inventory.

*** Stage 9 -- Live publication, observation coverage, and selective adoption

Close the authoritative commit → required publication intent → dispatch →
subscriber awareness → authorized acquisition → actual browser adoption path
without confusing any two of those events. Gesso Live transports advisory
invalidation and opaque progression requirements; it does not carry a
second copy of domain truth, grant a protected read, or install browser
content by publishing a notice. The selected authoritative provider owns
the meaning of consistency, basis comparison and read coverage, rather
than a universal integer revision or arrival-time ordering.

*** Derive publication obligations from authoritative source

The public model operation owns which committed changes require notice,
the affected domain scopes, permitted recipient disclosure, necessary
progression and the durability/recovery profile. Expansion rules may
derive invalidation scopes from the original change but cannot invent
new authoritative basis evidence or infer recipients' complete interests
from a local ephemeral cache.

Where the chosen profile promises publication across writer crash or host
replacement, incorporate *durable publication intent* together with the
guarded domain effect and command record under a suitable common atomic
boundary. Dispatch, retry, duplicate suppression, retention, dequeue,
acknowledgment and delivery ownership are later responsibilities with
explicit failure windows. An outbox record establishes that a notice
was owed, not that every client received it or that a protected
projection may now be revealed. A publisher failure after known commit
must never be reported as a rejected domain operation; residual notice
work remains owned. If a weaker profile is selected, state which crash,
retention, availability or user-recovery guarantees are *not* claimed.

Deliver:

- a source-derived change/invalidation/interest/fragment relation,
  independently qualified for its exact assembly and publication profile;
- an atomic durable intent and qualified dispatcher when required,
  with exact owner, handoff, retry, idempotency, expiration, failure and
  recipient/disclosure scopes rather than an implicit best-effort callback;
- per-authority opaque progression, with orderless composition of distinct
  requirements unless a trusted provider supplies explicit entailment
  or comparison evidence;
- explicit separation of advisory frontier, authorized acquisition
  requirements, actually covered read, physically adopted result,
  current source knowledge and still-outstanding obligation debt;
- initial fragment acquisition or a justified existing observation,
  with a usable protected read route even when no advisory arrives;
- registration/subscription fencing, replay or qualified gap detection,
  and reconnect/failover ownership for interests learned after an
  invalidation or transferred between hosts and routers;
- admissible authorization and scope binding for advisory payload,
  follow-up acquisition and negative/exclusion observations, including
  the cases in which an entity is undisclosed rather than absent;
- per-fragment logical single-flight, debt coalescing, retry/backpressure,
  bounded metadata and separately bounded physical resource remnants,
  with explicit retention and availability assumptions;
- a convergence or honestly weaker eventual-user-recovery contract
  that does not depend on receiving every transient notice or
  inventing provider progression from a successful HTTP response.

*** Establish the initial-interest and lost-notice recovery seam

A durable outbox by itself cannot close a missed-subscription gap.
At initial registration, a subscriber can discover its interest only
*after* an invalidation was computed or dispatched; a replacement
router can also lack the departed router's interest. The first
useful managed projection therefore needs an admitted observation
and a registration/acquisition fence, qualified replay, or a
documented gap/reacquisition protocol under the source authority.
Do not interpret silence from a router as authoritative freshness.
A subscription token, published cursor or membership in a coarse
routing filter is not automatically equivalent to a complete
provider read basis or physical board adoption.

A claimed strong multi-host path must make durable notice recovery,
interest registration, failover, redelivery and recipient knowledge
separate obligations. Idempotent invalidations may be redelivered
conservatively; their duplication cannot mint a second business
command, alter current authorization or consume a fresh user grant.
No particular broker, hierarchical router topology, Rust process,
global durable actor or fixed consumer graph is mandatory in the
timeless specification. The chosen topology must establish its own
coverage, takeover, ordering/gap and liveness premises. A single-node
profile obeys the same semantic contract with fewer physical owners.

An authoritative absence is a *positive qualified negative fact*:
it binds the permitted principal, query, entity/membership scope,
source basis, negative/disclosure semantics and physically adopted
coverage. An absent DOM node, local filter, failed request, undisclosed
entity, no matching interest or unfulfilled subscription does not
establish domain absence.

*** Acquire only with an authorized scope and selected product permission

An advisory requirement may make an existing board stale, but it
cannot authorize a subsequent database read or replace the DOM.
Acquisition checks current trusted principal, source/query scope,
permitted provider progression, current fragment/target owner and
the *separately selected grant policy*. A qualified observation
discharges only those requirements for which the provider proves
coverage, scope, temporal compatibility and physical installation.
An incomparable or insufficient basis remains outstanding; a
successful HTTP 200 or echo of a requested token is not that proof.
Newer observations may cover earlier advisory debt without
assuming all opaque tokens are linearly ordered.

For HumanHelp's selected ordinary remote Create and lifecycle
policy, the incoming notice remains *advisory only*. Bob's
one-use explicit Refresh grant authorizes exactly its selected
logical board acquisition under the currently admitted applied
query and disclosure context. A new notice arriving during that
cycle may be covered by its already qualified result if the
provider establishes entailment; otherwise its debt survives.
It never silently issues a successor acquisition, even if the
first physical HTMX attempt completes or fails. A new grant
requires the selected new user action or separately declared
authorized recovery policy. Creator-local acceptance, a
narrowly correlated own-command reread and trusted revocation
are different scopes, not general permission to auto-adopt
remote content. Local filters and visible cohort membership
do not become server read authority.

Acceptance must witness an eligible first acquisition, durable
commit-to-advisory path, authorized one-use Refresh and actual
scope-complete DOM installation alongside: crash after commit
before dispatch, duplicated/reordered notice, early registration
race, stale backup filter, failed router, dropped notice with no
recorded debt, insufficient or incomparable basis, omitted card,
partial OOB installation, authorization change, reconnect and
concurrent new notice during Refresh. The recorded result
distinguishes incorporated state, owed notice, attempted delivery,
recipient interest, permission to read, complete observation
and adopted presentation. If availability or fairness is not
established, safety may remain valid while convergence remains
explicitly conditional.

*** Stage 10 -- Whole-application construction and actual installed closure

The ordinary managed application is built from *one source-owned
construction*, not independently hand-maintained route, model,
browser, proof, recovery and diagnostic registries that merely
use compatible names. The product's independently chosen required
capabilities and current public policy are the denominator for
closure. A closed discovered graph can be a useful narrower
application but cannot define away a required HumanHelp operation,
Response-flow decision, protected writer, publication obligation
or uninspected correctness-relevant predicate.

*** Establish source, assembly, and physical-implementation joins

Deliver:

- the typed application graph linking original policy/rule subjects,
  public model operations, Choreo, role-local plan, generated artifact,
  route, transport, trusted handler, execution capabilities,
  transaction/command outcome, notice, fragment and acquisition;
- independently required product-capability and protected-effect
  inventories, cross-checked against the source-derived admitted
  operation catalog rather than reduced to what the generator found;
- source-derived requirements and selected evidence mechanisms,
  distinguishing proven, runtime-enforced, assembly-verified,
  conditional, trusted/opaque, unsupported, inconclusive,
  contradicted and uninspected claims at their exact scopes;
- closed required/supplied capability and FX invocation contracts,
  route method/path identity, collisions with anonymous HTMX,
  actual provider bindings, model/operation source versions and
  declared recovery paths for every required dynamic outcome;
- canonical browser manifest, checked build/compile provenance,
  exact artifact bytes, immutable publication set and serving/
  loaded-asset correspondence under generation/release identity;
- actual Biff/Ring lifecycle dependencies, before-exposure
  preflight, qualified installed handler/executor binding,
  protected writer/read capabilities and independently examined
  alternative route, worker, migration and backend paths;
- real dynamic rendered Hiccup/HTML/HTMX affordance checks at the
  earliest supported pre-serialization boundary, including
  deferred responses and any selected supported streaming scopes;
- one read-only, source-derived policy/operation/authorization/
  disclosure/lifecycle/dependency/assurance audit with
  exact completeness and opaque-premise annotations;
- stable typed errors, first knowable failure owner, incompatible
  versus missing resource distinctions, and nonexecuting repair
  guidance tied back to the same original source.

*** Fail at the earliest legitimate boundary, not at a convenient report

Static checks reject a known missing operation/view/route/plan/artifact/
model edge, unsupported effect/required recovery contract, inconsistent
command binding, ambiguous route or absent provider before installing
the affected managed behavior. Dynamic conditions need a *declared,
structurally admissible* continuation with a real owner, required
knowledge, permitted effects, trigger and terminal/uncertain outcome.
Preflight does not assert that Membership, XTDB, network, permissions
or clocks will remain valid. The trusted invocation/transaction
boundary rechecks facts whose truth becomes knowable only there.

A real rendered response is checked before the supported HTML
serialization step; that boundary does not automatically inspect
arbitrary pre-serialized Ring HTML or a later substituted handler.
The actual Biff module initialization/start order and the server's
adoption of `:biff.ring/handler` (or explicitly selected equivalent)
must be qualified independently. One inspected application map,
green assembly result or forged preflight report is not evidence
that the deployed listener served the checked handler, accepted
the issued executor, bound its protected writer or delivered the
recorded asset bytes. Under a strong production claim, an
uncalled optional checker or an unenumerated physical writer
is an open obligation that prevents stronger admission.

A rendering or transport failure *after* a qualified committed
Claim does not undo that commit. It may block browser installation,
leave unknown attribution at the client or create publication debt,
each with its own recovery owner. When canonical rendering fails
before response delivery, the supported fail-closed output gate
must not release an unchecked affordance. Concurrent startup,
reload, deferred output and version change preserve their
actual generation and resource lifetimes; stale artifacts and
obsolete handlers cannot borrow the successor's admission.

*** Audit policy honestly, including opaque or unexamined code

The semantic audit is a projection of the original admitted
semantic and assembly objects, not a second authority or a
complete interpretation of arbitrary Clojure source. Each
correctness-relevant predicate affecting authorization,
eligibility, disclosure, lifecycle, ownership, command
acceptance or transition either has an admitted source-owned
contract, a derived proof/inspection relation, or an
explicitly open opaque implementation premise. The checker
must report what it actually inspected and which other
callbacks, handlers, writers and external services remain
outside the scope. Zero escapes found in an incomplete scan
does not mean zero escapes in the application.

Independent source-policy adequacy is separate from formal
internal consistency. A perfectly checked but product-wrong
policy cannot earn full product completion. Missing or
unselected HumanHelp Response, Reassign or other required
lifecycle functionality remains visible even if every
discovered operation passes preflight. Genuine positive
Claim, Create, lifecycle, search/Refresh and browser
scenarios must remain possible under the specified profile;
a reject-everything application is not complete.

Acceptance tests the source-to-physical installed path with
a real eligible operation, then controlled removal or
substitution of every named important edge: missing
view/plan/route/provider, unbound clock/principal,
unrealizable dynamic recovery, stale build, wrong
browser target, anonymous HTMX route collision,
replaced Biff handler, pre-serialized response bypass,
direct XTDB writer, incorrect CSS/JS publication
generation, bogus audit claim and unsupported
opaque policy predicate. Errors occur at the first
boundary able to know the defect and otherwise retain
a typed *open* limitation. Independent observation of
actual serving, current authority and protected writes
is needed before declaring production-path enforcement.
Ordinary genuinely non-Choreo HTMX remains legal
under its own explicit contract and may not inherit
the managed application's stronger claims.

The later release, product and compositional-certification
milestones still require their own independent evidence.
Stage 10 establishes the source-owned application
construction, exact scope and admissible installed
enforcement boundaries; it does not establish all
possible arbitrary Clojure behavior, universal backend
semantics, full HumanHelp acceptance or actual proof
soundness merely by assembling a graph.

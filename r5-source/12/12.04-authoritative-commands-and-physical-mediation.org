*** Stage 5 -- Public authoritative operations and command resolution

Construct public model operations with explicit preparation, guarded incorporation,
and after-commit work. Choreography and views consume their public contracts
rather than private model internals. HumanHelp models remain the owners of
Request, Membership, Organization, and RequestAssignment meaning.

Deliver:

- typed inputs, current authority requirements, guarded dependencies, write and
  frame contracts, result/disclosure contracts, and durable-work obligations;
- stable =:gesso/command-id= binding before first possible send, covering operation,
  interface, canonical arguments, principal, and authority scope;
- compatible existing-command lookup before applying a new-command business guard;
- atomic command exclusion, authoritative mutation, result recording, and required
  publication intent;
- model outcomes =:committed=, =:rejected=, =:already-incorporated=, and
  =:commit-unknown=, with explicit presentation mappings;
- protected command reconciliation, retry, retention, and expiry behavior;
- public composition of cross-model constraints without pretending that separate
  transactions form one atomic operation.

Acceptance includes a successful command, typed guard rejection, compatible replay
after success, incompatible reuse, changed Membership or association, concurrent
delivery, and lost response after possible submission. One attempt's abort does
not prove the logical command failed if another delivery can incorporate it.

The Claim scope guards Request state, helper Membership, Organization policy,
association and primary-assignment conditions together with its writes. A
pre-read or a self-asserted eligible flag cannot establish those current facts
at incorporation. Backend realization is separately qualified in Stage 6.

*** Stage 6 -- Physical transaction mediation and provider qualification

Bind the authoritative plan to the actual submission boundary. Prefer constrained
lowering and an owned gateway for supported physical forms; retain an explicit
opaque adapter contract where the full language or provider requires one.

Deliver:

- immutable admitted plans and complete physical transaction derivation/checking;
- one submission owner that cannot accept appended raw writes or a substituted
  backend client/callback through its normal interface;
- guard footprints separate from write footprints, including absent/unique
  relationship predicates and all effective transaction options or hooks;
- frame preservation for whole-document replacement, with complete comparison,
  a justified revision discipline, or a qualified alternative update mechanism;
- local attempt and provider-return observation correlated to the issued invocation;
- provider-qualified outcome, read-basis, progression, and atomicity interpretation;
- a real writer inventory and mediation contract covering routes, jobs, imports,
  maintenance, migrations, and external write credentials where the claim requires it;
- explicit backend-assumption qualification and invalidation from Part X.

Acceptance uses actual backend concurrency and failure windows. It challenges
unguarded overwritten fields, changed associations, competing inserts, duplicate
commands, lost return, insufficient read progression, and changed adapter or
provider configuration. Supported values distinguish absence, null, false,
collections, and physical encoding.

A pinned external pre-read is not a transaction-internal state witness. A later
matching post-state is not proof of causality. A local return observation is not
a universal refinement theorem. A full-value guard alone does not exclude ABA.
When intervening history matters, a separate history-sensitive mechanism and
complete writer discipline must establish the required exclusion.

The stage reports exactly which physical forms are supported. One-document
coverage cannot satisfy HumanHelp's multi-record Claim contract. A host-supplied
writer list cannot prove that undisclosed direct writers are impossible.

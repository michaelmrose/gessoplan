## Material R4 obligations preserved

1. **Whole-application semantic audit is normative, not a
   decorative list of operations.** The admitted application
   must explain correctness-relevant policy, source ownership,
   actual typed transitions, dependencies, opaque boundaries,
   evidence and open obligations without requiring a reader
   to reconstruct the entire Clojure repository.
2. **One source, many derived projections.** R4 required
   business-rule catalog, operation cards, role/authorization
   view, lifecycle transition relation, rule dependency,
   opaque/trusted premise report and assurance coverage
   to derive from original admitted semantic and assembly
   objects; no separately maintained hand-authored policy
   or different execution registry may substitute.
3. **Rule meaning and exact enforcement are distinct.**
   R4's Claim active-Membership example records the rule's
   domain rationale, original source, guarded current
   Request/Organization/Membership semantics and
   authoritative transaction enforcement obligation.
   A declared guard binding can be assembly-verified
   while transaction implementation/concurrency
   conformance remains a separately named trusted premise
   until actual applicable evidence exists. A rule
   report is not proof merely because it quotes a source.
4. **Rule inventory is qualified; unknown never means no
   business rules.** The original R4 summary counts
   were illustrative, not a target number or quality
   metric. The revised 07.12 distinguishes known-closed
   empty, partial inspection and entirely opaque/unknown
   sources, so a zero reported list cannot falsely
   claim full human policy completeness.
5. **Authorization and disclosure stay current and
   differentiated.** Invocation, protected current reads,
   Live subscriptions, command-history disclosure and
   manager Reassign may have different legitimate
   policies. Previously rendered controls and audit
   metadata never authorize trusted model effects.
6. **State machine visualization uses the actual model
   enums and transitions.** R4's diagram was schematic;
   current HumanHelp state names cannot be silently
   renamed to match a friendly completed/cancelled label.
   When no admitted lifecycle relation is inspectable,
   that fact remains explicitly not inspected.
7. **Dependency edges retain their semantics.** A Live
   topic overlap is source-declared presentation impact,
   not a transaction read/write footprint or business
   predicate dependency. Rules and implementation
   dependencies require their corresponding real
   source/checked mechanism, and incomplete/opaque
   impact remains an open premise.
8. **Every assurance class belongs to its own
   proposition.** An abstract finite invariant, a
   conditional induction ledger, a constructed route,
   an issued executor, an observed guarded transaction,
   a real browser acquisition and an externally trusted
   callback may have different classifications.
   R4 explicitly prohibits collapsing them into one
   "verified" label/percentage. 07.12 preserves
   that accounting and names mechanism, bounds,
   subject and applicability for each row.
9. **Explanation includes original source-to-physical
   paths and genuinely open obligations.** A Claim
   explanation traces operation, role, actual controls,
   browser plan/build bytes, physical route, executor,
   trusted provider, model effect, command settlement,
   Live authoritative acquisition and installation.
   Known failed or unsupported edges remain visible
   even where another subgraph is green.
10. **Read-only, safe and provenance-correct.**
    R4's explanation APIs were illustrative, not
    guaranteed current Vars. An audit must not
    execute arbitrary callbacks, mutate sources,
    initiate transactions, automatically patch a
    near-match operation or leak privileged
    executors, principal scope, command data or
    private proof handles. Stale artifact/sidecar
    coordinates must not become current provenance
    through matching operation names.
11. **Audit may be useful on rejected candidates
    without issuing executable authority.** A
    partial candidate can identify source-local
    missing edges or incompatible providers; a
    successful descriptive report is not an
    admitted generation, certificate, transaction
    authority or current model authorization.
12. **Real positive acceptance remains independent.**
    A structural audit can be complete for a
    smaller app missing eligible Claim or
    Reassign. Product completeness is checked
    separately against source-owned user
    capabilities, and not invented in its audit.

The original long schematic explanation maps and
illustrative public API names are not copied as
current concrete Gesso APIs. Their functional
requirements and HumanHelp active-Membership
example are retained in a compact source-owned
rule/operation/evidence model and sixteen
positive/adversarial audit histories.

## R5's stronger design retained and improved

The existing R5 07.12 already specified ten
audit views, unknown-versus-empty policy, a
nonconfusable Live impact report, end-to-end
Claim explanation, and read-only redaction.

This revision adds:
- explicit generation/original-source,
  implementation/deployment and checker
  identity to every audit root;
- a coverage-qualified *inventory* for each
  count and view, with a complete, partial,
  opaque or unavailable distinction;
- a concrete active-Membership Claim
  rule contract with named authority,
  authoritative guard and its separate
  physical-conformance evidence;
- distinct authorization for invocation,
  current read, interest subscription,
  historical command disclosure and manager
  Reassign, plus actual lifecycle enum;
- provenance-typed dependency edges
  rather than copying Live presentation
  impact into a transaction conflict graph;
- source/model/host change-impact
  applicability without fabricating
  completeness of opaque dependencies;
- five-way evidence disposition and
  exact source/mechanism/proposition
  separation, retaining finite vacuity
  and original proof assumptions;
- current-report caller-precondition,
  source-sensitive redaction and
  read-only non-authoritative explanation;
- sixteen positive/adversarial histories
  that challenge false empty, fake green,
  forged report, incorrect provenance,
  unexplained writer and product-completeness
  conclusions.

## What Gesso v1622 actually exposes

- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/operation_summary.clj`
  builds sorted per-operation descriptions
  from a CURRENT OperationAcquisitionAssembly,
  routes, execution capabilities, settlement,
  compiled publication impact and fragment
  acquisition. It explicitly does not
  establish opaque model policy correctness
  or physical deployment.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/semantic_audit.clj`
  defines a source-derived, deliberately
  *scope-limited* audit projection. From a
  freshly checked ApplicationAssembly report
  supplied by its coordinator, it derives
  operation cards, declared Live overlaps
  and provenance, limited execution
  resolution construction indications,
  opaque model boundaries and distinct
  assembly/trusted assurance rows.
  It explicitly reports business rules as
  `{:status :not-derivable-from-current-operation-summary,
  :count :unknown, :rules []}`; authorization,
  domain lifecycle and dependencies are
  `:not-inspected`. An empty rule vector
  here is **not** proof that no policies exist.
- The `audit-from-current-report` API
  checks successful report *shape* and
  requires its coordinator to establish
  currentness. It does not authenticate
  caller-supplied forged reports.
  `gesso.live.application-preflight/explain`
  rederives current assembly report data
  on its canonical assembly path and
  attaches the audit only to a valid
  explanation. Copyable report data
  never issues execution rights.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/relational_rule_coverage.clj`
  offers an opt-in source-interpreted
  finite relational rule checker with
  exact current operation-ID coverage,
  finite initialized principal/argument
  domains and explored source-match
  counts. It explicitly does not
  establish full business intent,
  complete physical writer coverage,
  all opaque callbacks, real XTDB
  enforcement or universal safety.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/application_dependency.clj`
  derives a source-relative abstract
  assurance dependency graph from
  original declared catalogs and
  current source comparison, retaining
  open premises. It does not produce
  a whole-installed-app certificate.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/application_assurance_report.clj`
  recomputes conditional declared
  source/composition/writer joins,
  explicitly marking physical writer
  completeness, abstract-to-XTDB
  correspondence, browser correspondence,
  universal kernel soundness and
  certificate issuance as *not
  established*. A matching name or
  source graph is not a model
  implementation proof.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight.clj`
  exposes canonical `explain` and
  separately scoped bounded
  `explain-with-execution-evidence`
  (including an explicit opt-in
  formal variant), whose issued
  diagnostic handles are checked
  against exact current executors.
  The observational summary does
  not imply every operation execution
  was observed or that a selected
  finite model implies universal
  correctness.
- Source test namespaces inspected:
  `test/gesso/live/application_preflight/semantic_audit_test.clj`,
  `semantic_audit_integration_test.clj`,
  `operation_summary_test.clj`,
  `relational_rule_coverage_test.clj`,
  and the source-derived proof test
  namespaces. They distinguish
  unknown rule count, opaque model
  premises, current assembly audit
  derivation, and finite source
  matching. No fresh test suite
  was run as part of this editorial
  revision.

These observations describe the v1622
source-level boundary, not a promise
that Gesso already implements
automatic introspection of arbitrary
Clojure policies, whole-product
business-rule completeness or
physical deployment certification.

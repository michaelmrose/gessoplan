## Independent apparent-conflict decisions

### Trusted model outcome versus participant knowledge

Part I and Part IV distinguish authoritative
`:committed`, `:rejected`, `:already-incorporated`
and `:commit-unknown`; Part VII 07.08 keeps the
same set and explicitly maps participant presentations.
Evidence =:contradicted=, application admission
=rejected= and model `:rejected` are different types.
A successful Claim followed by broken response Hiccup
does not become a rejected command. If the browser
never received an admissible correlated settlement,
it remains =:unknown= until proper resolution.
The later current card and original C42 historical
outcome are different propositions. No conflicting
source of truth was found at this junction.

### Explicit user Refresh versus initial/reconnect repair

Part VI's one-Refresh semantics and Part VII's
initial/subscription-gap recovery are compatible
*only if the selected policy has different
authorization sources for the different reads*.
An initial baseline acquisition, a user-granted
remote Refresh, and a separately authorized
reconnect/fence/recovery read are distinguishable
acts. A dropped advisory or reconnection must
not automatically draw from a consumed Refresh
grant. If the selected application policy forbids
autonomous recovery acquisition, the system
retains visible stale/gap debt or an explicitly
weaker liveness guarantee. A replayable invalidation
cannot replace proof that an interest was known
when the invalidation was first created. These
requirements can be satisfied without claiming
unconditional convergence under offline or
uncooperative clients.

### Preflight before protected work despite Biff init ordering

The supported managed startup can initialize
private resources needed for checking, but
cannot permit an earlier Biff `:init` phase
to expose a protected Aleph listener, scheduler
or XTDB writer before admission just because
the preflight module is first in Biff's later
ordered `:start` phase. An actual fence,
private capability or independently admitted
effect contract is required. The claim is
conditional on *real physical adoption*, not
module-ordering rhetoric or a copyable system
map. This is a stronger but coherent end-state
requirement; it does not claim the v1622
startup universally enforces it.

### Full-language Clojure and optional formal checking

The source-owned application accepts ordinary
Clojure model/handler code with declared
effect, policy and trusted-boundary contracts.
When callbacks are opaque to the finite
semantic subset, their semantics and physical
conformance remain a stated premise or need
a qualified external check. Optional stronger
finite, induction or proof-kernel assurance
does not remove mandatory managed runtime
enforcement. Part IX's eventual generated
semantic/proof composition does not require
every downstream application developer
to hand-write an independent Recife, TLA+
or Quint program. No second business
model or proof authority is introduced by
Part VII's audit.

### Complete physical writers and scope of the invariant

A declared catalog and host-supplied writer
inventory are honest evidence only within
their inspected scope. Arbitrary Clojure
raw XTDB capability and unregistered external
writers do not disappear through application
assembly, despite complete route closure.
Section 07.15's "every required" language is
not a loophole to omit known protected effects:
a larger whole-writer safety claim requires
actual capability restriction or independently
justified exhaustive physical/interference
evidence. A policy may state weaker assumptions
but may not relabel them as a full security
proof. This matches Parts IV, IX and X.

### Part VIII's independent product profile is broader

Section 07.14's Create/Claim/Unclaim/complete/
cancel/Reassign/Refresh examples are **not**
a replacement for the full HumanHelp capability
oracle in Part VIII `req-humanhelp-required-capabilities`.
That oracle also includes authentication,
on-the-way, search/options, details/continuity,
the *separately selected response-flow contract*
and recovery. The response-flow selection remains
an explicit product design decision where it
has not yet been fixed. Part VII's normative
"includes" language and reliance on the
independent product profile retain those
requirements rather than declaring that
the shorter example list is exhaustive.
No HumanHelp implementation acceptance
is claimed.

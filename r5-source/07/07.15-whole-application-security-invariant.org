** Whole-application security and construction invariant

Preflight establishes the stated structural relationships and selected assurance
conditions. It does not confer a principal, disclose a protected result, authorize
a transaction, or establish that a mutable world still satisfies a prior guard.
Those premises are checked at the trusted boundary that owns them.

The browser can forge local metadata, operation names, command bytes, certificates,
and assembly IDs. Trusted ingress and model operations remain secure without
assuming that the browser follows the admitted implementation. Conversely, server
construction controls accidental misuse by trusted host code; a hostile JVM with
reflection or arbitrary bytecode replacement is outside that claim unless an
additional isolation mechanism is explicitly supplied and qualified.

A structural report, artifact receipt, process-local handle, proof certificate,
and runtime observation each has a defined role. None silently acquires the
others' authority. Production evidence is applied only to its actual subject,
scope, assumptions, and current applicability.

The whole-application invariant is:

#+begin_quote
One source-owned definition yields an admitted semantic and physical realization
under an explicit assurance policy. Every required operation, effect, output,
observation, and recovery edge has a compatible current binding, and the actual
installed paths enforce the required checks before their protected actions.
Construction, artifact, installation, invocation, and physical-completion facts
remain distinct. Missing structural edges reject rather than silently weaken
meaning; opaque application logic retains explicit contracts and premises.
Admitted assembly instances remain coherent through build, serving, asynchronous
work, reload, and retirement. Declared closure, production enforcement, and
independent product completeness are separately evidenced, and authority remains
with the trusted runtime/model boundaries that own the relevant current facts.
#+end_quote

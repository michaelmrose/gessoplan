** Authoritative outcome, acquisition, and propagation closure

*** Model outcomes and presentation settlements are explicit mappings

The authoritative command protocol uses Part IV's =:committed=, =:rejected=,
=:already-incorporated=, and =:commit-unknown=. Domain result payloads and browser
presentation settlements are separate types.

A Claim presentation may use =:accepted=, =:rejected=, and =:unknown=. Assembly
checks the explicit mapping, including a permitted recorded success from
=:already-incorporated=, incompatible command reuse, nondisclosable prior results,
and unresolved evidence. It does not substitute an assortment of transport
labels such as confirmed or superseded for the model outcome contract.

The trusted boundary checks permission to disclose a prior outcome. A successful
replay lookup does not confer disclosure authority on the caller. A local timeout,
HTTP 500, missing command row under an insufficient lookup, or present-state card
cannot be wired as unconditional proof of command rejection.

A direct response, durable resolver, and current-state acquisition have different
claims. Assembly closes the paths required by the operation rather than assuming
one route serves all three purposes.

 
*** Historical command truth, presentation, and disclosure

The four authoritative outcomes are not four interchangeable HTTP statuses.
The supported command protocol has an explicit, provenance-aware mapping to
participant-facing settlement and to the particular observation/recovery path
needed after ambiguous transport delivery.

| Authoritative outcome or evidence | Permitted participant interpretation |
|-----------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| =:committed= with admissible command binding and disclosure | =:accepted= for that command, with the required progression/acquisition contract; not proof that its card was installed. |
| =:already-incorporated= with exact recorded binding and authorized disclosure | A permitted replay of recorded incorporation without another domain write; incompatible reuse or undisclosable result has a separate guarded disposition. |
| =:rejected= from the authoritative operation for this command | =:rejected= with only the disclosed reason; not an inference from HTTP failure, current state, or a missing record. |
| =:commit-unknown= or uncorrelated/lost outcome | =:unknown= with a retained command-resolution owner; neither automatic repetition as a new command nor fabricated rejection. |
| A current authorized Request-card acquisition | Current projection at a stated scope and basis, which may differ from the historical effect of this particular command. |

A transient retry and a durable command are separately identified. Retries
preserve the original =:gesso/command-id=, principal/operation/argument binding
and the versioned protocol required to interpret it, while a new HTTP or
transport attempt may have its own correlation identity. A command ID is not
authority or permission to disclose another user's recorded outcome.
Reconciliation must validate the current disclosure predicate and the
*original* binding before revealing replay or enabling any further action.
An absent record in one read, even a pinned read, is not evidence of
nonincorporation unless the selected storage/consistency model establishes
the stronger negative proposition.

When a domain update and durable command record are claimed to be atomic,
the qualified physical transaction must actually write both in the same
guarded commit. An unrelated single-update writer plus a later diagnostic
lookup cannot establish that stronger command-record claim. The
source-owned assembly must retain the distinction in its assurance report.

*** Recovery includes an owner, trigger, and retained binding

A declaration that names a resolver endpoint is incomplete if nothing can invoke
it or the command binding is lost when the local execution retires. Required
recovery states identify:

- the work/command and sufficient retained binding or protected recovery reference;
- the current owner and lifetime, including transfer on retirement;
- the evidence needed to resolve the condition;
- current authorization and disclosure requirements;
- the actual trigger and permission for the next action;
- bounded automatic retry/backoff where permitted;
- behavior when plans, contracts, providers, or retained records are unavailable;
- the explicit terminal, blocked, deferred, or manual-reconciliation state.

Part VI's cross-deployment command recovery applies. A new plan cannot reinterpret
old arguments under changed operation semantics while keeping the old command ID.
The assembly identifies retained resolver/compatibility support independently of
new invocation support.

 
A recovery endpoint's mere existence is not a recovery *behavior*.
For a possibly submitted Claim, the resolver must preserve typed uncertainty
until it obtains admissible command-specific evidence or reaches a separately
defined blocked/manual state. Its invocation permission, authorized principal,
record retention, operation-version decoder and outcome-disclosure policy are
independently checked. A response renderer failure, timeout, disconnected
tab, browser refresh, retired generation or process restart must not
silently discard ownership of the outstanding resolution obligation.

Where a retry is admitted after uncertainty, the system establishes the
original operation/argument binding and declared deduplication guarantee
rather than treating an unobserved outcome as a fresh operation. A
read-only command-record lookup is not a retry permit or an
implementation of the authoritative operation. Loss of command
retention or an incompatible replacement plan produces an explicit
defer, block, transfer or manual reconciliation condition, never a
made-up successful or rejected settlement.

*** Progression remains scoped through the return path

The mutation, command record, publication/durable intent, acquisition, renderer,
and browser coordinator must agree on the meaning of their progression and
coverage requirements. Generic assembly code validates compatible provider
contracts; it does not calculate a universal maximum over unrelated tokens.

Acquisition requests carry the required scope and lower bound under the provider's
semantics. Returned evidence reports the actual established observation. An
installation uses that observation and the browser's current target/owner rights.
Adoption and debt discharge require the applicable physical completion, as in
Part IV and Part VI. HTTP completion is not a substitute.

 
The progression contract is typed by its provider and queried subject.
An XTDB transaction key, snapshot token, read-consistency requirement,
fragment advisory and physical DOM installation receipt may all contain
opaque values without sharing an order. Requirements compose only under
the declared scope and combination relation; generic maximum, string
comparison or receipt equality must not fabricate an advancement
witness. Where a mutation's returned basis is needed for a later
projection, the selected acquisition route must establish the required
lower bound and expose its *actual* satisfied observation, not simply
echo the requested token.

The browser may mark an observation adopted only after authorized
projection acceptance and the applicable physical installation/target
completion, under the selected progression and owner-generation policy.
A 200 response, swap attempt or Live advisory is insufficient. If
continuity restoration fails after a successful authoritative DOM
installation, that local failure must be represented separately; it
does not roll back the installed observation or the server's transaction.

A query membership obligation may require different evidence from a card-content
obligation. Initial server-rendered content is also an acquisition path with
provenance and coverage. An empty managed fragment needs a permitted acquisition
path and a truthful loading/unavailable state; absence of both initial knowledge
and acquisition is a structural defect.

 
*** Subscription establishment cannot open a silent knowledge gap

An initial server-rendered card and the subsequently active interest
subscription have a *relative* consistency problem: a relevant commit may
occur after the initial read but before the subscription is registered.
Likewise a subscription may become active first and then a later read
may cover already published changes. The selected strategy identifies
the baseline observation, interest-registration fence/receipt, replay or
buffering horizon, and the provider's consistency comparison by which
one phase covers the other. It may use re-acquisition instead of
replaying every invalidation, because an invalidation requests
fresh authority rather than carrying authoritative domain content.

A Live assembly that validates only a configured SSE route and GET
endpoint has not thereby established the no-gap property. Startup,
new subscription, reconnect, host change, authorization-context change,
interest evolution and router failover must each have a qualified
continuity/reconciliation path. The registration and baseline need
not be performed in one atomic database transaction if a suitable
fence and recovery argument closes the interval. Without one, the
application retains advisory/acquisition debt rather than claiming
an interest was up-to-date merely because no advisory arrived.

*** Required publication and durable work have recovery semantics

When operation semantics or product convergence requires publication, it belongs
in application closure. Naming a publisher function does not establish that it
runs after commit or survives a crash between commit and publication.

The assembly binds the chosen Part IV recovery strategy: durable publication/work
intent with a worker, a qualified replay stream, periodic reacquisition, explicit
Refresh under its conditions, or another declared mechanism. It identifies the
failure domain and liveness premises. There is no universal promise of event
delivery merely because an SSE connection exists.

A lost settlement or publication leaves incorporation intact and creates or
preserves the relevant delivery/recovery debt. Worker acknowledgment and retries
must agree with the durable work contract. External effects such as payment or
notification are not undone by browser retirement or response failure.

 
The commit-to-publication failure interval is a real failure boundary.
A completed XTDB mutation followed by process failure before an
in-memory SSE dispatch is not repaired by adding another function
named =publish!= to the assembly. An application requiring durable
convergence chooses an actual mechanism: e.g. transactionally bound
outbox/change intent, replayable write log with stated ordering and
retention, or a qualified independent periodic/permitted authoritative
reread. These strategies differ in latency, permissions and liveness,
and the assembly records which one closes the selected product promise.

A durable work processor acknowledges only after the relevant delivery,
handoff or recovery contract is satisfied; retry after crash must be
idempotent or have an admitted duplicate-safe effect protocol. A
publication may be redelivered because its meaning is advisory, but
repeated delivery must not issue repeated one-use Refresh grants,
new command IDs or unbounded acquisitions. Delivery loss, worker
failure and compaction/retention expiry lead to honest recovery debt
or an explicitly bounded unavailability, not an assertion that
the original guarded mutation was rolled back.

*** Live closure includes subscription continuity and adoption policy

The Live declaration binds the authoritative query/projection, relevant interests,
initial observation, subscription owner, progression provider, acquisition route,
and coordinator policy. The stable logical owner and replaceable physical target
have distinct identities and reconstruction rules.

The application closes the baseline/subscription gap and reconnect gaps using a
qualified fence, replay, buffering, polling, or reacquisition strategy. A process
that publishes changes only for interests known locally must justify how remote
or newly registered interests avoid missed updates. Absence of a locally known
interest is not proof that no other participant needs the change.

Shared subscriptions retain per-interest ownership and authorization. Connection
replacement, authorization-context changes, and lost continuity produce the
required recovery work even when no new advisory arrives. Coarse routing/filtering
can optimize delivery only under a contract that preserves the required coverage
or has a compensating recovery path.

 
The semantic interest is owned by its logical participant/fragment
scope, not by the accidentally selected SSE connection or router node.
One physical stream may carry multiple authorized interests, and a
router may hold coarse or hierarchical filters as a performance
optimization. Neither the absence of an interest in one process's
memory nor failure of a particular router establishes that no
remote participant needs a relevant refresh.

For distributed deployment, the same single-node semantic rule holds:
a change relevant to a registered or newly established interest must
eventually become recoverable by a permitted authoritative acquisition
under the declared fairness/availability assumptions. Interest
advertisement, invalidation routing, router replacement, replay
horizon, duplicate-safe redelivery and authorization are separate
contracts. A router that learns an interest after an invalidation
was forwarded requires a fence/replay/reacquisition argument; copying
a coarse filter to another node does not prove that the intervening
change was preserved. A failed router can be handled by conservative
re-delivery of a qualifying range when the protocol is idempotent
and bounded, but the availability of such a replay source is an
actual premise, not an implied fact about SSE.

The design does not require storing every successfully delivered
advisory for each client. It requires sufficient provenance,
retention, scope and failure detection to repair possible loss
without assuming exactly-once messaging. A single-node
implementation and a multi-node implementation realize the same
interest/progression/acquisition obligations; extra routers
introduce deployment evidence, not a different semantic model.

Advisory debt, acquisition permission, and adoption permission remain distinct.
HumanHelp may announce remote changes and wait for explicit Refresh. A newly
arriving advisory does not silently inherit an expired one-read grant. Closure
checks that the declared policy has a real trigger, retained debt, and a viable
path to the promised scoped adoption.

 
*** Positive and adversarial end-to-end settlement histories

The following cases must be exercised against the selected source-owned
application, qualified transport and relevant browser/Live boundaries.
A model trace or a synthetic SSE frame does not establish real
production-path acceptance by itself.

| History | Required result and evidence |
|---------+------------------------------|
| An eligible helper Claims an open Request; the guarded command incorporates and the initiating card is authoritatively reread and physically installed. | Preserve command-specific accepted settlement, scoped progression and a separate successful DOM adoption receipt. |
| Another helper receives an advisory about that Request, but HumanHelp policy requires explicit Refresh. | Retain advisory debt and show the prompt; do not silently fetch or install without a valid grant. |
| The remote user exercises one permitted Refresh, then a second advisory arrives after that grant is consumed. | The first authorized acquisition may adopt its observation; the new debt needs a newly permitted action and must not reuse the consumed grant. |
| A Claim commits, but its HTTP response disappears. | Server incorporation remains; the participant is unknown until admissible command-specific evidence resolves it. |
| A compatible retry locates the same disclosed durable command record. | Replay the recorded outcome without a new domain mutation, while retaining original binding and disclosure checks. |
| A pinned command-record read returns no row, or prior disclosure is not permitted. | Report unresolved or nondisclosable as appropriate; do not infer rejection, absence of a write or a retry permit. |
| A source-observed domain update exists but the selected physical writer did not atomically create the canonical command record. | Do not certify durable command incorporation or causal attribution from current state and a later lookup. |
| A later manager Reassigns a Request after a helper's earlier Claim incorporated. | The current card may show a new assignee while the earlier Claim remains historically incorporated; neither observation rewrites the other. |
| The acquisition route returns a card whose snapshot is older than the required mutation progression. | Retain unmet scope/debt and retry/defer under policy; HTTP 200 cannot establish advancement. |
| The authoritative HTML is installed, but browser continuity restoration then fails. | Preserve the adopted observation and classify continuity failure separately, without undoing the XTDB commit or fabricating rollback. |
| A fragment has neither initial authoritative content nor an admissible acquisition path. | Reject structural Live closure or mark the required capability unavailable; absence of advisories is not initial knowledge. |
| A change occurs between initial page observation and SSE interest registration. | Fence/replay/buffer/reread closes the gap or the no-gap claim remains open; loss is not disproved by silent stream startup. |
| The transaction commits and the server crashes before it sends the declared invalidation. | Recover from the selected durable intent/replay/reacquisition path, or disclose the unmet convergence obligation; no false transaction rejection. |
| One advisory is duplicated during failover or replay. | Preserve idempotent debt handling and grant accounting; do not create a second logical command or unrestricted acquisition. |
| Router A loses state while Router B learns an interest after the relevant invalidation. | Establish a valid replay/fence/reacquisition interval or retain a coverage gap; a coarse filter alone is not a delivery proof. |
| A Live stream reconnects with a changed principal or lost authorization. | Revalidate each interest and acquisition/disclosure permission, retire obsolete subscriptions, and recover knowledge without leaking protected projections. |
| A publication topic is declared but the actual model executor never emits it. | Structural application-summary closure may pass, but production publication/convergence remains unestablished and positive integration must fail. |
| The browser receives an advisory but its target was replaced before the response completes. | Enforce logical owner and current physical generation/target rights; do not adopt stale DOM or discharge debt without admissible completion. |

Successful acceptance requires usable Create/Claim/Unclaim/Reassign
histories where promised, including the remote advisory and user
Refresh policy, not just a structurally valid but inert graph.
No green assembly report establishes eventual progress without
named fairness, network, replay-retention, provider and user-action
premises. Parts VIII–X define stronger product, compositional and
physical correspondence evidence separately.

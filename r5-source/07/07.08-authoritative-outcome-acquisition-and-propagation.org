** Authoritative outcome, acquisition, and propagation closure

*** Model outcomes and presentation settlements are explicit mappings

The authoritative command protocol uses Part IV's =:committed=, =:rejected=,
=:already-incorporated=, and =:commit-unknown=. Domain result payloads and browser
presentation settlements are separate types.

A Claim presentation may use =:accepted=, =:rejected=, and =:unknown=. Assembly
checks the explicit mapping, including a permitted recorded success from
=:already-incorporated=, incompatible command reuse, nondisclosable prior results,
and unresolved evidence. It does not substitute an assortment of transport
labels such as confirmed or superseded for the model outcome contract.

The trusted boundary checks permission to disclose a prior outcome. A successful
replay lookup does not confer disclosure authority on the caller. A local timeout,
HTTP 500, missing command row under an insufficient lookup, or present-state card
cannot be wired as unconditional proof of command rejection.

A direct response, durable resolver, and current-state acquisition have different
claims. Assembly closes the paths required by the operation rather than assuming
one route serves all three purposes.

*** Recovery includes an owner, trigger, and retained binding

A declaration that names a resolver endpoint is incomplete if nothing can invoke
it or the command binding is lost when the local execution retires. Required
recovery states identify:

- the work/command and sufficient retained binding or protected recovery reference;
- the current owner and lifetime, including transfer on retirement;
- the evidence needed to resolve the condition;
- current authorization and disclosure requirements;
- the actual trigger and permission for the next action;
- bounded automatic retry/backoff where permitted;
- behavior when plans, contracts, providers, or retained records are unavailable;
- the explicit terminal, blocked, deferred, or manual-reconciliation state.

Part VI's cross-deployment command recovery applies. A new plan cannot reinterpret
old arguments under changed operation semantics while keeping the old command ID.
The assembly identifies retained resolver/compatibility support independently of
new invocation support.

*** Progression remains scoped through the return path

The mutation, command record, publication/durable intent, acquisition, renderer,
and browser coordinator must agree on the meaning of their progression and
coverage requirements. Generic assembly code validates compatible provider
contracts; it does not calculate a universal maximum over unrelated tokens.

Acquisition requests carry the required scope and lower bound under the provider's
semantics. Returned evidence reports the actual established observation. An
installation uses that observation and the browser's current target/owner rights.
Adoption and debt discharge require the applicable physical completion, as in
Part IV and Part VI. HTTP completion is not a substitute.

A query membership obligation may require different evidence from a card-content
obligation. Initial server-rendered content is also an acquisition path with
provenance and coverage. An empty managed fragment needs a permitted acquisition
path and a truthful loading/unavailable state; absence of both initial knowledge
and acquisition is a structural defect.

*** Required publication and durable work have recovery semantics

When operation semantics or product convergence requires publication, it belongs
in application closure. Naming a publisher function does not establish that it
runs after commit or survives a crash between commit and publication.

The assembly binds the chosen Part IV recovery strategy: durable publication/work
intent with a worker, a qualified replay stream, periodic reacquisition, explicit
Refresh under its conditions, or another declared mechanism. It identifies the
failure domain and liveness premises. There is no universal promise of event
delivery merely because an SSE connection exists.

A lost settlement or publication leaves incorporation intact and creates or
preserves the relevant delivery/recovery debt. Worker acknowledgment and retries
must agree with the durable work contract. External effects such as payment or
notification are not undone by browser retirement or response failure.

*** Live closure includes subscription continuity and adoption policy

The Live declaration binds the authoritative query/projection, relevant interests,
initial observation, subscription owner, progression provider, acquisition route,
and coordinator policy. The stable logical owner and replaceable physical target
have distinct identities and reconstruction rules.

The application closes the baseline/subscription gap and reconnect gaps using a
qualified fence, replay, buffering, polling, or reacquisition strategy. A process
that publishes changes only for interests known locally must justify how remote
or newly registered interests avoid missed updates. Absence of a locally known
interest is not proof that no other participant needs the change.

Shared subscriptions retain per-interest ownership and authorization. Connection
replacement, authorization-context changes, and lost continuity produce the
required recovery work even when no new advisory arrives. Coarse routing/filtering
can optimize delivery only under a contract that preserves the required coverage
or has a compensating recovery path.

Advisory debt, acquisition permission, and adoption permission remain distinct.
HumanHelp may announce remote changes and wait for explicit Refresh. A newly
arriving advisory does not silently inherit an expired one-read grant. Closure
checks that the declared policy has a real trigger, retained debt, and a viable
path to the promised scoped adoption.

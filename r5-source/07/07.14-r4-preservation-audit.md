## Preservation of R4's normative requirements

1. **Three noninterchangeable claims:** structural assembly
   closure, actual production-path enforcement, and independent
   behavioral product completeness. The first cannot substitute
   for a running guarded handler, and the second cannot prove
   the product exposes all promised capabilities.
2. **Real positive HumanHelp behavior:** creator Request creation
   and immediate visibility, helper Claim/Unclaim, completion,
   cancellation, manager Reassign, board acquisition and
   remote advisory/explicit one-use Refresh. A disabled button,
   hidden operation or reject-all strategy fails positive
   acceptance even if structural checks pass.
3. **Independent product profile without second model semantics:**
   the acceptance oracle names user-visible behavior and
   authoritative outcomes, not a separately executable
   competing business-policy registry. Inconsistencies are
   reviewed; neither model nor oracle is rewritten silently.
4. **Actual browser operation, not injected ideal commands:**
   top-level testing begins with the rendered control, generated
   runtime/HTMX and real route/model effect. A direct HTTP
   protocol fixture has a narrower valid scope.
5. **Installed enforcement and real fault injection:** a local
   checker or Biff handler-map comparison is not an attestation
   about real Aleph/host serving, nor are copied artifacts
   evidence of current loaded assets. The real output/effect
   guard must fail the corresponding controlled mutation.
6. **Precise failure and recovery:** model rejection, uncertain
   submission, postcommit response failure, broken Live
   subscription/reconnect and consumed Refresh grants retain
   distinct results. Expected diagnostics are asserted, not
   globally suppressed.
7. **Conformance must survive repeated and concurrent work:**
   one successful transaction or a consumed process-wide
   single-use slot is not a reusable application; admissible
   overlapping requests keep independent resources and outcomes.

R4 did not include every modern XTDB/checker impact table in 07.14.
Those added obligations strengthen, rather than claim to reproduce,
the narrower R4 material.

## Preserved R5 strengths and substantive additions

The original R5 section already required backend profile
qualification, scoped evidence, nonvacuous operation/rule/outcome
checks, sixteen scenarios and independent product tests.
The revision retains those scenarios and adds:

- Three explicit acceptance gates and positive product behavior
  independent of current declared operation inventory.
- Behavioral backend contract beyond version/option strings:
  guarded atomicity, command record, progression and retention,
  plus separations among tested interface, bounded observed
  behavior and retained unproved semantics.
- Typed change-impact graph covering model guards, operation
  implementation, ClojureScript assets, backend/adapter,
  Biff/writer installation, Live, proof/kernel and product
  oracle. Equal IDs, copied reports and filenames do not
  suffice for continued evidence applicability.
- Exact identity/profile dependency rules for incremental
  reuse, alongside unknown effects from opaque code.
- Seven-level qualification matrix from source to real
  product capabilities and eighteen additional adversarial
  histories, with corresponding positive controls.

## Gesso v1622 correspondence (scoped facts, not certification)

- `test/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_backend_compatibility_test.clj`
  checks the reviewed XTDB 2.2.0-beta1 API/option surface
  including staged one-shot submission and bounded observation.
  It fails closed on changed/unrecognized APIs but expressly
  does **not** prove backend semantics, authenticated execution
  causality, transaction-internal prestate, complete writers
  or universal refinement.
- `src/gesso/model/transition_conformance.clj`
  compares one supplied bounded XTDB observation to an
  original guarded transition and serial abstract model,
  expressly retaining provenance, physical submission and
  full writer coverage as external/non-established.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/relational_rule_coverage.clj`
  performs opt-in source-interpreted finite relational checks
  against exact current operation IDs; it does not show
  developer-specified rules are complete, or that opaque
  family callbacks reproduce every production behavior.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/application_assurance_report.clj`
  recomputes conditional, **non-authoritative** source
  assurance. Physical XTDB writer closure, real browser,
  installed server adoption, independent kernel soundness
  and full refinement remain explicit open obligations.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/physical_writer_capability_coverage.clj`
  reports host-declared potential physical write capabilities
  against a bounded protection domain. It cannot authenticate
  hidden writer absence or enforce an XTDB capability sandbox.
- `test/gesso/assurance/application_induction_mutation_test.clj`
  checks original-source identity and various real issued
  handle/route/source substitutions. Its test scope is an
  in-process host inventory, not complete production
  deployment or proof-checker soundness.
- The evidence above is from source inspection, not fresh
  execution of JVM, browser, XTDB or formal test suites.
  There is no claim Gesso has completed full HumanHelp
  acceptance or whole-deployment certification.

*** External formal verification completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: milestone-external-formal-verification
:END:

External formal completion is scoped to a declared theorem set and its production
transfer. It cannot be summarized as proof of all Gesso or all HumanHelp unless
that is the actual quantified and transferred claim.

The baseline external kernel scope follows Part X's concrete first target. It
includes:

- the formal syntax and semantics for the actual closed propositional rules;
- a machine-checked soundness theorem for those rules and formal acceptance;
- a justified correspondence from the production acceptance boundary to that
  formal judgment, including relevant decoding, normalization, and rejection;
- the typed-proposition bridge for the application-rule subset whose meaning is
  transferred through the theorem;
- binding of the formal subject and accepted evidence to the current production
  issuer, dependency interpretation, and applicable artifacts;
- explicit axioms, trusted toolchain/translation/runtime premises, theorem
  versions, and reproducible checking instructions.

This is a substantial bounded target. Further external claims about induction,
knowledge, projection, progression, or browser refinement each add their own
required theorem and correspondence obligations. A release declaring that broader
scope cannot substitute the smaller kernel target without changing its claim.

The direction of correspondence matters. For sound admission, production
acceptance must imply the applicable formal judgment under its premises.
Agreement on rejected examples or one instance of each inference tag does not
establish that statement. Execution safety additionally needs its trace or
refinement relation; liveness needs its own progress, fairness, and divergence
conditions.

A formal theorem may rely on a named trusted extraction or translation mechanism
where the selected profile permits it. The report identifies that conditional
claim and remaining trusted base. A handwritten claim that the Clojure checker
“implements the same idea” is review evidence, not the required machine-checked
correspondence. A mechanism demanded as proved cannot be replaced by conformance
vectors alone.

An exact finite audit remains exact evidence for its named finite domain. An
unbounded claim requires the relevant quantified theorem or sound abstraction
transfer. Timeout, partial exploration, unavailable checking, and unsupported
syntax remain non-passing results. A theorem about an outdated subject remains
historical until applicability to the current subject is established.

This milestone does not prove backend implementation correctness or deployed
host behavior merely because the formal kernel is sound. Those boundaries retain
the qualified premises and evidence defined in Part X.

*** Retire execution rights while preserving unresolved intent

Retirement revokes the old execution's continuation and resource permissions
before best-effort physical cleanup. Old callbacks, timers, responses, or DOM
identifiers cannot revive those rights. A late response may contain useful
command evidence, but admitting that evidence and applying its physical effect
are separate decisions.

Recoverable application context may include a stable logical route, domain
subject identifier, compatible local options, or an explicitly recoverable
draft. The current plan revalidates that context before it affects authority.
A preserved draft is not an admitted command and does not retain an old
principal's permission.

An unresolved command has a longer semantic lifetime than its originating browser
execution where the declared protocol supports recovery. Preserve its stable
=:gesso/command-id=, original operation/interface and canonical argument binding,
authority scope, and unresolved outcome obligation through the appropriate
recovery representation. A fresh delivery may have a new =:http-request-id=;
that transport identifier does not replace the command identity or the domain
Request ID.

A browser-held command reference proves neither incorporation nor permission
to disclose its result. Reconciliation uses current authentication and the
model's binding, compatibility, retention, and disclosure checks. A compatible
retry preserves the same command identity. Recovery must not silently allocate
a new identifier and repeat an intent whose first incorporation remains unknown.

Execution retirement does not convert =:commit-unknown= to =:rejected=. A current
card read can repair presentation without attributing the old command. If no
permitted command-specific evidence remains available, attribution remains
unknown even when the current card is correct.

Old observations may remain historical evidence or input to a supported recovery
contract. They cannot retain currentness, adoption rights, or effect permission
merely because their bytes survived. Similarly, provisional UI is retired or
reconstructed under the current plan; an old rollback callback cannot undo new
authoritative installation.

*** Release recovery preserves HumanHelp's adoption policy

HumanHelp's remote creation and remote lifecycle changes require explicit Refresh
under [[#decision-humanhelp-remote-lifecycle-adoption]]. Retirement, reconnect,
host replacement, or an advisory does not itself authorize acquiring and adopting
an unrelated fresh board.

The recovery contract names what local options and previously adopted membership
can be retained safely, what protected content must be retired after a known
authority-context change, and what acquisition grant permits reconstruction.
A newly admitted page initialization may have its declared baseline acquisition
grant; that fact must be explicit. It is not an accidental successor read caused
by release mismatch. If a new user action is required, the UI makes that action
available and preserves the unresolved obligation meanwhile.

Own-command settlement and specifically admitted local rejection or unknown
recovery keep their scoped exceptions from Part VIII. They cannot clear unrelated
advisory debt or import unrelated remote collection membership. A reconstructed
board also cannot regress newer locally installed authority merely because its
response belongs to the current release.

For example, Claim C was sent under the previous assembly, its response was lost,
and its browser execution was retired during deployment. Current recovery may
query C's retained record and, when permitted, display its recorded successful
outcome. It does not apply the previous execution's pending swap. A current card
acquisition determines the present Request/assignment projection. Bob's unrelated
remote board still waits for its own permitted Refresh.

*** Rolling-release acceptance cases

A release requesting overlap must demonstrate:

- every permitted client/server direction, using the actual artifact and decoder;
- every excluded direction rejecting at the intended boundary or being excluded
  by the qualified routing mechanism;
- late callbacks and deferred output remaining bound to the proper assembly and
  resource permission;
- a command incorporated before activation but resolved afterward without
  duplicate incorporation or false rejection;
- current authorization and nondisclosure after Membership or scope changes;
- asset availability and protocol retention throughout the promised window;
- sleeping-client recovery after that window ends;
- new and old workers respecting the supported durable-work and state formats;
- schema evolution and any claimed rollback eligibility;
- concurrent activation attempts preserving the declared compare-and-activate
  order;
- safe failure when a required old or new provider qualification is revoked;
- preservation of HumanHelp's selective acquisition/adoption rules.

A future-compatible decoder unit test alone does not establish a deployed
rolling-release claim. The evidence must cover the actual reachable combinations
and the mechanisms that constrain them.

*** Compositional certification completion
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: milestone-compositional-certification
:END:

This milestone establishes a working compositional assurance chain for the
required artifact classes and application scope. The exact judgments, issuer
rules, and required dependency closure are those of Part IX.

The baseline completion contract requires:

1. The closed typed rule language, its value-domain and evaluation semantics,
   conservative normalization, and malformed-input rejection to be operational
   for the supported subset.
2. Distributed use-site judgments for knowledge, provenance, authority,
   refinement, and progression, with unsupported cases remaining explicit.
3. Checked projection and executable-plan subject bindings for the claims made,
   with physical realization premises separately recorded.
4. The small proof kernel and dedicated checkers to enforce their actual closed
   rules; each accepted derivation is bound to its original subject and premises.
5. Required obligations and dependency closure to be derived from source and
   joined into the actual issuer/admission path. A merely acyclic supplied graph
   is insufficient.
6. Conditional induction, where requested, to include the actual initial-state
   and all operation-preservation obligations for every required invariant in
   its declared catalog. Unsupported operations cannot be removed from that
   catalog to keep its old claim green.
7. Assumption propagation and any claimed discharge to follow checked scope and
   substitution rules. Accepted premises stay visible in dependent conclusions.
8. Genuine in-process issuance, currentness checking, and supported serialized
   re-admission, with no copied-report path to an executable capability.
9. Actual operation, issued executor, handler, build, and assembly bindings to
   carry the appropriate evidence or explicit accepted premise.
10. Mutation and positive-control coverage that challenges these boundaries and
    exposes a useful first failure and repair owner.
11. The HumanHelp Claim pilot to connect the semantic, production, and browser
    conclusions required by [[#req-humanhelp-certification-pilot]].

The pilot must use Claim's real multi-record public contract: Request and primary
assignment, Membership and Organization dependencies, stable command binding,
compatible replay and disclosure, unknown outcome, and required publication
intent. A one-scalar-document theorem does not cover that operation merely
because the fixture is named Claim.

Abstract induction and physical writer coverage remain distinct. A theorem over
a supplied catalog cannot establish that no import, job, maintenance path, or
direct writer exists outside it. A claim requiring whole-domain preservation
must account for the real initialization and all relevant writer paths, or remain
explicitly conditional under a profile permitting those premises.

The application is allowed to use arbitrary host code behind a declared
implementation contract. Certification completion does not require proving all
that code. It requires that the supported judgment chain is real, the premises
are correctly accounted for, and mandatory proofs are actually checked. A root
report made mostly of unavailable placeholders is not the intended chain.

A conditional abstract theorem, a qualified production binding, and a passing
browser Claim case are separately identified results. Their joined report must
not promote the weakest relation into universal execution refinement. Nor does
the successful Claim pilot establish every HumanHelp product capability.

The executable semantic core remains the runtime's semantic source; the proof
kernel and dedicated checkers keep their separate roles. Optional proof sidecars
do not participate in transition decisions. Required admission gates still
control whether an object can be constructed or installed.

** Global non-goals and retained design freedom

These boundaries keep the ordinary framework smaller than a universal
distributed database, workflow engine, or theorem prover. They do not forbid an
application from choosing a stronger domain-specific mechanism.

*** Domain persistence and reconciliation remain application choices

Gesso does not require a second persisted DAG of every fact, provisional value,
command, and supersession edge. The application models and authoritative backend
own durable domain facts and the history required by their contracts. A proof
dependency graph is not a universal application fact store.

Gesso does not require Git-like branching history, event sourcing, CRDTs, or
three-way merges for every model. It may provide typed reconciliation structure;
the model owns the conflict meaning, eligible participants, and allowed
resolution. Optimism and command reconciliation do not imply automatic merge
policy.

No global integer stands for provider progression, domain revision, valid time,
command causality, and actor knowledge. Domain revisions may be monotonic where
the model declares an ordering and enforces its writer discipline. Other identities
are preserved according to their own scopes and lifetimes.

*** Placement does not require universal durable actors

Durable cross-user interactions ordinarily rendezvous through authoritative model
state and later short choreographies. A global directory of every actor's current
host, direct participant routing, and persistence of every suspended server
machine are not baseline requirements. A feature needing message receipt or
durable workflow continuation has its own explicit capability contract.

Gesso does not transparently migrate arbitrary suspended browser state between
incompatible releases. Bounded compatibility, retirement, and permitted current
reconstruction are the baseline. Domain-specific checked migrations may be added
without making all historical runtime states permanently supported.

*** The proof boundary is explicit and partial

The framework does not prove arbitrary Clojure or ClojureScript merely because
it can call the function. Full-language implementations remain first-class
through the managed interfaces and explicit premises defined in Part I. Closed
checkers reject unsupported syntax for their judgment; they do not forbid the
host-language implementation from participating under an appropriate contract.

The design does not require a universal host-language type system, unrestricted
dependent types, or application-authored proof-assistant scripts. Supported
semantic interfaces, generated obligations, and checkable evidence provide the
ordinary path.

Gesso does not prove the whole JVM, JavaScript engine, browser, HTMX, Biff,
authentication stack, XTDB implementation, operating system, hardware, or network.
The relevant contracts remain qualified premises as specified in Part X. This
is compatible with useful conditional proofs about the application's semantics.

A well-typed coherent policy may still be the wrong product choice. Gesso cannot
infer intended business policy from syntax or establish correct requirements by
checking only what the implementation chose to expose.

*** Runtime enforcement and concrete testing remain necessary

Proofs do not remove validation of untrusted inputs, current principals and
permissions, command bindings, actual provider observations, resource ownership,
or compatibility. They depend on those mechanisms supplying their concrete
premises.

Formal results do not eliminate real browser, backend, concurrency, load,
installation, or failure testing. Conversely, more tests do not silently upgrade
a tested claim into a theorem. Conditional progress retains availability,
fairness, retention, disclosure, and user-action assumptions. A timeout path does
not prove that an inactive browser or unavailable service will eventually recover.

No role is required to know every fact. Relevance, privacy, and authorization may
intentionally produce different valid projections. Agreement claims concern the
facts and frontiers actually promised.

*** Identity, sidecars, and backend choices keep their boundaries

Source identity is not semantic identity. Exact artifact identity is not semantic
proof. Optional proof and diagnostic sidecars remain nonsemantic to runtime
transitions; mandatory admission and runtime gates still enforce the selected
contract. Required durable audit or publication is an explicit effect rather
than an optional sidecar.

Database neutrality is not a goal purchased at the expense of a useful qualified
XTDB realization. Semantic contracts remain separate from accidental storage
representation, but the implementation may use provider-specific mechanisms
where they improve correctness or simplicity. Another provider must establish
its own correspondence and qualification.

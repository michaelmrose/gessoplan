*** The four scopes do not collapse into one status

Operational completion can precede external proof and production multi-node work.
A compositional pilot can proceed while other application capabilities remain
unfinished. A kernel theorem does not establish deployment resilience, and a
resilient deployment does not establish kernel soundness.

Scheduling can overlap where dependencies permit. Each completion record still
needs its own scope, requirement set, and evidence. The operational definition
cannot be weakened because proof work is advanced; the formal definition cannot
be weakened because the UI works.

*** Completion records and continued applicability

A completion record names the milestone, canonical requirements, supported
scope, admitted assembly and actual artifacts, accepted premises, evidence for
every required gate, environment, open product decisions, and exclusions. Its
complete status is computed from those records, not independently authored.

Historical completion remains evidence about the release and environment that
were checked. Later changes trigger dependency-based reconsideration of the
applicable claims. Unaffected results may be reused where their applicability is
established. Completion is neither a permanent property of a repository nor a
requirement to repeat every unrelated test after every edit.

Known premise violations invalidate dependent applicability even when the abstract
theorem remains mathematically valid. The record retains both facts. Operational
policy determines how affected work is suspended, recovered, or explicitly
restricted; an old completion badge cannot override that policy.

Documentation changes define or clarify requirements. They do not establish that
an implementation meets them.

**** Worked record: useful progress without a false completion claim

Consider a hypothetical candidate whose tests establish creation, Claim, board
options, explicit Refresh, and enforced assembly bindings, but whose response
interaction still lacks a selected product contract. It may also have useful
proof and two-host experiments. Its report must preserve the different answers:

| Completion scope | Hypothetical evidence | Reported conclusion |
|------------------+-----------------------+---------------------|
| Operational application | Several actual browser capabilities pass; required response contract and its scenario are missing | Partial and blocked for the full Part VIII milestone |
| Compositional certification | Typed Claim guard and source-bound conditional theorem pass; required broader judgment or production joins remain missing | Partial for the stated certification scope |
| External formal verification | One finite derivation audit passes; universal kernel/production correspondence is absent | Finite result reported; external baseline incomplete |
| Multi-node production | Two processes can read shared state; publication-loss and reconnect-gap cases are untested | Prototype; production milestone incomplete |

This example asserts no present implementation status. Each hypothetical passing
item would still require a real subject-bound evidence reference. The remote
lifecycle policy is selected and tested as explicit Refresh; it is not one of
the unresolved decisions in this record. The missing response contract cannot
be supplied by writing =:passed= into the aggregate map.

If a later candidate selects the response contract and satisfies every remaining
operational gate, it can become operationally complete while the other scopes
retain their own incomplete results. It may release under the permitted
operational profile. It cannot silently satisfy a requested stronger profile
by renaming its claim after that profile failed.

A downstream consumer can inspect the actual supported scope, what was checked,
which premises remain, and what blocks the stronger claim. The record should
make that answer simpler than reverse-engineering a long list of test names.

** Progress metrics and completion boundaries
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-completion-boundaries
:END:

Progress is measured by the original required behavior, supported
semantic judgments, usable physical capabilities and justified
evidence for the exact subject claimed. Revision counts, file size,
passing assertions, the number of emitted reports and the fraction
of a *conveniently discovered* operation list do not establish
release eligibility or any completion milestone.

A progress measure is a diagnostic view over source-derived
requirements and genuinely observed evidence. It is not a separate
issuer, an authorization capability, or a way to alter a release
profile after an inconvenient failure. Section 11.05 owns the
underlying root claim/evidence ledger; 11.02 owns the eight
gate-result dispositions and the four independent release
decisions. This section specifies what honest counts and
comparisons are allowed to say about that ledger.

*** Freeze the denominator before measuring the numerator

Every coverage measure identifies:

- the original independent product or assurance requirement set,
  its source owner and immutable identity for the claim being made;
- the exact claim scope, selected profile, required milestone,
  operation or artifact class, and applicable generation, host,
  provider and evaluation window;
- the predeclared inventory of required subjects, obligations,
  successful paths, failure classes and execution environments;
- the admissible evidence type, issuing checker or observing
  boundary, applicability and trust premises for each item;
- the numerator's actually qualified items, the denominator's
  complete required items, and an explicit inventory of not-yet-
  credited, failed, stale, unavailable or unsupported items;
- the validation of denominator completeness against the
  *original* source or an independently specified product
  capability contract, not merely a generated catalog; and
- any approved scope change, separate preview profile or
  genuinely inapplicable item, with the resulting changed
  claim identity and independently reviewable rationale.

A missing item has not become inapplicable just because the
application failed to construct its route, render its button,
discover a writer or issue a proof obligation. An implementation
with only three exposed operations may close those three
correctly and still fail the independently required six-operation
product scope. The exposed-operation denominator and the
required-user-capability denominator are two distinct metrics;
neither silently substitutes for the other.

A denominator may change when an independently authorized
business or release requirement actually changes. Reports
preserve the previous and new inventories, the approval and
effect on claims, and recompute coverage against the new
subject. They do not call a shrinking denominator successful
implementation progress on the unchanged original goal.
A selected stricter assurance profile cannot silently turn
a missing proof into a trusted premise, skipped test or
new smaller profile after the check fails.

A ratio is reported only where its denominator and
classification make sense. For an empty selected inventory,
report "not requested" only if that inventory was validly
excluded by the claim; report "missing requirement inventory"
where source completeness cannot be established. Empty,
unavailable or unobserved is not automatically 100% coverage.
Unknown and incomparable outcomes remain explicit rather
than receiving arithmetic credit.

*** Keep different progress dimensions separate

| Metric | Stable required denominator | What may count toward coverage | Invalid shortcut to expose |
|--------+-----------------------------+--------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Usable original product capabilities | Independently selected canonical user capability and decision inventory | Eligible positive actual-user histories and applicable recovery/rejection cases | Missing Response, Reassign or Refresh vanishes because its control is absent |
| Declared assembly closure | Managed source operations, affordances, routes, acquisitions, effects and required dependencies for the selected whole application | Each actual original-source obligation assembled and discharged at its named construction boundary | Caller-supplied closed subset replaces undiscovered source obligations |
| Runtime/production enforcement | Required real route, handler, writer, output and privileged effect-boundary classes for the actual installation | The intended current gate is reached on the production path, with exercised positive and negative cases | Test-only wrapper or locally constructed handler substitutes for deployed adoption |
| Typed semantic judgment coverage | Required type, placement, knowledge, authority, refinement and progression subject/judgment pairs | A supported checker establishes its precise source-relative conclusion | Unsupported Clojure body is relabeled a checked theorem |
| Required dependency and assumption closure | Original business, proof and application obligations, with their transitive dependencies | Every required input or explicit admissible qualified premise is accounted for | An acyclic, incomplete evidence DAG is called closed |
| Framework theorem/proof-kernel coverage | Predeclared properties and supported semantic artifact classes for the selected assurance profile | Exact required properties discharged with qualified issuers, subject identity and applicable premises | A successful one-operation finite fixture counts as whole-framework proof |
| Formal-to-executable correspondence | Named semantic-to-implementation, writer, transaction, browser, installation and deployment transfer obligations | Independently valid evidence of the specific correspondence and trusted boundaries | Source proof or matching digest is treated as physical execution |
| Mutation/defect rejection quality | Predeclared representative defect classes and intended first rejecting boundaries | Each defect is rejected at its correct boundary with useful diagnosis and a paired eligible positive | Reject-all checker, flakiness or rejection far downstream counts as success |
| Qualified substrate coverage | Actually selected backend, transaction adapter, compiler/browser runtime, host, configuration, providers and topology envelopes | Required tests/premises for those exact implementations and current conditions | A shadow adapter, old artifact or different XTDB environment is credited |
| Recovery and interruption coverage | Required loss, uncertainty, reconnect, replay, retirement and provider/host failure histories | Truthful outcomes, usable current recovery and correct owner/authority constraints under stated premises | Happy-path HTTP/SSE delivery is called eventual convergence |
| Release overlap/rollback coverage | Every actually reachable permitted or excluded client/server direction and retained work/format dependency | Qualified real routing exclusions, compatible operations, retirement and rollback state | One host switch is called globally atomic |
| Evidence reuse/impact accuracy | Applicable unchanged obligations plus affected semantic, physical and environmental change classes | Current reusable evidence and required rechecks both accounted for | An old green cache suppresses required invalidation |
| Test execution inventory integrity | All required jobs, cases, environments, shards and accepted result/receipt records | Each genuine completed and attributable test result in its declared scope | Missing worker result, truncated inventory or fabricated status is counted as passing |

Each row is a *separate* claim dimension. A release can
have complete semantic closure but lack actual installation
or user-facing acceptance; it can exercise every browser
capability without establishing a theorem; it can prove a
model invariant while its full physical writer set remains
unknown. Any combined presentation retains those separate
blockers, required evidence classes and dependency links.
No weighted "overall assurance %" can promote a missing
mandatory gate to success.

*** Measure coverage with evidence status, not optimistic arithmetic

The root evidence ledger retains the status of *every*
required gate as one of the eight classifications from
11.02: =:passed=, =:failed=, =:incomplete=, =:unavailable=,
=:unsupported=, =:skipped=, =:stale= or =:missing=.
Passing credit is possible only when the checked evidence
actually refers to the intended subject, source, current
installation/environment and selected profile. A copied
"passed" map is not evidence and has no numerator authority.

A report distinguishes at least:

| Category | Meaning for the original requested claim |
|----------+------------------------------------------|
| Required and verified in scope | Eligible to receive its precise checked credit |
| Required but not executed/observed | Still missing or incomplete; no success credit |
| Required but unavailable/unsupported | Blocking unless an independent new profile explicitly changes the claim |
| Executed and failed | Report failure, intended boundary and reproducible/unknown diagnosis |
| Previously checked but stale | Re-evaluate dependencies and required physical or source changes before reuse |
| Skipped by an approved narrower profile | Visible exclusion under a different claim, not completion of the original |
| Trusted external premise accepted by the selected profile | Conditional assumption disclosed with all dependent claims, not counted as independently proved |
| Truly inapplicable under original requirements | Exclude only with a justified, traceable scope rule, not a failing implementation |

For example, suppose the independent product requirement
set contains Create, Claim, Unclaim, Reassign, Response
and Refresh. If four have eligible actual positive-browser
evidence and Reassign and Response do not, the product
coverage is four of six, with the two missing controls
named. If the assembled UI exposes only the four successful
operations, it may have four of four *exposed-route closure*
but still has only four of six required product capability
coverage. The latter denominator does not shrink to make
the application look complete. An incomplete Response
product decision is an explicit blocker until the owner
selects and the application demonstrates its contract.

The same discipline applies to formal proof: a finite
check of selected states counts toward its *finite
subject* only. It does not count as an induction theorem,
universal checker soundness, independent proof-kernel
verification or the whole installed XTDB/browser refinement.
Counts of assumptions and trusted-base components are
audit aids, not a scalar probability of correctness.
An accepted environmental premise stays visible and
transitively attached to all claims that depend on it.

*** Diagnose first-boundary rejection and positive usefulness

Rejection quality measures the difference between a defect
discovered at the earliest justified owner and one allowed
to fail late, at an unrelated wrapper, or only after a
protected physical effect. For every included mutation
or controlled-defect family, state the intended violation,
first sound checking boundary, expected typed diagnostic,
physical effect/non-effect oracle and paired valid case.
The defect inventory is selected independently of the
mutations the implementation happens to reject.

An invalid forged principal must fail at the trusted
authorization boundary, not be "proved safe" because the
browser happened not to show the button. An incompatible
old-client command must be rejected before the real
protected writer, not merely by a test-only interceptor.
A malformed model rule rejected by a compiler only after
a missing provider crashes has not demonstrated the
intended early diagnosis. Conversely, a checker that
blocks *all* Claims, or only accepts trivial rules, has
excellent rejection arithmetic but fails positive
usefulness and the selected capabilities.

Failed or flaky tests remain accountable under the
declared test isolation, schedule and evidence policy.
A rerun that happens to pass is new observed evidence;
it does not erase a genuine unresolved nondeterministic
failure or justify excluding its original job from the
required inventory. A first-boundary requirement may
be structurally proved, physically observed, or still
an open transfer obligation; the reporting level is
not silently upgraded.

*** Preserve performance, reproducibility and reuse context

Performance measurements identify hardware, JVM/browser,
compiler, storage/backend version and configuration,
dataset and data distribution, worker count, routing
topology, resource limits, warm/cold state and executed
workload. A fast deterministic developer gate is
valuable for iteration; it is not a substitute for
required complete JVM, finite/formal, Chromium, real
XTDB, fault/reconnect or multihost coverage.

Parallel tests retain a deterministic selected inventory,
the exact job-to-shard assignment and result accounting,
isolated side effects and proof that required preflight
ran for the proper scope. Missing, timed-out, duplicated
or otherwise unauthenticated worker completion cannot
turn a release gate green. A summary from an orchestration
tool must be tied to actual executed tasks and failures
under its declared trust boundary; a copyable report
cannot authenticate execution by itself.

Incremental proof or test reuse is measured by correct
dependency invalidation and current applicability, not
only by jobs avoided. When source meaning remains stable
but actual browser bytes, handler, provider or backend
incarnation changes, the abstract theorem may remain
reusable while physical correspondence evidence must
be requalified. Source, environment, trusted premise,
toolchain and original-required-set changes have
distinct invalidation effects. Report both avoided
work *and* required reruns; reducing elapsed time by
dropping required work is a false optimization.

The release and completion milestones in 11.13–11.18
use these metric definitions to explain partial
checkpoints, not to replace the respective mandatory
gates. Project management can track iteration count,
time and document size as resource measures; these
are never evidence that a business behavior, theorem
or installed browser effect is correct.

*** Positive and adversarial measurement controls

| Honest measurement | Invalid or misleading nearby measurement |
|--------------------+------------------------------------------|
| Six independent product capabilities remain required when four are implemented | Four exposed controls are reported as 100% of the original product |
| Missing Response product decision is shown as a blocker | Response is silently removed from the requirement inventory |
| Exposed operations close against their actual original assembly source | A caller-generated subset is called complete whole-application closure |
| Current installed protected routes and writers are independently challenged | A test-only wrapper is counted as production-path enforcement |
| Supported checked rule form earns its exact theorem/judgment credit | Unsupported arbitrary Clojure is relabeled checked because it was executed |
| Trusted premise remains attached to dependent abstract proof | Accepted assumption is counted as discharged independent theorem |
| A finite model run credits only its bounded initial/transition domain | Finite sample is shown as universal XTDB/browser correspondence |
| Original mutation inventory and positive cases are predeclared | Reject-all verifier records perfect negative test percentage |
| Invalid route fails before the intended effect with useful diagnostics | Late crash after write or unrelated wrapper rejection is called first-boundary success |
| A valid real Reassign executes under the same guard challenged by rejection | Disabled or unavailable button is counted as positive capability |
| Actual compiler/host/backend configuration is measured | A passing different adapter or staging deployment counts as production |
| Command C42 remains unknown with explicit permissible recovery | Timeout is scored as a successful rejection or terminal resolution |
| A/B and B/A actual routing exposures are measured or excluded by tested guards | Happy-path sticky session is called full rolling-deployment qualification |
| New browser asset invalidates required physical checks while reusing sound abstract proof | Cached source-equivalent theorem is treated as current loaded-asset evidence |
| Each required parallel job is accounted for and its failures remain inspectable | Missing shard, stale log or rerun success silently cleans the denominator |
| Physical load measurement includes hardware, topology, limits and data | Fast one-worker test is advertised as production throughput |
| Original and changed scope are separately recorded after legitimate requirement approval | A failed requirement is dropped, producing a fake improvement trend |
| Empty required inventory without established source completeness is blocked | Empty set is treated as automatic 100% proof coverage |
| Root report exposes all missing/stale/unsupported obligations | Fabricated passing rows make a release candidate "certified" |
| Real browser positive and adversarial histories use deployed source artifacts | Generated fixture with no user control or physical effect is called end-to-end acceptance |

These are prospective measurement and acceptance obligations;
the table is not itself observed test evidence. A complete
and accurate report can be useful while the release is
blocked. It is the distinction between *what is required*,
*what has actually been established* and *what remains
open*, not a decorative percentage, that makes progress
visible and keeps completion claims honest.

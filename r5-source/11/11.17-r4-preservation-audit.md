## Editorial choice and why it matters

Retain R5's clear explanation of one release yielding four different
answers. Restore R4's valuable concrete assembly-generation,
browser-artifact, original capability set and evidence-index
coordinates, while **not** resurrecting the misleading reading that
a self-authored EDN map can admit its own release. The normative
worked data is explicitly an illustrative *explanation* with open
Response decision and missing evidence, never a deployed certificate.
Avoid pretending the currently selected advisory-only explicit
Refresh policy remains an unresolved choice; its implementation
and physical test obligations can still be incomplete.

Separate two different quaternities: admission/installation/
activation/completion are four **release decisions**, while
operational/compositional/external-formal/multi-node are four
**independent completion milestones**. A candidate can be
admitted without installation; an operational release can be
legitimate while unselected external proof remains partial.
A mandatory stronger originally requested scope cannot be
silently downgraded because it fails. This distinction
supports faithful reports, safe deployment and precise
scheduling without dictating development order.

## Thirty-two preservation and strengthening decisions

1. Four completion milestones have separate original-source and
   independent product/assurance denominators.
2. Milestones are not stage numbers or forced sequential delivery
   gates; work may overlap.
3. No generic single "Gesso complete" bit substitutes for four
   justified judgments.
4. Candidate admission, installation, activation and completion
   are another four distinct release decisions.
5. Abstract semantic success does not authenticate installed
   handler adoption or runtime operation.
6. Runtime/product success does not independently prove kernel
   soundness, complete formal transfer or multi-node resilience.
7. A specifically permitted operational profile may release with
   unselected stronger milestones incomplete.
8. Mandatory originally selected stronger profiles cannot be
   renamed or downgraded after failed checks.
9. Every record names original independent required-capability
   and assurance obligation inventory before examination.
10. Exposed route closure and original product coverage are
    separate denominators; missed source cannot disappear.
11. Original product owner selections remain explicit; HumanHelp
    Response is independently unresolved until actually chosen.
12. HumanHelp remote lifecycle has selected explicit Refresh,
    not a second open policy decision.
13. Real semantic assembly and exact candidate-generation/
    browser artifact identities are distinct coordinates.
14. The actual installed Biff handler, host, provider/backend,
    writers and assets have separate current physical subjects.
15. All gate results retain the closed eight-status vocabulary,
    with each genuine issuer/observer and evidence reference.
16. The human summary and machine-readable record derive from
    the same validated underlying claims and requirements.
17. A copyable root report or issuer-looking EDN map is never
    runtime permission or authentic physical test receipt.
18. Trusted premises and mandatory open obligations are
    separately visible and accurately propagated.
19. Failure, skipped, stale, incomplete, missing, unavailable
    and unsupported mandatory gates never count as passing.
20. Unknown/ambiguous source completeness is not 100% coverage
    because a smaller inventory happened to close.
21. A partial checkpoint retains precise completed and missing
    product and assurance scope without becoming a certificate.
22. Worked record is deliberately illustrative, with explicit
    fictional IDs and non-authoritative passing labels.
23. A former completion is historical evidence about the
    exact observed release and environment, not permanent
    property of a repository or version string.
24. Changed source/normalizer/checker/issuer demands the
    appropriate semantic or formal invalidation.
25. Changed backend, provider, handler, writer, serving
    browser asset or routing topology demands the
    appropriate physical requalification.
26. Unaffected genuine proof/evidence may be reused where
    its *current* applicability is independently established.
27. A mathematically valid abstract theorem may remain
    valid after physical correspondence becomes stale.
28. Known false trusted premise or revoked Membership
    fences current protected effects independent of any
    historical badge or printed release success.
29. Failed replacement cannot fall back to a now-unsafe
    previous assembly simply because it had once passed.
30. Documentation/editorial acceptance can change or
    clarify requirements but cannot certify implementation
    or replace executed physical acceptance.
31. Uncertainty, advisory debt, release compatibility,
    finite bounds and conditional liveness stay attached
    to the appropriate exact milestone scope.
32. The release-change acceptance in 11.18 must test
    credible positives and negatives at real
    evidence/issuer and installed-effect boundaries.

R4's detailed worked example is preserved in its essential
identity, artifact, four-milestone and evidence/assumption
dimensions. Its hypothetical already-closed Response flow
is updated to reflect Part VIII's selected/open distinction.
R5's improved fidelity on one incomplete release with
partial but useful work remains. No essential R4
scope/record applicability guarantee is dropped.

## Implementation correspondence and limits — Gesso v1622

The following source files were inspected at the pinned
application-assurance commit; this is code/source review,
not execution of the user's current application.

- src/gesso/assurance/report.clj builds source-bound
  **reporting-only** summaries over specified in-process
  executor/certificate/obligation inputs and optional
  assembly. Its own contract excludes independent theorem
  proving, runtime authority, authentic external test
  receipt verification and deployment admission.
  Caller-reported test observations remain caller claims.
- src/gesso/assurance/proof/application_assurance_report.clj
  recomputes a conditional original-source application
  summary with identified open proof/writer/XTDB/browser
  transfer obligations. It explicitly refuses to
  treat a copied source report as admission.
- src/gesso/assurance/proof/certificate.clj checks scoped
  in-process proof handles and source/dependency
  currentness; these are not signed deployable
  release certificates, all-writer completeness or
  independent kernel meta-soundness.
- src/gesso/live/application_preflight.clj establishes
  selected application and canonical Biff/rendered
  surface preflight checks, with explicit deployment
  escape routes and no proof that an installed listener
  uses all the checked bindings.

These are valuable narrower building blocks, not a
completed independent four-milestone root issuer.
Their source-relative reports are not authenticated
physical observations. No JVM, XTDB, Chromium,
external formal checker, current HumanHelp,
installed host or multi-host acceptance test
was run for this editorial revision.

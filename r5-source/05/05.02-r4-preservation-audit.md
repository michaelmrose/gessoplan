# R5.1 Part V — 05.02 R4 preservation and Gesso correspondence

**Baseline:** `gessoplan/main`, R4 Part V's worked optimism/Claim
example and the sections "Provisional state cannot confer authority",
"Settlement and authoritative observation are complementary",
"Authoritative supersession prevents obsolete restoration", and
"Multiple provisional views do not fork authority".
**Candidate:** `r5.1` source 05.02, building on reviewed 05.01,
accepted Parts I–IV and the production HumanHelp model.
**Disposition:** substantive 05.02 review, not whole-Part-V
acceptance or a tested/certified deployed implementation.

## R4 requirements preserved

- R4's semantic provisional state is neither a predicted database
  commit nor an authorization token. A trusted model operation, not
  the browser, decides actual Claim eligibility and incorporation.
- C42 semantic command, E42 local execution, settlement timer/owner,
  admitted observation and visible provisional DOM have separate
  identities and lifetimes; callbacks cannot transfer retired rights.
- The R4 direct confirmation, actual rejection, timeout, authoritative
  reread/supersession and conditional rollback histories survive
  in more precise R5.1 form. Recovery of the current view is not
  proof of original command causality.
- An old snapshot may be restored only under current physical
  ownership, scope and observation compatibility. A late failure
  cannot replace newer authoritative content.
- R4's two conflicting Alice/Bob provisional views do not fork the
  authoritative Request history; the model resolves concurrency.
  The follow-on R5.1 05.03 owns the full local overlay conflict policy.
- The later R4 requirement that browsers must not silently downgrade
  a semantically optimistic operation remains in 05.03 and the
  prepared-view/application-assembly obligations.

## R5 improvements retained and focused repairs

1. **Trust and disclosure.** Kept current new-command authorization
   distinct from immutable historical command binding, authorized
   resolution and permission to disclose past outcomes. Revocation
   neither undoes a prior incorporation nor confers future disclosure.
2. **Attempt versus command.** Added a separate physical-attempt row
   to participant state. Terminal rejection requires the qualified
   binding-scoped exclusion/fencing guarantee from Part IV. A failed
   A1 guard does not settle a compatible A2 or all of C42.
3. **Production RequestAssignment.** The admitted initial projection
   may summarize the absence of an active primary helper only through
   its qualified RequestAssignment relationship. Neither a scalar
   helper field nor a stale pre-read becomes a commit-current guard.
4. **Semantic retirement versus physical display.** Made explicit
   that releasing E42 does not elevate a marked provisional DOM node
   to authoritative. Real physical completion and independent
   acquire/adopt obligations remain necessary.
5. **Independent recovery and later Reassign.** Success of an old
   C42 Claim remains historical when a later Reassign establishes Bob
   as current helper. A new recovery owner retains its own rights;
   command C42 alone does not revive E42's retired state.
6. **Conditional physical rollback.** Restoring a captured visual
   baseline requires the current owner, scope, disclosure and physical
   mutation rights. A failed/partial compensation retains repair debt.
7. **Paired acceptance histories.** Added five positive/adverse
   cases for success versus DOM adoption, attempt versus rejection,
   timeout and inherited rights, historical replay versus current
   Reassign, and safe versus stale/partial restoration.

The R5.1 architecture is preferred to simply copying R4's long,
illustrative helper-id code: model policy stays source-owned, the
browser projection is not domain authority, and the full-language
presentation implementation remains available under admitted contracts.
These normative requirements describe finished Gesso, not the
limitations of a particular existing browser adapter.

## Actual Gesso code consulted

`gesso/application-assurance` at `1b5e10e7` (v1622):

- `src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc` defines distinct optimistic
  terminal dispositions. Qualified confirmed/reconciled settlement
  returns `:await-authority`; timeout/network/incompatible-protocol
  follows guarded rollback-and-refresh or refresh. A local resolution
  name is not the backend's authoritative proof.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/optimistic.cljs` leaves provisional DOM
  marked until canonical Live/HTMX replacement, correlates settlement
  with execution/command IDs, and does not let retired callbacks
  directly adopt current authority.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/optimistic_htmx.cljs` uses HTMX
  request preparation/physical correlation, preserves malformed
  settlement as a diagnostic rather than inventing a model outcome,
  and has separate failure/retirement hooks. This does not certify
  trusted principal authorization or actual XTDB causal commit.
- Source-inspected tests include
  `timeout-rolls-back-and-requests-authoritative-refresh-test`,
  `stale-timeout-callback-after-settlement-has-no-physical-authority-test`,
  `uncertain-transport-failure-uses-adapter-recovery-without-settlement-test`,
  `authoritative-supersession-releases-provisional-without-rollback-test`
  and `rollback-refuses-to-overwrite-physically-newer-owner-test`.

No Gesso tests were executed. Those source/test inspections do not
establish all physical completion or recovery behavior, application
policy adequacy, the production RequestAssignment guards,
installed Biff/XTDB correspondence, or the universal Choreo/browser
refinement expected of the completed framework.

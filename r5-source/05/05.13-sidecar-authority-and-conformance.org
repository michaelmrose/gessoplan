*** Sidecars carry no authority

A browser-side sidecar cannot establish principal identity, current model
permission, authoritative state, subscription authorization, or permission to
read protected data. It cannot authenticate a principal, authorize a
protected Request/RequestAssignment, warrant a model transaction outcome,
claim a current board observation, establish a Live subscription or extend
the disclosure of a historical result. The server accepts commands under
its trusted boundary, regardless of the browser's proof-looking material.

Nor may a digest-matching sidecar turn a reported compiler theorem into
the browser's own permission to send, install, reread, settle, reveal or
retry. The trust boundary must be the same with no sidecar, a valid
sidecar, or a maliciously forged one. A user-visible diagnostic may
truthfully explain why an action is unavailable, but never supply a
missing trusted authorization or an executable workaround.

Sidecars can themselves reveal source details, policy names, or private values.
Production packaging omits them by default; authorized diagnostic distribution
follows the application's disclosure contract. Compiler source paths,
policy names, proof premises, operation IDs, observation references,
diagnostic traces and redacted values are each assessed for disclosure.
The reader's identity, permitted Organization/Location, tenant scope,
retention period and intended diagnostic purpose govern distribution
independently of exact executable identity. A complete copied sidecar
or trace from another authorized context is not implicitly disclosable
to the current browser or user. Normal execution must remain
complete when sidecars are absent; a mandatory semantic audit or
build-time proof admission remains separately mandatory.

The implementation can enforce part of this separation by dependency direction
and closed runtime formats, and test it with missing, malformed, mismatched,
throwing, and resource-heavy observers. Such checks support their stated scope;
they do not establish a sandbox for arbitrary same-process trusted code.
An optional observer that requires unbounded time, possesses mutable
owner/DOM handles, or can block required effects needs a different isolation
contract; merely ignoring its return value does not ensure noninterference.
Optional diagnostic failure is not equivalent to a failed command or
installation.

*** Build output and assembly closure

Build output supplies machine-checkable correspondence among the current
semantic subject, projected plan set, registered implementation closure, runtime
contract, packaged assets, and optional exact sidecars. Test, diagnostic, and
production modes execute the same admitted semantic payload for a given build.
The construction evidence explicitly binds the complete role/operation
inventory, trusted route and handler closure, physical transport, semantic
implementation contracts, generated plan set, runtime interpretation, and
actual asset bytes for every served entrypoint within the claim's coverage.
Compiler output, an in-memory ApplicationAssembly report and the
installation/serving environment remain distinct subjects.

Part VII's [[#req-assembly-construction-phases][assembly admission phases]] verify
that the served application loads the required artifacts and bindings. A source
file containing a correct plan is insufficient when the browser actually loads
an older bundle. A browser's claimed artifact identity is likewise not server
authorization. A valid rendered-surface preflight report applies to the
surface and handler path actually inspected; it does not prove that every
possible dynamic Clojure branch, pre-serialized HTML, alternate Biff
entrypoint or later middleware/asset replacement was covered.
Changes to the effective route, handler, compiled asset,
semantic dependency or accepted backend premise require the
applicable construction/recovery/requalification policy.

The build may legitimately transform or optimize code. Any transformation that
changes the object covered by a claim needs the declared correspondence or
requalification. Sidecar separation avoids a second proof-annotated runtime;
it does not excuse testing only an unoptimized development realization while
claiming the production shell has been exercised. Exact plan identity,
accepted source-relative structural proof, bounded JVM/Node/Chromium
semantic conformance, physical HTMX/DOM execution, trusted XTDB
write/readback correspondence, and a served production bundle each
require evidence at their own boundaries. A successful HTTP 200,
sidecar match or copied build stamp cannot substitute for any
missing physical or product-positive observation.

** Conformance and acceptance obligations

The same canonical semantics must support construction checks, runtime
interpretation, and focused conformance cases. Part IX supplies certification
judgments; Part X supplies the stronger soundness and implementation
correspondence arguments. This part requires the following cases at their
appropriate boundaries.

The acceptance report maintains three independent judgments:
(1) model/business-rule correctness under stated provider and writer
premises; (2) closed application assembly and actually enforced
rendered/served path coverage; and (3) behavioral completeness of an
independently specified, usable HumanHelp product. None implies either
other judgment. Each result records its exact source/version, premises,
positive and adverse witnesses, coverage, scope and unresolved or
inconclusive obligations. A failing or absent witness is not rewritten
as an accepted assumption to produce a green overall status.

|------+-------+-------------------|

*** Independent positive HumanHelp witnesses

The independent Part VIII product catalog determines the expected
capabilities before inspecting what the view happened to render. The
following witnesses must be reachable under eligible fixtures, with
genuine source-owned model transitions and actual browser presentation:

| Product path | Positive user action and observable result | Nearby adverse control |
|--------------+--------------------------------------------+------------------------|
| Authenticate and Create | A real eligible user opens Create, enters valid content and commits one Request in the authorized Organization/Location; a qualified correlated result becomes promptly visible to the creator. | A provisional preview, modal close or success toast alone is not incorporation; other actors do not automatically adopt that Request. |
| Claim and retry | An eligible helper claims an open Request through the installed control; the production model establishes the active primary RequestAssignment, and a compatible C42 retry reports its historical outcome without a second incorporation. | Stale Membership, competing Claim, invalid request state and failed physical submission retain their different authoritative/unknown classifications. |
| Lifecycle and manager Reassign | Eligible actors can Unclaim, mark on the way, complete or cancel under their respective real model policies; an authorized manager discovers a different eligible helper, selects and confirms Reassign, and observes the guarded replacement plus assignment history. | A forever-disabled Reassign picker, self-Claim masquerading as Reassign, or a selected helper whose eligibility was never rechecked does not count. |
| Local board and continuity | An authorized complete active-and-terminal Request cohort for the selected Location supports local search, newest/oldest, mine-first, unclaimed-first, terminal visibility, open details, focus and scroll preservation over its actually adopted cohort. | Local filtering does not silently broaden authoritative membership, leak protected content or overwrite a newer post-capture edit; showing terminal entries cannot silently acquire another cohort. |
| Selective adoption and one Refresh | Bob receives an advisory for Alice's R but R stays absent; Bob creates S and sees his own correlated S even while a local filter excludes it; one explicitly granted Refresh installs an adequate observation covering R and S and discharges only covered obligations. | Changing search cannot pre-adopt R; clearing the filter before asserting S's independent visibility, testing before the advisory arrives, or silently issuing extra refreshes would make the scenario vacuous. |
| Uncertainty and recovery | The UI distinguishes qualified acceptance, terminal rejection and unknown delivery, and provides usable recovery for stale view, disconnect and partial installation under declared availability/fairness premises. | Neither a new card nor an HTTP response is silently treated as the old command's attribution; old callbacks cannot clobber newer installed authority. |
| Response interaction | The product exposes and exercises the separately selected response-flow contract, with its independent declared authoritative/presentation result. | An as-yet-unselected response meaning cannot be invented from Claim merely to satisfy a capability count. |

For every promised capability, a complete positive witness exercises
the actual admitted operation and physical result, not an inspection
fixture that simply returns =:available= or =:installed=.
The default authorized board cohort includes active and terminal Requests
under its declared complete collection bounds; local Show terminal,
text search and sorting cannot manufacture a fresh acquisition grant.
The R/S history permits one explicitly granted Refresh cycle. An
invalidation arriving during its installation remains an independent
obligation unless the admitted completed observation actually covers it;
a hidden second Refresh cannot make a defective test pass.
Controls legitimately unavailable under product policy remain valid
negative cases, but an eligible action permanently hidden or
unconstructible is a behavioral-completeness failure even when
assembly safety checks pass.

*** Evidence boundaries and acceptance disposition

For each conformance case the report identifies the operation, accepted
premises, independently expected behavior, test entrypoint, exact
version/build/subject, observed trustworthy source and physical
effects, comparison oracle, and coverage exclusions. Pure plan
tests prove only their scoped transition relation; Node or JVM parity
covers sampled semantics, Chromium tests cover exercised host
interactions, finite model exploration covers its declared bound,
and trusted backend correspondence depends on qualified physical
writer/readback evidence. A source-derived diagnostic or matching
digest is not itself an application certificate.

A negative test must reach the particular rejecting boundary
instead of failing earlier due to an unrelated malformed fixture.
A positive control with the minimally changed valid premise
must succeed at that same boundary. An unknown/unavailable
provider, inconclusive proof budget or missing production
deployment evidence remains an explicitly unresolved claim
rather than an empty success.

Whole-Part-V acceptance additionally requires reconciliation of
all thirteen reviewed source sections against R4, their internal
joins and Parts I–IV/VI–X, plus independent inspection of the
assembled publication and evidence limits. Completing the 05.13
sectional audit alone does not accept Part V or certify the
framework implementation.

Negative tests must fail for the intended reason, not because an unrelated
malformation prevents reaching the behavior under examination. Positive cases
must retain ordinary raw HTMX, explicit non-optimistic modes, valid retries,
full-language implementations, permitted partial observations, and useful
application-defined resolution strategies under their contracts.

Pure tests compare admitted transitions and effects; shell tests exercise actual
completion, ownership races, and failure handling; cross-host tests exercise the
portable representation; asset checks bind the shipped realization. Qualified
production model tests must also witness the protected RequestAssignment
transition and applicable writer/invariant coverage. Passing these
tests does not promote asserted callbacks, backend premises, or browser
behavior into general proofs. Different test types may share a
semantic plan without sharing an automatically proven implementation.

Completion means the declared presentation path can execute and recover with
truthful state classifications. A page that starts, emits valid-looking markup,
or returns HTTP 200 has not thereby satisfied this part.

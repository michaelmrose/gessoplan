*** Binding sidecars to the actual compilation

Compiler correspondence is generated alongside the executable plan from the
same admitted semantic subject. It records the actual mapping, including
synthetic states or compacted continuations, rather than reconstructing it later
from similar state names. The compiler's source-to-runtime mapping includes
direct and synthetic states, semantic continuations and every source
alternative intentionally collapsed into one receive or runtime locator.
One compact locator need not mean one unique authored predecessor; a
diagnostic explanation may not silently discard other sources.

The sidecar, structural projection evidence and concrete correspondence
witnesses are bound to the admitted global semantic subject and the
actual complete role-to-ExecutablePlan set. Identical local plans may
still arise from distinct global choreographies with different terminal
outcomes. Exact plan identity cannot authenticate global source meaning
or a claim about installed opaque implementations.

A sidecar inspection view can be:

#+begin_src clojure
{:sidecar/version 1
 :role :browser
 :executable-identity executable-identity
 :semantic-subject-ref semantic-subject-ref
 :checker-result-ref checker-result-ref
 :implementation-closure-ref implementation-closure-ref
 :locations
 {installation-complete-locator
  {:semantic-id :request-claim/presentation-installed
   :source-ref source-ref
   :claims
   [{:proposition-ref adoption-proposition-ref
     :mechanism :checked-derivation
     :evidence-ref derivation-ref
     :premises [shell-completion-premise
                current-owner-premise]
     :coverage-ref coverage-ref}]}}}
#+end_src

The locator comes from the actual emitted plan mapping. The symbols above denote
bound values; replacing them with copied identifiers cannot authenticate the
claims. The sidecar may contain checked and unverified claims with distinct
status. Its existence does not prove every listed obligation was discharged.
A structural projection certificate establishes its named structural
property; a lockstep or weak concrete correspondence witness establishes
only its replayed schedule and premise-relative observable trace.
A correctly bound *failed* witness remains failed, not successfully
proved merely because exact digest matching succeeded. Finite replay
does not quantify over all fair schedules, nor certify actual browser
installation, backend execution or HumanHelp product capability.

Attaching behavioral correspondence evidence requires the exact semantic and
executable subject to agree with the artifact being explained. Evidence for
choreography A cannot be composed with an artifact generated from choreography
B merely because their operation names or state counts match.

Diagnostic admission first validates the sidecar format and exact executable
identity. Where tooling labels claims checker-verified, it additionally validates
provenance, the checker result, applicable premise set, and implementation
binding. A fabricated sidecar can copy the digest. Exact correspondence and
trusted claim acceptance are separate checks. A copied =:valid? true= or
=:mechanism :checked-derivation= is a claim, not a trusted checker result.
The admission mechanism must identify its proof/checker version,
source inputs, witness, result, named assumptions and dependencies
under an accepted trust boundary. A source-bound conditional theorem,
a finite witness, an externally asserted action and a physically
installed realization have distinct evidence statuses.

If construction requires a proof or preflight result, enforce that
required gate at build/assembly admission, independently of optional
runtime diagnostics. Omitting a sidecar from production neither
revokes a previously admitted executable nor waives a required build
failure. Attaching one cannot authorize a rejected construction.

If exact identity mismatches, diagnostics must refuse correspondence for that
plan. They cannot guess by timestamps, similar names, or near-matching runtime
coordinates. Ordinary execution may continue if its own admission and
compatibility remain valid. An unavailable explanation is not an executable
failure and is not evidence of a safe transition either.

*** Observe the selected transition, not a recomputation

A trace contains the locator selected by the actual semantic step, its admitted
subject identity, and a bounded description of its result/effects. The observer
uses that locator to retrieve correspondence. It must not rerun guards or
transition selection independently to guess what happened. A stale,
ignored, malformed or rejected-before-selection event may have no
selected transition locator; do not invent a successful one for it.
Where a generated locator has multiple source origins, preserve the
actual admitted alternative and the complete compiler mapping.
Host-effect failure after a valid semantic step is a distinct,
correlated physical outcome, not a retroactive rewrite of its locator
or a fabricated domain rejection.

Recomputation can diverge if callbacks are effectful, registries change, state is
not identical, or the observer has different inputs. Even when the pure core
would reproduce the same result, reporting the actual selected locator gives a
clearer correspondence contract.

Optional diagnostic access is read-only. It does not receive mutable live
objects or authority-bearing handles it can modify. Redacted immutable trace
values are preferable to whole contexts, DOM captures, credentials, or unrestricted
command payloads. Missing or redacted data is reported as such; an explanation
must not invent an unavailable premise. The observer receives an immutable,
bounded diagnostic projection rather than aliases to mutable owner
registries, resource handles or privileged server facts. Redaction
must precede disclosure to a less-trusted observer; copying a current
scope token or executable digest into the trace does not grant
its recipient authority or permission to read private data.

*** Diagnostic noninterference

For the same admitted initial state, canonical plan, runtime interpretation,
and normalized event sequence, adding, removing, corrupting, or replacing a
sidecar must not change semantic transitions or abstract effects, apart from
permitted diagnostic output.

#+begin_example
semantic-trace(run(plan, initial-state, events))
  = semantic-trace(run-with-diagnostics(plan, initial-state, events))
#+end_example

This is a conditional noninterference claim over fixed semantic inputs. It does
not claim instrumentation has no wall-clock, memory, scheduling, or resource
cost, or that every real browser receives the same event sequence with debugging
enabled. The fixed-input comparison includes step classifications,
portable semantic state, command and resource ownership, outstanding
acquire-and-adopt obligations, and the ordered required abstract effects,
modulo optional diagnostic observations. A stronger assertion about
physical scheduling or fairness requires separately stated nonblocking
and effect-delivery premises. Stripping diagnostic-only adapter effects
from a pure test is not a proof that arbitrary same-process custom
observers cannot block a real browser.

Excluding a sidecar parameter is necessary but insufficient. Ambient mutable
registries, shared callbacks, exception propagation, hidden source lookups, or
observer decisions fed back into admission can reintroduce a control path.
Module/API boundaries and implementation contracts must prevent those paths.

Optional diagnostics must not gate required effects or completion delivery. The
normal path records the actual transition and issues its required work according
to the semantic contract. A bounded best-effort trace queue can feed observers
independently; overflow drops diagnostic work under a visible diagnostic policy,
not semantic work. Diagnostic capture, redaction and bounded queue
admission must not delay or reject a required semantic effect merely
because a trace sink is full or malformed. A required correctness
or compliance audit instead belongs to a declared semantic effect
with a real durability and failure/recovery contract, not an
optionally dropped diagnostic queue.

Catching an observer exception does not contain a nonterminating or blocking
observer. In-process custom observers require an explicit bounded/nonblocking
premise, or execution isolation adequate for the promised containment. The
framework cannot prove arbitrary observer code harmless just because its return
value is ignored. Redaction and diagnostic failure reporting must also be
bounded and contained. A =try=/=catch= around user observer code
does not stop a callback that never returns. The claim must either
name an admitted bounded/nonblocking observer premise or provide
isolation and enforceable resource budgets appropriate to the
promised containment. Diagnostic failure may not be reclassified
as command rejection, accepted incorporation or physical installation.

If an audit action is required for the operation's correctness or compliance
contract, it is a declared semantic effect with its own failure policy. It must
not hide inside an optional observer whose loss is advertised as irrelevant.

*** Paired sidecar and observer conformance cases

| Eligible positive case | Adverse or incomplete case | Required result |
|------------------------+----------------------------+-----------------|
| The actual selected runtime locator is reported with all merged receive alternatives and generated origins. | An observer reruns a branch guard, guesses a nearby source name, or drops the other predecessor of a compacted state. | Use the selected locator and full emitted compiler correspondence; do not manufacture a unique source branch. |
| A structural certificate and a valid concrete witness are checked against the exact current role-plan set and admitted global source. | Another global choreography yields the same executable plans but a different terminal proposition; alternatively a correctly bound witness fails. | Bind actual source and checker premises separately from digests; report failure and concrete-witness scope honestly. |
| The same admitted state/event corpus runs with no, matching, and corrupted sidecars. | A diagnostic lookup affects ownership, accepted events, required effect ordering or adoption debt, or falls back to similar locators. | Identical semantic traces modulo diagnostic output; refuse bad explanations without changing independently valid execution. |
| A bounded observer reads a redacted immutable trace; overflow drops optional diagnostics alone. | A throwing, indefinitely blocking, mutable-registry-writing or credential-leaking observer is given host authority. | Enforce isolation/bounded premises and prior redaction; diagnostic failure cannot affect required semantic work. |
| A required audit is declared and executed as a protected semantic effect with recovery obligations. | A compliance-critical audit is hidden in a lossy optional trace queue and silently dropped. | Preserve the audit's declared correctness and durability requirements independently of sidecar presence. |

Positive tests must exercise the intended compiler, transition and host
effect boundaries; neither a copied digest nor a reject-all observer
can establish full correspondence or noninterference.

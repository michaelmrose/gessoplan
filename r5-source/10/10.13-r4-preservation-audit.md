## Preservation decision

The R5 contract is stronger than R4 in requiring selected-backend, observed, versioned and limited qualification. Keep it. Recover R4's explicit logical, browser, transport, authentication, provider, host, toolchain and cryptographic trust inventory, and preserve its machine-proved/kernel-checked/exact-finite/assembly/runtime/test/asserted/trusted distinctions. An assurance ledger is not proof by itself. R4's later security and proof-artifact examples remain owned by 10.14 and must be reviewed there, rather than duplicating them in 10.13 or losing them across a section split.

## Retained and strengthened requirements

1. Every root guarantee has an exact source, proposition, premise closure, implementation and deployment scope; no global proof boolean substitutes for it.
2. The original model/operation/invariant/writer/actor/host/capability universes and sampled physical observations remain separate coverage obligations.
3. Machine proof, kernel checking, exact finite checking, assembly verification, runtime enforcement, tests, assertions and trust do not imply one another.
4. Unknown, stale, unsupported, refuted, unrun and inconclusive remain explicit result classes; missing evidence is not a pass.
5. Small proof kernels, source inventories and namespace line counts are useful audit scope but not soundness theorems.
6. R4's JVM/Clojure/runtime/library premises survive, qualified by the behavior actually used.
7. R4's JS/CLJS/browser primitive and effect-time ownership premises survive; reducer-level checking does not prove DOM effects.
8. R4's HTMX/SSE/Ring/Aleph/network request, delivery and actual-response behavior survives as a separately named boundary.
9. Authentication, current session/principal, protected read/write/subscription and disclosure are not conferred by certificate-looking data.
10. XTDB transaction, basis, encoding, connection and consistency behavior is a claim-scoped, actual-provider contract.
11. Host/OS/network/durable state/clocks and liveness/fairness premises are not mathematical theorem results.
12. External formal checkers, axioms, importer/parsing and kernel implementation are named at their exact checked result.
13. Normalization, compilation, emitted asset and deployment are external correspondence links, not denotational equivalence by digest.
14. Cryptographic hash, origin, key/canonicalization assumptions cannot establish proposition truth without independent evidence.
15. A root ledger preserves transitive trusted, opaque and asserted premises; evidence quantity is not proof confidence.
16. Test evidence names actual issuer, observed node, basis, environment, time and checker/test run, never just caller metadata.
17. Backend qualification consists of original-source coverage, selected lowering, independent real-provider challenge, and installed-path applicability.
18. A controlled test database is not automatically the production backend, and one pinned read is not the transaction-internal snapshot.
19. Qualified guard and update atomicity must be challenged under concurrent Claim/Membership and writer contention.
20. Complete-document frames require absent versus nil, added/removed/top-level/nested, codec and concurrent-field treatment.
21. Absence, relationship and uniqueness assumptions need competing insert and association-change schedules.
22. Public submit ID, execute return, assertion rejection, exception, lost reply and durable command outcome have different epistemic meaning.
23. Read basis, waited-for state, valid time, transaction/system time and application revision cannot be conflated.
24. Committed publication intent, crash windows, dispatcher retry and acknowledgment need actual recovery observables.
25. Complete history and ABA/revision claims require all writers, delete/recreate, restoration and overflow qualification.
26. Qualification monitors known changes and observed contradictions; no finite corpus guarantees discovery of every future defect.
27. A tested backend behavior does not establish original-source-to-installed-writer correspondence or production authorization.
28. Original Gesso/Clojure authoring remains canonical; users do not maintain a parallel backend/formal specification language.

## Actual v1622 correspondence and open limits

- `src/gesso/assurance/assumption.clj`: subject/scoped **unadmitted** premise descriptions; no discharge or opaque callback proof.
- `src/gesso/assurance/report.clj`: in-process source-bound root report; user-supplied observations are not independently authenticated.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/physical_writer_inventory.clj`: host-observed registration survey, not completeness of direct/external writers.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_writer_coverage.clj`: issued callback identity for surveyed host registrations, not deployed writer or database authorization.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_complete_guard_execution.clj`: bounded top-level CAS plus field-count ASSERT; SQL ASSERT behavior is a premise, not an XTDB isolation theorem.
- `src/gesso/model/tx_refinement_xtdb.clj`: controlled node pre/post and optional ledger-window observations, not transaction-internal read-state or production provenance.
- `src/gesso/live/consistency/xtdb.clj`: explicit `submit-tx` ID-only versus `execute-tx` return and per-query consistency semantics.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_submission_outcome_correlation.clj`: one attempt and ledger corroboration, without authenticated submitted payload or node.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_atomic_submitted_readback.clj`: pinned readback linked to local source-captured return; no universal causality or external-writer closure.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_command_outcome_bridge.clj`: v1622 disclosure-gated agreement with caller-reported request outcome, without independent authentication of principal, arguments, reported response or installed plan.
- `deps.edn`: current XTDB 2.2.0-beta1, Biff 2.0.2, Clojure 1.12.5 selections are version-specific implementation facts, not timeless requirements or proven provider correctness.

These modules are meaningful conditional precursors, not a closed qualification ledger or completed real-backend fault-injection suite. No current universal all-writer, production XTDB causality, transport, formal kernel or browser guarantee follows.

## Eighteen paired positive/adversarial acceptance controls

| # | Positive condition | Adversarial/negative companion |
|---|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| 1 | Ledger preserves exact proposition and inherited premises | One missing critical opaque premise must block unconditional claim |
| 2 | Checked result refers to an actual trusted issuer/run | Copied checker label or fingerprint cannot manufacture proof |
| 3 | Observed serving/node identity is qualified | Caller-invented deployment metadata cannot establish provenance |
| 4 | Real XTDB eligible Claim completes under guarded write | Concurrent revocation or prior stale pre-read cannot bypass the guard |
| 5 | Domain and command history incorporate atomically | Duplicate command/failed assertion cannot leave half a pair |
| 6 | Full replacement preserves unrelated fields | Added, deleted, absent/nil or nested field causes detectable frame error |
| 7 | Qualified one-primary assignment remains unique | Competing inserts and association changes cannot both win |
| 8 | Public execute receipt means its documented result | Submit ID, exception or lost response cannot be promoted to commit |
| 9 | Pinned observation matches tracked state | Separate read basis or ledger co-observation cannot prove causality |
| 10 | Explicit read basis is truly satisfied | Echoed await token or lagging reader cannot claim freshness |
| 11 | Historical command is resolved with valid disclosure | Revoked/mismatched principal cannot leak history or automatically retry |
| 12 | Committed outbox intent recovers after fault | Crash between commit and dispatch cannot silently lose required work |
| 13 | Covered writer revision obeys the declared discipline | Raw writer, delete/recreate, restore or overflow defeats universal history claim |
| 14 | Codec preserves semantic presence and values | Unsupported encoding/nil handling cannot alter guards silently |
| 15 | Qualified provider/adapter version retains tested semantics | Version, driver, topology or transaction change requires new evidence/bridge |
| 16 | Incomplete/unsupported environment is reported honestly | No backend available cannot count as passing tests |
| 17 | Abstract theorem stays valid under original premises | Changed physical provider makes current applicability stale, not theorem false |
| 18 | Diagnostics are useful and redacted | Protected documents/credentials/command history or raw exception must not leak |

All eighteen are *future* test obligations, not tests run here.

## R4 boundary split and next section

This review covers R4's assurance ledger and explicit trusted boundaries. The same R4 parent also requires forged certificate rejection, runtime authorization on hostile browser inputs, hash-versus-proof discipline, shared-bug mitigation, no circular certificates, no assertion inflation, fingerprint under/over-approximation, sidecar noninterference, and honest conditional verification. R5.1 assigns those to existing **10.14**, whose substantive review is next. 10.15 retains the physical validation/failure ladder; 10.16–10.20 the distributed trust contract.

Neither this preservation audit nor a successful structural reassembly asserts that Gesso v1622 or HumanHelp passes any unexecuted provider/backend gate.

** Formal proof strategy and production correspondence
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-formal-production-correspondence
:END:

Formalization removes a *named* trust gap in the executable Gesso
semantics. The source-owned semantic core, original-source obligation
derivation, small proof kernel, specialized semantic checkers,
generated formal subjects, proof-producing elaboration and external
proof/model checkers participate in one correspondence architecture.
They are not alternative applications, proof certificates for
arbitrary Clojure, or a request that application developers maintain
a second handwritten TLA+, Quint, Recife or Lean system. A successful
formal result applies to installed software only through the separate
relation and premises actually justified at that boundary.

Part IX governs canonical judgments, source-complete derivation,
issuer-controlled admission, dependency evolution and premise
accounting. Sections 10.01–10.03 specify the one-source semantic
ownership and theorem quantifiers. This section defines the
*bidirectional correspondence and evidence-transfer discipline*.
10.05–10.09 own the selected transaction, guard, writer and
HumanHelp-specific realization obligations; they are not
preemptively proved by a formal correspondence report.

*** Source-derived correspondence record and two directions of traceability

For every formal claim whose guarantee is to be transferred,
the original-source authority and trusted consumer derive an
inspectable correspondence record with these distinct components:

1. The original admitted model/rule/operation/Choreo source,
   normalized semantic subject, required original initialization,
   operations and invariants, and exact versioned interpretation.
2. The formal encoding and proof obligation, quantification and
   environment domain, proof or model-check result, checker and
   translator identities, required evidence class and all
   remaining formal premises.
3. The proposed executable realization: issued operation and plan,
   callable/closure, route and model binding, compiled/browser
   artifact, assembly and selected installation generation.
4. An explicit representation/abstraction relation with *direction*,
   event observation alphabet, guards, state frames, permitted
   internal steps, failure and concurrency semantics, and the
   initial-state relation to which it applies.
5. For each transfer premise, its owner, original-source dependency,
   checked derivation, independent physical evidence or allowed
   opaque/trusted qualification, scope, invalidation conditions
   and current consumer policy decision.
6. Source locations, reproducible generation and checking
   instructions, privacy-appropriate counterexample translation,
   unsupported status and the first unestablished enforcing
   boundary.

The record supports **forward semantic derivation**:

#+begin_example
original Gesso/application model and Choreo declarations
                  |
         checked semantic interpretation
                  |
       admitted normalized semantic subject
            /                      \
   generated formal subject       issued operation/plan
            |                      |
    proof/check result        host/asset/model binding
            \                      /
       explicit relation, premises, and evidence
                  |
       qualified production transfer claim
#+end_example

It also supports **reverse traceability**:

#+begin_example
formal obligation, premise, or counterexample
                  |
   exact source/translation/correspondence identity
                  |
   originating model rule, operation, or Choreo step
                  |
   production binding, effect boundary, source location
                  |
   evidence class and still-open implementation obligations
#+end_example

Reverse traceability makes a proof result explainable and a
counterexample repairable. It does *not* prove the converse
inclusion of all abstract traces in real execution, nor
automatically prove that every production execution is
represented by the abstract model. A cached record, matching
digest, correct diagram or copied =:checked= map cannot issue
a certificate, prove an installed route, or authenticate an
XTDB transaction. Required closure is freshly derived from
the trusted *current original source*, not a successful
submitted subset or yesterday's matching graph.

*** Admission soundness, completeness, execution refinement and progress

The theorem claims have different directions and quantifiers.
Let S be the supported formal source semantics, A the actual
admission mechanism, I the installed executable under explicit
environment E, and alpha an abstraction from relevant I
states and observations into S:

#+begin_example
Admission soundness:
  For all supported x, A(current-source, x) = admitted
    => FormalJudgment(S, x, admitted-premises).

Admission completeness (separate, where promised):
  For all supported x satisfying the required judgment and
  independent admission conditions, A can admit x.

Execution safety transfer:
  For every relevant I/E execution starting in the related initial
  state, alpha maps its relevant steps/observations to a valid S
  execution, including the represented failures and concurrency.

Conditional progress transfer:
  Every relevant I/E execution satisfying named fairness, delivery,
  retention, continuing-execution and user-action premises meets
  the selected progress obligation, without forbidden divergence.
#+end_example

These are schematic logical statements, not Gesso API calls.
They leave the actual relation, allowed silent/stuttering
steps, observation projection and premise qualifications
to the admitted source and checker. Agreement over rejected
examples does not establish admission soundness. Refusing
every user operation may avoid some unsafe transitions
but fails required positive capabilities, and is not
admission completeness. Proof of an admitted source
does not mean the installed server invokes that source.

A safety simulation may allow unbounded internal stuttering
while transferring a safety invariant. It cannot thereby
establish liveness; a progress theorem must exclude the
relevant divergent executions under explicitly qualified
fairness and recovery assumptions. Failure of those
assumptions preserves safety and honest unknown outcomes.
Neither a timeout nor a reachable terminal state is an
unconditional eventual-settlement proof.

*** Worked Claim slice: same source, separate formal and physical obligations

The first proof-transfer pilot uses HumanHelp's actual
public production Request, RequestAssignment, Membership,
Organization and Location model interfaces, not a substitute
toy single-map Request with an invented helper field.
The domain owns the authorization and one-primary-assignment
policy. The browser supplies a Request argument, operation
proposal, stable logical command identity and perhaps
untrusted observed basis; the server supplies the
authenticated principal and current authoritative context.

A schematic correspondence *fixture* may have this shape.
Its keys illustrate record semantics rather than prescribing a public
Clojure API; the sample itself is not a checked certificate:

#+begin_src clojure
{:source
 {:operation :request/claim
  :domain-models
  #{:humanhelp/Request :humanhelp/RequestAssignment
    :humanhelp/Membership :humanhelp/Organization
    :humanhelp/Location}
  :semantic-subject :request/claim-O31
  :rules :request/claim-rules-C21}
 :client-proposal
 {:request/id :request-R17
  :command/id :command-C42
  :observed-basis :untrusted-client-observation}
 :trusted-invocation
 {:principal :authenticated-server-principal
  :request :current-authoritative-request
  :membership :current-authoritative-membership
  :organization :current-authoritative-organization
  :location :current-authoritative-location}
 :formal-claim
 {:subject :formal/request-claim-O31
  :proposition :one-active-primary-assignment
  :direction :source-induction-and-execution-refinement}
 :production-obligations
 #{:bind-command-to-principal-operation-and-request
   :check-current-request-and-membership-and-organization
   :enforce-one-primary-assignment-at-atomic-write
   :atomically-record-command-result
   :include-all-relevant-physical-writers
   :bind-issued-executor-to-installed-route}
 :evidence
 {:source-check :required
  :abstract-induction :required
  :installed-model-correspondence :open
  :atomic-tx-implementation :open
  :physical-writer-completeness :open}}
#+end_src

The fixture separates *admission correspondence* (the
particular source-issued Claim plan admitted by production
satisfies the intended checked semantic judgment) from
*execution refinement* (every relevant execution of the
actual installed Claim writer respects the specified
atomic RequestAssignment transition, its command record
and actual failure outcomes). A theorem for the
first does not imply the second. The formal tool
cannot authenticate caller-supplied model Vars, route
IDs, production node or a handwritten
=:installed-model-correspondence :checked= status.

A protected Claim transition may atomically depend on:

#+begin_example
current Request is eligible and still open
current Membership is active for the authenticated actor
Request, Membership, Organization and Location associations
  meet the real public model policy
logical command is bound to operation, Request and principal
one active primary assignment is preserved
domain changes and durable command result share the
  required authoritative transaction/commit contract
#+end_example

The allowed physical mechanism may be an appropriately
guarded XTDB transaction, a qualified conditional write,
or another independently justified atomic implementation.
The theorem needs the *same* relevant atomic boundary,
not identical source syntax. A pair of true pre-reads
followed by unguarded writes fails that obligation.
A command ledger/readback matching the post-state
can corroborate one observed history but does not
alone prove causation, the transaction-internal prestate,
all writer coverage, current actor validity or all
future execution traces. Missing physical evidence
remains an open premise or a failed strong profile;
it is never silently discharged by the abstract proof.

An adversarial implementation may add an unmodeled
priority restriction or take the helper identity from
browser-provided fields while its formal model still
shows the right atomic transition. These are different
correspondence failures: the former can omit concrete
behavior or required positive eligibility, while the
latter violates principal authority. Shared names,
hashes or tests of a happy path repair neither.
A real product acceptance control must demonstrate
the eligible Claim and correctly reject wrong-actor,
revoked-Membership and incompatible-command attempts.

*** Atomic formal actions create specific production obligations

Formal atomicity does not mandate a single thread or
node but does mandate a production correctness boundary
adequate for the claimed property. If a transition
treats current authority checks, domain state changes
and command-result recording as one atomic operation,
the implementation must justify that correspondence
and all external premises used. A process-local
one-shot submission handle alone cannot establish
durable exclusion among independently issued retries
or separately running nodes.

The relevant effect ledger distinguishes:

- source-checked normalization and restricted
  transaction lowering of the declared operation;
- trusted request-local principal/tenant/operation
  and command identity binding, and current relevant
  public model policy;
- the invocation-specific immutable transaction
  submitted by its actual selected gateway/client;
- backend-defined actual receipt, conditional
  commit/conflict and failure/unknown outcomes;
- physical domain and command-result atomicity,
  transaction-local guard and write frame;
- durable historical reconciliation after H1/H2
  response uncertainty, without a second Claim;
- all relevant initializer, job, raw and
  external physical writers that could invalidate
  the protected invariant;
- source-to-deployed route/asset binding and
  browser permission/adoption where claimed.

An API return, a local receipt object, an opaque
TransactionKey, a matching command row and a pinned
readback may be related observations with distinct
evidence origins. A report identifies exactly
which relation is established, and which still
requires the provider's contract or independent
causal proof. If an exception occurs after
submission, the public outcome cannot be
manufactured as =:rejected=. No post hoc
diagnostic check can turn a completed unsafe
write into pre-effect prevention.

The authoritative submission architecture,
guard footprints and physical writer completeness
belong to 10.05–10.09. This section makes their
proof-transfer dependencies unavoidable without
specifying a second competing transaction API.

*** Evidence mechanism, transfer limits and qualified authenticity

| Evidence mechanism | Warranted statement | Additional unproved or qualified boundary |
|--------------------+---------------------+-------------------------------------------|
| External machine-checked theorem | Formal conclusion under exactly the encoded definitions, proof rules and assumptions | Source-to-encoding fidelity and production realization |
| Source-issued checked derivation | Original declared subject satisfies its supported checker judgment and retains open premises | Independent checker metatheory and actual runtime behavior |
| Generated/extracted checker or compiler | Artifact derives from the formal or semantic subject by a selected process | Translation correctness, runtime/ABI/codec semantics, loaded identity |
| Proved representation or simulation | Specified concrete-to-abstract relation holds over its quantified domain | Production object and environment actually in that domain |
| Exhaustive finite comparison | Compared behaviors agree over the enumerated state/input space with recorded coverage | Outside-domain behavior, abstraction validity and real deployment |
| Independent conformance/fault test | Selected actual executions agree with their expected behavior at observed boundaries | All other schedules, writer completeness and eventual liveness |
| Controlled process-local gateway observation | Captured local call inputs/result and related observations at that owned call site | Remote node authenticity, transaction causality, other writers and real served route |
| Inspected deployed host/asset report | Matching source/route/callable/asset facts in the inspected host scope | Which process/bytes users actually reach, dynamic replacement and bypasses |
| Human/code review of handwritten relation | Reviewer judged specified correspondence under recorded assumptions | A machine theorem or authenticated physical occurrence |

A translator can be left inside an explicitly named
trusted computing base or justified by a separately
checked translation/refinement argument. Generated
outputs are *derivatives*, not proof of their own
correctness. An untrusted elaborator may propose
proof terms; a smaller checker must validate the
original-source judgment. Likewise, generated
formal models must not be hand-edited to evade
a counterexample and then presented as still
representing the actual application.

Authenticity is relative to the chosen threat model.
A private JVM registry can establish one
process-local handle origin under a trusted JVM
premise without providing a cryptographic remote
attestation. A controlled captured XTDB client
can justify a local call-site observation without
authenticating a caller-supplied node as production.
A copied report loses process-local identity
warrant unless a supported durable rechecking
or attestation contract restores it. Automatic
signature demands do not replace stating which
boundary is authoritative and who can substitute it.

*** Bind evidence and preserve currentness before substitution

The producer and consumer retain original-source
identity, required obligation coverage, exact
proposition, proof rules/checker profile, used
premises, source dependencies, semantic interface,
selected runtime plan, callable, install/asset
and resource/instance identities as *different*
objects. A certified consumer checks the
current trusted source and relevant
dependency closure before accepting
a producer's purported guarantee.
A successful report or matching old
source plus old graph is not currentness.

An abstract proof and its exact immutable
source closure may be reused without
running its checker on every click if
the actual dependencies and trusted
lifecycle remain valid. Reuse does not
suppress fresh model authorization,
command replay/retention, actor and
tenant context, browser effect ownership
or current installation validity.
A changed opaque callable, regenerated
asset, replaced route, provider or
runtime generation may leave an abstract
theorem intact while invalidating the
stronger source-to-production claim.

When a selected strong profile requires
external checking, timeout, unsupported
syntax, stale subject, failed checker
or missing model correspondence must
yield a typed unestablished/rejected
admission, not a cached green success
or invisible fallback to trusted code.
A subsequent checker outage does not
automatically invalidate a still-valid
independent proof accepted under its
lifecycle, but a refuted required
premise or changed current binding
cannot be hidden behind it.
Weaker conditional use requires an
*explicit separately selected profile*
with its genuine remaining assumptions.

*** Counterexamples return to source rather than creating a second model

For a modeled violation, Gesso retains
correspondence metadata to display the
named invariant, original source/operation,
actor/authority context, smallest useful
trace prefix, failing abstract guard/frame
step, formal translation and abstraction
assumptions, related production binding
and the evidence needed to decide whether
a real implementation shares the defect.
Sensitive Request/actor details are
redacted to the authorized reviewer.

For example, a *candidate defective
semantic model* that checked open
status only at a prior read might allow:

#+begin_example
A observes Request R as open
B observes Request R as open
A incorporates Claim for R
B incorporates a second primary Claim without an intervening release
one-primary-assignment invariant fails in the candidate model
#+end_example

This is a valid abstract counterexample
*only if the formal candidate actually
allows that unguarded commit action*.
It is not a counterexample to a
correctly atomically guarded formal
transition, and it is not proof
that the installed XTDB writer
executed the trace. Conversely,
a correct formal guard can coexist
with an unguarded deployed writer;
that is an open or falsified
*production refinement* obligation,
not an abstract invariant failure.
Model approximation may also
admit a spurious trace; concrete
realizability must be independently
assessed before blaming an actual
producer.

A developer-facing diagnostic
might identify a failing source
guard and a required
=net.humanhelp.site.model.request.core/claim=
implementation check, marking
the actual physical conclusion
=:not-established=. The
illustrative model binding is
not evidence that a particular
runtime route invoked it.
Where a trace does not
uniquely identify the defect,
source-model, translation,
assumption and production
possibilities remain distinct.

The normal repair loop is:

#+begin_example
Clojure/Gesso source and approved model policies
         -> checked semantic construction
         -> generated formal subject
         -> proof/model checking
         -> scoped counterexample or unsupported obligation
         -> interpretation at the original source and physical boundary
         -> human- or assistant-proposed source/translation/binding repair
         -> full regeneration, admission and independent conformance checks
#+end_example

Ordinary application developers
should not need to author or
patch formal-language files.
Framework maintainers may
repair a faulty lowering,
proof checker or external
backend as their own
source changes. Product
policy remains owned by
the application. An
automated assistant may
propose repairs but cannot
silently weaken a named
invariant, mark an opaque
premise discharged, modify
the generated specification
just to pass, or issue
a certificate. Every
approved repair is
re-derived and rechecked
under the appropriate
profile and independent
positive tests.

*** Acceptance criteria for the correspondence strategy

At a minimum the finished
system must demonstrate
paired cases for:

- **One source:** correct
  generated formal Claim
  and issued plan
  correspond to the
  original operation;
  a separate handwritten
  formal F and
  independently executed
  G without a justified
  relation cannot pass.
- **Admission direction:**
  accepted source has
  the required formal
  judgment and checked
  obligation closure;
  an admitted malformed
  rule or pruned original
  catalog violates
  admission soundness.
- **Positive completeness:**
  eligible real Claim and
  required lifecycle
  operations remain
  constructible;
  reject-all construction
  cannot qualify
  even if its abstract
  safety is vacuous.
- **Execution relation:**
  a checked supported
  production trace
  refines the original
  abstract operation;
  copied equal final
  snapshots with
  unmodeled intermediate
  unauthorized effects
  cannot establish
  the same claim.
- **Actor binding:**
  trusted current
  Membership/
  Organization policy
  authorizes an
  eligible Claim;
  browser-supplied
  actor identity
  cannot authorize
  the protected write.
- **Atomicity:** a
  guarded concurrent
  two-helper Claim
  preserves one active
  primary and durable
  command attribution;
  competing stale
  pre-reads and
  unguarded writes
  fail the claimed
  correspondence.
- **Command history:**
  C42 replay after
  lost H1 delivery
  recovers its true
  durable outcome;
  failed H2 or a
  later Request card
  cannot invent
  rejection or a
  second incorporation.
- **Physical evidence:**
  one owned gateway
  call, actual return
  and pinned readback
  support their bounded
  local witness;
  matching records
  are not accepted
  as universally causal
  or as complete
  writer inventory.
- **Provenance:** source,
  deployed operation
  and current plan
  bindings have
  genuine issuer/
  host evidence;
  a raw copied
  proof report,
  node ID or
  stamp cannot
  authenticate them.
- **Currentness:**
  an unaffected pure
  theorem can be
  reused under
  checked source
  compatibility;
  a swapped callable,
  served asset or
  revoked provider
  assumption requires
  renewed corresponding
  physical evidence.
- **Counterexample
  interpretation:**
  the defective
  candidate formal
  action returns
  a specific
  source-relative
  trace;
  the report does
  not automatically
  assert the
  installed writer
  did it.
- **Repair boundary:**
  repairing the
  true source/
  translation and
  rechecking succeeds;
  editing only a
  generated invariant
  cannot change
  production semantics
  or qualify the
  original claim.
- **Liveness:** the
  chosen fair
  recovery/user
  action premises
  justify a specific
  debt resolution;
  a safe stuttering
  trace or timeout
  alone does
  not establish
  eventual completion.
- **Topology:** one-
  and multi-node
  executions use
  the same Claim
  semantics;
  extra routing,
  registration-frontier,
  replay/gap
  and failover
  premises stay
  open until
  qualified.
- **Privacy:** authorized
  reviewers can
  trace a failure
  to its source;
  copyable reports
  cannot leak
  private principals
  or gain
  transaction
  authority.

Each case predeclares
the original source,
positive control,
adversarial perturbation,
expected first
source/checker/physical
boundary, independent
effect observation and
actual open
premises. The
acceptance decision
must be claim-specific,
not one global
=:certified?= flag.
A successful
formal proof,
source checker,
physical test
and product
capability are
different required
pieces of the
strongest
end-to-end
acceptance.

*** External formalization has a concrete first target

The first external theorem target is the *closed propositional proof
kernel* and its acceptance relation, because its language and trusted
judgments are small enough to specify independently of arbitrary
Clojure, XTDB transactions, the browser and the full Choreo machine.
The purpose is to remove a specific trust assumption from Part IX's
proof issuer, *not* to assert that having a proof assistant has already
proved the framework or every application using it.

Ordinary application authors define rules, model operations and
Choreo constructions in Gesso's admitted Clojure/data interfaces.
The framework derives the formal subjects and proof obligations.
They do not maintain a second handwritten HumanHelp specification
or write Lean tactics, Quint, TLA+ or Recife models to use an
ordinary admitted operation. Framework maintainers may choose a
proof assistant such as Lean, another proof-producing checker or
a mechanized semantics with a suitably small trusted checker.
The architectural requirement is the theorem and evidence-import
contract, not a permanent commitment to one tool vendor or syntax.

Part IX supplies the original subject, typed proposition source,
small proof rules, source-derived dependency closure, premise
inheritance, checker admission and explicit open assumptions.
Sections 10.01–10.04 specify formal/theorem versus runtime
correspondence; 10.09 names the independent production discharge
obligations. External formalization must preserve these boundaries
rather than add an unrelated parallel assurance scheme.
10.11 owns exact-finite exploration/abstraction and 10.12 owns
the broader reproducible evidence package.

**** Specify the closed inference system before proving it

The initial formal calculus covers the same *seven* inference rules
as the admitted Gesso propositional kernel, with their exact
well-formedness, arity, ordering, reference and conclusion
restrictions:

| Admitted rule | Semantic consequence and required shape |
|---------------+----------------------------------------|
| =:premise= | A declared named premise P remains open and entails P |
| =:truth-introduction= | Truth is derivable without taking an unrecorded atom as true |
| =:implication-reflexivity= | For the same well-formed P, P implies P |
| =:conjunction-introduction= | From admitted P1 ... Pn, n at least two, derive the matching conjunction in the selected source order |
| =:conjunction-elimination= | From a checked conjunction derive exactly its selected indexed conjunct |
| =:implication-elimination= | From P implies Q and P, in the required reference order, derive Q |
| =:disjunction-introduction= | From P derive a disjunction containing exactly that P at the declared supported position |

A calculus statement quantifies over well-formed finite formula trees,
finite derivation sequences, all valid rule parameter values and
all Boolean valuations of the distinct admitted *opaque atoms*.
The atom declarations are inert for this propositional theorem:
the theorem does *not* decide whether an embedded age,
Membership or Request policy predicate is true of real
application state. Formula syntax admits its defined truth
connectives; the seven *inference rules* do not thereby acquire
additional proof operations. Neither the presence of
=:not= or =:false= in a proposition nor a valid semantic
tautology licenses an unimplemented classical proof rule,
arithmetic solver, arbitrary rewriting, atomic-truth axiom
or hypothetical-context discharge.

Let =Gamma= be the named open source premises, =D= a
well-formed source derivation, =P= its exact goal and =v=
a total Boolean valuation of the opaque atoms in the
admitted formulas. The initial external soundness target is:

#+begin_example
For every supported original source Gamma, D, P:
  If the defined small-kernel derivation relation accepts D
  from Gamma with conclusion P, then
  for every Boolean valuation v:
    (every premise in Gamma that D actually depends on
     is true under v) implies P is true under v.
#+end_example

The antecedent must use the *defined inference rules*, not an
opaque call to Gesso's own implementation checker—otherwise the
proof could accidentally assume precisely the correctness it is
meant to establish. The formal theorem states how used premises
are computed by rule references and what happens to declarations
not used by a particular step. The source admission path may
additionally require all declared premises/steps to participate;
that stricter input discipline must not be confused with a
logical rule that discharges unused assumptions. A finite
closed derivation with =Gamma= empty is unconditional
*only for the admitted opaque-Boolean interpretation*.

Proofs may be vacuous under inconsistent premises; a valid
logical entailment does not prove that the application can
ever reach a state satisfying those premises. Nonempty
initial state, reachable eligible operations, authenticated
actor context and real product capability have different
independent obligations. A theorem proving
=Gamma |= P= cannot silently produce =P= unless
=Gamma= is actually discharged or separately justified.

**** Five distinct theorem and implementation claims

The formalization has at least five separately reported
objectives. A later result may depend on earlier ones, but
a single passing artifact does not collapse them.

1. *S0 — Inference-rule soundness.* Every admitted rule
   schema preserves Boolean entailment for all legal
   formulas, arities, indices and reference patterns.
   Formalize the rules independently and prove their
   preservation lemmas, including the premise rule's
   open-assumption effect.

2. *S1 — Derivation-system soundness.* Induct on the
   finite ordered derivation. Each source step inherits
   exactly the used named open premises of its valid
   predecessor steps, and the final step entails the
   declared goal. Forward references, cycles,
   wrong-conclusion substitution, undeclared premise
   use and unsupported rules are outside the admitted
   derivation relation rather than trusted exceptions.
   A mathematical induction proof is not evidence
   that a particular Clojure parser enforces it.

3. *S2 — Actual Clojure checker correspondence.*
   For all supported concrete inputs accepted by
   the *production* parser, normalizer, proof checker
   and current admission path, establish a semantics-
   preserving relation to the formal derivation
   relation of S1. Include original-source normalization,
   nominal variable/subject identity, premise and step
   references, source rejection, malformed encodings,
   resource limits and implementation/version binding.
   This can be a proven implementation/refinement,
   verified extraction, an independently checked
   translation or a separately qualified trust
   premise. Simply invoking the production checker
   from the formal model is not independence.

4. *S3 — Typed atomic-proposition correspondence.*
   Relate opaque Boolean atom valuations to the
   actual supported typed-rule evaluator under its
   original source, reader footprint, presence/nil
   semantics, runtime variable bindings and
   evaluation/error contract. The conditional
   propositional theorem cannot establish any
   arbitrary Clojure implementation predicate,
   model eligibility or current server input.
   A bounded typed-atom oracle establishes only
   its enumerated original bindings unless an
   additional abstraction/correspondence theorem
   justifies wider scope.

5. *S4 — Issuer/composition and source-to-production
   transfer.* Bind the exact source derivation and
   checked theorem to the trusted current source,
   original-source-required obligation ledger,
   certificate issuer, selected admission profile,
   premise inheritance, compatible consuming
   operation and installed execution boundaries.
   The actual server model guard, XTDB write,
   loaded browser asset or protected read needs
   its own qualification under 10.04–10.09.
   A correct calculus theorem does not mint
   runtime authorization or a durable
   transferable certificate.

A final application claim must state exactly which S0–S4
edges were established and which remain conditional,
tested, source-checked or unknown. It is legitimate
to prove S0–S1 before S2–S4. That yields a useful
formal result but *not* an externally proven Clojure
checker, sound whole-application composition or
formally verified HumanHelp deployment.

**** Checker semantics, resource bounds and fail-closed admission

The actual acceptance relation includes more than
the names of seven inference rules. It must define
which original source forms are admissible: metadata-
free/plain structures where required, qualified IDs,
exact keys, typed nominal contexts, formula
depth/arity, atom encoding, duplicate or unused
steps, strictly prior references, all required
dependency/assumption bindings and the exact goal.

A bounded production checker can soundly reject
inputs because of a configured depth, formula-node,
proof-step, premise, atom or computation budget.
Such conservative rejection does not refute
*soundness* of what it does admit.
Completeness—for example, admission of all
otherwise valid derivations in the advertised
supported/bounded domain—is a separate
usability/property target. The strong
product acceptance must not be satisfied by a
checker or constructed application that
rejects every legitimate Claim.

A successful proof result, a genuine semantic
counterexample, a malformed-source rejection,
an unsupported rule/judgment, exhausted
resource budget, external tool failure and
unavailable checker are distinct dispositions.
If a selected profile requires S2 or another
external theorem at construction, an unavailable,
timed-out or mismatched check fails that
*new* admission. The framework may continue
serving a previously admitted still-current
assembly under its actual lifetime contract;
it cannot silently downgrade the profile,
invent a checked theorem or grant
an unauthorized protected operation.

Preprocessing and normalization belong inside
the correspondence boundary. An independently
sound rule calculus cannot compensate for a
normalizer that drops a premise, changes the
formula tree, coalesces distinct typed atoms
incorrectly or maps source proposition A
to theorem subject B. Revalidating just
the resulting normalized formula is not
necessarily validation of the *original*
source claim. A checked trusted source-to-
normal-form semantics relation is needed.

**** External backend and theorem evidence import

The external backend must produce evidence whose
meaning is independent of source-level report
labels. The evidence package identifies at least:

- the immutable formal theorem statement, explicitly
  admitted axioms or trusted primitives, proposition
  and proof-rule definitions, theorem/checker version,
  backend/toolchain configuration and claim category;
- the canonical original-source semantic subject,
  normalized representation, dependency/obligation
  closure, source-to-formal encoding and all versions
  required to interpret them;
- a proof term or independently checkable result
  where the backend supports one, together with the
  exact trusted checker/admission mechanism;
- finite-domain or generated conformance coverage,
  resource/execution budgets and any unsupported
  language features when these are supplementary
  *tests*, not the external universal theorem;
- current exact issuer/consumer binding, selected
  semantic source, required open/used premises,
  installed asset/plan identity if the claim
  transfers to production, and all unresolved
  S2–S4 implementation/environment obligations;
- a reproducible invocation and observed disposition
  with privacy-appropriate diagnostics, source
  correspondence and invalidation/rechecking triggers.

A Lean proof might be one implementation of the S0–S1
evidence. A separate kernel/checker or supported
proof-producing tactic may be used, but the trusted
assumption set must state the actual proof-checking
kernel and any imported axioms. A successful CLI
exit code, copyable EDN report or saved
"proved" label is not itself trusted proof origin.
A checker-based import should recheck the actual
proof/evidence at the selected current consumer,
or rely on a properly qualified persistent
attestation/lifecycle contract rather than
raw purported hashes.

The external checker does not need physical
XTDB/network/Chromium access to prove S0.
That is precisely why the abstract theorem and
its independently qualified runtime transfer
are separate. Proof/diagnostic sidecars must
not change an already admitted runtime
state transition or become a principal,
one-Refresh grant, command retry authorization
or browser effect lease.

The tool-independent exchange need not be a
single universal serialization format, but
every backend must preserve the exact canonical
judgment and evidence class. A backend may
reject unsupported constructs honestly;
it cannot silently translate them to
axioms, omit difficult cases or assert
all application obligations discharged.

**** Independent oracles, mutations and common-mode bugs

Gesso's own independent rule-interpretation
and finite audits are valuable *falsifiers*
of claimed correspondence. They can expose
a changed inference index, missing premise,
malformed step, normalized atom mismatch
or deliberate invalid rule. Running the
same Clojure normalizer in two wrappers
does not independently validate the
normalizer; an independently written
reference can discover more bugs but
requires its own domain/soundness
qualifications.

An exhaustive truth table over one
finite set of opaque atoms proves
only the particular local entailment
within that declared domain. Checking
one representative for each of the
seven rule tags does not quantify
over every formula tree, premise
combination, conjunction arity,
conjunct index, depth or
malformed source form.
A compositional source-level
induction over a finite derivation
is stronger for *that derivation*
than checking only its final
Boolean valuation sample, but
its local rule and interpreter
premises must remain explicit.
Neither result automatically
establishes the universal
mathematical metatheorem or
production Clojure implementation.

A required acceptance corpus includes paired
positive/adversarial controls:

1. A valid derivation of a source premise is
   accepted *with that premise open*; the
   same source with the premise omitted
   must not yield an unconditional theorem.
2. Premise-free truth introduction and
   implication reflexivity are checked
   for arbitrary supported well-formed
   formulas; an invented arbitrary
   typed-atom truth axiom is rejected.
3. Correct two-or-more conjunction
   introduction and indexed elimination
   preserve their source order; reversed
   premise order, invalid arity or
   out-of-range index is rejected.
4. Implication elimination requires its
   exact implication and antecedent;
   swapped references or changed
   consequent fails the rule.
5. Disjunction introduction requires
   the indexed embedded source
   conjunct; a different injected
   atom or unsupported position
   cannot be inferred.
6. Valid references use previously
   established derivation steps;
   forward/circular references
   and fabricated prior-step
   conclusion are rejected.
7. The complete original source
   and normalized formula denote
   the same supported proposition;
   a dropped premise, altered
   atom or forged typed context
   cannot inherit the proof.
8. A finite independent truth
   valuation matches a supported
   source step; truncation or
   over-budget enumeration is
   incomplete, not proof of
   all valuations.
9. Formal S0/S1 applies to every
   supported rule/formula shape
   under its premises; one
   checked instance or sample
   catalog is not promoted
   to universal soundness.
10. The source-level Clojure
    checker accepts an admitted
    derivation under its exact
    grammar; a deliberately
    faulty parser or
    normalizer admits the
    wrong original subject
    and fails S2.
11. The typed-atom bridge
    agrees over its qualified
    source-binding domain;
    an opaque Membership
    or Organization-policy
    atom is not marked
    universally true from
    finite examples.
12. The trusted issuer
    rechecks current
    original-source
    obligations and the
    genuine proof;
    a copied successful
    report or stale
    assembly/certificate
    cannot mint a new
    current proof.
13. An external proof
    artifact with exact
    statement, axioms,
    backend and checker
    is reverified in
    the declared
    admissible scope;
    changed axioms,
    malformed proof,
    unsupported syntax
    or exit-code-only
    success cannot
    create the claim.
14. A valid abstract
    Claim proof may
    coexist with an
    independently
    qualified physical
    implementation
    witness; an
    unguarded actual
    XTDB writer is
    not made safe
    by the kernel
    theorem.
15. Retired browser
    owner/resource
    cannot mutate
    a reused target;
    proof-term
    reuse or matching
    artifact ID
    is not
    effect-time
    permission.
16. Genuinely eligible
    HumanHelp Claim
    and the other
    independently required
    lifecycle/product
    positives remain
    executable; a
    reject-all source
    machine is not
    accepted as a
    completed product.

Each case names the original source,
selected proof checker and
quantified domain, exact
positive control and mutant,
first rejecting or enforcing
boundary, actual evidence
class, remaining premises
and independently observed
effect where physical
correctness is claimed.
A theorem that is
still open or a
tool run that was
not performed
must be reported as such.

**** Order later formal targets by the trust gap they eliminate

After the propositional theorem and its concrete
checker-binding work, expand only where a
specific original-source judgment and transfer
edge can be defined. Appropriate later
subjects include:

- typed-rule evaluation and semantic
  preservation of proposition
  normalization/atom meaning;
- guarded-transition consequence
  and simultaneous assignment/
  presence-aware frame rules;
- source-complete conditional
  induction and exact certificate
  premise substitution;
- role-local knowledge/provenance
  and protected authority
  non-escalation;
- conservative progression/
  observation algebra and
  requirement discharge;
- global-to-local Choreo
  projection and portable
  executable-plan lowering;
- browser adapter/resource
  refinement and selected
  physically enforced
  authorization/XTDB
  transitions.

Theorem selection is driven by
the original operation language,
the size of the remaining
trusted base, independent
counteroracles and real
correspondence needs.
A formal theorem for one
closed calculus cannot
automatically cover arbitrary
host-language conditionals
or all multi-node schedules.
The aspiration is high
assurance of the original
source and deployed
application, without
forcing developers to
author formal syntax or
pretending the full
general-purpose Clojure
runtime has been proved.

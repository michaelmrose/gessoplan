## Governing editorial choice

Retain the preferred R5 concise semantic-ownership/claim-evidence
structure, but restore R4's concrete forbidden formal-model split and
the eight distinct assurance evidence families in terms consistent
with accepted Part I. Expand the *transfer problem*: no external theorem,
formal state graph or source-equivalent execution witness qualifies the
actual installed host without an explicit scope/quantifier, direction,
refinement relation, implementation generation and environmental
assumptions. Keep ordinary full-language Gesso authoring and future
automated formal extraction as design goals; the current implementation's
limitations belong here rather than weakening the normative source.

## R4 and accepted-plan preservation decisions

1. **One semantic owner, not two independently authored applications.**
   Proof obligations, models and plans derive from admitted Gesso
   semantics. Independently implemented checkers are useful
   counteroracles, but must exhibit their subject/transfer relation.

2. **Strong evidence classes remain plural.**
   Restored R4's eight classes: external machine proof,
   kernel derivation, exact-finite, assembly, dynamic
   enforcement, trusted premise, explicit assertion and
   tested observation. None is a numerical confidence rank.

3. **Claim scope controls classification.**
   Prop/subject, quantifiers, source/current generation,
   premises, evidence mechanism, observation domain and
   policy decision remain separately inspectable.
   Conditionality and liveness are not ninth classes.

4. **Formal theorem does not prove production.**
   Universal abstract safety requires a separately
   qualified concrete-to-abstract simulation and
   actual source/host/writer coverage. One witness
   does not satisfy universal trace quantification.

5. **Production source still owns business semantics.**
   The HumanHelp public model decides Claim/Membership/
   Organization/RequestAssignment truth. The
   framework cannot prove that the product owner
   chose the correct policy without an independent
   accepted statement of intent.

6. **Proof kernel, dedicated checks and external
   prover are different mechanisms.** Kernel
   checks only its admitted inference language;
   an external proof assistant does not
   automatically check the JVM, CLJS or XTDB.

7. **Elaborator submissions are not their own
   authority.** Original-source-derived obligation
   completeness and checker independent validation
   prevent safely proving an adversarially pruned
   operation catalog under the original source name.

8. **Common semantics can mean common bugs.**
   Sharing an evaluator prevents drift but does
   not create independent soundness evidence.
   Differential reference implementations
   must preserve their versioned source meaning.

9. **Source-side closure and installed physical
   coverage are different.** Complete declared
   model operations do not prove every startup,
   migration, background, raw or external
   writer was discovered. Real routes and
   loaded browser code also require installed
   correspondence.

10. **Typed/nominal status is not proof.**
    Same-shaped maps, successful reports and
    application adjectives do not issue a
    theorem or real authorization. The
    selected profile's admission decision is
    not a proposition's evidence mechanism
    or a physical command outcome.

11. **Conditional and genuinely discharged
    premises differ.** General abstract proof,
    current actor authorization, observed
    transaction result and liveness fairness
    have distinct life/instance scopes.
    Checked substitution of P with E under Q
    retains Q; re-labeled trust does not
    erase an unsupported mandatory proof.

12. **Full-language opaque paths remain legitimate.**
    Record typed I/O, reader/writer footprints,
    authority/effects/failure, selected callable,
    provider and generation applicability,
    along with relevant claims and uninspected
    implementation behavior.

13. **Unrelated opacity is not contagious.**
    A renderer's incidental opaque formatting
    does not invalidate a separate command
    theorem; an opaque relevant policy
    predicate must remain a visible premise
    or be checked at a qualified boundary.

14. **History, current model and presentation differ.**
    C42 prior incorporation is not a current
    Request card, HTTP 200 or browser acceptance.
    A static certificate is not a stable
    principal privilege, read grant, or
    authoritatively adopted DOM observation.

15. **Safety and progress have different premises.**
    Conditional user Refresh, retry, fairness,
    provider availability and retention do not
    become unconditional liveness by naming
    a timeout or checking one reachable state.

16. **Same semantic meaning across host topology.**
    One- and multi-node Gesso use the same
    source-authoritative Claim, acquisition
    and resource contracts. Additional
    placement/routing/replay/failover
    evidence is not a new authorization model.

17. **Explicit positive and adversarial controls.**
    An eligible Claim and its wrong-actor,
    lost-delivery, incomplete writer,
    stale asset and ungranted Refresh
    neighbors challenge real boundaries.
    Reject-all can make a weak safety
    statement vacuous while failing
    essential product acceptance.

18. **Timeless plan and transparent current status.**
    Parts XI–XII own completion order, and
    current code facts are not normative
    assertions of universal proof.

## Current Gesso v1622 correspondence (inspected, not executed)

  `:kernel-checked` in a copied map does not

  restricted source-rechecked propositional

  (v1622) compares an issued plan-bound

  explicitly keeps opaque model-policy

  misleading.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/application_assurance_report.clj`
  recomputes a copyable conditional
  source-level report and permanently
  retains independent kernel soundness,
  installed adoption, physical writer

The current source provides meaningful
precursors with carefully described
boundaries. It does not currently
demonstrate full generated all-schedules
formal-to-production Gesso proof,
universal XTDB/HTMX implementation
soundness, mandatory independent
proof of opaque HumanHelp policy,
or complete deployment-wide physical
writer enumeration. These remain
future system guarantees, named
premises or explicitly rejected
strong profile claims.

## Positive and adversarial conformance pairs

1. **Source/formal identity:** generated
   model follows the original Gesso
   operation. **Adverse:** a
   second handwritten machine
   omits protected Unclaim.
2. **Independent checker:** a
   valid derivation is accepted
   with its correct open premise
   set. **Adverse:** same
   success map or mismatched
   proof rule is admitted.
3. **Finite scope:** a complete,
   nonempty finite abstract
   search is reported as such.
   **Adverse:** timeout,
   truncation or empty Init
   becomes universal proof.
4. **Physical trace:** an actual
   guarded writer and emitted
   result correlate to a
   qualified operation.
   **Adverse:** matching
   ledger and current card
   are accepted as causal
   proof without provenance.
5. **Original-source coverage:**
   source ledger contains
   every required operation
   and invariant. **Adverse:**
   structurally valid pruned
   graph passed as full.
6. **Current implementation:**
   changed callable/provider
   invalidates dependent
   realization evidence.
   **Adverse:** unchanged
   interface name extends
   old physical proof.
7. **Legitimate opaque:**
   named Organization
   policy implementation
   remains an explicit
   profile-permitted premise.
   **Adverse:** incomplete
   arbitrary Clojure code
   is marked universally
   checked by its name.
8. **Unrelated opacity:**
   abstract Claim command
   identity theorem survives
   incidental renderer
   change where qualified.
   **Adverse:** changed
   domain writer inherits
   unchanged renderer scope.
9. **Actor authority:** genuine
   eligible Alice Claim
   respects current model
   and host guards. **Adverse:**
   browser helper flag,
   wrong tenant or revoked
   Membership authorizes a
   new protected incorporation.
10. **Known command outcome:**
    C42 committed history
    is separately recovered
    after response loss.
    **Adverse:** current
    Request card invents
    historical incorporation.
11. **Source/asset install:**
    actual route and loaded
    asset correspond to the
    admitted plan. **Adverse:**
    stale copied stamp
    claims current install.
12. **Physical adoption:**
    creator-local Create and
    authorized one Refresh
    perform their specific
    declared acquisitions.
    **Adverse:** received
    SSE advisory grants
    extra whole-board read.
13. **Progress:** safety
    survives a lost delivery
    and honest unknown;
    conditional fairness
    can justify a named
    liveness theorem.
    **Adverse:** timeout
    or one happy retry
    becomes unconditional
    termination.
14. **Host topology:** single
    and multi-node
    semantics agree for
    admitted operations,
    with additional qualified
    routing/replay/failover
    evidence. **Adverse:**
    router loss/drop is
    silently equated
    with client convergence.
15. **Product non-vacuity:**
    real eligible Claim
    remains usable along
    with adversarial actor
    rejection. **Adverse:**
    permanently disabled
    Claim control passes
    product acceptance.
16. **Honest reporting:**
    every source, check,
    trusted premise,
    physical bound and
    product gap remains
    attributable. **Adverse:**
    unqualified global
    `certified?` overrides
    one missing mandatory
    boundary.

These are future oracles, not claims
of tests executed for this edit.
Each oracle must predeclare
expected class and first
responsible checking/physical
boundary and include a real
positive control.

## Editorial disposition

10.01 now more precisely defines
the transfer obligation from
one original source-owned
semantic machine to separately
checked formal, runtime,
installed-host and physical
claims. It retains the
R4 examples, restores the
accepted Part I evidence
vocabulary and marks opaque
full-language ownership
without restricting
future formal ambitions
to today's v1622 modules.

XTDB/Chromium/Node test or
formal prover was executed,
no current deployed
HumanHelp product was
certified, and no
whole-Part-X editorial

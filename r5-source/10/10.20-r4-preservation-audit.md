## Editorial disposition and R4 precedence

The preferred R5 ending retains the stronger semantic/physical boundary separation, trusted runtime enforcement, conditional provider assumptions, unknown-command semantics and single/multi-node invariant. R4 supplies two explicit closure requirements that must not become merely implicit: original-source *complete obligation coverage* before claiming a whole application, and four independent milestone statuses (operational, compositional, external-formal, multi-node). The revision preserves the original R5 quote intact, then clarifies its exact claim-transfer dependencies, evidence classes, directional refinement, safety/liveness, changing applicability and separate milestones.

Part XI's release-gate mechanics and Part XII's work ordering remain theirs; Part X terminates with the correctness conditions that those processes must respect.

13. **All relevant writers,:** All relevant writers, initializer predicates, guard dependencies and structural frames remain separate closure debts.
14. **Implementation/provider faults do:** Implementation/provider faults do not invalidate true abstract theorems but invalidate their unestablished production applicability.
15. **Diagnostic observation failures:** Diagnostic observation failures after submission cannot retroactively reject committed or still-unknown commands.

18. **Multi-node placement changes:** Multi-node placement changes hosts but not semantic authority, operation meaning or Choreo role rights.
19. **HumanHelp explicit Refresh:** HumanHelp explicit Refresh applies to remote creation and remote lifecycle changes, with own-command/local recovery exceptions.
20. **Safety under interruption:** Safety under interruption and eventual progress under named fairness, permissions, retention and user action are separate.
21. **Known changes in:** Known changes in source, provider, asset, host or assembly trigger dependent requalification without arbitrary full-graph proof churn.
22. **Operational success is:** Operational success is not compositional application certification.
23. **Compositional certification is:** Compositional certification is not independent external formal correspondence.
24. **External formal correspondence:** External formal correspondence is not production multi-node completion.
25. **Multi-node operational completion:** Multi-node operational completion is not universal assurance across unsupported deployments or product choices.
26. **A project can:** A project can report a narrower supported release profile without silently shrinking its original requested full-completion claim.
27. **Part XI owns:** Part XI owns release admission and four distinct decisions; Part XII owns implementation ordering.
28. **Editorial acceptance of:** Editorial acceptance of a normative target is not present-tense formal certification or deployed compliance.

## Separate R4 preservation challenge

The whole-part reconciliation provides a fifteen-topic detailed crosswalk. This acceptance instead challenged the following loss-prone R4 obligations in the **revised normative section text** rather than accepting a preservation-audit citation alone:

1. **Layered assurance and semantic ownership.** The full original source supplies the executable meaning; original source/abstract proof/physical execution are not one evidence class.

2. **Eleven system-wide target families.** Choreo, knowledge, types, authoritative models, progression, optimism, browser, portable artifacts, whole-app closure, browser/server authority and conditional progress have individually scoped proof and implementation responsibilities.

3. **Ten original theorem-family targets.** Certifiable composition, type, provenance, knowledge, authority non-escalation, projection, executable plans, stale certificates, progression and browser adapter safety remain explicit with quantified limits.

4. **Actual HumanHelp Claim correspondence.** One-primary RequestAssignment atomicity, protected Membership/Organization scope, command C42 record, observed current Request, browser optimism and actual installed XTDB writer/handler remain different facts.

5. **External proof, abstraction and source repair.** The first seven-rule kernel theorem, independent soundness, exact finite bounds, abstraction and counterexample-to-ordinary-Gesso repairs survive without requiring developers to author formal syntax.

6. **Trusted boundaries and adversarial artifacts.** R4 certificate forgery, cached artifact/semantic dependency drift, circular proofs, shared-bug risk, under-/over-inclusive fingerprints, assertions and optional sidecar noninterference are explicitly retained.

7. **Real validation ladder.** Testing at actual physical Biff/XTDB, Chromium and multi-node boundaries with independent positive and forbidden-effect oracles survives, with unknown and unrun cases honestly open.

8. **Multi-node rendezvous and direct messaging.** A-to-C host replacement, durable Request/outbox/fence/replay, the independently admitted ephemeral typing receipt, real two-process failure tests and the nonnecessity of persistent workflow stacks survive.

9. **Closing invariant and independent milestones.** R4 six original anchors and final theorem-versus-physical split remain. The formerly implicit proof-hardening/two-process feedback principle was restored in the preceding reconciliation.

All eight R4 parent thematic areas remain represented in Part X's eight second-level thematic areas, with six original `CUSTOM_ID` anchors preserved in order. The successor's longer and more specific form is not required to preserve obsolete schematic API spellings or R4's exact paragraph order. R5.1 improves the contract when it refuses to invent a physical theorem from a conditional abstract proof, provides better explicit counterexamples and distinguishes trusted source, qualified backend and installed runtime.

Both remote creation and remote lifecycle changes require explicit user Refresh, subject to documented own-command/scoped-recovery exceptions. The independently selected response-flow product decision is still open.
- **Part IX:** original-source obligation closure, issuer-controlled restricted certificates, independent checker soundness, premise-substitution and source-relative proof classes remain intact; Part X adds actual production transfer rather than changing their theorem subjects.
- **Part XI:** candidate admission, installation, activation and completion are different decisions. Operational, compositional, external formal and multi-node milestones each have independent evidence requirements; Part X does not issue release decisions.
- **Part XII:** realization ordering remains separately owned. It is valid for proof hardening and small physical multi-node experiments to inform one another without changing the same semantic business policy.

No contradictory new product policy, mandatory broker/language, parallel hand-authored formal application, or claim of complete installed assurance is needed to accept Part X's design. The proof/R4 preservation and actual deployed validity remain different questions.

## Open implementation, proof, product and release obligations

1. Independent metatheoretic soundness for the kernel or proof import, universal Gesso semantic/physical refinement, all-schedules XTDB isolation, and all Clojure callbacks or physical writers are *not* demonstrated by this editorial acceptance.
2. Actual deployed Biff handlers and credential boundaries, source-complete model/writer inventory, rendered HTMX/DOM effect-time correctness, real participant-message delivery and recipient authorization, and actual production asset adoption require separate implementation evidence.
3. Live multi-node outbox, distributed router registration/replay gap closure, mixed-version host replacement and fault-injected two-process success are future requirements; in-process Gesso Live, SSE, Choreo and process-local readback do not establish them.
4. Current Gesso `application-assurance` v1622 assurance/report and certificate modules are bounded and conditional. The reviewed tests were not rerun here and their existence is not a passing result or root production certificate.
5. HumanHelp's remote Create/lifecycle explicit Refresh is selected, but Part VIII's independent response-flow product decision remains unresolved. Other real HumanHelp model/workflow and user-level acceptance gates remain mandatory.
6. R5.1 Parts XI–XII remain unsectioned/unreviewed; full plan acceptance, all four release milestones, and actual deployment admission are not granted by accepting Part X's future-system design.

These open implementation/product obligations do **not** block editorial acceptance of a timeless normative design because the section states the required witnesses, failure behavior, premise scope, and unproven state. They **do block** stronger current-implementation, release, certification or complete HumanHelp claims. A future R5.1 revision must not silently weaken those requirements simply because the first implementation passes fewer gates.

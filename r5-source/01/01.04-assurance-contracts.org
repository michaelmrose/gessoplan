** Assurance model
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-assurance-classification
:END:

Gesso uses several kinds of evidence, and the design must not collapse them
under a single word such as =verified=, =safe=, or =proved=.

The governing question for every assurance claim is:

#+begin_quote
What proposition is being claimed, over what object, under what assumptions,
and what evidence justifies accepting it?
#+end_quote

Every claim has separately identifiable dimensions:

| Dimension | Required meaning |
|-----------+------------------|
| Proposition | The statement, variable bindings, quantifiers, and observation scope |
| Subject | The semantic interface and, where relevant, concrete implementation or artifact |
| Mechanism | Derivation checking, dedicated checking, runtime enforcement, testing, or an explicit assertion |
| Premises | Assumptions, dependency guarantees, and concrete premises required for application |
| Coverage | Supported language, bounds, abstractions, and correspondence relation |
| Applicability | Versions, configuration, validity conditions, and evidence that the claim still applies |
| Status | Established at the stated scope, accepted as a premise, open, contradicted, or inapplicable |

These dimensions need not appear as one literal map in every API, but they must
be recoverable in the assurance explanation. Conditionality and boundedness
qualify a claim; neither supplies an evidence mechanism. Several mechanisms can
support different parts of the same argument. There is no single total ranking
that turns all evidence into a universal confidence score.

A claim's *disposition*, its *evidence mechanism*, an individual check's
*result*, and the selected application's *admission or release decision* are
separate meanings. An established judgment states what its evidence actually
established within its quantification and observation scope. Its evidence may
be a proof, an exact finite check, or a completed test of expressly named
scenarios; sharing a claim disposition does not make those mechanisms equal.
A declared enforcement requirement is not evidence that the actual installed
host enforces it. A named trusted premise remains a premise even if policy
accepts it. Part XI's =:passed=, =:failed=, and other closed gate results
describe whether required checks completed at their scopes, not the semantic
strength of the propositions or the authority of their subjects.

The assurance model distinguishes:

1. properties established mechanically from explicit semantics or checked
   derivations;
2. premises enforced dynamically when a concrete execution meets the external
   world;
3. assumptions that remain outside the mechanically discharged core;
4. whole-application closure properties checked over assembled artifacts;
5. liveness claims whose validity depends on named environmental assumptions;
6. test evidence over trusted implementation boundaries and concrete systems.

These classifications are related, but they are not interchangeable.

For example:

#+begin_example
proved semantic property
!=
exact finite-state check
!=
closed application assembly
!=
runtime-enforced premise
!=
trusted assumption
!=
green integration test
#+end_example

A complete assurance argument may require several of them simultaneously.


A store checkout provides a useful worked example because one ordinary button
crosses nearly every assurance class without any one class subsuming the rest.

Suppose the semantic operation is declared approximately as:

#+begin_src clojure
(def place-order
  {:operation/id :checkout/place-order
   :actor :customer
   :input [:map [:cart/id uuid?]]
   :invocation
   {:requires [:authenticated-customer :admitted-command-binding]}
   :new-incorporation
   {:guard :cart/checkout-eligible?
    :requires [:bound-current-cart]
    :atomic-with :command/result-record}
   :repetition
   {:requires :command/result-disclosure-authority
    :result :permitted-recorded-outcome}
   :provisional
   {:view :checkout/submitting
    :owner :gesso/execution-id
    :correlate :gesso/command-id}
   :effect
   [:model/invoke :order/place
    {:cart-id [:cart/id :arguments]
     :customer :authenticated-customer
     :command-id :gesso/command-id}]
   :settlement
   {:accepted
    {:learns [:command/recorded-success :gesso/command-id]
     :reacquire [:order/by-command :gesso/command-id]}
    :rejected
    {:learns [:command/typed-rejection :gesso/command-id]
     :reacquire [:cart/current [:cart/id :arguments]]}
    :unknown
    {:recover [:order/reconcile-command :gesso/command-id]}}})
#+end_src

The command envelope supplies the stable command identity. Every presentation
execution and managed resource has its own applicable ownership identity.
Payment authorization, if required, is a separately declared external protocol;
a database transaction must not pretend to make a payment provider atomic with
an order write. The operation must define which provider outcome or durable
obligation permits order placement and how unknown external outcomes recover.

An assurance report during assembly may include both accepted claims and an
open progress obligation:

#+begin_src clojure
{:subject
 {:assembly :store/web
  :operation :checkout/place-order
  :plan :plan/checkout-place-order
  :artifact-digest "sha256:..."}

 :claims
 [{:id :checkout/rule-well-typed
   :proposition [:rule/well-typed :cart/checkout-eligible?]
   :status :established
   :evidence {:class :semantic-checked
              :mechanism :typed-rule-checker
              :result :check/cart-checkout-rule}}

  {:id :checkout/principal-required-at-runtime
   :proposition [:runtime/requires-current-principal
                 :checkout/place-order]
   :status :open-obligation
   :required-evidence
   {:class :runtime-enforced
    :boundary :server/authenticated-checkout-handler
    :obligation :actual-installed-boundary-correspondence}
   :premises #{:authentication/correct-principal-binding}}

  {:id :checkout/payment-provider-contract
   :proposition [:provider/validated-acceptance-means
                 :payment-authorized]
   :status :accepted-as-premise
   :evidence {:class :trusted
              :owner :external/payment-provider
              :assumption :payment/provider-contract-v4}}

  {:id :checkout/application-path-closed
   :proposition [:application/closed-path :checkout/place-order]
   :status :established
   :evidence {:class :assembly-verified
              :report :assembly/store-web-A12}}

  {:id :checkout/browser-realization-tested
   :proposition [:implementation-corresponds-in-scenarios
                 :checkout/place-order
                 #{:accepted :rejected :unknown}]
   :status :established
   :coverage {:scenarios #{:accepted :rejected :unknown}}
   :evidence {:class :tested
              :suite :chromium/checkout-lifecycle}}

  {:id :checkout/retry-eventually-recovers
   :proposition [:eventually [:checkout/settled-or-reconciled]]
   :status :open-obligation
   :environment-premises
   #{:eventual-provider-availability
     :eventual-authoritative-read
     :continued-participant-execution
     :fair-recovery-scheduling}
   :required-evidence :checked-progress-argument
   :evidence []}]}
#+end_src

Names in this report resolve to source-bound evidence or accepted premises;
they are not self-authenticating flags. In particular, the authentication row
is an *open physical enforcement obligation*. Naming a server boundary does
not establish that an actual running handler adopted it, and neither this
declaration nor a test of an isolated checker supplies a principal for a
future request. Actual adoption needs its own evidence under Part VII.

The browser row's =:established= disposition concerns only the expressly
listed scenarios and their completed, source-bound test evidence. Its
=:tested= evidence class does not prove universal browser correspondence,
and a suite name alone is not evidence that a current test ran or passed.
This row would remain open if the required run or artifact binding were
unavailable. The example's status labels illustrate these distinctions;
they do not introduce a rival schema for Part XI release-gate results.

The last row is deliberately open. Naming environmental premises is not a
progress proof. A release whose acceptance policy requires that progress
argument cannot claim completion from this report. If a suitable argument is
later checked, its mechanism and scope replace the open status while its
environmental premises remain attached.

The report is deliberately heterogeneous. Consider what happens if each claim is
misread as another:

#+begin_src clojure
;; Wrong: a type-check result does not authenticate this request.
(when (checking/accepted? :check/cart-checkout-rule)
  (treat-request-as-authenticated! request))

;; Wrong: a green Chromium test does not prove the provider contract.
(when (tests/green? :chromium/checkout-lifecycle)
  (assume-provider-cannot-lie!))

;; Wrong: assembly closure does not imply eventual provider availability.
(when (assembly/closed? :store/web)
  (claim-unconditional-termination!))

;; Wrong: the trusted provider premise does not prove the browser adapter.
(when (trusted? :payment/provider-contract-v4)
  (claim-browser-correspondence-proved!))
#+end_src

The correct composition keeps the propositions separate. A downstream release
claim may depend on all of them, but the resulting explanation should still be
able to say exactly which part is mechanically checked, which is dynamically
enforced, which remains trusted, which is assembly evidence, which is tested,
and which is conditional on future environmental behavior.

That is what the assurance vocabulary is for. It is not a taxonomy applied after
the fact; it prevents evidence from silently acquiring a stronger meaning as it
moves through the application.

The classification belongs to the *claim and its evidence*, not merely to the
source file, namespace, subsystem, or tool that produced it.  A single
component may therefore participate in several assurance classes.

For example, the browser adapter may have:

- machine-checked transition invariants;
- runtime-enforced event normalization and plan-identity checks;
- trusted assumptions about DOM/browser primitives;
- Chromium integration tests providing tested evidence of implementation
  correspondence;
- conditional liveness claims requiring eventual event delivery.

The root assurance report must preserve these distinctions rather than reducing
them to a single boolean such as =verified?=.

Nominal or otherwise admitted semantic categories participate in this model but
do not add a new assurance class by themselves.  For example, an admitted
=VerifiedChoreography= value may be the product emitted after verification, but
its assurance comes from the verifier/checker and its stated premises, not from
the runtime type name.  Likewise, an =AuthoritativeObservation= representation
is authoritative only when the trusted acquisition path establishes that
provenance.

This distinction prevents the implementation convenience of nominal typing from
being mistaken for proof:

| Boundary | What acceptance establishes |
|----------+-----------------------------|
| Representation validation | The value has the permitted shape |
| Semantic-category admission | Supported code may treat the value as category C at this boundary |
| Checked judgment or certificate | The stated proposition follows under the recorded premises and mechanism |

Those stages may be represented by different types, by checked constructors, or
by other existing mechanisms.  Their assurance meaning remains distinct even
if an implementation combines some of the physical representations.

*** Admission policy and unresolved proof obligations
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-admission-assurance-policy
:END:

Admission is a decision under a declared policy, not a synonym for proving every
application function. The policy identifies mandatory structural checks,
required evidence for particular claims, and which explicitly owned contracts
may remain asserted. The normal policy supports full-language implementations
while retaining their assumption dependencies.

Construction derives the obligations relevant to its declared claims and runs
the supported automated checks under an explicit resource policy. Results must
identify unsupported reasoning and exhausted checking budgets rather than omit
the obligation. An accepted assumption must not hide an available failed check.

Mandatory identity, interface, authority-source, effect-binding, and required
recovery checks cannot be disabled by adding an assertion. A named opaque
functional contract may be an accepted premise where policy permits it. The
result remains conditional; the premise is not marked discharged.

The checker and assembly must distinguish these outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning and consequence |
|---------+-------------------------|
| Established judgment | The stated proposition follows by the identified mechanism over its stated scope |
| Accepted assumption | A named premise is permitted by policy and remains in dependent explanations |
| Unsupported proof fragment | The reasoning mechanism lacks a rule or representation; this does not establish that the program is false |
| Inconclusive check | A limit, cancellation, or tool failure prevented a result; no success may be inferred |
| Counterexample | The checked model contains a violation; report its scope and whether concrete correspondence is established |
| Malformed declaration | Required semantic structure does not compose; reject construction |
| Failed runtime premise | A concrete execution cannot proceed on that premise; follow declared rejection or recovery |
| Inapplicable evidence | The subject, scope, dependency, or backend profile no longer matches; dependent acceptance requires re-establishment |

An unsupported or inconclusive proof obligation may be retained as an explicit
assumption only by a policy that permits that exact kind of premise and an
independently identified, scoped semantic or implementation contract whose
truth is being assumed. An exhausted proof budget, unavailable checker, or
unsupported expression is a checking outcome, not itself an assumption
asserting the original proposition. The policy must identify the original
obligation, selected weaker basis, affected dependent claims, and still-missing
evidence; a mandatory gate cannot be satisfied by renaming its failure.
The system must not silently convert a checker failure into trust. A known counterexample
cannot be erased by asserting the contradicted proposition under the same
claim. A correction to the specification, abstraction, implementation, or claim
scope must itself be explicit and checked as applicable.

The interface must make clear which obligations block admission and which
remain accepted premises. A component may be structurally admitted yet lack a
particular application theorem or release qualification. The root report must
not confuse these states.

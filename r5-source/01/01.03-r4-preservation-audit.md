# R5.1 — Part I, section 01.03 preservation audit

Baseline: R4 on `main`. Working candidate: R5.1, section 01.03.
Reviewed correctness layers, application closure, production enforcement,
behavioral completeness, operational testing, and required capabilities.

## Preserved and strengthened

- All seven R4 subsections survive unchanged in scope, including distinct
  declared closure, production enforcement, and independently required
  behavioral completeness.
- R5 replaces the R4 toy hand-maintained assembly registries with one
  source-owned application definition and derived realization edges while
  retaining the obligation to check real routes, plans, artifacts and providers.
- A self-consistently omitted Claim remains a product failure. A manually
  effective but unchecked POST remains an enforcement failure. A passing
  isolated checker cannot establish host adoption.
- R4's operational testing dimensions, positive eligible-path obligations,
  and explicit narrow-milestone limits remain.
- Deliberate host-language escape paths do not inherit a false closed-world
  writer-coverage or invariant claim.

## Focused revisions

1. An illustrative `assembly/check` `:result :accepted` is expressly a
   checked closure result, not issuance, runtime authority, or installed-host
   evidence. Its report cannot be promoted by copying it.
2. A positive Claim oracle requires Request status and an active primary
   RequestAssignment. An outcome label is not a new persisted field or API.
3. Refresh has two distinct positive scenarios: *without any advisory* and
   *with pending advisory debt*. A notice is not an acquisition grant, and a
   successful read or response is not completed adoption. Debts discharge
   only when the actually installed observation sufficiently covers them.

These revisions align the excerpt with R5 Part VIII (required capabilities,
Refresh, and actual assignment semantics) and Part XII Gates I, J, L.

## Remaining scope

The examples are illustrative; they do not assert existence of
`assembly/prepare`, `assembly/check`, `biff-host/start!`, or corresponding
executable APIs. Part VII still owns production host, writer, rendering and
artifact-adoption coverage. HumanHelp owns the real business and schema truth.
No R4 requirement was intentionally removed during this section review.
Neither full Part I nor the twelve-part plan has completed its preservation
audit. This textual review is not formal or deployed-runtime evidence.

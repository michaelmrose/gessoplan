** Full-language application logic and checked construction
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-full-language-construction
:END:

Gesso supports ordinary Clojure application logic. Functions, recursion,
collections, libraries, and computations outside a decidable or supported proof
fragment remain available. The checked rule language is a reasoning
representation, not the boundary of the application programming language.

The normal application path is checked managed construction. An application
does not move to an unmanaged second architecture because one policy predicate
or computation is opaque to a prover. Its operation identities, contracts,
authority boundaries, effects, settlement, and recovery still compose through
the same admission system.

Full-language support is relative to the declared execution target. A
JVM-specific Clojure function does not become browser-executable merely because
its interface can be serialized. Server logic may remain behind a public model
operation; a portable or browser-local realization must separately satisfy its
target contract. Projection preserves the declared placement and communication
boundaries rather than pretending to translate arbitrary host code.

An opaque implementation exports a semantic contract. The contract states the
input and output meanings, relevant preconditions and postconditions, permitted
effects, and the assumptions on which dependent reasoning relies. Claims of
purity, determinism, termination, or bounded resource use are required only when
the enclosing guarantee depends on them. Their evidence must then be explicit.
No such property follows merely from the function's name or declared result type.

There are three complementary ways to establish a needed property:

- derive it from supported semantic definitions and checked inference rules;
- enforce an instance-specific premise at the appropriate runtime boundary;
- accept a named asserted contract under the applicable admission policy and
  retain it in every dependent claim.

These mechanisms may be combined within one operation. An opaque business
predicate need not prevent proof that a transition preserves an unrelated
field, that a command identifier survives retries, or that a retired execution
cannot mutate a replacement owner. A conclusion that depends on that predicate
implementing the intended policy remains conditional on its contract.

*** Ordinary Clojure behind an explicit semantic interface

The following sample Organization policy requires the helper to hold the skills
listed for the Request's category and requires claiming to be enabled. This is
an illustrative policy for the example, not a mandatory HumanHelp product rule.
HumanHelp's actual policy remains application-owned.

#+begin_src clojure
(defn organization-allows?
  [{:keys [organization membership request]}]
  (let [policy (:organization/policy organization)
        required (get-in policy
                         [:skills-by-category (:request/category request)]
                         #{})
        held (set (:membership/skills membership))]
    (and (= :enabled (:claim policy))
         (every? #(contains? held %) required))))

(def organization-claim-policy-contract
  {:contract/id :organization/claim-policy
   :inputs {:organization Organization
            :membership Membership
            :request Request}
   :returns :boolean
   :state-inputs #{:organization/policy
                   :membership/skills
                   :request/category}
   :effects #{}
   :meaning
   {:true-iff [:and
               [:policy/claim-enabled]
               [:membership/holds-required-category-skills]]}})

(def organization-claim-policy-binding
  {:contract organization-claim-policy-contract
   :implementation #'organization-allows?
   :assertions
   [{:id :organization/policy-implementation-contract
     :proposition [:implementation/refines-contract
                   :organization/claim-policy]
     :owner :humanhelp/model-maintainer}
    {:id :organization/policy-effect-contract
     :proposition [:implementation/has-no-undeclared-effects
                   :organization/claim-policy]
     :owner :humanhelp/model-maintainer}]})
#+end_src

The two symbolic policy clauses refer to the displayed policy meaning: claiming
is enabled, and every required category skill is held. The contract vocabulary
and binding syntax are illustrative. Admission must resolve each symbol and
schema, validate its interface, and bind the assertions to the exact contract,
implementation realization, and applicability scope. Merely placing an
assertion map next to a function is not admission.

The framework can check declared argument/result categories and the surrounding
operation's use of them. It can evaluate the function on bound current inputs.
It must not label the implementation's policy correctness or lack of hidden
effects proved solely because those declarations are well formed.

For this effect-free contract, the declared state inputs determine which
authoritative facts must be protected when its result is used to authorize a
new commit. Data obtained before submission is suitable only when the commit
mechanism validates all relevant dependencies or recomputes the decision under
an equivalent atomic contract. A caller assertion that a pre-read is current
does not close that race.

An effectful computation instead declares its effects and their realization
boundaries. External service calls, clocks, randomness, mutable process state,
and asynchronous completion must not be hidden inside a contract claiming
deterministic evaluation over supplied values. Full-language support permits
these operations; their semantics and assumptions remain explicit.

*** A small authoring surface and a richer internal representation

Application authors provide domain meaning and genuine implementation choices.
Gesso derives the information it can derive soundly, including references,
footprints for supported expressions, obligations, and evidence dependencies.
Generated artifacts must be independently validated at their required boundary.

For opaque code, a declared effect or read footprint is an explicit contract,
not automatically an exact static analysis result. The assembly must retain
the evidence or assumption that connects it to the implementation. Where a
managed capability or candidate validator enforces a narrower property, that
property can have stronger evidence without upgrading the rest of the contract.

Ordinary authors must not have to write theorem-prover languages, proof DAGs,
transitive certificate maps, or hand-maintained copies of derivable metadata.
Proof-producing elaboration and dedicated checking belong inside Gesso.
Explicit data and ordinary functions are the canonical authoring basis; helper
macros may improve ergonomics but must not define a competing semantic system.

** Design priority and dependency direction

Gesso is organized around a strict distinction between semantic truth,
distributed coordination, physical realization, and evidence about that
realization.

At the highest level:

| Layer | Responsibility |
|-------+----------------|
| Domain model | Own business meaning and authoritative state transitions |
| Public model operations | Expose admitted operations and their contracts |
| Global choreography | Coordinate operations and participant knowledge |
| Role projections | Derive actor-local machines from the choreography |
| Executable plans | Carry canonical executable semantics and identities |
| Runtime realization | Perform declared effects through adapters and transport |

Evidence flows alongside this path:

Declarations export normalized semantic interfaces. Checking derives and
validates their obligations. Certificates, proof sidecars, and explanations
record the resulting judgments, evidence, and premises.

Evidence may justify acceptance of an executable artifact, but evidence does not
become an executable authority channel.

That distinction separates *admission evidence* from *runtime authority*.

A certificate, derivation, proof sidecar, exact artifact digest, preflight
report, or other assurance artifact may establish that an =ExecutablePlan= was
constructed from the declared semantics, depends on the expected semantic
interfaces, satisfies the checks claimed for it, and is therefore eligible to
run.  Once admitted, however, that evidence does not grant the running program
new principals, permissions, authoritative facts, capabilities, branch
discriminators, or current authorization.

For example, a certificate may establish:

#+begin_quote
A new incorporation of this Claim command can occur only through a declared
path requiring its current claim-authority premise. A compatible replay reaches
its recorded result through a separately declared disclosure-authority check.
#+end_quote

The certificate does not establish:

#+begin_quote
This particular HTTP request is currently made by principal P and P is currently
authorized to claim Request R.
#+end_quote

Those concrete premises still come from trusted authentication, current model
state, runtime authorization, and other declared runtime boundaries.


A fuller HumanHelp Claim slice makes that separation visible in code. The
operation is defined once and reused by later assembly and view examples.
The declaration syntax is illustrative; its distinctions are required.

#+begin_src clojure
(def ClaimInput
  [:map [:request/id uuid?]])

(def claimable-rule
  {:rule/id :request/new-claim-allowed
   :inputs {:principal Principal
            :arguments ClaimInput
            :request Request
            :membership Membership
            :organization Organization}
   :returns :boolean
   :expression
   [:and
    [:= [:request/id :request] [:request/id :arguments]]
    [:= [:membership/user-id :membership] [:user/id :principal]]
    [:= [:membership/organization-id :membership]
        [:request/organization-id :request]]
    [:= [:organization/id :organization]
        [:request/organization-id :request]]
    [:= [:request/status :request] :open]
    [:= [:membership/status :membership] :active]
    [:call :organization/claim-policy
     {:organization :organization
      :membership :membership
      :request :request}]]})

(def claim-request
  {:operation/id :request/claim
   :actor :helper
   :input ClaimInput
   :entry-knowledge [[:knows? :helper [:request/id]]]

   :invocation
   {:requires [:authenticated-principal :admitted-command-binding]
    :model-operation :request/claim}

   :new-incorporation
   {:guard claimable-rule
    :policy-binding organization-claim-policy-binding
    :transition
    {:set {:request/status :claimed
           :request/helper [:user/id :principal]}
     :preserve :other-request-domain-fields}
    :atomic-with :command/result-record}

   :repetition
   {:binding [:operation :semantic-arguments :principal :authority-scope]
    :requires :command/result-disclosure-authority
    :result :permitted-recorded-outcome
    :incorporate-again? false}

   :effect
   [:model/invoke :request/claim
    {:request-id [:request/id :arguments]
     :principal :authenticated-principal
     :command-id :gesso/command-id}]

   :provisional
   {:view :request/claiming
    :owner :gesso/execution-id
    :correlate :gesso/command-id}

   :settlement
   {:accepted
    {:learns [:command/recorded-success :gesso/command-id]
     :reacquire [:request/card [:request/id :arguments]]}
    :rejected
    {:learns [:command/typed-rejection :gesso/command-id]
     :reacquire [:request/card [:request/id :arguments]]}
    :unknown
    {:recover [:request/reconcile-claim
               {:request-id [:request/id :arguments]
                :command-id :gesso/command-id}]}}})
#+end_src

=:actor= describes a semantic participant position; it does not authenticate a
browser. Schema names denote admitted domain interfaces. Their relationship
fields are shown explicitly so that a same-shaped Membership or Organization
cannot be substituted merely because it is active or has a permissive policy.
An implementation may establish these bindings through checked acquisition
contracts, explicit guards, or both; the obligation is not optional.

The policy call uses the contract defined above. Its argument/result checking
is mechanically available. Any undischarged implementation or effect assertion
remains a dependency of the corresponding Claim guarantee.

The guard belongs to new incorporation. It is not a precondition for recognizing
an already incorporated command. Otherwise a successful retry would be rejected
because its own first incorporation changed the Request from open to claimed.
Disclosure of a recorded result has its own current authorization policy; it
must not be confused with permission to perform a new claim.

Verification of the declaration produces claim-specific evidence. For example:

| Proposition | Required evidence and scope |
|-------------+-----------------------------|
| The new-claim guard is well typed | Dedicated semantic checking over its declared inputs and policy-call interface |
| New incorporation requires the guard | A checked reachability/transition argument over the admitted operation semantics |
| Compatible replay does not incorporate again | A checked command-protocol argument, conditional on the authoritative command-record contract |
| Replay disclosure requires its own authority | Checked semantic requirement plus concrete runtime enforcement |
| The declaration covers accepted, rejected, and unknown settlements | Exhaustive checking over that finite declared outcome set |
| The policy implementation realizes its contract | The named assertion unless stronger corresponding evidence is supplied |

These are separate judgments. A certificate may collect them only with their
actual evidence, scope, and premises. Merely emitting the table as a map does
not establish any row. A rule checker cannot decide whether Alice's future
Membership is active, and a plan proof cannot by itself establish that an
independently written handler enforces its declared guard.

A trusted invocation boundary binds the concrete caller and passes the command
to the public authoritative model operation:

#+begin_src clojure
(defn invoke-claim!
  [{:keys [session now] :as ctx}
   {:request/keys [id] :gesso/keys [command-id]}]
  (let [principal (auth/current-principal! session)]
    ;; The admitted model binding validates the command envelope and owns
    ;; replay, current disclosure permission, and new-incorporation guards.
    (request-model/claim!
      (assoc ctx :biff.fx/now now :principal principal)
      {:request-id id :command-id command-id})))
#+end_src

The context is supplied through the admitted server binding. The principal does
not come from the command body. The public model operation must establish the
relationship between the principal and the Membership it uses, rather than
trust a browser-supplied helper identifier.

*** Claim command identity, replay, and uncertainty

Before the command's first possible submission, its command boundary allocates
a stable =:gesso/command-id=. Its allocation may be local or use an earlier
trusted issuance protocol. Allocating the bytes is separate from establishing
their trusted binding. At the authoritative boundary, authentication and command
admission establish or validate the operation, canonical semantic arguments,
principal, and authority scope to which the identity is bound. The binding must
be compatible with the admitted operation version. Browser-carried bytes alone
confer no authority.

A retry preserves that command identity. A new transport attempt has a new
transport-request identity, and a new presentation execution has its own
execution and resource-instance identities. Neither changes which command is
being reconciled.

The public =request-model/claim!= operation checks for a compatible recorded
command before interpreting the Request as a new claim. It validates the
recorded binding and current permission to disclose the result. A permitted
successful replay returns the recorded success without another transition,
including when the Request is now claimed or has subsequently changed again.
Incompatible identity reuse is rejected without disclosing another principal's
record or outcome.

For new incorporation, the Request identity and state, the principal's active
Membership, the Organization identity and policy, and every relevant
relationship or predicate dependency are protected by the same atomic contract
as the ownership change and successful command-result record. A pre-read may
prepare a candidate; it cannot replace commit-time protection of those facts.

Two attempts can both initially observe no command record. If one commits and
the other's guard fails, the failing attempt must use the command protocol to
determine whether the same command was incorporated concurrently. It must not
report a fresh domain rejection merely because its attempt lost a race.
Similarly, absence observed at one read basis does not establish that an
outstanding attempt cannot later commit. Part IV owns the authoritative
resolution, retry, retention, and terminal-rejection contracts.

Part IV distinguishes model outcomes =:committed=, =:rejected=,
=:already-incorporated=, and =:commit-unknown=. This example presents
=:accepted=, =:rejected=, or =:unknown= to the participant:

| Model outcome | Presentation settlement |
|---------------+-------------------------|
| =:committed= | =:accepted= when permitted successful command evidence is delivered |
| =:rejected= | =:rejected= when an authoritative typed rejection for the applicable command scope is delivered |
| =:already-incorporated= | The permitted recorded result; recorded success maps to =:accepted= without a second transition |
| =:commit-unknown= | =:unknown= until authorized outcome evidence resolves it |

A delivery failure may leave the participant uncertain even after a commit.
Conversely, accepted command evidence establishes that the command succeeded at
its recorded point; it does not establish that the Request is still owned by
that helper at every later observation. Current presentation comes from an
authorized acquisition with its own basis and scope. Reading a currently
claimed Request does not by itself identify the command that claimed it.

The assembly checks these mappings and the required acquisition/reconciliation
paths. Acquisitions still enforce current read permission and must represent
denial, absence, or an unavailable dependency under their declared contracts.
Neither accepted nor rejected settlement grants a new right to read the card.

An admitted Claim plan may have strong structural and transition guarantees
while remaining conditional on a policy assertion, an authentication boundary,
or a qualified backend contract. Production correspondence must establish which
realization enforces those requirements. A certificate cannot manufacture the
principal, Membership, Request state, Organization policy, or current authority.
Sending it back from the browser adds no authority.

The two decisions remain separate:

| Stage | Basis of the decision |
|-------+-----------------------|
| Artifact admission | Appropriate proof, checking, and preflight evidence under the admission policy |
| Concrete execution | The admitted runtime semantics and current trusted premises |
| Authority decision | The applicable principal, resource, policy, scope, and current state |

The following inference is forbidden:

#+begin_quote
A certificate says "valid", so the runtime grants authority, accepts a current
fact, or selects a semantic branch without establishing its required premises.
#+end_quote

This applies more broadly than authorization.  Assurance evidence must not
secretly decide which role the current user has, which principal is
authenticated, whether a current authoritative fact is true, whether a stale
basis is acceptable, which settlement is canonical, or what transaction should
commit.

A separately declared runtime credential, observation receipt, or other evidence
protocol may supply a runtime premise only through its own trusted validation
contract, with its subject, scope, applicability, and authorization meaning made
explicit. That is a distinct runtime input category. It does not turn a proof
sidecar, build report, or matching artifact hash into authority.

Proof and diagnostic sidecars are therefore runtime-nonsemantic.  Running the
same =ExecutablePlan= with or without a matching read-only sidecar must produce
the same semantic transitions and abstract effects, modulo diagnostics.
Evidence explains or justifies why an executable was accepted; it does not
become a bearer capability or an alternate semantic control path.

The dependency direction is intentional:

- application business meaning belongs in application models;
- choreography coordinates use of public model semantics;
- projection derives local behavior from the global choreography;
- runtime code executes projected behavior;
- proof/checking artifacts explain why construction was accepted;
- physical browser, network, database, and process mechanisms realize declared
  effects under explicit assumptions.

A lower layer must not acquire higher-level meaning merely because doing so
would make an implementation convenient. For example:

- a DOM node does not become a semantic Request merely because it has an id;
- a web node does not become Request authority merely because it handles the
  request;
- a browser-supplied principal id does not become authenticated identity because
  it has the expected shape;
- a map with =:verified? true= does not become a verified choreography;
- a matching hash does not establish the truth of the proposition represented
  by the hashed artifact;
- a Live invalidation does not establish the changed authoritative fact;
- a rendered button does not confer authorization to execute its operation.

** Normative language

This design uses the following terms deliberately.

- *MUST* or *must* identifies a correctness requirement of the intended design.
- *MUST NOT* or *must not* identifies behavior that would violate the intended
  correctness boundary.
- *SHOULD* or *should* identifies the ordinary design path.  Deviations require
  a concrete reason and must preserve the relevant invariants.
- *MAY* or *may* identifies an implementation freedom that does not change the
  semantic contract.
- *proved* is reserved for mechanically established properties under named
  assumptions.
- *verified* may also describe a mechanically checked closed construction whose
  result is not claimed as a theorem over an explicitly formalized semantic
  relation.  The result classification must remain precise.
- *trusted* does not mean "probably correct."  It means the proposition is
  outside the proof currently being claimed and is accepted as an explicit
  assumption.

A green test suite never silently upgrades a tested property into a proved
property.

A successful schema validation never silently upgrades a value into trusted
authority.

A valid certificate identity never silently upgrades the certificate's claims
into truth unless an appropriate checker has accepted the certificate under the
premises and evidence classification attached to those claims.

*** Interpretation of examples and implementation freedom

Examples illustrate the enclosing contract. Their concrete syntax is
illustrative unless the owning section explicitly defines it as the required
public or wire representation. Their stated required behavior must still obey
the normative invariants.

Examples using symbolic operators must identify their semantic contract and
whether they denote checked expressions, runtime premises, opaque calls, or
inspection output. Unknown operators and unresolved references are construction
errors; illustrative syntax is not permission to accept arbitrary forms.

An abbreviated diagram does not waive an authorization, admission, scope,
ownership, or outcome-attribution condition merely because the diagram omits
that step. Worked examples must identify consequential policy choices instead
of silently selecting behavior that the specification leaves open.

The implementation may choose a representation or mechanism where the design
allows alternatives. It must state which supported contract was chosen and
provide evidence appropriate to the resulting claim. Implementation freedom
does not permit silent weakening of a required capability or assurance class.

A contradiction between normative passages is a specification defect to be
reconciled at the owning section. It is not permission to select whichever
passage makes an implementation easier to accept.

A declared recovery path establishes available modeled behavior. It does not
by itself establish that the environment will cause that path to execute or
that every execution terminates. Liveness claims retain their named premises.

# R5.1 Part I — 01.07 semantic evidence vocabulary preservation audit

Baseline: canonical R4 on `main`. Candidate: R5.1, section 01.07.
Status: section-level source comparison, not a proof or complete plan audit.

## Preserved or strengthened

- All twelve original R4 vocabulary subsections survive: identities,
  executable core/checkers, raw declaration, admitted semantic value, semantic
  interface, assumption, guarantee, obligation, derivation, certificate,
  semantic identity, and proof DAG.
- R5 keeps the distinct command, execution, resource, transport, assembly
  and compatibility identities; it adds submission-attempt and concrete
  capability-instance identities without collapsing their lifetimes.
- R5's stronger separation of the executable semantic core, small proof
  kernel, dedicated semantic checkers, and their trust bases remains intact.
- A nominal or same-shaped value cannot gain admission, runtime authority,
  semantic truth, or certificate status merely through representation.
- Semantic identity is a normalized, versioned binding; it is not a general
  Clojure-program equivalence oracle and cannot automatically transfer
  implementation/test applicability.
- A proof DAG remains acyclic; accepted recursion/fixed-point reasoning
  requires an independently formalized inference rule. Namespace cycles
  are not proof recursion.

## Focused clarifications

1. A semantic interface distinguishes established guarantees from the
   *required assumptions* under which those guarantees hold. Exporting a
   dependency does not itself discharge it.
2. Assumption identity includes proposition, subject, bindings, scope and
   appropriate runtime context. Two different principals' Membership claims
   are not the same premise simply because a label matches. Policy acceptance
   retains a conditional premise rather than upgrading it.
3. A derivation is proposed machine-readable evidence until the correct
   checker validates the judgment; proof-kernel implementation soundness
   remains a separately classified obligation.
4. A certificate-shaped report is raw until the appropriate checker/issuer
   establishes source-bound claims, dependencies and applicability. Copying
   a report or digest is not re-admission.
5. A valid, acyclic selected proof DAG may still omit *source-required*
   obligations. The mandatory ledger and required dependency edges are
   derived from the actual source and selected profile, and checked against
   the chosen DAG; an omitted guard, writer or authorization edge cannot be
   hidden by submitting a smaller valid graph. Alternative justifications
   inherit only their real premises.

These are definitions and requirements, not assertions that v1622 Gesso
has already proved complete application certification. Part IX owns the
checker, derivation and issuer rules; Part X owns soundness and physical
correspondence; Part XI owns release re-admission and applicability.

No R4 normative requirement was intentionally discarded. Other Part I
sections and the complete twelve-part R5.1 preservation audit remain open.

# R5.1 Part II — 02.10 Authoritative Observation

**Baseline:** R4 `main`, Part II, “Authoritative observation as a knowledge
path.” **Candidate:** `r5.1` source section 02.10.
**Status:** focused R4 preservation review; not a tested application,
authenticated receipt implementation, physical realization proof, or
whole-Part-II acceptance.

## R4 requirements preserved

- Authoritative rereads through admitted HTML projections can establish
  semantic knowledge without a dedicated universal choreography `:observe`
  instruction; authority remains with the model/database.
- Live invalidation is advisory possible-change information, not a fact that
  the indicated domain state changed or that a command committed.
- Acquiring a later observation and adopting it are separate decisions
  governed by user/application policy and explicit liveness assumptions.
- Observations are scoped to authority, subject/projection, query/temporal
  meaning and justified progression. Independently acquired facts do not
  automatically form one globally consistent snapshot.
- A bare client `:observed-basis` is useful but forgeable protocol context,
  not evidence of prior server issuance or current authorization.
- A security-sensitive prior-observation premise needs server-verifiable
  issuance evidence with the stated cryptographic or trusted-session premises.
- An authenticated receipt attests its actual issuance proposition, not
  browser delivery, installation, human understanding or current permission.
- Receipt replay, one-shot capabilities, command idempotency, and expiry are
  different contracts; a MAC alone is neither encryption nor single-use.
- A later short choreography can use newly acquired scoped entry knowledge;
  ordinary Live need not create an indefinitely suspended execution.
- Knowledge correctness is representation-independent conditional on the
  specified projection and trust boundary.

## Superior R5 requirements retained

- Detailed observation result/coverage distinctions: complete, partial or
  paged, authorized not-found, privacy-masked denial and unavailable reads.
- Subject/query/projection/visibility/temporal equivalence is required before
  declaring conflicting observations or assuming one basis orders another.
- Acquisition, physical installation and semantic adoption are separately
  identified, including late callbacks and pending advisory requirements.
- Receipt payloads bind issuer, version, audience, key, principal, authority,
  projection, scope, basis, temporal semantics, semantic digest, purpose,
  issuance event and lifecycle. Verification derives trusted keys and
  contracts server-side rather than allowing algorithm/issuer selection by
  client data.
- A provider-qualified basis is not a permit for unbounded waiting, choosing
  another backend, or evading authoritative transaction guards.

## Changes in this revision

1. **Preserve observation scope under render reuse.** Cached and
   singleflight-shared HTML retains its source read/basis, coverage,
   principal/visibility and query/temporal context. Render reuse does not
   independently confer fresh authority or proof of a later observation.
2. **Require key-equivalence and current disclosure.** Cache and in-flight
   sharing must be separated by render-affecting dimensions or an established
   semantic equivalence. Scope alone or a short TTL does not establish
   permission; explicitly bounded grants remain possible under their contract.
3. **Respect selective adoption.** A deliberately retained historical board
   may remain visible without being claimed as a newer read or silently
   discharging a stronger advisory requirement.
4. **Tie receipt issuance to actual content and event.** Reused content does
   not become a new physical database read through a fresh receipt, nor may
   one principal inherit another's issuance context.
5. **Add seven negative cases.** Wrong-principal cached results, incompatible
   minimum-read sharing, incomplete query absences, stale receipts, pending
   advisories, retired physical owners, and wrong semantic scope receive
   distinct outcomes.

## Current Gesso implementation and open obligations

- `gesso.live.fragment` has cached/singleflight rendering, normalizes a
  `:progression` key dimension, and provides `strict-fragment-key` requiring
  `:fragment`, `:scope`, and `:user-key`; it explicitly leaves all
  render-affecting dimensions and sharing policy to its caller.
- `gesso.live.progression` preserves orderless sets of opaque bases rather
  than inventing an ordering from arrival time or numbers; truthful ordering
  evidence remains a trusted adapter premise.
- `gesso.live.browser.adapter` gates HTMX request/swap generations and
  coalesces queued refresh requirements but does not by itself attest read
  provenance, authorize content, or prove physical DOM execution.
- `gesso.choreo.knowledge` records authoritative observation provenance
  conditionally on external read/authority premises.
- `gesso.assurance.proof.xtdb-poststate-basis-acquisition` reports bounded
  pinned poststate/ledger agreement, not transaction causality or complete
  physical writer correspondence.
- The inspected source does not establish that every application fragment
  cache, subscription, signed observation receipt, or deployed route has been
  qualified under the entire R5 security/observation contract.

## Cross-part obligations

- Part II 02.09, 02.11 and 02.12: knowledge support, semantic agreement,
  scoped frontier comparisons and selective adoption.
- Part IV: provider basis/coverage, durable incorporation, read consistency,
  and causality or explicitly unestablished premises.
- Parts V–VII: Live coalescing, cache/fragment construction, physical HTMX
  ownership, and actual authorization and application-path coverage.
- Parts IX–X: independent proof evidence and physical read/callback
  correspondence without converting readback diagnostics into certificates.

The `req-authoritative-observation` and `req-observation-evidence` anchors
are unchanged. No R4 normative observation or receipt distinction was
intentionally dropped. This audit states requirements rather than proving
that Gesso currently fulfills all of them.

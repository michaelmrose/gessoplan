** Facts, time, and authoritative progression

The design distinguishes at least the following dimensions.

*** Valid/effective time

Valid or effective time describes when a domain fact applies in the modeled
world.

Examples:

- Membership became active on one date;
- a Request was open during one interval;
- Organization policy changed at a domain-effective time;
- a correction applies retroactively to an earlier period.

Valid time belongs to domain meaning.

*** Recording time and correction history

Recording or system time identifies the authoritative history under which a
fact was recorded, corrected, or withdrawn. It differs from the valid/effective
time at which that fact applies in the domain. A correction recorded today may
change the authoritative interpretation of an earlier valid-time interval.

An observation that depends on both dimensions must retain both, directly or
through its admitted read contract. A read basis that bounds transaction
visibility does not alone select the valid time or historical correction view.
The database provider defines the supported relation between these coordinates
under its qualified backend profile.

*** Domain or causal relation

Domain or causal relations describe how semantic facts relate.

Examples include:

- based on;
- produced by;
- superseded by;
- reconciled by;
- rejected by;
- confirmed by;
- corrected by;
- derived from.

These relations are not necessarily database transaction order.

*** Authoritative database progression

Authoritative database progression describes the consistency/basis frontier at
which authoritative facts are visible.

For HumanHelp's primary persistent authority, this is supplied by the admitted
XTDB provider under its [[#req-backend-assumption-qualification][qualified backend contract]].
The provider owns token interpretation and the evidence for a comparison or
read guarantee. Generic choreography code does not decode an XTDB token and
invent those semantics independently.

A basis is scoped to its authority/provider context and relevant database
incarnation or equivalent identity. Equal raw tokens from different databases,
tenants where applicable, or reset histories are not automatically comparable.

An authoritative progression value may advance because unrelated data changed.

Therefore:

#+begin_example
database advanced
!=
this Request changed
#+end_example

*** Actor knowledge frontier

An actor knowledge frontier describes what authoritative information a
particular runtime actor has acquired through its admitted observation paths.
It is scoped by the facts, authority, projection, and relevant temporal context
of those observations. It need not be one token covering all actor knowledge.

For the supported browser implementation, the frontier records semantic
acquisition and incorporation by that implementation. It is not a server proof
that an adversarial browser or the human using it retained or understood the
information.

Two browser actors may observe the same authoritative database at different
frontiers.

The same principal may therefore have two browser tabs with different
knowledge.

The database's current state and an actor's current knowledge are distinct.
Acquired evidence, content selected for adoption, and content successfully
installed in a presentation may also be distinct. A protocol must identify
which transition advances each claimed frontier; merely scheduling a read or
allowing a DOM swap does not establish its completion.

*** Physical delivery time

Physical delivery time may matter operationally without becoming semantic
authority.

An SSE notification may arrive after another message.

A callback may fire after an execution has been retired.

A browser may wake after a deployment.

The fact that an event arrived later in wall-clock time does not by itself make
the event semantically newer or authorized to mutate current state.

*** Do not collapse dimensions into one revision integer

The framework must not collapse:

#+begin_example
valid time
recording/correction time
domain fact identity/version
causal relation
XTDB progression
actor knowledge frontier
command identity
execution identity
artifact compatibility identity
#+end_example

into one global monotonically increasing application revision.

A global revision number would erase distinctions that matter to correctness.

For example, XTDB may advance while Request R is unchanged.

Likewise, Request R may have meaningful valid-time history independent of when a
database transaction was recorded.

A browser may hold knowledge through frontier X17 while authority has progressed
to X23 without any relevant Request fact changing.

That is ordinary distributed execution, not necessarily staleness requiring
rejection.

*** Progression as provenance

An observed authoritative basis records something approximately like:

#+begin_quote
The representation from which this actor formed its command was justified by an
authoritative read consistent through this frontier.
#+end_quote

It does not mean:

- the entire database is still at that frontier;
- every fact the command depends on is unchanged;
- the client owns authority over that frontier;
- the client can prove merely by sending the token that it really performed the
  observation;
- the framework should reject every command formed from an older frontier.

The authoritative-operations and progression part defines the exact command,
scope, and XTDB semantics, including
[[#req-progression-scope][scoped progression requirements]].

At the ontology level, authoritative progression is one provenance coordinate
among several and is not a universal domain revision.

*** Scoped progression decisions and requirement composition

A comparison result binds its exact operands, comparison context, and admitted
provider contract. Generic code can validate the representation and binding of
a witness such as "B2 advances B1"; that check does not prove the assertion is
true. The provider or another declared reasoning mechanism must establish its
meaning, with any backend assumptions retained.

An explicit comparison distinguishes equivalence, advancement, precedence,
known incomparability, and unavailable or invalid comparison evidence where the
contract supports them. Failure to establish an ordering is not proof of
incomparability, and incomparable observations need not be individually invalid.

Pending acquisition requirements compose as a conjunction of scoped
obligations. A conservative representation retains an orderless set of distinct
requirements and removes exact duplicates. Replacing it with one basis, taking
a numeric maximum, or discarding a requirement as subsumed needs an admitted
coverage/implication argument. No join or total order is assumed merely because
the provider can compare some pairs of bases.

An observation discharges a requirement only when its authority, subject,
projection/query scope, temporal meaning, principal context where relevant,
and progression satisfy that requirement. A later card read cannot by itself
discharge a board-membership acquisition requirement. Part IV owns the concrete
algebra and provider contract.

Bounds on retained requirements must preserve this meaning. Coalescing may
produce a conservative stronger reacquisition obligation when the contract
justifies it. Silently dropping debt to meet a memory bound is not permissible.
When no sound bounded representation is available, the runtime follows its
declared backpressure, retirement, or reacquisition policy.

# R5.1 Part II — 02.02 Business Rules and the Auditable Semantic Surface

Baseline: R4 `main`, Part II's business-rules section. Candidate:
`r5.1/r5-source/02/02.02-business-rules-and-auditable-surface.org`.
This is one sectional normative-document review, not executable proof or
completion of the full Part II R4-preservation audit.

## Preserved from R4

- All of R4's business-rule categories remain: authorization, disclosure,
  ownership, lifecycle, protected mutation, conserved resources, command
  incorporation, knowledge and invariants. The examples remain *possible*
  policies, not newly mandated HumanHelp choices.
- A condition that Membership status equals `:active` does not itself prove
  claimant, Organization, Request or commit-time relationship eligibility.
  The illustrative rule still binds all subjects and requires new
  incorporation-time protection, not a browser or stale server pre-read.
- Ordinary Clojure, named contracts, and compact auditable semantics are
  preferred over mandatory macro DSLs or duplication of business policy.
- Rules have both a machine-facing meaning and a human-auditable rationale.
  Comments improve review but are not mechanically checkable evidence unless
  represented as appropriately retained metadata/documentation.
- Opaque correctness-relevant computations stay named dependencies. A same-
  named function or true result does not make its semantic contract proved.
- The compact review surface continues to coexist with arbitrary application
  implementation code and must not make every conditional an explicit rule.

## R5.1 improvements in this focused revision

1. **Rule AST boundary.** The sample's field-path vector is clearly
   illustrative semantic-interface notation, not Part IX's admitted
   `[:read reader-id]` AST. A status conjunct alone can be a closed typed
   rule given a declared reader/nominal enum. The full rule still needs
   checked source elaboration and relationship/principal/commit-time
   obligations. Otherwise it remains a scoped opaque contract, not a proved
   result.
2. **One owner per policy fact.** The public model contract owns its business
   meaning and effective bindings; Choreo, UI, and proof artifacts consume
   that meaning rather than maintain a second Claim eligibility rule.
3. **Audit completeness is scoped.** A declared rule inventory is not proof
   that arbitrary host Clojure contains no other consequential decisions.
   Actual decision/writer paths must be accounted for within the selected
   checking profile; uninspected internals are conditional, not silently
   empty. An observed consequential escape outside admitted rules, runtime
   premises and accepted opaque contracts is reported as such. Part IX
   retains ownership of the detailed coverage and certification judgment.

## Follow-up boundaries

Part IV owns concrete atomic Claim enforcement and the still-known older
synthetic helper-field example. Part VIII owns the production
RequestAssignment and Membership relationships. Part IX owns the exact typed
rule grammar, source-derived obligation ledger, and policy-escape checks.
Part X owns any universal proof/implementation correspondence claims.

No R4 normative rule was intentionally removed. R5's superior full-language
managed-construction model remains the target. The combined twelve-part plan
is still `r5-draft`; a preservation audit does not establish deployment
or proof-kernel correctness.

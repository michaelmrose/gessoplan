** Agreement frontiers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-agreement-frontiers
:END:

For evolving values, actors may agree only through a common frontier for the
relevant facts and relation. A frontier is not necessarily one globally ordered
position for all actor knowledge.

For a single modeled chain whose prefix observations are retained and justify
the claimed agreement, for example:

| Participant | Justified retained prefix of the modeled chain |
|-------------+------------------------------------------------|
| Actor A | F1, F2, F3, F4 |
| Actor B | F1, F2, F3 |
| Common prefix for this relation | F1, F2, F3 |

The example requires the stated prefix knowledge. Observing only the latest
snapshot at F4 does not automatically establish that the actor knows every
intermediate transition through F4. Snapshot agreement and agreement on an
event-history prefix are different relations.

The application universe may change continually.

Complete instantaneous agreement among all relevant actors may never occur.

That is not itself a correctness failure.

Correctness instead requires:

- recorded observations retain their historical meaning, while domain
  corrections and the applicability of their interpretations remain explicit;
- relevant divergence is explicit rather than silently erased;
- actors possess enough justified knowledge for the behavior they perform;
- where convergence is required, the protocol provides a valid path toward a
  newer common authoritative frontier;
- under quiescence and the choreography's stated liveness assumptions, required
  agreement debt can fall to zero.

*** Frontier is relation, not necessarily arithmetic

A frontier need not be represented by an integer.

The browser must not invent arithmetic ordering over opaque progression values
unless the authoritative system explicitly provides a supported ordering
operation.

The relevant semantic questions are relational:

- is this observation at least as strong as the requirement that triggered it?
- are two authoritative observations compatible for the fact whose agreement
  is being claimed?
- has this actor incorporated the authoritative change it was required to
  observe?
- may this local projection continue under the current compatibility contract?

The concrete XTDB and scope-coverage mechanisms are specified in the
authoritative-operations and progression part. An ordering relation must not be
assumed to provide a join, a greatest common frontier, or complete observation
coverage unless the supported contract actually establishes those properties.

*** Different actors may have different valid frontiers

For example:

| Participant | Justified scope in this example |
|-------------+----------------------------------|
| Authority | Modeled chain through F5 |
| Actor A | Required prefix observations through F5 |
| Actor B | Required prefix observations through F3 |
| Actor C | Only its privacy-permitted subset through F4 |

There may be no meaningful statement that all three actors share one universal
application frontier.

Agreement and knowledge are scoped to the relevant facts and authorities.

*** Advisory knowledge and adopted knowledge

An actor may learn that authority advanced without immediately adopting the new
authoritative projection.

This distinction is important for application behaviors such as a multi-user
board:

| Transition | Advisory state | Adopted projection |
|------------+----------------+--------------------|
| Relevant advisory received | Records the justified scoped reacquisition requirement | Existing projection remains selected |
| Explicit Refresh succeeds under the installation policy | Discharges only requirements covered by the adopted observation | Selects the newly admitted projection |

The advisory requirements and adopted authoritative observations are therefore
distinct pieces of actor state. The word frontier is shorthand for the scoped
knowledge relation; it does not require either state to be represented by one
maximum scalar token.

A correct application may intentionally preserve that distinction.


HumanHelp's board can represent that distinction explicitly instead of
overloading one current-revision slot. Suppose Alice creates Request R3 while
Bob's adopted board contains R1 and R2. The examples below are abbreviated
inspection and pure-transition fixtures; they are not browser assertions that
the server must trust.

The selected board projection is complete for its declared search/filter query,
not necessarily for every Request in the Organization. Each acquisition records
its concrete temporal context under the current-state read contract. X17, X18,
X19, and X20 are opaque basis labels; the admitted provider establishes the
relations used in this example.

#+begin_src clojure
(def board-scope
  {:organization-id O
   :projection :request/board-v1
   :query {:search "" :show-terminal? false :order :newest}
   :coverage-contract :complete-selected-query
   :time-policy :current-state})

(def bob-board
  {:actor [:client :bob-tab]
   :principal-context :session/bob-1
   :owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
   :projection board-scope
   :adopted
   {:requests [{:request/id R1 :request/status :open :request/title "R1"}
               {:request/id R2 :request/status :open :request/title "R2"}]
    :observation
    {:authority [:authority :request-board O]
     :scope board-scope
     :principal-context :session/bob-1
     :basis :X17
     :temporal-context T17}}
   :advisory {:requirements #{}}
   :acquisition nil})
#+end_src

For this example, the producing authority can attach a justified lower bound
X18 to the advisory generated by its committed change. The advisory does not
say that R3 is visible to Bob, and its issuance/disclosure follows the admitted
subscription contract.

#+begin_src clojure
(def create-advisory
  {:event/class :board-advisory
   :owner
   {:subscription/id :subscription/bob-board
    :source-instance/id :source/bob-board-1
    :scope board-scope}
   :requirement
   {:scope board-scope
    :authority [:authority :request-board O]
    :minimum-basis :X18}})

(def current-board-subscription
  {:board-subscription
   {:subscription/id :subscription/bob-board
    :source-instance/id :source/bob-board-1
    :scope board-scope
    :principal-context :session/bob-1
    :status :open}})

(defn record-advisory
  [board admitted-advisory]
  (let [requirement
        (advisory-admission/require-requirement-for!
          admitted-advisory
          {:scope (:projection board)
           :principal-context (:principal-context board)})]
    (update-in board [:advisory :requirements]
      requirements/conjoin requirement)))

(def admitted-create-advisory
  (admit-board-advisory
    admitted-board-source-context
    current-board-subscription
    create-advisory))

(def bob-after-advisory
  (record-advisory bob-board admitted-create-advisory))

(get-in bob-after-advisory [:adopted :requests])
;; => still R1 and R2

(get-in bob-after-advisory [:advisory :requirements])
;; => the scoped requirement for at least X18
#+end_src

=requirements/conjoin= retains the conjunction of obligations, conservatively
as an orderless set unless admitted implication evidence permits simplification.
It does not choose whichever token arrived last. The admission functions above
resolve registered source/owner contracts; calling a map admitted does not
supply that evidence. The local source context belongs to the cooperating
browser realization and is not a server credential.

The symbols =O=, =R1=, =R2=, =R3=, =A2= and the example observation/advisory
values are prebound inspection fixtures, not executable definitions. Functions
under =advisory-admission=, =requirements= and =board-runtime= are schematic
contracts for this worked example, not claims that those named public Gesso
Vars currently exist. Their actual implementations must be checked against
the admitted application, producer registration, progression comparison and
browser effect/owner semantics before any resulting guarantee is reported.

A requirement saying only "later than the browser's X17" would not necessarily
cover the change at X18. The browser must not manufacture X18 from an advisory
with no such bound. A tokenless notification needs its separately declared
fresh-acquisition or source-fence protocol. Part IV defines how that protocol
establishes sufficient coverage.

Bob has learned that reacquisition may be needed or available for the scoped
board. He has not thereby learned R3's title, status, requester, or visibility.
The application may show a toast and Refresh affordance without replacing the
adopted rows.

The Refresh interaction separates a pure browser transition from the server
acquisition and the subsequent installation:

#+begin_src clojure
;; A2 is a fresh resource instance issued by the managed runtime.
(def refresh-start
  (board-runtime/step
    bob-after-advisory
    {:event/class :board/refresh-requested
     :trigger :user
     :resource-instance/id A2}))

;; Illustrative effect emitted by that pure step:
[:acquisition/request
 {:owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
  :resource-instance/id A2
  :principal-context :session/bob-1
  :scope board-scope
  :requirements
  (get-in bob-after-advisory [:advisory :requirements])}]

;; The host performs the authorized read outside the pure step. The trusted
;; server derives the real principal from authentication, not from this effect.
;; The returned observation is admitted under the request/response contract.
(def refresh-completion
  (board-runtime/step
    (:state refresh-start)
    {:event/class :acquisition-complete
     :owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
     :resource-instance/id A2
     :acquisition authorized-board-observation-X19}))

;; For an asynchronous realization this requests installation; it does not
;; yet assert that the new projection was physically installed and adopted.
(:effects refresh-completion)
;; => a scoped installation effect for the admitted observation

;; Another relevant advisory arrives before installation completes.
(def bob-before-install
  (record-advisory
    (:state refresh-completion)
    admitted-board-advisory-X20))

(def installed
  (board-runtime/step
    bob-before-install
    {:event/class :presentation/installed
     :owner [:fragment :bob-request-board]
     :resource-instance/id A2
     :installation-result admitted-installation-result-X19}))

(def bob-after-refresh (:state installed))
;; => adopts the permitted projection at X19
;; => discharges the X18 requirement when its full scope is covered
;; => retains the X20 requirement and every unrelated uncovered requirement
#+end_src

This example's adoption policy permits installing the result that covers the
captured X18 requirement while retaining a newer X20 requirement. A policy that
requires all pending requirements to be satisfied before adoption must instead
defer that installation and reacquire; the runtime must not silently choose
between these policies.

The illustrated =board-runtime/step= has the intended pure transition shape:
next state plus requested effects. The present Gesso browser adapter exposes
a related general =AdapterState × NormalizedEvent → State × Effects= boundary,
but that does not prove these particular HumanHelp fixtures or schematic
function names are compiled or running in the current application.
A corresponding checked realization must bind callbacks to registered
instances, enforce installation rights against the current owner, and report
actual completion or failure. The final ownership check and target mutation
must share a declared serialization boundary; a check followed by an
unprotected asynchronous mutation leaves the same race. An abstract
=:presentation/installed= event is not evidence that a hostile browser
actually performed the physical DOM effect.
An installation acknowledgement supplied as arbitrary client data is not
accepted as evidence
of this local execution. An adversarial browser can still falsify its own
display; the server's security does not depend on this local report.

The transition uses the latest state, including intervening advisories and
owner changes. Reusing the state captured when Refresh began could erase X20.
If another acquisition already adopted a stronger result, the query changed,
the account context changed, or A2 was retired, this completion cannot install
through the old ownership. A separate admitted path may retain independently
useful outcome or observation evidence.

A synchronous shell may combine installation and its successful semantic
completion when its correspondence contract justifies doing so. In either
realization, effects are not performed inside a retriable pure state update,
such as a =swap!= function. Only effects from the accepted state transition are
performed, under their declared identity and retry contract.

If Bob is authorized to see R3 and it belongs to the selected query, the new
board may contain it. If privacy or filtering excludes it, a complete authorized
acquisition can still discharge the covered board requirement without revealing
the hidden Request. It establishes the authorized projection, not all
Organization state.

Now consider agreement between Alice and Bob. They have separate admitted
observations. An agreement check states the proposition and relation it needs:

#+begin_src clojure
(agreement/check
  {:actors [[:client :alice-tab] [:client :bob-tab]]
   :proposition [:request/status R3 :open]
   :relation :compatible-authoritative-fact
   :observations [alice-observation bob-observation]})
#+end_src

The result can establish agreement only if those observations justify the same
scoped proposition under that relation. Equal =:open= values and larger-looking
basis labels are insufficient. The observations may have different physical
delivery paths and compatible rather than identical read contexts; the required
compatibility is evidence, not an inference from their labels.

Even established agreement that R3 is open does not establish agreement that it
belongs in Bob's authorized board. Alice may never have learned Bob's visibility
policy, query, or membership. That is a separate proposition with separate
premises. Nor does a shared observation of openness authorize either principal
to claim the Request now.

Privacy can make the shared proposition strictly smaller than either actor's
total knowledge:

#+begin_src clojure
(def alice-projection
  {:request/id R3
   :request/status :open
   :request/title "Need help moving a cabinet"
   :request/requester-phone "+1-555-0100"})

(def bob-projection
  {:request/id R3
   :request/status :open
   :request/title "Need help moving a cabinet"})
#+end_src

Given the appropriate admitted observations, they may agree on the shared
Request fact while Bob has no permitted knowledge of the private phone number.
The illustrative domain maps alone do not establish either provenance or
agreement. No convergence rule requires disclosure of the omitted field.

Bob can therefore retain board-adoption debt while holding newer justified
knowledge about an unrelated Request or account setting. The meaningful
question is agreement about which proposition, under which authority, scope,
relation, and observations.

*** Convergence does not require replay of every intermediate change

A browser offline from X17 through X900 need not replay every invalidation
between those points.

If the application semantics permit it, the actor may converge by acquiring a
current authoritative observation at X900 or later.

The correctness obligation is not "deliver every historical notification."

It is:

#+begin_quote
Provide a valid path by which the actor can acquire sufficiently current
authoritative knowledge for the behavior it needs to perform.
#+end_quote

That path must address the interval between establishing an interest and the
read/notification stream that covers it, as well as reconnect and source
replacement. Missing an invalidation before registration cannot be repaired by
assuming that all future invalidations will arrive. A supported contract may
use an acquisition/subscription fence, durable cursor or replay, gap detection,
or a declared periodic reacquisition trigger. The chosen mechanism and its
coverage must be explicit; this part does not require a particular broker or
multi-node routing topology.

The critical race is between the authorized baseline observation and the
subscription's effective coverage. A read before subscription admission may
miss a change that occurred before interests were registered; subscribing
before the read may still leave ambiguity unless the producer and reader
establish which changes the read covers. The supported protocol must establish
a join/fence, durable continuation/replay, gap-detection-and-reacquisition, or
other equivalent evidence that no *relevant, required* change disappears
between those boundaries. A source/router replacement must similarly preserve
or re-establish that coverage. Matching subscription IDs, event delivery,
local interest maps, or a tokenless advisory do not themselves discharge
the gap obligation. A valid recovery need not replay every historical event
when an authorized read can re-establish the required current projection.

Eventual convergence also needs an eventual trigger. Dependency availability
alone does not make a browser refresh if it never learns that its observation
may be incomplete. Where the product deliberately permits indefinite manual
deferral, the convergence claim remains conditional on the required user action
or another declared trigger.

*** Agreement debt

Where the choreography requires actors to converge, the difference between
their relevant justified knowledge may be understood as agreement debt.

The design does not require one universal numeric debt metric.

The useful property is qualitative:

- divergence is represented;
- required convergence paths exist;
- stale or provisional actors do not pretend agreement already exists;
- under stated liveness assumptions and quiescence, the required divergence can
  be eliminated.

The existence of a recovery path is distinct from its eventual execution.
Where adoption requires a user to activate Refresh, required eventual adoption
is conditional on that action or another permitted acquisition trigger. Where
the product permits indefinite deferral, remaining advisory information is not
by itself a violation of an unconditional convergence guarantee; such a
guarantee was never established for that deferred adoption.

A liveness or debt-elimination proof must state its actual fairness and
environment assumptions: eventual triggering when required, availability
of an authorized acquisition, recovery from disconnection or lost events,
sufficient read/progression coverage, and a period of relevant quiescence
when convergence needs one. A mechanism that could refresh is a safety or
reachability fact, not a proof that the actor eventually refreshes. Likewise
the mere presence of an opaque later-looking basis cannot certify that all
independent pending requirements were satisfied.

*** Privacy constrains convergence

Agreement is always scoped by authorization and relevance.

The system must not satisfy a convergence property by leaking protected facts to
actors that are not entitled to observe them.

A correct convergence path may therefore involve:

- one actor learning the full authoritative fact;
- another actor learning only an authorized projection;
- both actors agreeing on a smaller shared semantic proposition.

For example:

#+begin_example
authority knows:
    full Request details + private requester metadata

helper A may know:
    Request is available for claim

observer B may know:
    Request count increased

A and B need not agree on private requester metadata.
#+end_example

*** Frontier and convergence counterexamples

The following cases are normative acceptance and correspondence obligations,
not claims that today's browser or multi-node runtime already satisfies them:

| Case | Required treatment |
|------+--------------------|
| Actor A sees snapshot F4 but has no event-history coverage | Do not infer knowledge of the full F1–F4 transition prefix |
| Two opaque basis tokens look ordered numerically or by arrival time | Require authority-qualified progression evidence; do not invent a global order or join |
| A change commits between initial board read and effective subscription registration | Use the admitted coverage fence, replay, or gap-recovery path; do not mark the interest gap closed merely because later SSE events arrive |
| A tokenless advisory arrives without a trusted source fence or sufficient reacquisition | Do not manufacture a lower bound for an unseen transaction |
| A newer X20 requirement arrives while an X19 result is awaiting installation | Adopt X19 only if the declared policy permits it; retain X20 and all uncovered debt |
| A2's completion arrives after owner, query, or principal context replacement | Retire the stale installation right; do not mutate the successor's adopted projection |
| Bob receives a privacy-filtered board that does not show R3 | Conclude only the authorized query's established coverage, not R3's global absence or entitlement to private details |
| The browser is disconnected and the user indefinitely defers manual Refresh | Keep convergence conditional on an eventual trigger; do not claim unconditional liveness |

A diagnostic or formal obligation should distinguish a missing observation
premise, unqualified ordering, advisory coverage debt, stale owner, privacy
restriction, and unsatisfied liveness assumption. None becomes a success by
comparing raw timestamps or making a stale view look current.

*** Foundational invariant

The foundational agreement invariant is:

#+begin_quote
An admitted Gesso execution never treats mere co-presence, shape equality, physical
delivery, or coincidental local computation as semantic agreement.  Agreement
must be justified by a declared communication, compatible authoritative
observation, checked derivation, or explicit trusted assertion, and any claim
over evolving facts is relative to an explicitly justified temporal and
observation relation. Assertions remain premises, and a claimed common frontier
does not imply a universal order, complete history, or current authorization.
#+end_quote

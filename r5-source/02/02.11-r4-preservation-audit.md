# R5.1 Part II — 02.11 Agreement

**Baseline:** R4 `main`, Part II, “Agreement.”
**Candidate:** `r5.1` source section 02.11.
**Status:** focused normative preservation audit, not a proof of general
cross-actor agreement or completed whole-Part-II reconciliation.

## R4 requirements retained

- Agreement is a justified semantic relationship for specified actors,
  subjects, values or propositions, scope, and frontier; equal shapes do not
  suffice.
- An admitted send/receive correspondence may establish communicated
  agreement, but not acknowledgement, higher-order knowledge of receipt,
  domain truth or enduring current equality.
- Two actors may independently acquire compatible authoritative observations
  through different hosts and packets without requiring physical identity.
- Deterministic derived agreement requires related inputs and a justified
  semantic derivation; matching Clojure function names do not prove it.
- Explicit agreement assertions remain visible trusted premises, not
  automatically generated proofs or substitutes for mandatory failed gates.
- Malli shape equality is narrower than semantic agreement; Request,
  authority, basis, valid-time, provenance and observation identity matter.
- Legitimate privacy and relevance constraints mean actors need not agree on
  the entire application state.

## R5 improvements preserved

- Distinguish shared observations from knowing that another actor received
  them or from mutual knowledge. Additional acknowledgements need additional
  protocol semantics.
- Agreement is scoped to a declared relation and context rather than
  globally equal database snapshots or arbitrary basis arithmetic.
- Independently observed facts require compatible subjects, temporal meaning,
  authority and projection. Same displayed value need not imply unchanged
  history across intervening transitions.
- Opaque derived agreement retains deterministic and implementation premises.
  Assertion identity binds actors, relation, proposition and frontier, and
  untrusted actors cannot forge the accepted assertion owner.

## Changes in this revision

1. Require the checked agreement claim to retain actor, subject, proposition,
   relation, context/frontier and support, resolved directly or via a closed
   admitted contract.
2. Distinguish an outbound send request and receiver-side observation from
   the full matched emission, delivery and receive correspondence. Concrete
   witness replay is not an all-schedules theorem.
3. Add query filters, visibility and coverage to authoritative observation
   compatibility when they affect the compared proposition.
4. Add seven negative cases: unpaired send/receive, equal displayed statuses,
   same token but unequal scope, temporal history inference, same-named
   derivations, forged proof/assertion, and changed permission.
5. Keep unknown compatibility, untrusted provenance and explicit opaque
   assumptions separate from a proved disagreement or unconditional theorem.

## Current Gesso correspondence and limitations

- `gesso.choreo.verify` provides role-local and definite-authoritative
  must-analysis. That can reject unavailable inputs and unsupported knowledge
  use in its model, but not a general cross-actor agreement theorem.
- `gesso.choreo.knowledge` records five provenance kinds and scoped
  authoritative-observation identities, with explicit progression witnesses;
  data constructors do not authenticate real origins or prove cross-actor
  equality of opaque computations.
- `gesso.choreo.correspondence` checks concrete lockstep or weak witness
  schedules against global and projected realizations; its own contract
  excludes all-schedules projection refinement and physical transport proof.
- `gesso.live.browser.refinement` explicitly exposes candidate observations
  requiring sender/delivery/global schedule evidence, not a verified complete
  distributed trace.
- `gesso.live.progression` treats basis values as opaque and keeps independent
  requirements unless trusted progression establishes a relation.

# R5.1 Part II — 02.12 Agreement Frontiers

**Baseline:** R4 `main`, Part II, “Agreement frontiers.”
**Candidate:** `r5.1` source section 02.12.
**Status:** individual R4 preservation audit only; whole-Part-II
reconciliation and end-to-end implementation correspondence remain separate.

## R4 requirements preserved

- A common agreement frontier is a relation over justified scoped facts,
  not a universal arithmetic version shared by every actor.
- Participants need not agree on all data or all history. A common-prefix
  example is valid only when knowledge of that modeled prefix is established.
- Divergence, advisory knowledge, adopted authoritative observations and
  eventual agreement debt are distinct states and obligations.
- HumanHelp's multi-user Request board may retain an adopted projection
  while a Live advisory offers a Refresh path without disclosing a new Request.
- A permitted acquisition may satisfy old advisory requirements but must
  retain newer or unrelated ones, and must not install through retired
  ownership or erase intervening advisories.
- A pure retriable state updater cannot perform irreversible effects.
- Agreement depends on proposition, authority, read context, scope and
  admitted evidence; privacy-scoped convergence may be smaller than full
  database or requester-detail equality.
- Successful convergence need not deliver every historical invalidation
  when a sufficiently current authorized acquisition can re-establish the
  required knowledge.
- Eventual convergence is conditional on quiescence, acquisition triggers,
  operational fairness and the declared liveness environment.

## Superior R5 treatment retained

- Corrects R4's overly strong blanket immutability language: historical
  observations remain meaningful while facts may be corrected and
  interpretations/applicability may change.
- Prefix knowledge is distinguished from a latest-snapshot observation.
- Requirements accumulate as an orderless conjunction unless trusted
  implication evidence permits simplification. They are not max timestamp.
- Browser refresh has separate acquisition, installation and adoption
  stages, with instance ownership, query/principal scope and completion
  obligations, including X20 arriving during X19's pending installation.
- R5 distinguishes tokenless advisories, explicit acquisition policy,
  provider-qualified coverage, and a reconnect/interest inception gap.
- Conditional user-triggered convergence does not become unconditional
  merely because a refresh affordance is available.

## Changes in this revision

1. Explicitly mark `advisory-admission`, `requirements`,
   `board-runtime/step`, and related example values as schematic, not
   claims of public Gesso Vars or compiled HumanHelp browser code.
2. Correct the statement about current implementation: Gesso has a pure
   browser adapter/abstract-effect boundary; correspondence between the
   generic adapter and the shown HumanHelp board transitions is still
   an application-level obligation, as is physical actuation.
3. Make the read/subscription coverage gap explicit for both orderings
   (read-before-registration and subscription-before-read). Require an
   admitted fence, replay, gap recovery or comparable evidence including
   router/source replacement; do not prescribe a broker or topology.
4. Name actual fairness, event-delivery/recovery, authorized-read,
   quiescence and triggering premises for any eventual-debt claim.
5. Add eight negative cases for prefix inference, fake basis ordering,
   interest-inception gaps, tokenless wakeups, intervening advisories,
   stale owner callbacks, filtered absence and manual deferral.

## Current Gesso correspondence and open premises

- `gesso.live.progression` implements orderless opaque requirements and
  closed progression-witness shapes, but explicitly delegates witness
  truth and semantic scope to the trusted authority adapter.
- `gesso.live.browser.adapter` has fragment-generation controls,
  coalesced queued requirements, continuity retirement, and abstract
  effects. It does not prove that the schematic `board-runtime/step`
  example is installed in HumanHelp or that a physical browser obeys it.
- `gesso.choreo.knowledge` retains authority/observation-scoped
  provenance and explicit progression validation, but it does not
  authenticate the basis or infer universal agreement.
- `gesso.live.consistency.xtdb` implements qualified consistency
  helpers, but does not itself own Live subscriptions or provide a
  verified read/subscription fence across multi-node routing.
- No code inspected in this revision establishes universal agreement
  liveness, complete multi-node interest coverage, or malicious-browser
  physical correspondence.

# R5.1 Part II — 02.03 Facts, Time, and Authoritative Progression

**Baseline:** R4 `main` Part II section "Facts, time, and authoritative
progression." **Candidate:** `r5.1` source section 02.03.
Status: one focused normative editorial review, not a qualification test or
completed Part II cross-section acceptance.

## R4 guarantees retained

- Valid/effective time, domain/causal relation, database progression,
  actor-relative knowledge, physical delivery time, non-universality of one
  global revision, and progression-as-provenance remain intact.
- R5 additionally distinguishes recording/system time and correction history,
  plus explicit scope-aware progression comparisons and conjunctive debt,
  without weakening any R4 temporal dimension.
- XTDB progression is interpreted by the qualified authoritative provider,
  not by parsing numeric-looking tokens or comparing HTTP delivery times.
- A newer database frontier need not mean the relevant Request changed.
  Different actors/tabs may have justified knowledge at different frontiers.
- Browser delivery, acquisition, installation and adoption are not
  interchangeable. Older observations are not automatically invalid solely
  because unrelated authority advanced.

## Changes in this revision

1. **Minimum, actual, and commit evidence.** A requested/confirmed minimum
   visibility requirement differs from a read's actual observation basis.
   Neither proves an exact immediate post-transaction snapshot or that a
   particular command incorporated. The qualified provider determines which
   guarantee it can establish; Part IV owns that transaction/read contract.
2. **Authenticated provenance versus client claim.** The section's historical
   statement that an "observed basis" explains command provenance is narrowed
   to admitted acquisition evidence. A browser-supplied `:observed-basis`
   remains useful *non-authorizing* protocol context; prior observation claims
   need separate trusted issuance validation under section 02.10.
3. **Comparability is scoped and weaker than coverage.** Byte equality alone
   cannot transfer provider equality across incompatible authority/incarnation,
   time, or scope; even an admitted progression comparison cannot by itself
   prove a relevant domain effect or complete query coverage.

## Cross-part references

- Part IV: actual commit disposition, conditional guards, backend-qualified
  read progression, scope, coverage and required visibility.
- Part VI and this Part II's 02.10/02.12: observed versus adopted/installed
  knowledge, browser adversarial input, scoped convergence obligations.
- Part I: provenance, bounded trust premises, assurance classification.
- Part X: implementation correspondence and qualified backend assumptions.

No R4 normative obligation was intentionally removed. The section does not
claim that an opaque XTDB token is universally comparable, that a database
revision encodes domain history, or that any current Gesso provider has already
met the whole contract.

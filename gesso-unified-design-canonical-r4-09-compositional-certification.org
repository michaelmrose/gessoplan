* Part IX — Compositional certification
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-09-compositional-certification
:PART_REVISION: 09-20261108
:END:

This part specifies how Gesso checks, composes, and preserves assurance about the
semantic artifacts and applications defined in Parts I–VIII. It makes the
certification boundary concrete: which judgments are checked, what evidence can
establish them, how dependencies are derived, and what remains conditional on
runtime enforcement or an explicitly accepted contract.

The specification describes the intended system from its foundations. It does
not assert that every judgment or correspondence mechanism is implemented. A
supported certification profile has an executable contract and conformance suite;
an unsupported judgment cannot be made available by giving it a reassuring name.
Operational admission, compositional certification, external formal assurance,
and multi-node deployment retain their separate scopes.

A certification profile names the exact propositions it promises, its source
and implementation subjects, authorized assumption classes, concrete admission
stage, required evidence and observable rejection behavior. A lower profile
can remain useful when a stronger judgment is unavailable; it is not an
implicit substitute for a requested stronger profile. Full application
assurance additionally needs the independent product, physical writer,
authenticated execution and deployment obligations owned by later parts.
A checked abstract theorem about an issued plan never authorizes a database
commit or proves the current browser has installed that plan.

Examples use ordinary data notation. Unless explicitly described as executable
source for a named language, their purpose is to expose a contract. Writing a
certificate-shaped map, matching a fingerprint, or displaying an accepted report
does not cross an admission boundary.

** Governing compositional contract
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-compositional-certification
:END:

A downstream claim may rely only on propositions actually established by its
checked dependencies and on assumptions explicitly admitted by its selected
policy. Every premise retains its subject, scope, applicability, and evidence
class. Neither a namespace's reputation nor an upstream artifact's general
“verified” label supplies a missing proposition.

In particular, the downstream consumer must identify the *specific*
required proposition, the upstream exported guarantee, its type and subject,
and any still-open premise. A matching proof object is insufficient when its
schema, rule source, declared operation catalog, application generation,
actor/subject binding, executable lowering or selected runtime contract has
changed. Exact source identity is a change detector; compatible evolution
must have its own checked relation and cannot be assumed from equal names.

Judgment results distinguish rejected construction, an accepted unconditional
judgment within a stated formal model, a checked conditional derivation, a
finite bounded result, a trusted/opaque assertion, and a runtime-observed
fact. A passed conditional abstract checker never becomes an unconditional
proof merely because all required source files were present. A composed
judgment inherits the applicable open premises and trust of its required
dependencies; it does not average them into a stronger green status.

Certification concerns a claim about a subject. It is not a universal property
of a namespace, function, application, or certificate object. A rule can be well
typed while its business meaning is wrong. A choreography can have a checked
projection while its server realization violates the model contract. A finite
model can preserve an invariant while an omitted physical writer breaks it.

The certified construction path must make these distinctions inspectable and
make unsupported substitutions fail before the protected downstream construction
or effect. It must also remain useful: declarations and public model contracts
are authored once, and Gesso derives the routine identities, obligations,
dependency edges, proof candidates, and diagnostics.

The checked construction path must begin from the real source-owned
declarations and the selected application/host graph, not an auditor's
second hand-maintained list of "approved" operations. Every exported
judgment has a specified coverage obligation over its relevant language:
terms, branches, required operations, relationships, message contracts,
effects, initial states and declared termination/recovery cases as needed.
A checker may establish closure for its *inspected* subset; it cannot infer
that arbitrary Clojure bodies, raw XTDB capabilities or external processes
contain no uninspected business-policy writer.

Source completeness and intended product meaning are separate obligations.
An internally consistent machine that has removed HumanHelp Reassign may
pass the remaining derivations while failing the independent product
capability oracle. An omitted rule, dependency or protected writer cannot
be excused by a proof DAG that correctly checks every submitted node.
The scope of a failed or unknown coverage check must remain visible in
both the certification decision and the next downstream diagnostic.

*** Full-language applications remain first-class

Ordinary Clojure and ClojureScript remain available under
[[#req-full-language-construction]]. A closed proof language is a tractable
reasoning fragment, not a replacement application language. Arbitrary algorithms,
libraries, recursion, effects, and opaque policy functions can participate through
declared contracts on the same managed construction path.

An opaque component identifies its inputs, results, effects, relevant state
and authority dependencies, implementation binding, and the assertions on which
dependent claims rely. Purity, determinism, termination, or bounded resource use
are required only when the claimed property needs them. A callback's type or
name cannot establish those properties.

The framework proves supported surrounding properties even when one component is
opaque. For example, command identity preservation, required result variants,
unchanged frame fields, and retired-effect exclusion need not all depend on the
truth of an Organization policy predicate. A conclusion that does depend on that
predicate's implementation remains explicitly conditional on its contract.

Unsupported proof syntax is rejected by that proof profile. It is not silently
executed as host code, silently replaced by an assumption, or used to downgrade
a requested strong build. The author may deliberately bind a supported opaque
contract or select another profile; the resulting claim and accepted premises
must be visible.

A policy predicate that decides permission, disclosure, ownership, lifecycle
transition or command incorporation must be accounted for in the semantic
subject, through a checked dependency, a runtime-enforced condition or a
named opaque contract. If Gesso discovers an unmodeled decision in the
implementation subset it actually inspects, it reports a semantic-policy
escape with the affected proposition and repair choices. Silence while
examining only a structured rule AST is not evidence that hidden policies
do not exist in an uninspected Clojure body. Human-facing policy rationale
and independent tests can challenge whether the *right* rule was declared;
neither automatically becomes a theorem in the proof kernel.

Opaque contracts remain useful at the edge of a narrower theorem: Gesso may
still establish command identity, result-variant coverage or frame safety
that do not depend on the opaque implementation. Claims that *do* depend
on the predicate's truth, purity, termination, current actor or database
consistency inherit its specified proof or trust premise. The requested
assurance profile determines whether that open premise is permissible.

*** Three semantic responsibilities

| Responsibility | Contract |
|----------------+----------|
| Executable semantic core | Defines and executes admitted application transitions and abstract effects from current state and input/events. |
| Proof kernel | Checks derivations in a small, closed inference language under explicit premises. It creates no runtime fact or transaction authority. |
| Dedicated semantic checker | Establishes a specified judgment such as rule typing, dependency closure, projection admissibility, or exact finite exploration under its own stated checking contract. |

These responsibilities support one *canonical semantic vocabulary*
but expose three different acceptance paths. The executable core interprets
admitted transitions and effects, with runtime enforcement for current
principal, transaction and physical resource rights. The proof kernel
checks closed derivations over named propositions and premises; a derivation
supplied by the compiler does not become true because the compiler generated
it. A dedicated checker can establish a well-defined alternative judgment
without pretending it was checked by the proof kernel. Each result records
which checker/source version and trusted mathematical or interpreter
assumptions it depends on. Testing the kernel against itself is not an
independent soundness oracle.

#+begin_example
One source-owned HumanHelp Claim:
  Request model declares current actor, Request, assignment and
    Organization/Membership dependency contracts.
  Gesso derives a guarded operation, Choreo projection and the
    applicable proposition/coverage obligations.
  A small proof checker may establish a conditional abstract
    invariant for the declared transition subject.
  A concrete XTDB/host boundary separately checks the current
    guarded write and independently permitted disclosure.
  Browser installation establishes its own target/generation effect.
  A certificate-shaped map cannot substitute for any of those steps.
#+end_example

These responsibilities can share canonical terms and versioned primitive
semantics. They must not develop competing meanings for the same construct.
Sharing code does not prove correctness, however, and it is not independence
when a test oracle simply invokes the checker being tested.

The canonical semantic definitions are executable specifications: accepted term
schemas, interpretation, judgment inputs and outputs, side conditions, and
negative cases. They may be implemented in focused namespaces such as
=gesso.assurance.rule*= and =gesso.assurance.proof.*= and the Choreo semantic
modules. A logical =gesso.semantic= surface, if exposed, organizes those contracts;
it must not create a parallel interpreter or require a wholesale namespace move.

*** Certified admission, composition, and failure behavior

Certified construction is a checked *boundary*, not a shape convention.
After a stage requires an admitted semantic interface, its supported public
consumer must not accept an unexamined map with copied =:verified?=,
=:proof-status= or subject digest as a replacement. Appropriate
implementations include private issued handles, checked constructors and
freshly validated certificate references; the design does not require
nominalizing every ordinary Clojure value. Ordinary cooperating use must
not accidentally cross a required certification gate.

The construction sequence is conceptually raw declaration, normalization,
elaboration, complete obligation generation, appropriate checking,
qualified certificate/admission, downstream composition and installed
realization. Not every profile requires a proof-kernel certificate, but
every claimed stage must have its own actual checked admission. A failing
required obligation yields no value of that *certified* artifact class;
an intentionally partial/conditional diagnostic is a different
artifact whose status cannot masquerade as fully admitted.
The first boundary with enough evidence reports an exact typed failure
and the downstream artifacts not issued.

A dependency graph may have recursive executable behavior, but its
justification relation cannot use a cycle of certificates that establish
each other's premises. A recursive theorem needs a specifically checked
inductive, guarded or fixed-point rule. A certificate's exported semantic
interface contains only the guarantees permitted for downstream use,
together with its assumptions, evidence class and applicability scope.
It is not permission to inspect arbitrary private implementation data
and silently promote it to a proposition.

Proof sidecars, checked reports and trace diagnostics are allowed to decide
*build-time admission*. Once the executable meaning has been admitted,
optional proof data cannot mutate its actual transition relation or grant
new runtime authority. Source/plan identity accompanies the issued artifact,
but that identity alone is not proof of matching Clojure, XTDB, browser
or transport execution. A proof kernel may validate a conditional
derivation while a separate source-to-executor or deployed-host
correspondence obligation remains open.

The accepted subject becomes stale when a required declaration, implementation
binding, checker contract, dependency, browser build, installed handler or
runtime premise changes beyond its declared compatibility relation.
A trusted consumer must revalidate the relevant current facts and invalidate
dependent claims; it must not accept a saved green report from a previous
assembly. Conditional reuse may avoid redoing unrelated checks when identities
and all required applicability premises remain genuinely valid.

*** Non-vacuous boundaries and honest failure reports

Positive controls accompany every rejection family. A certified-path test
must admit a genuinely well-formed Claim, a supported opaque contract and
an appropriate narrow-profile construction, while rejecting a malformed
rule, absent required guard/capability, forged proof, false source identity
and an unjustified stronger certificate. Rejecting every operation or
granting every request is not evidence of correct composition.

The report distinguishes malformed syntax, unsatisfied semantic judgment,
unsupported requested profile, absent obligation coverage, open but
permitted assumption, stale application binding, failed trusted runtime
premise and unknown physical correspondence. It identifies the exact
proposition, original source, first applicable check, dependents not built,
safe remedy and confidential facts withheld from the diagnostic audience.
An unobserved backend writer or incomplete independent product requirement
does not become success because its name is absent from the graph.

** Claims, judgments, evidence, and applicability

The following distinctions are mandatory throughout construction and reporting.

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------+---------|
| Raw declaration | Unadmitted source data, even when its representation passes a shape check. |
| Semantic subject | The exact normalized object about which a claim is made, with its interpretation and dependency identities. |
| Proposition | A well-formed statement in an identified logic or contract vocabulary. Well-formedness does not establish truth. |
| Judgment | The result of a particular checking relation over explicit subjects, environments, premises, and evidence. |
| Obligation | A required judgment derived for a subject and requested claim/profile. |
| Derivation | A proposed justification using recognized inference rules and bound premises. |
| Assumption | A scoped proposition accepted without having been proved by the current construction. |
| Certificate candidate | Serializable source/evidence offered for checking; its claimed status is untrusted. |
| Admitted certificate | The result of the required checking and admission boundary for a stated claim and profile. |
| Report | Copyable explanation of judgments and dependencies; not an admission or execution credential. |
| Runtime evidence | Evidence about a particular invocation, observation, resource, or effect at its actual boundary. |
| Semantic category / admitted value | A meaning whose actual checked-construction or trusted acquisition boundary was crossed; same shape is not admission. |
| Assertion | Intended policy or proposed fact supplied by source; declaration alone does not establish truth. |
| Guarantee | A checked proposition under named premises and an explicit evidence class. |
| Semantic identity | Versioned normalized interface identity; distinct from proof, source location, and implementation fidelity. |
| Applicability | The identities and conditions under which a checked conclusion remains usable in a particular composition or deployment. |

A guarantee is a checked conclusion together with its remaining premises. Calling
it a guarantee does not remove those premises.

*** Semantic category, admission and truth remain distinct

An EDN map that looks like a Request observation is still raw data until the
applicable trusted read, constructor or checker actually admits it. The
semantic category describes its intended meaning; admission establishes
that the required construction boundary was crossed, not that all facts
represented by the value are proven. A concrete authoritative observation
also retains current principal, read scope/completeness, basis, disclosure
and installed-acquisition premises.

#+begin_example
same-shaped value
  != admitted semantic category
  != established proposition about the subject
  != presently authorized runtime effect
#+end_example

Gesso constructors, model operations, checked projections and acquisition
boundaries ordinarily produce these categories automatically; application
authors do not manually wrap every ordinary value. A checked downstream API
rejects copied =:valid?= flags, fabricated nominal values, old handles or
a serialized report where an admitted value is required.

The certificate candidate remains untrusted even if its structure matches
an admitted one. The admitted certificate establishes only its exact
checked proposition, context, assumptions and profile. Separate judgments
still govern proof-kernel soundness, completeness of generated obligations,
source-to-executor correspondence and actual physical effects.

*** A judgment identifies more than a Boolean result

Every judgment must identify:

- its kind and semantic interpretation version;
- the exact subject and relevant input/type/context bindings;
- the proposition or obligation addressed;
- the checker, proof rules, or interpretation procedure used;
- the checked evidence and its scope, including finite bounds where applicable;
- inherited premises and any checked discharge/substitution;
- the result and structured reason when the requested judgment is unavailable.

The result vocabulary distinguishes established, conditional, counterexample,
invalid, unsupported, missing evidence, incomplete/resource-exhausted, and
inapplicable/stale cases as appropriate to the judgment. These need not all be
one public enum, but they cannot collapse into one success/failure flag.

A malformed proof is not a counterexample to its conclusion. A timed-out search
is not a proof that the property is false. A sound conditional theorem can remain
valid while its premises are not established for this deployment.

An exact finite result concerns the enumerated state/operation space and
chosen initializers; it is not an unbounded result. A supported unbounded
theorem from an abstraction needs the separately checked or admitted
abstraction/refinement relation. Static typing, finite exploration,
conditional abstract theorem, trusted implementation premise and
runtime-observed effect never become interchangeable outcomes.

A trusted consumer must know whether "conditional" names a *checked*
implication with open premises, merely an asserted contract, or a copyable
candidate result. Accepting an explicit assumption under the chosen policy
is not discharging it. A timeout cannot establish falsity, and a malformed
proof cannot refute a well-formed underlying proposition.

*** Evidence classes remain attached to propositions

Use Part I's [[#req-assurance-classification][assurance classifications]]. In
particular, distinguish checked derivations, exact finite results,
assembly verification, runtime-enforced instance facts, trusted or
application-asserted contracts, and test evidence.

A report should express classification, assumptions, and applicability as separate
axes. “Conditional” is not an excuse to omit which checker established the
conditional implication. “Runtime enforced” must say whether the evidence is a
contract requiring enforcement, an installed enforcing mechanism, or an observed
successful check for one invocation. Those are different propositions.

A static certificate can establish that an admitted receive transition preserves
a knowledge invariant if its runtime premises hold. It cannot establish that a
future browser has received a particular observation. A contract for a Claim
executor can require current authorization; its certificate cannot supply Alice's
current Membership.

A particular physical read and its trusted read boundary establish only
their scoped facts. A postcommit Request snapshot does not prove the
transaction-internal prestate, historical attribution to C42, complete
writer inventory, or a recipient's DOM adoption. An authenticated
receipt may attest to origin/bytes under its issuer trust contract; it
does not prove that the issuer's business assertion is correct.

Evidence classifications are not a single numerical strength ranking:
a conditional proof may establish a general abstract relationship while
a particular guarded XTDB read proves narrower but indispensable current
eligibility. A root claim preserves the distinct propositions it requires
rather than announcing one "most verified" status.

*** An immutable obligation ledger with derived status

Keep original obligations and supplied evidence distinct. The source-derived
ledger records what the claim requires. Checked evidence records how particular
obligations are established or which premises remain. A report computes status
from those bindings instead of deleting inconvenient obligations from the ledger.

Adding a certificate may establish certificate-admission or dependency-accounting
obligations. It does not automatically discharge implementation correspondence,
physical writer coverage, or checker soundness. Unknown new obligation categories
remain visible and unresolved until a profile explicitly classifies them.

The selected policy may accept a named premise, but acceptance is not logical
discharge. An unresolved mandatory obligation cannot be converted into an accepted
premise by an elaborator's failure handler.

The ledger is derived from the original declared construction and any
independent capability/product obligations. It verifies coverage of the
relevant operation catalog, initial conditions, transition cases,
relationship/guard dependencies, effects, and required negative/recovery
branches; physical writer coverage and correspondence are added when
the selected claim needs them. Valid derivations for every *submitted*
obligation do not certify omitted obligations or a deleted product
capability. A model with Claim but no required Reassign fails the
independent HumanHelp oracle even if its closed Claim theorem succeeds.

Each entry retains original obligation identity, semantic subject,
current source/dependency version, checker/host boundary, required
proposition, result/evidence class, open or discharged premises,
applicability and dependent conclusions. Rechecking a changed source
re-derives the current ledger, preserving historical evidence as
history while invalidating affected live conclusions. Unchanged
unrelated propositions may be reused only when their own current
identity and applicability really remain valid.

#+begin_example
For invariant "at most one active primary per Request":
  initial configuration preserves uniqueness
  Claim preserves uniqueness
  Unclaim, Reassign and other declared writers preserve uniqueness
  physical writer scope and abstract/XTDB relation are qualified

Checking Claim alone never discharges omitted Reassign, a hidden raw
  XTDB writer, or complete guard/absence semantics at commit.
#+end_example

** Minimal semantic interfaces and ownership

A semantic interface exports the commitments downstream reasoning may use:
types and variants, relationships, preconditions and postconditions, relevant
state/effect footprints, outcomes, authority and disclosure requirements,
observation contracts, and named premises. It does not expose every private
implementation detail or serialize a namespace for convenience.

A minimal semantic interface is the only contract surface downstream
reasoning may use. It names its public owner, normalized terms and
versions, nominal type/presence/nullable meaning, input/output,
effect/authority/disclosure scope, assumptions and justified guarantees.
A downstream proof depends on the exported *required* commitments,
not arbitrary incidental implementation fields or filesystem layout.

Exact semantic dependency identity is the conservative default. A
later checked compatibility relation may allow changed interfaces
without violating a dependent's closed-world assumptions. A digest or
same operation name alone does not prove that relation. Source
reformatting need not invalidate meaning-level evidence, while a
changed implementation function, current provider, handler, or
browser binary may independently invalidate concrete correspondence
even if its abstract public contract did not change.

The hierarchy follows semantic ownership, not file layout:

| Interface or evidence subject | Typical checked commitment | Boundary retained |
|-------------------------------+----------------------------+-------------------|
| Schema/type | Fields, optionality, nominal categories, variants, representation constraints. | Existing database values still require valid acquisition or an explicit data-conformance premise. |
| Domain/relationship | Well-formed relationships, typed paths, refinements, selected invariants. | Reachability/cardinality declarations are not current authorization or proof of stored data. |
| Rule | Supported evaluation semantics, Boolean result, safe reads/refinements, declared dependencies. | Intrinsic validity does not establish correct product policy or availability at a distributed point. |
| Public authoritative operation | Inputs, outcomes, guard/write dependencies, command policy, effect and recovery contract. | Arbitrary implementation conformance and current atomic enforcement need their own evidence. |
| Choreography | Knowledge, provenance, authority placement, communication, branches, continuations, and resource obligations. | An abstract protocol argument does not prove network or browser behavior. |
| Projection | The named global/local correspondence property over specified observables. | Local simulation alone does not establish correctness of the composed participants. |
| Executable plan | Checked lowering/binding to the admitted projection and runtime format. | A copied source identity proves no lowering correctness. |
| Application assembly | Closure of required declarations and compatible realization bindings. | Installed host adoption and independent product completeness remain separate. |
| Browser build/served asset | Correspondence under the generated-entrypoint and qualified build/serving contract. | A digest is not a semantic analysis of arbitrary JavaScript. |
| Concrete render/install | Checks on the actual representation and current physical target/effect. | One accepted render is not a proof about all future renders. |
| Root application claim | Proposition-specific closure of public model, Choreo, projection, generated runtime and application realization under the selected profile. | A coherent report does not prove independently required product policy, installed host identity or completeness of outside physical writers. |

Public model interfaces remain the cross-model boundary. The certificate graph
must not reach into Organization's private FX code to obtain authority that its
public contract does not export. Inspection of implementation source for
correspondence is permitted, but does not turn private internals into an implicit
public dependency.

Schemas containing arbitrary Malli predicates do not all become a closed theorem
language. The supported schema fragment can be translated and checked. Opaque
predicates retain named validator/implementation contracts. Concrete validation
of one value proves only the relevant instance result under that validator's
contract, not universal data validity or the intended business meaning.

*** Implementation correspondence, current dependencies and reuse

HumanHelp Request Claim consumes the *public* Organization, Membership,
User and Location semantic contracts. Gesso may check the rule subset
and explicitly assume the opaque Organization implementation's
conformance where a stronger profile permits it; it may not reach into
private Organization FX data and treat implementation details as a
public authority guarantee.

A relationship interface can establish path typing and cardinality
declarations but not prove the current existence of every referenced
record, current authorization, or transaction-guarded absence.
Trusted acquisition and current model/XTDB guards must establish
the needed concrete facts at their physical boundaries.

Likewise a checked projection is not automatically correct compiled
JavaScript; a source-closed ApplicationAssembly is not automatically
the installed Ring/Biff handler; a matched browser stamp does not
prove HTMX behavior; and a current card does not prove a historical
command result. Reuse of abstract contracts and reuse of concrete
execution evidence have different identities and invalidation rules.

A copyable report, unsigned cached certificate or purported signed
claim re-enters as untrusted data unless the applicable rechecking,
issuer-authentication and dependency boundary is satisfied. A
signature authenticates the named issuer/bytes when its key contract
holds; it does not itself establish the proposition. In-process
opaque handles help prevent accidental substitution by ordinary
Clojure consumers but do not confine equally privileged JVM code
or transfer admission across serialization.

Unqualified opaque callbacks, raw XTDB writers and outside host
effects remain explicit trust or open coverage premises. The
framework can still establish unaffected propositions without
promoting a partial root report into a full release certificate.

*** Paired positive and adversarial interface judgments

The acceptance matrix requires a genuine positive and a nearby
negative at the *same* relevant admission boundary: checked public
Claim interface versus equally shaped raw map; current Organization
identity versus stale dependency; full Claim/Reassign obligation
catalog versus omitted Reassign; valid conditional opaque premise
versus forged =:discharged= flag; issuer-checked proof versus copied
certificate description; properly qualified transaction read
versus a caller-crafted report; and a supported narrow profile
versus unsupported strong-profile downgrade.

Each rejection identifies the first responsible checker, the
exact affected subject/obligation, expected and actual category,
which downstream values were not constructed, and an actionable
safe repair. No result may claim that a hidden writer does not
exist merely because the selected enumerator did not inspect it.
A checker that rejects every candidate fails the positive gate.

** Closed typed rule language
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-certified-rule-language
:END:

The baseline rule language is a small data AST with explicit reader declarations,
nominal type information, strict Boolean behavior, and no embedded host evaluation.
It is independently useful for guard fragments, invariants, model checks, and
atoms inside the proof language.

An application may use ordinary functions outside this fragment. The fragment's
closed syntax exists to make the claims about it precise, not to make unsupported
application logic impossible.

This bounded *certifiable* fragment is not the only language in which
application authors may write rules. Gesso can elaborate supported Clojure
source or accept explicit typed rule data, provided the generated AST binds
the original source, public types, dependencies, translation semantics and
applicable preservation evidence. A manually copied predicate with an
agreeing example result is not original-source correspondence.

R4's broader target includes typed field/relationship access, nominal
variant predicates, deterministic derivations, explicit capability/role
facts and exhaustive closed-variant match. These are **extensions**, not
unacknowledged baseline operators. Each new construct must register closed
syntax, typing, evaluation, failure, effect/reader footprint, normalization
and checking obligations. An extension that invokes arbitrary Clojure remains
an explicit opaque contract; Section 09.04 owns its detailed bridge.

*** Three different rule judgments

| Judgment | Established conclusion | Still unestablished |
|----------+------------------------+---------------------|
| Syntax | Supported data AST, exact operators/arity/readers and resource bounds. | Nominal type correctness or policy truth. |
| Intrinsic type | Strict Boolean result under named types and safe binding refinements. | Available knowledge, current authority or protected transaction state. |
| Concrete interpretation | Boolean value for one admitted immutable binding environment. | Universal theorem, correct human policy, source-to-physical refinement or permission to commit. |

A separate Choreo-point admissibility judgment requires actual participant
knowledge, provenance, authority and observation-basis scope (Parts II–III).
Operation contract and actual XTDB guards (Part IV), finite/inductive
properties (Part IX), and implementation correspondence (Part X)
remain separately checkable. A valid rule evaluated to true cannot
silently discharge any of them.

*** Source shape and baseline operators

A rule declaration has an exact reader set and expression. For example, this is
only the open-state conjunct of Claim eligibility:

#+begin_src clojure
(def open-request-rule
  {:readers #{:request/status}
   :expression [:= [:read :request/status] [:literal :open]]})

(def open-request-types
  {:request/status
   {:type :enum
    :name :humanhelp/RequestStatus
    :values #{:open :claimed :on-the-way :done :cancelled}}})
#+end_src

The actual type declaration is derived from the production public schema. This
example's =:done= is the HumanHelp terminal state; “completed” is display language,
not another persisted variant. Checking this conjunct does not check the complete
Claim policy.

Real Claim additionally needs trusted actor/target identity, Request
and assignment state, current Membership/Organization/Location policy,
guarded absence, command binding and permitted authoritative effect.
Those may be represented by further source-owned typed rules, public
model contracts or named opaque dependencies. A theorem about this one
conjunct must not certify a complete Claim policy or a catalog that
omits a separately promised HumanHelp operation.

The baseline grammar is:

| Form | Meaning and constraints |
|------+-------------------------|
| =[:literal value]= | An admitted scalar or finite homogeneous scalar set under the literal/type profile. |
| =[:read reader-id]= | Read one declared binding from the same immutable evaluation environment. |
| =[:present [:read reader-id]]= | Test binding presence without evaluating an absent value. |
| =[:not e]= | Strict Boolean negation. |
| =[:and e1 e2 ...]= | At least two Boolean operands, evaluated left to right with short circuit. |
| =[:or e1 e2 ...]= | At least two Boolean operands, evaluated left to right with short circuit. |
| =[:implies p q]= | Rule-level conditional evaluation: evaluate q only when p is true. |
| =[:= a b]= and =[:not= a b]= | Equality/inequality under compatible declared semantic types. |
| =[:< a b]=, =[:<= a b]=, =[:> a b]=, =[:>= a b]= | Ordering for the explicitly supported ordered type; the baseline numeric domain is integers. |
| =[:in item finite-set]= | Membership with compatible element type and enum-domain constraints. |

The initial language permits plain bounded vectors, qualified reader
identifiers and literals consisting of nil, Boolean, mathematical integer,
string, keyword or a nonempty finite homogeneous set of such scalars.
A future empty-set expression must specify its element type; an empty
untyped literal is not inferred from host collection behavior.
Expression ASTs admit no host functions, arbitrary object, custom
comparator, executable metadata, macro invocation or ambient Var.
External input is decoded by a non-evaluating EDN reader and validated
before use; in particular no reader-eval or arbitrary tagged constructor
is admitted by this certified syntax.

The declared grammar checks unary =:literal=, =:read=,
=:present=, =:not=; binary comparisons, equality,
=:in= and =:implies=; and two-or-more-operand =:and=
and =:or=. Its selected version specifies finite node,
depth, arity, readers, set and literal budgets. Exceeding
one of them is a typed incomplete/rejected result, not
a Boolean value or invitation to run unrestricted Clojure.
Current numeric limit constants in the reference
implementation are not timeless design requirements.

The reader set equals the actual syntactic footprint. Missing and unused reader
declarations are rejected in this baseline. A declared read footprint is still
only a footprint over the semantic source; it does not prove the absence of
hidden reads in an unrelated opaque implementation.

Reader type declarations have exactly the same keys as syntactic
readers; no implicit reader acquisition is permitted. A nominal
enum has a qualified identity and explicit nonempty finite
domain. A raw keyword resembling a variant is not automatically
a value of an unrelated nominal enum. Typechecking rejects
unknown variants, mismatched nominal equality, ordering over
unordered types, heterogeneous membership and non-Boolean
connective operands.

Optionality and nullability are independent: absent, present
nil and present false differ. A read of an optional binding
requires a path that established presence. A non-null-required
operator additionally needs a path that established non-nil
when the field is nullable. Section 09.04 owns the detailed
refinement, normalization and path-join calculus; this
section establishes that type checking and interpretation
share the same reader identity and left-to-right semantics.

A rule's exact syntactic reader footprint does not certify
that a separate Clojure implementation has no hidden
database, clock, network, random or authentication
dependencies. Such dependencies are either source-derived
and covered, explicitly opaque, or remain open/unsupported.

Baseline value types are Boolean, integer, string, keyword, and named closed enum,
with independent optional and nullable attributes. Relationships, records, UUIDs,
money, dates, and additional nominal types enter through explicitly specified
schemas/codecs or later registered extensions. They cannot be silently reduced
to interchangeable strings/keywords when a proof relies on their distinct meaning.

The language and its literal/set limits are versioned. Empty sets or broader
collection expressions require defined typing behavior before admission; they
are not inferred from host coercion. There is no implicit recursion, arbitrary
function call, query, clock read, random value, or ambient principal lookup.

A program may be syntactically valid but intrinsically ill typed:
for example, =[:< [:read :request/status] [:literal 10]]=
cannot compare RequestStatus enum to an integer. Presence
and non-nil checks must dominate their dependent reads in
the specified short-circuit path. A valid rule may still
be inadmissible where a browser has not learned an
authoritative Request state or where an operation requires
a different principal and trust boundary.

*** Evaluation and totality

The evaluator consumes a validated binding snapshot containing required readers
and any present optional readers, with no undeclared bindings. Present nil and
absent are different. Boolean operators require Boolean values; ordinary Clojure
truthiness is not the rule language's semantics.

A well-typed rule in this total baseline must evaluate to a Boolean for every
valid binding in its declared domain, subject to the interpreter/primitive
correctness premise. Input rejection is separate from evaluating a valid rule
to false. Resource limits may stop checking/evaluation but cannot yield a
successful truth result.

=:and= evaluates operands left-to-right until false;
=:or= until true; and =:implies= checks its consequent
only when the antecedent is true. These are observable
order and error semantics, not merely Boolean identities.
A optimizer, normalizer or CLJS lowering must preserve
guarded-read order unless its stronger rewrite is separately
proved or qualified.

The interpreter validates the entire binding snapshot:
all required keys present, no undeclared keys, correctly
typed values, accepted enum members, nonnullable rules,
and valid optional readers. A missing required binding
or nil for a nonnullable field rejects; present false is
not absent, and present nil cannot be silently interpreted
as false. It returns a typed observation about one
provided snapshot, never an assertion that the snapshot
came from a trustworthy database or authenticated session.

The baseline integer meaning is mathematical integer comparison. It does not
implicitly certify JVM fixed-width arithmetic or JavaScript Number behavior.
Portable execution chooses a checked representation/range/codec and rejects
unrepresentable values, or uses a separately qualified exact representation.
A JVM/CLJS agreement test over small integers cannot prove all integer behavior.

A portable profile binds representation, codec, valid range and
rejection at the boundary. Mathematical integer comparison can
use separately qualified BigInt/exact arithmetic or a proven
safe-range restriction. JavaScript Number rounding, JVM
fixed-width overflow, numeric string coercion, and silently
changing integer into floating point are forbidden when
the checked rule requires exact integers. String, keyword,
nominal enum and optional/nullable serialization also
require explicit correspondence where JVM and CLJS
representations or coercions differ.

Evaluation to true is a concrete value result. It is neither a universal theorem
nor authority to commit. The acquisition and transaction boundaries must establish
where the bindings came from and whether every dependency used by the guard is
protected at the actual commit.

*** Source coverage, admission and adversarial acceptance

An intrinsically checked syntax/type/interpreter result is
copyable descriptive evidence, not a certified theorem or
an admission credential. A normalized rule fingerprint
names its interpreted subject; neither it nor a proof-status
label grants authority. Stronger requested profiles must
establish their own original-source and proof/physical
correspondence, rather than reclassifying this report.

The acceptance corpus pairs an eligible valid open-state
Claim conjunct with an incompatible enum/integer ordering,
unknown variant, omitted/unused reader, wrong enum identity,
optional access without presence, nullable comparison
without a non-nil guard, literal set type mismatch,
unknown input key, absent/nil/false binding, overflowing
or unrepresentable numeric transport, unsupported host
form and incomplete resource budget. Each failure
records the first responsible syntax/type/interpreter
or trusted acquisition boundary and expected diagnostic.

Independently, a complete Claim certificate that only
models =:open= must be rejected if the promised policy also
depends on actor, Membership or assignment guards.
Deleting Reassign from both the formal language inputs
and the available HumanHelp implementation also fails
the independent product capability oracle, even if every
remaining rule is intrinsically valid.

#+begin_example
Valid intrinsic rule for R17:
  status is :open          -> evaluated conjunct true

Wrong rule type:
  status < 10              -> rejected at intrinsic typing

Wrong distributed placement:
  browser branches on unobserved authoritative status
                           -> rejected at Choreo admission

Wrong physical authority:
  stale helper issues Claim -> current model/XTDB guard rejects
#+end_example

A reject-everything implementation cannot pass the
valid positive tests. An unsupported rule is reported
unsupported or bound to a separately admitted opaque
contract, never silently executed by host Clojure
and then labeled a verified rule.

*** Path-sensitive presence and null refinement

A certified rule's type judgment is indexed by its exact reader-type
environment, binding/observation identity, and the evaluation path on which
its refinements hold. A refinement is a justified *conditional fact about
one observed binding*, not a reusable current fact about the underlying
database entity or another participant. It cannot migrate between Request
IDs, actor sessions, changed provider instances, basis/progression epochs,
an older read and a replacement read, or precommit and postcommit states
without a separately established compatibility/correspondence judgment.

The baseline uses two distinct refinements for a reader =r=:
=:present= (the supplied immutable binding snapshot contains =r=), and
=:nonnull= (that same present binding is not nil). Required fields may be
present by their validated type contract; optional fields require an
established presence fact before =:read= can be evaluated. Nullable values
may be present nil; ordering and any other non-null-only operation additionally
require a non-nil fact. Present false and integer zero remain present
values, not Clojure-falsey absence. A typed premise about a reader must
use the same nominal type and declared source as the dependent expression.

*** Short-circuit path transfer and joins

For Boolean expression =e=, the checker derives conservative facts valid
*if the same expression evaluates true* and facts valid *if it evaluates
false*. These are conditional judgments, not claims that either outcome
occurred. The evaluator and proof/lowering interpretation must agree on
the exact short-circuit order:

| Form | Path where later child is evaluated | Facts available there |
|------+-------------------------------------+-----------------------|
| =[:and e1 e2 ...]= | Each preceding child evaluated true. | Facts established by those true evaluations. |
| =[:or e1 e2 ...]= | Each preceding child evaluated false. | Facts established by those false evaluations. |
| =[:implies p q]= | Consequent =q= evaluated only if =p= is true. | Facts established by true =p=. |
| =[:not p]= | Evaluation of =p= precedes Boolean negation. | True/false outcome facts are exchanged, not invented. |
| Ordinary equality/inequality | Only when both operands are safe under this exact environment. | A checked nil comparison may establish non-nil on the appropriate outcome path. |

At a point reached through more than one possible predecessor, retain
only facts valid on *every* incoming reachable path, unless a separate
checked disjunctive/refinement rule justifies more. A false =:and= may
have stopped at any one of several operands; it does not generally
establish that *every* conjunct is false. A true =:or= similarly does
not make every disjunct true. Unreachable or contradictory branches
must be recognized by an explicit valid judgment, not used to infer
arbitrary policy truth or to erase reachable side conditions.

For an optional nullable score, this order is safe:

#+begin_src clojure
{:readers #{:case/score}
 :expression
 [:and
  [:present [:read :case/score]]
  [:not= [:read :case/score] [:literal nil]]
  [:>= [:read :case/score] [:literal 10]]]}
#+end_src

The first condition establishes only presence; the second rules out
present nil. Reordering the comparison before either guard is a type
error, not a false business-rule result. A valid alternative
uses the false-path refinements of disjunction:

#+begin_src clojure
{:readers #{:case/score}
 :expression
 [:or
  [:not [:present [:read :case/score]]]
  [:= [:read :case/score] [:literal nil]]
  [:>= [:read :case/score] [:literal 10]]]}
#+end_src

The disjunction returns true for absent or present-nil score and
compares only when the score is present and non-nil. This is a *different
business policy* from the conjunction; successful typechecking of
both is not a license to substitute one for the other. Its exact
typing and interpretation must be checked against absent, nil,
false, zero and valid integer inputs. In particular, a Boolean
truthiness test cannot act as a presence or non-nil guard.

R4's future richer refinements—closed variant discrimination, path
cardinality, actor/subject facts, relationship existence, current
capability and trusted observation basis—require dedicated
sound transfer and join rules. A path-sensitive rule that is
intrinsically safe to evaluate may still be inadmissible to
a browser that does not know the premise or to a server
that has lost the premise's current trusted source.

*** Conservative normalization

Canonicalization preserves every distinction relevant to the
*typed interpreter's actual meaning*, including failure and
defined evaluation paths, not merely the mathematical Boolean
truth table of some idealized expression. The baseline may
normalize plain map/set insertion order, equivalent exact
mathematical integer representations, and omitted type flags
that explicitly default to false. It preserves operator and
operand order, nominal enum names/domains, reader IDs,
optional versus nullable, missing versus present nil,
and source/interpretation context where identity requires them.

Do not commute, sort, flatten, fold, eliminate, distribute or
rewrite short-circuit terms merely because a Boolean algebra
identity would preserve a truth table. Such transformations
can change safe read order, the first error, resource use,
whether an optional binding is accessed, branch-refinement
facts or source obligation coverage. The permitted baseline
equivalences have a specified finite meaning; stronger
rewrite rules require an independently checked
semantics-preservation theorem, or an explicitly admitted
trusted transformation premise with its exact assurance class.

Normalization of author syntax and normalization of an
already-typed, closed term are different boundaries.
Original source must first be validated and elaborated
under a named translation contract; accepting a cached
report or arbitrary value bearing a fingerprint is not
equivalent to rechecking original syntax and nominal types.
If a certificate refers only to a normalized term,
correctness of the source-to-term elaboration remains
a separate checked or stated trusted premise. The
normalizer must not issue its own proof of its soundness.

A canonical identity binds at least the rule language,
nominal reader-type declarations, interpreter semantics,
normalization/encoding profile and the semantic subject.
It need not include whitespace or diagnostic source
locations when those are semantically irrelevant.
Conversely, changing an enum domain, optional or nullable
flag, reader name, operator, argument order or encoding
version cannot be hidden by stable source-path text.
Structural identity is not a logical equivalence decision,
a certificate of policy truth, or an issuer's authority
to execute an operation.

A dependent abstract proof may be reused only when
the required current normalized semantic interface
or an explicitly checked compatibility relation
still holds. A changed Clojure callback, model provider,
deployed function body, installed Ring handler or
browser asset can invalidate implementation
correspondence even when a pure abstract rule
fingerprint remains identical.

*** Opaque policy and relational extensions

An application may have a genuine correctness-relevant
policy outside the closed rule AST. A production
Organization eligibility function, external service
or ordinary Clojure predicate can participate through
a named contract owned by its public model/extension
interface. It identifies the semantic subject and
implementation boundary, input and output types,
result variants, effect and disclosure scope,
termination/cancellation expectations, current
actor/Request/Organization dependencies, and
the propositions whose checking depends on
that opaque implementation. Its actual trust class
and any open conformance premises remain explicit.

Checking a supplied output Boolean proves at most
that a *provided* value satisfies the declared
typed interface under the checker's interpretation.
It does not establish who supplied it, whether
the producer was the declared function, whether
the function was deterministic, pure, terminating,
confined to declared effects, or correct about
the current business policy. A timeout request is
not proof that arbitrary JVM code or network
I/O can be forcibly interrupted. Declaring an
effects inventory is not proof no extra effects
occurred, and a claimed pure function is not
automatically an enforceably pure one.

A successful opaque policy result may enter a
closed rule as a typed reader only under an
explicitly qualified *provenance and freshness*
contract. It must be bound to the trusted actor,
actual input/subject identities, issuer/implementation,
relevant observation basis or transaction
snapshot, authorized disclosure, and known
authoritative dependency set. For a policy
that determines whether Claim may commit,
either the policy is evaluated (or safely
validated) inside the same guarded
transaction/linearization boundary, or
a checked equivalence/validation protocol
establishes that its cached result remains
correct at that boundary. Merely seeing
=:organization/eligible? true= in browser
data, an earlier request, or an unauthenticated
cache does not satisfy the guard.

Where the opaque policy depends on mutable
Membership, assignment, Organization or Request
facts, each dependency is protected by the
appropriate current read/guard/transaction
contract. A result may be stale even if its
boolean type and original source fingerprint
remain valid. A remote result cannot
declassify protected information or grant
authority by being marked =:runtime-enforced=
in a caller-produced map.

*** Semantic-policy escape and coverage

The finished assurance system makes
correctness-relevant policy visible inside
the source and implementation scope it
actually checks. A decision that determines
authorization, ownership, disclosure,
lifecycle permission, command incorporation,
protected domain effects or terminal-state
rights must correspond to an admitted rule,
a public certified dependency, a current
runtime-enforced premise or a named opaque
implementation-conformance assumption.

If Gesso detects a policy-changing branch
in a source subset it claims to inspect,
but finds no corresponding semantic rule
or named trust boundary, it reports a
*semantic-policy escape* with the affected
operation, proposition, source location,
dependency and possible repairs.
An explicit opaque premise is not a
detected unresolved escape, but its
implementation conformance remains open
or trusted under the chosen profile.

Conversely, not observing an escape in
an *uninspected* arbitrary Clojure
function does not prove that no hidden
policy branch or effect exists. No
whole-program completeness claim follows
from inspecting only rule ASTs, a declared
reader set, or a closed operation registry.
The checker identifies the exact program
surface for which it has established
coverage, and records external bodies and
writers that could change the proposition.
An independent HumanHelp requirement
such as Reassign cannot disappear just
because every remaining closed predicate
has a correct derivation.

A business rationale, human-readable
example or heuristic review finding is
useful policy-audit evidence, not a kernel
premise without explicit declaration
and checking. Changing a business rule
after such review is a visible semantic
decision, not an implementation-only
repair that can retain old certificates.

*** Rich relational/transition judgments

Relational schemas, quantified relationship
constraints, finite-family rules, guarded
multi-record transitions and richer closed
variant matches may use dedicated
interpreters and checkers rather than
inflating the minimal Boolean interpreter.
Each extension supplies the closed grammar,
nominal types, data/access/authority scope,
finite or unbounded domain, initial/transition
meaning, side conditions, normalization,
proof obligations and source-to-executor
correspondence needed by its conclusion.

A nominally valid relationship path
does not prove that its endpoint currently
exists, that a reference is visible to
this actor, that the query is complete,
or that no concurrent writer can change
it. A universal relational theorem over
a modeled domain does not establish
all deployed XTDB writer/absence behavior
without the corresponding physical
refinement and writer-coverage premises.
A typed keyword such as =:relationship/valid=
is not an inference rule merely because
an application uses it in a guard.

The final composition keeps independent
categories of evidence: intrinsic rule
validity, checked abstract theorem, named
opaque assumption, current guarded
execution, and installed browser/host
correspondence. No one of these silently
promotes the others or becomes the
business owner's chosen policy.

*** Positive, negative and changed-source acceptance

The conformance corpus runs genuine
accepted typed optional/nullable rules
beside variants whose presence/non-nil
guards are reordered, whose =:or= false
path is unsound, or whose refinement
belongs to a different Request/snapshot.
It exercises opposite policy results
of the two valid score examples,
including missing, nil, zero and
boundary integer scores.

Normalization tests admit only the
declared equivalences (map/set order,
exact integer representation and
default-false descriptor flags).
They reject commutation of guarded
conjunction, changed enum domains,
optional/nullable flags, reader
renaming, altered operator/threshold,
unsupported host metadata and
cache/report substitution. A matching
pure rule identity paired with a
changed execution implementation
must still invalidate relevant
concrete correspondence.

For opaque policies, tests distinguish
valid named contract + current protected
issuer/inputs from forged browser
eligibility, stale Membership or
Request, wrong implementation,
undisclosed effect, withheld output,
unavailable service, uninterruptible
work and a merely requested timeout.
A legitimate opaque execution may
succeed under an expressly weaker
profile without acquiring a strong
proof or blanket authorization.

A hidden policy branch in the *inspected*
source fragment is reported as an
escape; an arbitrary body outside
inspection is an open conformance
premise, not a falsely passed
exhaustive search. A true relational
conclusion in an abstract finite
model is separately checked against
actual guarded database absence,
cardinality and writer interference.
Every negative has a nearby
legitimate positive at the same
first enforcing boundary; rejecting
all rule expressions or every Claim
is not acceptance.
** Executable judgment contracts

The design requires executable definitions for the following judgment families.
The notation is descriptive; production terms use closed, versioned data schemas.
Their implementation may be split among focused checkers, but their meaning and
admission result cannot be left to each caller's interpretation.

A judgment is a versioned, typed relation between named *original*
semantic sources, its input context and the exact proposition it establishes.
For each admitted family the standard defines: accepted source and
elaboration form, nominal variable/subject identities, required context and
dependencies, the deterministic meaning of the conclusion, finite or
unbounded scope, checking and inference rules, coverage obligations, permitted
assumptions, statuses, resource limits, and its current applicability.
Implementation namespaces may divide these responsibilities, but a caller
cannot supply a preferred truth/status field and thereby select the result.

The finished Gesso API retains a distinction between a raw declaration,
a parsed or typed *description*, a checked derivation or dedicated
checker result, a source-bound admitted certificate, and a runtime
observation/effect. Each stage needs its own authority boundary.
An immutable Clojure map, matching fingerprint or old proof report is not
a substitute for an issued current checked result. Application authors
normally express ordinary Clojure/Gesso rules; proof terms and ledger
bookkeeping are generated and checked by Gesso rather than separately
hand-maintained by HumanHelp authors.

| Judgment | Inputs and conclusion | Required coverage |
|----------+-----------------------+-------------------|
| Rule typing | Reader types and path refinements establish a Boolean expression with safe reads. | Every expression node, actual reader, operator, and refinement use. |
| Rule evaluation | A valid typed rule and admitted binding snapshot produce its Boolean value. | Exact interpreter semantics and binding domain; no claim about unknown input origin. |
| Propositional derivation | A fixed subject/context and named premises entail the exact goal. | Every reachable proof step, rule side condition, and inherited premise. |
| Guarded-transition consequence | The declared relation matching pre/input/post entails the exact transition conclusion. | Guard, simultaneous assignments, read/write/frame interpretation, and proof steps. |
| Invariant preservation/induction | Initialization and every declared transition establish and preserve the invariant. | Every invariant and every operation in the declared catalog. |
| Distributed admissibility | A rule/action is permitted at the specified role and program point. | Knowledge, provenance, authority placement, refinements, observation scope, and relevant resource/continuation cases. |
| Projection/lowering correspondence | The actual projected/lowered subject satisfies the named correspondence relation. | All required behaviors/observables under the declared communication and runtime semantics. |
| Certificate composition | Checked evidence establishes required claims for the actual dependency closure and selected profile. | Both graph validity and source-derived required dependencies/obligations. |

Each judgment also declares the scope it *does not* establish.
An intrinsic Boolean rule does not imply distributed role knowledge;
a propositional derivation cannot prove the truth of an atomic business
fact from its type; abstract guarded consequences cannot assert that
a specific XTDB transaction ran; and a matching generated plan does
not prove its deployed browser artifact is the matching physical
program. Required bridge judgments connect those distinct subjects,
preserving their open assumptions instead of laundering them into
one "verified" label.

*** Obligation generation is independently checked

For each claimed artifact class, a source-derived checker must establish
that the required obligation ledger is complete for the original
normalized subject and requested profile. Validating every derivation
the elaborator volunteered is insufficient. The ledger covers every
declared operation, relevant reachable branch, declared reader/effect,
message and continuation case, initial state, protected resource exit,
public semantic dependency, and independently required product
capability *within the selected guarantee*. Requiring a dependency to
be covered does not imply that the checker has inspected an arbitrary
Clojure implementation body or all physical writers; the latter
remain separate coverage/conformance premises.

A missing branch cannot make a theorem easier. If a source retains
both an approved and not-approved Claim branch but the proposed
obligation list contains only the approved branch, coverage checking
rejects the stronger artifact before certificate issuance. A proof
about a Claim-only operation catalog also does not satisfy
HumanHelp's independently required Reassign capability. The
accepting boundary derives or checks required coverage against
the original source and public capability contract, not merely
against the elaborator's own submitted proof tree.

A judgment over a truly restricted model may legitimately pass
while a larger required application proof remains open. Such a
result records its actual subset, justified exclusions, opaque
external calls, relevant writer set, completeness assumptions,
and whether the expected next stage may consume it. In particular,
"unreachable branch" requires an appropriate checked
unreachability argument, not a missing test or a hard-coded
assumption that a UI button is disabled.

*** Results, failed proofs and assurance scope

Judgment outcomes distinguish: checked with no logical premises
under the specified calculus; checked conditionally with named
premises; a model counterexample to a stated property (where the
dedicated checker can establish one); invalid syntax or derivation;
unsupported language/assurance request; incompletely checked
resource-limited exploration; mismatched or stale source/dependency;
and unavailable physical/trusted evidence. A checked conditional
conclusion remains conditional even if an operator has accepted
the premises for a particular deployment profile.

A counterexample to a *claimed model invariant* is not the same
thing as a malformed proof term, an incomplete search, or a
misstated HumanHelp business policy. Likewise the absence of
a finite counterexample is not evidence about unenumerated
states, external writers or the actual deployed browser.
Diagnostics identify the exact proposition, original source,
first responsible checker, relevant context, status, dependencies,
and what must be repaired or qualified; they redact protected
data for the recipient. A result accepted only under a
named opaque premise does not get reported as a
discharged unconditional theorem.

Each judgment has positive, negative, unsupported, and resource-limit conformance
cases as applicable. Parser rejection, a checker report, certificate admission,
and runtime authorization remain separate products. The rest of this part fixes
the baseline proof rules and the composition obligations rather than relying on
an indefinitely deferred universal judgment calculus.

** Small proof kernel and dedicated proof checkers
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-proof-kernel-boundary
:END:

The proof kernel checks proposed derivations independently of the elaborator's
claimed success. It accepts original source terms under a closed rule catalog,
revalidates their subject/type bindings, and computes the conclusion and premise
dependencies. It does not accept an alleged earlier checking report as a proof.

“Small” refers to a bounded logical responsibility and an inspectable trusted
implementation. It does not mean one universal checker must contain rule typing,
all model exploration, database refinement, browser effects, and every future
theory. Dedicated checkers can establish different judgments and participate
through explicit bridges.

The kernel's trusted surface includes the chosen logical interpretation,
its parser and typed subject representation, every primitive inference
rule and side condition, the checked implementation and relevant
runtime/representation assumptions. Keeping the kernel small makes
review possible, not automatically sound. Checker meta-soundness and,
where stronger assurance is requested, an independent mathematical
or mechanized argument and adversarial conformance evidence are separate
obligations. A proof-producing elaborator and a checker that simply
calls back into that same elaborator cannot count as independent
fault containment.

For proof admission, the kernel recomputes the expected conclusion
and inherited premises from the actual original proof steps. It
does not trust user-provided =:conclusion=, =:valid?=,
=:proof-status=, a compiled-report hash, or a
previously checked but changed derivation. The certificate issuer
subsequently checks required dependency/current-source bindings
and the requested assurance scope; the kernel does not
issue an application execution credential.

*** Baseline propositional language

The propositional layer has =true=, =false=, typed Boolean atoms, negation,
conjunction, disjunction, and implication. Each atom contains an independently
well-typed rule declaration. The proposition binds one exact semantic subject
and one nominal variable context; declared variables equal the actual union of
atom readers in the accepted source.

All proposition constructors have closed grammar and exact arity:
=[:true]=, =[:false]=, a typed =[:atom rule]=,
unary =[:not p]=, binary =[:implies p q]=, and
at-least-binary =[:and ...]= and =[:or ...]=. Every
atomic rule is rechecked against its declared nominal
variable context, reader types and original source;
undeclared and unused variables are rejected. The
finite source and proof budget is part of the checker
profile. An unsupported term cannot execute arbitrary
Clojure or be interpreted as an implicit theorem.

An atomic rule denotes a Boolean proposition only under its
specified interpretation. Its typecheck does not supply
the atom's truth; logical constants remain distinct from
runtime evidence that an Organization policy actually holds.
In particular, introducing a *premise* =[:false]=
produces a conditional derivation dependent on that
premise, not an unconditional contradiction or proof
that the system's current state is inconsistent.

Propositional connectives express logic. They are not the rule interpreter's
control-flow operators. In particular, a presence atom in the antecedent of a
propositional implication does not make an otherwise ill-typed consequent atom
safe. Put that guarded evaluation inside one well-typed rule atom, or use a
separately specified refinement-aware proof judgment.

This independence rules out a subtle unsoundness: the proposition
=[:implies [:atom present?] [:atom unsafe-read]]= is
not well typed if the second atom's reader is optional and
has not been safely refined *inside that atom*. A logical
implication can reason about two already well-typed
Boolean propositions, but does not import the rule
interpreter's short-circuit presence facts across
separate atom boundaries. Later refinement-aware proof
constructors must make the stronger relation explicit
and prove its typing/evaluation meaning.

Logic here permits ordinary reuse of established
propositions; it does not imply consumption or duplication
of runtime resource leases. Section 09.06 owns
resource-sensitive transition consequences and the
proper treatment of actual one-use authority, and
Part V owns physical effect/ownership lifetimes.

The minimal inference catalog is:

| Rule | Premises | Conclusion and side conditions |
|------+----------+--------------------------------|
| Premise | Named P in the declared premise environment. | P, retaining that exact scoped premise as open. |
| Truth introduction | None. | True. |
| Implication reflexivity | None. | P implies P, with exactly matching well-formed P. |
| Conjunction introduction | P1 through Pn. | Their ordered conjunction, with at least two operands. |
| Conjunction elimination | Conjunction P1 through Pn. | The child at a checked index. |
| Implication elimination | P implies Q, and P. | Q, with exact antecedent matching. |
| Disjunction introduction | P. | A well-formed disjunction whose checked indexed child is exactly P. |

Each rule is a *constructor-specific checked inference*, not
a generic "trust this consequence" function. Premise introduction
preserves the exact named premise dependency; truth introduction
uses no premises; implication reflexivity proves only
=[:implies P P]= for the exact same well-formed P.
Conjunction introduction maintains the declared ordered
conjuncts; elimination uses a checked valid index.
Modus ponens requires the exact antecedent match and
ordered implication/antecedent inputs; disjunction introduction
checks the requested child position and well-formed whole
disjunction. These rules do not assert independent
truth of an atomic =P= and contain no magic closure
under semantic or arithmetic equivalence.

This catalog intentionally does not include
an unchecked "assert" axiom, arbitrary atomic truth,
a parser shortcut for falsehood elimination,
natural-deduction hypothetical discharge,
untrusted rewriting or a Clojure callback that
can register additional inference tags.
A further rule is admitted only after a
separate rule semantics/soundness obligation,
source schema, exact subject/variable matching,
conformance corpus and checker-version upgrade.

Proof steps have unique identities and can reference only earlier steps. The
final checked step must establish the exact goal. Premise dependencies are
computed through the used steps. The baseline rejects unused proof steps and
unused declared premises, making an allegedly relevant premise or hidden dead
proof fragment visible rather than silently carrying it along.

Each step has a unique identity, a recognized rule-specific exact
source shape, only references to earlier steps, an explicit
conclusion bound to the same proposition subject/context, and
verified side conditions. The final step must equal the stated
goal under the checking relation. The checker computes
all *reachable* step dependencies from the final goal and
rejects unused declared steps or premises rather than
letting dead proof fragments conceal a forged assumption
or apparently discharged obligation. This also prevents
simple cyclic, self or forward references from appearing
as an admissible finite proof DAG.

A valid propositional derivation with no named premises
is a no-premise judgment under this stated logical
calculus. It is *not* automatically a theorem
about the executable state machine or the physical
database, and it does not establish the kernel's own
meta-soundness. A proof with named premises retains
each exact one even when the premise is a Boolean
constant or an opaque business-policy statement.
A dependency graph of certificates may itself
not close a circular logical justification
without an explicitly checked induction or
fixed-point rule.

This catalog has no arbitrary atomic-truth rule, no arithmetic solver, no
hypothetical-context discharge, and no rewriting by an unproved equivalence.
Additional rules require explicit source schemas, interpretation, side conditions,
soundness obligations, and conformance cases. They cannot be installed through
an application-supplied “trusted inference” callback.

*** What a checked derivation actually certifies

The checked conclusion has a versioned logical subject and
nominal variable context. It is logically about those
propositions under the accumulated premise environment.
The framework separately checks whether the original
Gesso model/Choreo/operation, the generated abstract
projection, and the actual application runtime are
the *same relevant semantic subject*. Source-to-proof
elaboration, proof-to-operation relation, and
operation-to-deployed XTDB/browser behavior are distinct
bridges. If any required bridge is absent,
the derivative claim remains conditional/open.

A trusted application consumer validates the current
certificate's issuing checker, source dependencies,
selected profile and applicability. A map reconstructed
from serialized EDN with a copied certificate ID is
not an admitted checked value. Even a valid local
proof certificate grants neither an authenticated
session principal, current database permission
nor a physical browser installation permit.

*** Fault containment against a buggy elaborator

The architecture is tested with defects injected at
the *actual proposed* boundary, not fake isolated
checker data invented to look like the application.
A large elaborator may generate an invalid derivation,
an incomplete obligation set, or valid proof data for
the wrong normalized source. These are three different
failures with different responsible checkers.

#+begin_example
1. Invalid derivation:
   Helper Claim branch asks for private-review fact.
   Elaborator emits :knowledge-from-global-syntax.
   Small kernel: unknown rule; no knowledge theorem.

2. Omitted required obligation:
   Normalized source retains Claim and Deny branches.
   Elaborator proposes evidence only for Claim.
   Source-derived coverage checker: missing Deny/continuation.
   No certificate for the requested closed construction.

3. Valid proof for wrong executable:
   Certified subject S1 and proof are legitimate.
   Lowering actually emits S2 but copies S1's stamp.
   Source/projection/executable correspondence rejects.
   The proof kernel did not inspect the emitted plan.
#+end_example

These cases must not be credited to a kernel that
does not inspect the relevant defect. In particular,
all volunteered proof steps can be correct while
the required branch/operation ledger is incomplete;
an entirely valid kernel theorem about S1 can
coexist with a faulty or substituted executable S2.
A stronger deployed correspondence claim must
also check the actual served browser assets,
installed Ring/Biff handler, guarded XTDB
writer boundary and authorized observation
where those are required.

*** Positive and adverse proof-kernel acceptance

At minimum acceptance exercises the concrete
conditional derivation below and a truly premise-free
truth/reflexivity proof; correct conjunction/
disjunction introduction/elimination and exact
implication elimination; false as an *open*
named premise; two distinct nominal contexts;
a malformed or omitted rule argument; an
unknown rule; a forward/self reference;
unused step/premise; a copied source report
or forged certificate; a changed checker
version; oversized input/terminated checking;
and a well-typed rule atom improperly relying
on refinement from a different proposition.

Each negative names the first enforcing
boundary and preserves a nearby admitted
positive. An unsupported arithmetic relation
is not "proved false" when the kernel lacks
an arithmetic rule; it is an unsupported
judgment until a dedicated checker provides
a separately qualified bridge.

*** A concrete conditional derivation

The following source states a deliberately small conditional claim. The variables
are abstract Boolean inputs; they are not authoritative HumanHelp facts.

#+begin_src clojure
(let [p [:atom {:readers #{:example/eligible?}
                :expression [:read :example/eligible?]}]
      q [:atom {:readers #{:example/allowed?}
                :expression [:read :example/allowed?]}]]
  {:subject {:kind :operation
             :id :example/conditional-rule
             :interface {:operation :example/conditional-rule}}
   :variables {:example/eligible? {:type :boolean}
               :example/allowed? {:type :boolean}}
   :premises {:example/eligibility p
              :example/policy [:implies p q]}
   :steps [{:id :step/policy
            :rule :premise
            :premise :example/policy
            :conclusion [:implies p q]}
           {:id :step/eligibility
            :rule :premise
            :premise :example/eligibility
            :conclusion p}
           {:id :step/result
            :rule :implication-elimination
            :from [:step/policy :step/eligibility]
            :conclusion q}]
   :goal q})
#+end_src

The conclusion is Q under the two named premises. The derivation does not prove
that eligibility holds, that the policy implication is the right product rule,
or that either is satisfied by a particular request. Removing the eligibility
step, changing its subject, changing the policy antecedent, or relabelling the
goal must fail the appropriate check.

This is an authentic *conditional* proof of Q, not proof
of either assumption's actual validity, correctness of the
business-owner's policy, the original application
elaboration, execution of an appropriate Request
transition, or browser knowledge. The separate
qualification path must still establish required
premises at their true model/transaction/acquisition
boundaries and prove or explicitly assume the
applicable implementation correspondence.

An atom whose rule compares an integer with 7 remains an atom to this kernel.
The kernel cannot infer that =x > 7= entails =x >= 7= merely because a human sees
the arithmetic relationship. A dedicated arithmetic/typed-semantic judgment can
establish that implication under its own explicit scope and bridge contract.

*** Facts are not linear capabilities

Logical facts and concrete resources have different structural rules.
A reusable assertion can be cited twice in a proof. A current
one-use Refresh grant, command-attempt authorization, browser
target lease, transaction capability or cleanup obligation
cannot be copied, widened, consumed or transferred by an
inference rule that mentions its name. The actual resource
has a trusted issuer, owner, scope, generation, live
instance and permitted effect set. The execution layer
checks all those conditions at use.

A resource-sensitive checker may employ separately typed
linear/affine/ownership contexts with explicit rules for
issuance, transfer, split, join, consumption and retirement.
An affine resource may permit discard where a linear one
requires accounting; neither is freely duplicable.
An abandoned request may transfer a recovery obligation
without preserving permission for its retired callback
to install DOM. The proof's claim about such a rule
does not issue any token or establish its physical use.

#+begin_example
Logical fact: "G7 permits at most one scoped Refresh read."
Actual G7: issued to Alice, cohort C, execution E2
  first qualified read consumes its read authorization
  later SSE advisory cannot reuse G7
  retired E2 cannot reinstall a stale response
  any remaining repair obligation has an explicit owner
#+end_example

The logical kernel can establish conditional propositions about
resources; actual authority issuance, one-use enforcement and
physical effect cleanup are separate runtime judgments.

Ordinary propositional facts can be reused in derivations. A runtime authority
capability, effect lease, resource ownership token, or one-use installation permit
cannot therefore be duplicated by conjunction introduction or repeated premise
use. Logical statements about such resources are distinct from the resources.

Resource judgments carry their own ownership context and consumption/retirement
rules. A bridge from a checked resource proposition to a permitted runtime act
must still pass the actual authority and lifecycle boundary. A proof of “the
plan requires a current permit” does not produce that permit.

*** Guarded-transition consequence language

This is an explicitly versioned abstract relation, not a
second production interpreter. Its exact subject includes
nominal state/input schemas, guard, read/write sets,
assignment source and frame complement. The abstract state
is a selected *projection* of application data; it is not
automatically the whole database or all active writers.
A transition descriptor checked for shape is not yet
a universally valid consequence theorem, much less
an admitted physical XTDB operation.


The baseline abstract transition has a typed state projection, disjoint typed
inputs, a Boolean guard, exact read/write declarations, and simultaneous
assignments. Each assignment initially supports a scalar literal or a required
prestate/input reader. Unwritten declared state fields are framed unchanged,
including their presence as well as their value.

Its meaning is a relation T(s, x, s'):

- s and x satisfy the declared domains;
- the guard holds in s and x;
- every assigned poststate field has its specified value computed from s and x;
- every framed field preserves its declared value and presence;
- s' satisfies the declared state domain.

There is no implicit sequencing between assignments. Reading a field on an
assignment's right side reads the prestate, not an earlier assignment's output.
Deletion, fresh allocation, relational updates, arithmetic expressions, external
effects, and opaque transforms need their own specified transition constructors.
The baseline must reject them rather than pretending a scalar assignment model
covers them.

The consequence checker can derive:

| Rule family | Established conclusion under the matching-relation premise |
|-------------+-----------------------------------------------------------|
| Guard | The transition's exact guard held in the protected abstract prestate/input. |
| Assignment | The specified poststate field equals its literal or prestate/input source. |
| Frame | An unwritten declared field preserves value and presence. |
| Conjunction introduction/elimination | Compose or select conclusions for the same relation and bound context. |
| Equality substitution | Replace a whole equality operand using an earlier checked equality with compatible types and pre/input/post identities. |

A consequence is conditional on T matching. It does not establish that an actual
XTDB transaction executed T. Optional prestate/input fields are not total value
terms without the required refinement. A written optional poststate field can
be a total term when the assignment establishes its presence. Framing an absent
optional field must preserve absence, not coerce it into present nil.

The frame covers only the declared projection. It says nothing about unmodeled
database fields until a separate physical footprint/correspondence argument
establishes that relationship.

*** Original-source, total-term and physical boundaries

A consequence judgment has the form:

#+begin_example
For all compatible s,x,s':
  T(s,x,s') => Consequence(s,x,s')
#+end_example

An accepted =:guard= fact asserts the abstract guard only
*assuming* the triple satisfies T. It does not prove
that the current Request is claimable, the actor is
authorized or the real transaction committed. An
assignment conclusion refers to an explicitly written
poststate field; a frame conclusion preserves both
presence and value for an unwritten state field.
Equality substitution uses a prior checked same-type,
same-phase whole-operand equality, not arbitrary
predicate rewriting, numeric solving or a fresh
authoritative observation.

Optional prestate/input fields are partial terms
without an applicable checked presence refinement.
Written optional poststate fields may be total when
the assignment establishes their presence. An
unwritten optional field retains absent versus
present-nil identity under the frame, even when
both might appear as nil after an unsafe Clojure
lookup. Readers cannot borrow refinements from
a different Request, actor, basis or transaction.

The checker re-parses original source, verifies
actual source-bound proof steps and final goal,
and retains the matching-relation premise.
Proof reports and structural fingerprints
remain copyable descriptions; stronger
certificate admission independently rechecks
its source and premises. A valid finite witness
for one transition is not a universal proof.

The closed scalar baseline does not model
deletion, allocation, relational cardinality,
arithmetic state updates, multi-document writes
or external effects. Adding these requires
distinct typed transition constructors,
sound consequence rules, declared
read/write/frame relationships and
source-to-executor correspondence. Actual
HumanHelp Claim/Reassign can involve guarded
Request *and* RequestAssignment documents;
a proof over RequestStatus alone cannot
establish unique active primary assignments.

Positive cases for guard, literal assignment,
prestate copy, frame equality and same-field
optional absence accompany negative cases
for forged guard, wrong post literal, a
written field called framed, absent input
treated as a total equality, mixed nominal
phases, unsupported proof rule and stale
source. Rejection occurs at the first
responsible checker and cannot be credited
as a proven unsafe real database state.

*** Invariant preservation and conditional induction

Let =Inv(s)= denote the conjunction of all
declared invariants =I_j=. The per-operation
preservation judgment for =I_j= may rely on
*every* =I_k= holding in the common prestate:

#+begin_example
Initial(s)             => I_j(s)       for each j
Inv(s) and T_i(s,x,s') => I_j(s')      for every i,j
#+end_example

This is a *simultaneous inductive invariant*:
it is not automatically a proof that each
clause preserves itself in isolation.
The checker derives the required base cases
and full operation-by-invariant cross-product
from the original common catalog rather
than trusting a volunteered list.
A catalog's closedness is relative to
the declared source, not evidence that
every production writer or required
HumanHelp capability was included.


An operation catalog declares one state interpretation, one initial condition,
one invariant set, and an exact set of abstract operations. It does not duplicate
the invariant independently in each operation's proof input. The checker derives
the required base and preservation obligations from the catalog.

For each invariant I, the argument requires:

#+begin_example
Base:          every declared initial state satisfies I.
Preservation:  for each declared operation T,
               I(s) and T(s, x, s') imply I(s').
Conclusion:    I holds in every state reached by a finite sequence
               of those declared transitions from a declared initial state.
#+end_example

Every invariant and every operation must be accounted for. An unsupported base
case or one unsupported preservation case leaves that invariant's induction
claim open. A conjunction of unrelated successes is not a whole-invariant proof.

Useful preservation constructors include unchanged-frame reasoning, literal
postcondition reasoning, typed conjunction, and same-origin copy equality.
Evidence from different constructors may be combined only by an explicit checked
composition rule over the exact same transition, invariant clauses, variables,
and remaining premises. Unioning two success sets is not such a rule. Conversely,
there is no architectural reason to forbid a sound conjunction proof merely
because its children were established by different dedicated checkers.

To establish a mixed invariant from different
families, a new checked composition rule must
bind all constituent judgments to the same
transition, invariant clauses, typed pre/input/post
identities and remaining premises. Merely
unioning source-family success sets is unsound.
Likewise a checker cannot make a missing
operation disappear from the independent
required-capability ledger. A supported
narrow family may succeed while the wider
invariant stays open.

The original source-derived obligation
ledger remains intact: conditional proof
checking does not rewrite open initial-state
existence, complete operation coverage,
source correctness, checker soundness,
authorization or physical refinement
obligations into discharged facts.

The induction conclusion is about the declared abstract machine. Applying it to
production additionally requires initialization coverage, relevant physical writer
coverage, and a correspondence relation for actual transitions. Those premises
remain explicit. Membership revocation, imports, administrative writes, and
background jobs cannot be omitted because they lack a browser control.

The mathematical induction argument does not by itself require an inhabited
initial predicate, but an empty initial state set yields a vacuous result. Reports
must expose satisfiability/existence evidence separately, and useful application
completion requires appropriate positive initialization and enabled-operation
scenarios. Safety induction also does not establish termination, fairness, or
eventual user Refresh.

*** Non-vacuity, physical writer correspondence and product scope

An empty initial-state class makes a universal
base implication formally vacuous. The report
must separately expose initial-state
satisfiability, at least one legitimate
initializer, actual production initialization
coverage and eligible enabled-operation
histories. A checker that recognizes literal
false may reject that obvious case; failing
to detect a subtler contradiction is not
evidence of existence. A mathematically
correct safety proof does not itself provide
user-visible progress or live resources.

For transfer to real Gesso/HumanHelp executions,
the application must qualify an abstraction
from actual Request, RequestAssignment,
Membership/Organization and other relevant
data into the declared abstract state.
The selected XTDB version's transaction
isolation, guard, absence, atomicity and
current prestate conditions, along with
every writer that could affect the invariant,
must match a modeled transition or be
independently proved irrelevant.
Background repair, imports, administrative
writes and raw external XTDB access matter
even without a browser control.
An unknown/unobserved writer is an open
coverage premise, not permission to certify
the entire production application.

“At most one active primary assignment
per Request” is a different invariant
from “exactly one when the Request is
claimed.” The latter additionally requires
conditional existence and status-to-
assignment consistency. Declaring an
enum invariant over RequestStatus proves
neither active primary uniqueness nor
absence of concurrently committed competing
assignments. A meaningful dual-Claim test
challenges the real guarded transaction;
a Reassign test separates authorized actor
from eligible target. Those physical tests
support correspondence but cannot prove
unbounded abstract induction.

A completed abstract safety induction
also does not imply termination, fairness,
eventual notification or inevitable user
Refresh. Under the selected advisory-only
remote adoption policy, a user who never
Refreshes may safely retain older adopted
content. Claims of eventual command
reconciliation, outbox work or resource
cleanup need their own qualified availability,
retention, scheduling and recovery premises.

#+begin_example
Admitted abstract conclusion:
  all declared reachable states satisfy I

Still separate:
  a valid initial state actually exists
  all required user operations are declared
  all relevant physical writers are covered
  concrete XTDB updates refine T
  implementation/checker rules are sound
  browser effects and recovery are current
  user eventually invokes Refresh
#+end_example

*** Positive and adversarial checks

Genuine accepted cases include frame
preservation, literal postcondition,
same-origin equality, one supported
mixed-clause invariant, a valid initial
consequence, and complete declared
conditional induction. Malformed or open
cases include missing base, one unproved
operation, changed shared invariant,
shrunk operation catalog, impossible
initial class, forged conditional status,
mixed-family proof-ID union, invalid
optional frame, wrong state subject,
unmodeled primary-assignment writer,
and a Request-only status proof asserted
as a RequestAssignment uniqueness
certificate. Every rejection records
its actual first boundary and keeps a
nearby valid positive. Unsupported
proof constructors remain open rather
than falsely proving or refuting the
claim; reject-all is not certification.

*** Exact finite checking and bounded diagnostics

An exact finite result is a judgment about an *explicit
finite transition system*, not about every state that happens
to fit a convenient Clojure map. The checked source identifies
nominal state/input domains, initialization, every admitted
transition and nondeterministic choice, state comparison,
invariant or temporal property, and observation semantics.
The checker records whether its exploration was exhaustive
relative to **that** finite subject. It cannot silently
substitute a finite sample for a declared infinite type.

The result classification is not a Boolean:
| Result | Supported interpretation | Not established |
|--------+--------------------------+-----------------|
| Exhaustive safe | Every reachable state/transition of the specified finite model was explored under the stated semantics and satisfies the checked safety property. | Correspondence to a larger or deployed system. |
| Genuine counterexample | A valid finite-model initial state and enabled transition trace violate the property, with a replayable source/operation path. | That the same violation is physically reachable without refinement. |
| Incomplete | A state/case/depth/time/memory budget was reached, or the language/transition coverage could not be established. | Success, falsity of the property, or absence of counterexamples beyond the explored prefix. |
| Invalid or unsupported source | A syntax/type/domain/interpretation requirement fails. | A mathematical counterexample or a theorem. |

For safety, a complete algorithm enumerates all declared initial
states and enabled transition alternatives, adding new states until
the reachable set reaches a fixed point without dropped successors.
If an enabled successor falls outside the enumerated state universe,
either the model's declared transition/closure contract rejects it
or a checked abstraction maps it to a covered state. It cannot
silently be discarded and still report exhaustive success.
Visited-state deduplication itself uses the correct nominal and
presence-sensitive state equality. A bounded trace-prefix
enumeration is not closed-world exploration unless an independent
completeness argument justifies the bound.

The finite result also names its exact property and quantifier.
Safety assertions about every reachable state, trace-invariant
assertions, deadlock freedom, fairness, termination and leads-to
are different judgments. Temporal conclusions need the
appropriate finite-state temporal semantics and scheduling/
fairness assumptions; merely checking an invariant on visited
states does not prove eventual delivery, user Refresh or
progress after a process failure.


A completed exact finite exploration establishes its stated property over that
finite model. Its evidence includes domains, initial states, transition relation,
property, exploration algorithm/profile, completion status, and counterexample
when one exists. A fixed trace-depth bound is a bound on explored prefixes, not
proof of all paths unless a separate completeness argument establishes it.

*** Domains, closure, non-vacuity and model relevance

A finite enum or Boolean domain can be covered exhaustively when
its nominal declared values and interpretation are exact. A
bounded set of integer, string, Request ID, timestamp, actor or
database-state representatives is generally a *sampled subset*.
Even when every selected sample is explored, the report must
identify the restriction and avoid claiming correctness for
unrepresented values. Numerical boundary choices, nil/absent
values, role/tenant scope, competing RequestAssignment rows,
retry attempts and multiple client generations are meaningful
dimensions; omitting them can hide an intended counterexample.

An initialized finite model can be empty or can disable every
transition. Formal safety may then hold vacuously. Stronger
product acceptance requires separately checked or witnessed
eligible initial states, meaningful enabled operations and,
where relevant, contention, recovery or failure paths.
An invariant that says "no two active primary assignments"
cannot be certified for HumanHelp by a finite state made only
of one RequestStatus field; the abstract property needs the
assignment relation or a justified mapping to it.

The finite checker distinguishes *relative model completeness*
from *application-source coverage* and *physical correspondence*.
The source-owned operation catalog must include every required
declared transition/branch for the selected claim; production
coverage additionally needs all relevant initializer and
writer paths, including administrative/import/background/raw
writes, and a justified state abstraction. A closed catalog
that intentionally or accidentally omits Reassign can be
model-checked exactly while still failing an independently
required HumanHelp capability.

To transfer safety from a finite abstraction to a larger
concrete system, a checked relation must explain concrete
initial-state inclusion and how each relevant concrete step
is simulated by an abstract allowed step (or invariant-preserving
stutter), and preserve the observed property. Under-approximate
samples cannot establish this relation by themselves. The
state-space reduction, symmetry or partial-order method
must expose its own soundness premises. An abstract
counterexample requires concretization or a separately
checked feasibility argument before it becomes evidence of
an actual XTDB/browser exploit.


A finite Boolean truth-table check can establish the validity of one instantiated
propositional implication for all valuations of its distinct atoms. That does not
prove a universally quantified rule schema over all formulas/arity or prove the
production checker's implementation correct.

*** Truth tables, typed atoms and replayable witnesses

For a *fixed well-formed propositional formula*, complete
enumeration of every Boolean valuation can decide its
truth-table validity under the specified propositional
semantics. Treating typed atoms as independent Boolean
variables is appropriate for checking a tautology:
validity for all valuations implies validity for any
typed interpretation that maps atoms to Booleans.
It does not show that all independently assigned
valuation combinations are reachable from a real
typed Gesso source or current Request snapshot.

For a candidate *counterexample*, a Boolean valuation
in which two related typed atoms disagree might be
impossible for any common input state. A typed-atom
bridge therefore checks real nominal domains, reader
presence/nullability, shared subject bindings and
the simultaneous interpretation of correlated atoms.
A sound abstract violating valuation is not automatically
a feasible typed counterexample; the report labels the
valuation as propositional-only until a typed
binding witness or concretization is established.

Conversely, a typed counterexample must include the
actual source data, qualified variable environment,
initial/input bindings, evaluated rule terms, step
sequence and property failure so that an *independent*
interpreter can reproduce the violation. A compact
counterexample with no original-source provenance or
one that was merely emitted by a checker being tested
is not yet independent semantic evidence.
Counterexample minimization may improve diagnosis,
but must preserve the original violation and replay
relation rather than substituting a different subject.


A typed-atom bridge instead enumerates actual binding domains, evaluates atoms
under their typed semantics, and derives the corresponding Boolean valuations.
It must not fabricate impossible combinations of correlated atoms and call them
reachable application states. All-Boolean-valuation validity can establish a
stronger propositional tautology; a counterexample with impossible typed atom
values is not automatically a concrete typed/application counterexample.

The checker must preserve distinctions among:
an invalid syntactic derivation, a true proposition for a
bounded finite model, a discovered model counterexample,
an inconclusive timeout, and a proposition proved
conditionally under named unverified premises. A
counterexample does not necessarily refute the
formal *inference rule schema*; it may instead reveal
a false model premise or a source-to-concrete mismatch.
The actual first accountable checker and source are
reported with the result.


Independent bounded interpreters, differential JVM/CLJS tests, rule-instance
truth tables, and mutation corpora are valuable evidence. They remain precisely
scoped. Increasing the sample count or giving a module “soundness” in its name
does not create an unbounded meta-theorem.

*** Independent oracles and evidence of rule soundness

Independence is *semantic and implementation-specific*, not
a claim that two different namespaces have different names.
An oracle that invokes the production rule evaluator, its
normalizer, the production proof checker, or a wrapper around
the same captured implementation shares those assumptions
and bugs. That may still detect substituted public results,
but it is not independent support for the reused rule.

The assurance inventory identifies which code and semantics
each oracle shares: parser, typechecker, optional/null codec,
integer comparison, transition interpreter, proof rules,
step-closure algorithm, source catalog and mutation harness.
A separately implemented finite oracle or test-local
truth-table interpreter can independently challenge some
of those layers. It must not be promoted into a complete
meta-soundness result merely because it agrees on every
enumerated test input. Deliberately injected unsound
proof rules and transition mutants should be rejected
by the specific independent oracle at the expected
boundary, while an admitted positive source still passes.

A meaningful property claim about the *proof kernel*
needs a different kind of evidence from checking one
proof instance. The checker-soundness goal is:
for every accepted well-formed derivation under the
specified semantics, the conclusion follows from
its computed premises. Establishing this universally
requires a mathematical/mechanized rule-by-rule
argument or a suitably trusted proof, together with
a verified relationship to the actual parser,
JVM implementation and runtime primitives.
An independent finite audit may falsify an
unsound rule schema with one example; surviving
a finite mutation corpus does not discharge
unbounded checker meta-soundness.

External theorem tools (Recife, TLA+/TLC, Quint,
or a proof assistant) are *implementation aids for
Gesso*, not an obligation for ordinary application
authors to maintain a separate formal model. An
external result is imported only with the source
transition/typed semantics, exact theorem, model
and version, assumptions, checked execution status,
tool trust and correspondence bridge. A successful
TLC exploration of a two-operation finite Request
machine is valuable bounded regression evidence,
not a proof about all XTDB writers or production
browser/Live behavior.


*** Trusted checking base and fault containment

The trusted checking base is assessed *per judgment*.
A proof-kernel certificate, exact finite-state result,
typed atom interpretation, source/plan fingerprint
and physically acquired XTDB observation may rely
on different primitives and trusted execution
paths. A single "fully verified" flag erases
essential distinctions and is forbidden as a
substitute for the root proposition/assumption
ledger. Reports remain source-bound, versioned
and tamper-aware, but self-reported status or
hash equality alone never crosses a certified
admission boundary.

Proof search, budget scheduling and an elaborator's
optimization heuristics need not live inside the
small trusted kernel. Its actual admission parser,
inference rule verification and primitive meaning
still belong to the trusted surface. The complete
application certificate additionally names the
source-coverage, original-code elaboration,
projection/lowering, physical writer, served
browser and installed host bridges that this
kernel does not check.


The checking base includes parser/decoder behavior, canonical representations,
primitive interpretations, inference/checker code, dependency resolution, and the
admission policy implementing the claimed judgment. A smaller kernel reduces
that base; it does not certify its own correctness by being small.

The elaborator may propose wrong terms, omit obligations, or emit the wrong plan.
Different boundaries catch those failures:

| Failure | Required boundary |
|---------+-------------------|
| Invalid inference or changed premise | Proof checker validates the actual derivation. |
| Required obligation omitted | Artifact checker derives/checks complete coverage from the subject and profile. |
| Source normalized into a different program | Source-to-normalized correspondence or its explicit trusted translation contract. |
| Correct proof paired with another projection/plan | Projection/lowering correspondence over the actual payload. |
| Correct plan paired with wrong served bytes | Qualified build and serving correspondence. |
| Correct declarations paired with a bypassing handler | Installed implementation/effect-boundary evidence. |

Recomputing with a captured reference implementation can reject substituted
public checker results. It is useful accidental-tampering protection in the
trusted process, but it is not an independent oracle for bugs in that same
implementation. Multiple wrappers around the same procedure do not multiply
its logical assurance.

*** Reproducibility, failure modes and host portability

For every finite or independently checked result,
record the original source identity and canonical
semantics, named domain enumeration and whether
it is complete or representative, initial states,
operations, property and quantifier, exploration
strategy/algorithm, stopping reason, budgets,
tool/checker version, trust and external assumptions,
and a replayable counterexample or certified
completion witness as appropriate. A duration,
test count, fingerprint or green exit code
without this scope cannot substitute for a
claim-specific evidence record.

An exceeded case/state/time/memory budget and
interrupted search must return incomplete, never
safe or false by default. Invalid AST/typed
domain, unsupported external operation, unresolved
source correspondence and a legitimate model
counterexample have distinct typed failure
classes. A failed reference interpreter, missing
Node process, unavailable TLC/Recife runner or
different JVM/CLJS integer codec is an
uncompleted check, not evidence that the
application policy was disproved.

JVM-versus-CLJS portability requires actual
independent host executions and comparison of
the same normalized result/failure corpus;
running the JVM half of a shared test file
is not host agreement. Mathematical integer
ranges, nil versus absent, nominal enums,
short-circuit evaluation, Unicode/keyword
encoding and nondeterministic iteration must
be qualified for the chosen host/runtime.
A finite cross-host regression test can
expose divergence and support a selected
portable profile; it is not proof of all
host computations.

The root application accepts a requested
strong assurance profile only when all
*required* judgment types and obligations
are discharged under their stated trust
contracts. A finite diagnostic report can
support testing and conditional decisions
without becoming a runtime execution permit,
a cryptographic certificate, an authenticated
read, or proof of product correctness.


External formal evidence may establish rule-schema soundness or a checker theorem
under named semantics. Its bridge must bind the theorem's statement, definitions,
versions, and assumptions to the exact claim consumed by Gesso. A theorem about
a logical calculus alone does not prove the parser, JVM implementation, database,
or browser adapter.

*** Non-vacuous positive and adversarial checks

Acceptance includes a genuinely closed finite
Boolean/enum model with valid reachable initial
states and a proved safety invariant; a
nearby source mutation producing an actual
replayable counterexample; and an otherwise
legitimate model whose exploration exceeds
a small budget and is therefore *incomplete*.

A complete fixed-formula truth table checks
an accepted tautology and a non-tautology.
The latter's Boolean counterexample must
not be presented as a typed application trace
until correlated atoms have a satisfiable
shared binding. A typed bridge must also
accept a genuine correlated typed witness
and reject inconsistent nominal, optional
or nullable bindings.

The regression corpus independently exercises
guarded frame/assignment cases, finite
multi-operation orderings, abstract induction
cases, propositional rule mutations, and
the exact result of each declared checker
rather than its display label. A deliberately
faulty proof elaborator, an omitted operation
or branch, altered original source and
modified executable/served bytes must fail
the appropriate *distinct* coverage,
kernel, normalization, lowering or host
correspondence boundary. A small finite
checker cannot be credited for observing
external XTDB writers it never enumerated.

A valid conditional theorem can retain
open physical correspondence and checker
meta-soundness premises without being
discarded as useless. A requested stronger
certificate must fail closed if any of
its required premises remains unresolved.
A malicious copied proof report, mismatched
checker version, incomplete partial trace
or all-rejecting test implementation is
not acceptable evidence of completed
certification. Successful local proofs
and exact finite cases remain useful,
precisely scoped evidence for future
composition.

** Distributed admissibility and projection judgments

Intrinsic rule validity is necessary but insufficient at a distributed program
point. A role may evaluate a valid predicate only with the knowledge and provenance
required by that use, and may perform an effect only through its applicable
authority and resource boundaries.

The distributed environment includes the choreography subject, role, program
point, typed value/observation bindings, knowledge, provenance, scope requirements,
capability requirements, and relevant ownership state. It is generated from the
admitted protocol and verified dependencies, not accepted as a caller's list of
facts that happen to make the branch check pass.

*** Exact role-local judgment and conditional knowledge

The checked proposition identifies a role, original Choreo source,
reachable program point, branch and role-local fact, with its nominal
type, observation/snapshot identity, issuer, actor/tenant scope,
basis/progression, resource/generation, and intended use.
This is a *point-indexed* judgment, not a claim that every role can
read every well-typed global fact. The verifier derives the
environment from admitted construction and original producer
paths; an application-supplied list of facts cannot manufacture
the stronger knowledge or authority it happens to need.

A useful decomposition separates (1) definite value availability,
(2) role-local knowledge of the particular observation,
(3) justified provenance and disclosure strength, (4) authority
for an effect, (5) required current basis/transaction compatibility,
and (6) actual resource ownership. A well-typed Boolean rule,
globally declared fact, historical command outcome, or copied
certificate cannot discharge the other judgments by itself.
A symbolic statement “after authorized O the browser knows R.status”
remains conditional until O actually occurs at its trusted boundary.

The definite-facts analysis computes a *must* result: at a join,
retain only facts available with compatible provenance on every
reachable incoming path, unless a checked conditional/disjunctive
representation explicitly retains the per-branch qualifications.
A fact produced on one branch does not become unconditional at
the join. Replacing an authoritative observation with a later
local/asserted/communicated value does not preserve authority
merely because the fact key is spelled the same. Refined presence,
nullable fields and observation generation are tracked together;
a refinement cannot migrate to a different Request, recipient,
basis or client session without a new qualified relation.

*** Required knowledge and provenance constructors

The baseline distributed checker needs explicit cases for:

- entry inputs admitted under the role's entry contract;
- communication through a matching admitted send/receive contract;
- permitted authoritative observation at the declared acquisition/receive point;
- deterministic derivation from already available inputs under its checked or
  explicitly assumed contract;
- conditional branch refinements and conservative joins;
- explicitly named assertions whose weaker provenance remains visible.

Each constructor binds the exact subject and value observation, role, program
point, source path, and any scope/basis requirements. A rule occurrence in global
syntax does not give every participant knowledge of its operands. Receiving a
command ID does not give a browser authority over its recorded outcome.

Consider a browser branch on a private review field. The field can be well typed
and the rule Boolean, but the use must fail when the browser has no permitted
knowledge path. The repair is not automatically to send that private field. The
protocol may keep the branch server-side or disclose only an authorized derived
outcome. A diagnosis must respect that choice and the disclosure policy.

For a conditional observation step, the checked statement is: if the declared
runtime boundary admits observation O covering this subject/field, the successor
role state has the corresponding knowledge with that provenance. It does not
assert that O already exists, that the whole board is current, or that a future
mutation is authorized.

*** Transport, disclosure, and authoritative observations

Participant communication and environment delivery are
different constructors. A sender must actually own/know
each required field and obey its disclosure contract.
A receiver gains knowledge only from the matching admitted
send/receive occurrence, subject, correlation, payload
scope and acceptance path. Optional message fields remain
conditional, and open-envelope extras do not silently
become typed authoritative facts. Duplicate, delayed,
dropped or replayed envelopes cannot create new model
authority or a new trusted observation.

A declared =:await= alternative does not establish that
an environment message arrived. A declared authoritative
reread grants conditional knowledge only after a real
qualified acquisition, with the exact issuer, authenticated
recipient, authorized Request/field subset, observation
identity and basis/progression contract. Opaque basis tokens
are compared only by their declared provider relations,
not as client-controlled timestamps or universally
ordered numeric counters. Source-level provenance metadata
is not an authenticated witness of actual transport delivery.

The private-review example has an important positive repair:
retain the protected predicate server-side, then communicate
only an independently authorized public derived outcome
under its own disclosure and provenance contract. Sending
the private review field merely to make browser typing pass
is not an admissible repair. An asserted Boolean, or a
declassification rule not approved by the public model
policy, cannot create recipient access to protected inputs.

Every branch, receive and environment alternative needed by
the original Choreo, including rejection, no response, unknown
command outcome and explicit recovery, requires a checked
continuation. Unreachable exclusion needs an actual checked
reachability argument; eliminating a negative branch from
the proposed proof ledger is not a proof of branch closure.

*** Authority and transactional use

Authority placement distinguishes a browser role from a trusted server role.
Claims about allowed operation invocation retain the trusted principal/capability
premises. Claims about successful new incorporation additionally retain the
current atomic guard and dependency obligations from Part IV.

A proof of an old observation's authenticity cannot promote it into a current
commit guard. A fact that an actor knew at one point may remain known historically
while no longer establishing current eligibility. Freshness, command binding,
and protected prestate are separate judgments.

For outcomes, the certified model interface uses =:committed=, =:rejected=,
=:already-incorporated=, and =:commit-unknown= with variant-specific payloads and
permitted disclosure. Choreography and presentation separately map those results
to =:accepted=, =:rejected=, and =:unknown=. A total mapping includes replayed
historical success and unknown delivery; a card read cannot substitute for
command-specific attribution.

*** Invocation, incorporation, adoption, and selective Refresh

The client may be authorized to *submit* a Request operation
while still lacking current authority to commit it. The
trusted server re-derives principal/role, Membership,
Organization and Request scope, command identity, and
guarded transaction prestate at the real model/XTDB
boundary. A successful abstract proof about an earlier
observation cannot establish current Claim eligibility.

Submission, authoritative disposition, and browser effect
installation are distinct stages. The committed,
rejected, already-incorporated and commit-unknown outcomes
retain historical command attribution and variant-specific
disclosure. A later Request card read does not prove
that an earlier C42 succeeded, nor may a transport-lost
commit be reported rejected solely because the browser
never received a response. The presentation mapping
also tracks accepted/rejected/unknown independently
from authoritative incorporation.

HumanHelp's selected advisory-only remote lifecycle
contract is a direct compositional test: receiving an
SSE invalidation may increase advisory debt without
adopting remote R or automatically requerying the
board. A creator's correlated local Create S may be
installed without importing that remote R. Collection
Refresh requires its separately issued one-logical-read
permission, current query/scope, physical installation
and coverage evidence. A proof of conditional future
knowledge cannot mint a Refresh permission or erase
uncovered advisory obligations. A narrow affected-
Request command recovery is not a general right to
adopt a new collection cohort.

*** Projection and executable correspondence

A projection claim identifies global and local semantics, observables, internal
steps, participants, message/correlation rules, transport assumptions, and whether
it concerns trace inclusion, safety preservation, behavioral equivalence, or
progress. These relations are not interchangeable.

For example, checking that each local step matches an allowed global step does
not by itself prove that the composed participants can agree on branches, avoid
deadlock, or make progress under failures. The composition theorem must account
for the actual communication and scheduling model. Liveness claims retain their
fairness, availability, timeout, and user-participation premises.

The artifact checker covers every relevant branch, receive, outcome, continuation,
and terminal/resource case. It may exclude unreachable cases only under the
supported reachability judgment. Omitting a troublesome branch from the proposed
obligation list does not remove it from the subject.

*** Structural, concrete-witness, and quantified relations

These are distinct typed projection judgments, not
incremental versions of one green result:

| Judgment | What it can establish | What remains open |
|----------+-----------------------+-------------------|
| Structural source/projection preservation | Every required reachable authored boundary, message, continuation, origin and terminal contract has a compatible local representation. | All generated schedules and dynamic transport behavior. |
| Concrete lockstep witness | One specified global execution matches one projected multi-role schedule under exact observations. | Different schedules and future inputs. |
| Concrete weak witness | One specified pair matches after permitted hidden/stuttering steps, with causal origin and terminal compatibility. | Universal weak simulation, scheduler liveness or deployed physical origin. |
| Trace inclusion/refinement | Every admitted projected observable trace is matched by a permitted global trace under a stated simulation/abstraction and assumptions. | Converse equivalence or progress unless separately proved. |
| Behavioral equivalence | The named relation holds both ways under its explicit assumptions. | Automatically follows from one-way inclusion. |
| Temporal/progress | The chosen leads-to, termination or fairness property under qualified scheduling and recovery premises. | Follows from structural terminal reachability or one witness. |

Global semantic communication may project into separate sender,
queue and receiver steps. Message send, enqueue, deliver, drop,
duplicate and retry are modeled as distinct physical/distributed
actions when the theorem depends on them. An environment event
cannot impersonate a participant receive. Role-local return
is not the same as a completed global outcome; terminal
compatibility must account for all roles, pending messages,
admitted outcomes and resource retirement. Permitted hidden
local steps can commute where independent, but a checker
cannot arbitrarily reorder authoritative commits or pretend
a dropped message was delivered.

For a universal result, obligations quantify over every
admitted local scheduler/transport choice, or use a proved
finite abstraction/simulation covering them. One successful
witness proves neither every schedule safe nor that deadlock
is impossible. Likewise proving each local step has some
global origin does not by itself prove the independently
composed participants agree on branching and termination.
Fairness, network availability, user participation and
crash recovery are explicit temporal assumptions.

The coverage checker derives from actual original
branch/await/message/operation/continuation/terminal
sources, including negative, timeout, retired-generation
and recovery cases required by the claim. Correct
volunteered local proofs cannot certify an omitted
reachable branch or an independently required HumanHelp
Reassign/Cancel/Refresh capability.

Lowering binds the admitted projection to the actual executable plan payload.
Reconstructing expected output under a deterministic lowering contract and checking
payload equality is a useful strategy. It still depends on that lowering
contract; a shared emitter/checker bug requires independent correspondence evidence
or remains an explicit premise.

Optional diagnostic proof sidecars never alter runtime transitions after plan
admission. Required application-policy checks still occur at construction,
installation, and the specified effect boundaries. That admission role does not
turn a sidecar into runtime authorization.

*** Physical projection correspondence and acceptance cases

Source-checked projection, issued ExecutablePlan and actual
served browser bytes have different identities and
enforcement boundaries. The proof binds original Choreo
subject, role-local plan, compatible runtime format,
generated manifest and emitted payload. Equality with a
deterministic recompilation can establish a qualified
emission property, but an emitter/checker common-mode
bug remains an open or independently checked premise.
A copied plan stamp does not prove the installed browser
runs its corresponding program.

Actual installation needs qualified Ring/Biff handler
and trusted route, current session, physical HTMX/DOM
adapter behavior, true transport origins, resource
instance/generation and model writer boundaries.
A browser trace with the same shape/order as an
abstract witness is not an authenticated observation
of real messages, XTDB incorporation or actual terminal
state. Compiler-side proof sidecars do not authorize
additional runtime effects.

The positive/adversarial corpus exercises a valid
entry fact, matched required message field, proper
authoritative observation and permitted server-derived
public decision. Its nearby negatives omit communication,
forge recipient knowledge, assert current authority
from an untrusted Boolean, use private fields in a
browser branch, substitute wrong actor/tenant,
lose optional-field refinement, reuse an old
basis/generation or forge a model outcome.

Projection cases include matched sender/receiver
correlation, legal hidden-local commutations,
concrete weak and lockstep witnesses, rejected
send-origin substitution, overlapping receive
alternatives, omitted terminal/continuation,
unsupported message reordering, duplicate and
retired-message delivery. The report names the
first responsible static, source-coverage, dynamic
witness, physical-host or authoritative
enforcement boundary; a structural check is not
credited with catching physical defects.

An actual valid Claim and a permitted selective
Create/Refresh sequence are independent positive
product controls. Rejecting all Choreographies,
all Claims or all transport input is not an
acceptable proof of admission correctness.
A strong all-schedules result remains open
until its separate simulation/abstraction and
trusted transport assumptions are established.
** Certified construction and certificate admission

Certified construction is a checked semantic boundary, not a declaration with
an approval flag. Its subject is an exact source closure, requested proposition
and assurance profile. The issuer derives required judgments from that subject,
checks every mandatory local one, accounts for permitted open premises, and
issues only the artifact category actually earned. A theorem about a selected
abstract model must never silently become a theorem about deployed Clojure,
XTDB, HTMX or browser execution.

*** Original-source authority and coherent construction

The supported application constructor obtains source-owned schemas, rules,
operations, choreography, views, routes, dependencies and installed artifacts
from the application's selected construction authority. It must not let a
caller-selected digest, proof graph, report, or declared inventory redefine
what source is actually assembled. A standalone proof constructor may accept a
caller-selected abstract source; the resulting claim is explicitly relative to
that abstract source, not the installed application.

Source admission records original declarations and diagnostic locations,
semantic category, the chosen checker/profile semantics, imported interfaces,
trust boundaries, callable bindings and versioned interpretation. It is
necessary to distinguish a source declaration from its normalized term, its
compiled implementation and the physical artifact it may eventually emit.
For opaque Clojure functions, dynamic Var resolution, external effects and
otherwise undiscoverable dependencies, the constructor requires an explicit
implementation/coverage premise or rejects a stronger profile; an asserted
source inventory is never independent proof of its own completeness.

A coherent admission owns a stable source and dependency generation. Internal
checking can use stages, parallel work and caches; however, replacement of a
model writer, route, rule or required proof dependency between acquisition and
issuance must force consistent rechecking or fail closed. There is no legitimate
issued product consisting of an old proof, a new handler and a differently
versioned browser plan merely because their separate stamps look valid.

*** Checked pipeline and obligation ownership

| Stage | Required checked result |
|-------+-------------------------|
| Source admission | Identify selected original source, semantic categories, origin, trust and immutable construction scope; do not execute untrusted proof input. |
| Normalization | Obtain an exact canonical term under a versioned interpretation/preservation contract, keeping meaningful order, absent/nil, nominal and variant distinctions. |
| Elaboration | Resolve names, roles, types and proposed derivations; treat elaborator outputs as candidates rather than proof. |
| Obligation derivation | Independently regenerate all judgments required by the original source, its claim and requested profile. |
| Evidence checking | Check mandatory inference steps with the appropriate small proof kernel or dedicated semantic checker, subject to the named checker trust contract. |
| Dependency closure | Check the justification DAG and the source-derived completeness of required nodes, edges, original producers, claims and assumptions. |
| Premise accounting | Bind every inherited/open premise to exact source, scope, proposition and allowed profile, recognizing only checked discharges. |
| Issuance | Construct the typed admitted product only after its entire mandatory local set and accepted premise policy hold for one coherent generation. |
| Consumption | Verify the product against the consumer's own current subject, dependencies, implementation, installation and policy requirements. |

This table specifies logical responsibility, not a mandatory nine-pass build
algorithm. An implementation can fuse stages or reuse immutable verified
subresults while preserving independent checking and precise failure reasons.

The elaborator cannot choose the list of obligations it wants to pass. The
accepting checker must independently establish the required case/edge set
relative to the *original* source and proposition. A structurally valid DAG
which omits a difficult operation, proof branch, invariant, reachable outcome,
required model policy or source-derived writer does not establish complete
certification. A check confined to a declared abstract catalog must say so:
real physical writer and installed-source completeness need separate inventory
and conformance evidence under Parts IV, VII and XII. Source-derived obligation
coverage and physical coverage are different evidence subjects.

*** Proposition scope, premise discipline and non-vacuity

An admissible claim binds (1) its exact proposition and subject; (2) a complete
set of mandatory local judgments; (3) the checked inference/semantic rules
used to discharge them; (4) required dependency guarantees and their scope;
(5) every open premise permitted by the selected profile; (6) any required
non-vacuous positive product control; and (7) the actual correspondence or
implementation assumption where a stronger realization is claimed.

Checking an implication over no initial states may satisfy a formal formula
without demonstrating a usable application. A checker that rejects every
operation can be safe by vacuity without implementing Claim or Create. Where
the requested claim requires inhabited initialization, an enabled positive
path, all declared operations, or complete outcome variants, those are
separate admission obligations. The profile must not conceal their absence
inside a generic =:certified= label.

A trusted assertion is a visible assumption, not an inference of domain truth.
An abstract rule, exact finite model check, conditional induction or structure
check establishes only its actual scope. In particular, no such check by itself
proves checker meta-soundness, arbitrary Clojure implementation equality,
physical XTDB writer coverage, browser effect correspondence, or that the
served program is the admitted one.

*** No self-certification or report-to-certificate promotion

An issuer does not accept =:valid?=, =:checked-result=, =:verified?=,
=:discharged?=, a copied semantic ID, a developer-authored successful proof
report or an unverified graph as authoritative checking evidence. It reruns
the checker on original source or consumes an authenticated checker-issued
internal result bound to the same source, claim, rule versions, dependency
generation and profile. It independently checks both the derivation and
whether that derivation addresses the full required obligation family.

Readable proof reports, diagnostic sidecars and fingerprints are copyable
descriptions, not admission handles. The checker must distinguish claim,
evidence, inference rule and checked conclusion. An author cannot establish
an assumption solely by declaring its desired guarantee, nor can two
certificates discharge each other's premises through a cyclic proof graph.
A checked induction/fixed-point rule inside a component is different from
cyclic justification; runtime retry loops are different again.

The ordinary public API may be conceptually as small as:

#+begin_src clojure
(certify! {:source application-owned-source
           :requested-profile selected-profile
           :dependencies admitted-dependencies})
#+end_src

This is a desired contract, not the name of a currently guaranteed exported
function. Gesso derives normalized subjects, required dependencies, proofs
and reports mechanically. Application authors write normal Clojure/Gesso
domain rules and construction; they need not maintain a second model in
Recife, Quint or TLA+, copy fingerprints, or hand-build proof DAGs.
Where the truth is application-owned or opaque they may declare an explicit
scoped trusted premise, which remains open unless separately discharged.

*** Issuance classes and exact conditional guarantees

The result is typed, not Boolean. A raw declaration, admitted structural
identity, checked dependency DAG, unadmitted proof report, checked abstract
derivation, conditional certificate, verified application assembly, issued
ExecutablePlan and installed/runtime-authorized effect are different products.
A public consumer requiring one class cannot silently accept a weaker,
same-shaped map or a weaker handle. The failure diagnostic explains the missing
category, proof, source, dependency or enforcement obligation.

A conditional certificate may be fully admitted *as a conditional certificate*
when all mandatory local checks succeed and the requested profile expressly
permits each remaining premise. It cannot promise the same proposition with
those premises deleted. A failed mandatory check, missing required dependency,
stale source or unknown physical gate causes no stronger artifact to be issued.
If a different, weaker category is useful it is returned and named separately,
not smuggled through an API expecting the strong product.

The issuer's report maps each guarantee to subject, inference/checker, proof
scope, assumptions, remaining obligations, evidence owner and applicable
source/dependency generation. Partial diagnostics are useful but are not
partial implementations of an otherwise required full certificate. Untrusted
model contracts and implementation assumptions never become authoritative
model facts by virtue of nominal packaging.

*** Process-local handles and explicit lifecycle

One supported JVM representation is an opaque handle registered by object
identity after all relevant checks succeed. Registry state binds the source,
exact claim/profile, dependencies, premise set and applicable checker/context.
A manually constructed instance, same-shaped record, serialized EDN map or
copied success report cannot create an admitted entry. A previously checked
structural handle is not automatically a proof certificate.

Such a handle is local to its process and trust boundary. It is not a signature,
a durable credential, a proof of arbitrary JVM function behavior, browser
authorization or portable theorem. Weak references can avoid retaining dead
objects; they do not establish revocation, freshness, semantic retirement,
resource cleanup or concurrent-use safety. The issuer and consuming boundary
must enforce synchronization, generation and invalidation rules explicitly.

This protects cooperating Clojure code against accidental category confusion
and specified forged data through supported APIs. It does *not* claim to
defend against hostile reflection, code/Var replacement or equivalent
privileges inside the trusted JVM. A stronger attacker requires a stronger
isolation or independently authenticated boundary.

*** Durable packages, data decoding and rechecking

A persistent package can contain original/normalized source, derivations,
assumption/claim interfaces, dependency references, checker and interpretation
versions, provenance and integrity material. It cannot persist an in-process
handle's registration by printing it. Imported data must first be parsed as
inert, bounded data; arbitrary reader evaluation, unknown tagged readers,
custom executable accessors and uncontrolled graph traversal are excluded
from the trusted checking boundary.

On import, validate the exact supported format and semantic/rule versions,
actual source and claim, all required local judgments, transitive dependencies,
premise scopes, evidence identity and the requesting consumer's policy. Reject
missing, stale or cyclic references, fabricated result fields, invalid steps,
unsupported tags, and incomplete finite checking represented as exhaustive.
Depth, node, CPU/time and other resource budgets are part of a specified
admission contract; exhaustion is not successful certification.

A signature authenticates bytes or a claimed issuer under its key/trust
policy; it does not establish a theorem. Locally cached unsigned data may
be usable after sufficiently complete rechecking. External packages may
need authentication *and* proof checking. Neither a signature nor a
fingerprint makes yesterday's implementation the current installed one.

*** Reuse, currentness and atomic publication

Correctness binds checks to immutable subjects, not reparsing the full proof
DAG after every browser action. Reusable checked subresults are keyed by their
exact proposition, source/semantic identities, dependencies, inference rules,
checker/profile policy, premise bindings and implementation context required
by the particular claim. Reuse and cache invalidation follow their
claim-specific scope; an unchanged abstract interface may reuse its abstract
proof without reusing an unverified changed implementation binding.

Currentness must be supplied by the *consumer's trusted* assembly/deployment
context. An attacker can supply an old source and old graph that agree with each
other; this establishes only their old abstract subject, not currentness.
Changed callable bindings, route/asset manifests, policy or checker versions,
dependency guarantees and revoked profiles cause re-admission or separately
checked compatibility. Runtime instance retirement is not reversed by static
semantic identity.

Hot reload checks one new coherent candidate before switching the admitted
application generation. A failed candidate cannot partially replace live
source/proof/handler/asset generations. The selected deployment policy decides
whether to retain a last compatible generation, refuse new operations or stop
safely. A proof-sidecar may inform build admission but never alter runtime
transitions after plan issuance.

Separate checks occur at the stages that actually own their facts: per-build
semantic claims; per-installation route, served asset and physical source
binding; per-invocation actor, authorization, current Request/Membership and
command context; and per-effect target/resource/transaction authority. A
cached proof that a rule is well typed does not cache Alice's current
authorization, a new XTDB basis or a successfully installed HTMX response.

*** Product and adversarial acceptance boundaries

For HumanHelp Claim, a legitimate checked abstract rule still requires the
real Request model writer, current principal/Membership/Organization, atomic
guard and stable historical command identity with distinct =:committed=,
=:rejected=, =:already-incorporated= and =:commit-unknown= outcomes. HTTP 200,
a current Request card or a copied browser stamp cannot independently establish
historical command incorporation. A checker claiming unconditional Claim
correctness must produce the corresponding actual enforcement evidence,
not point to an abstract rule certificate.

For Create, the creator-local selective installation and remote advisory-only
path remain separate, with one explicitly authorized remote Refresh read and
qualified scoped adoption. An admitted plan does not authorize a second read,
erase newer local state or prove physical target installation by assertion.
The independent positive HumanHelp acceptance requirements of Part VIII
remain gates even when abstract proofs are sound: rejecting all operations
does not implement the application.

Acceptance cases include genuine conditional admission and authorized use,
alongside adversarial copied reports, forged fields, invented handles,
missing operation/invariant/branch edges, changed dependency or model writer,
old/current generation mixing, proof cycles, missing inference steps,
silent assumption discharge, vacuous initial states, partial finite results,
untrusted package readers, exhausted resource budgets, substituted installed
assets, omitted dynamic writers, unsupported profile downgrade and reused
historical command evidence. Each rejected case names the first responsible
semantic, proof, source-closure, admission, deployment or physical gate.
A positive equivalent semantic edit and separately checked compatible
replacement must also be tested to prevent unnecessary false rejection.

** Dependency composition and semantic identity

:END:

Composition checks the actual propositions and scoped dependencies the consumer
requires. Exact semantic identity is the default. A compatible certificate kind
or matching friendly name is insufficient when the subject, rule semantics,
nominal types, premises, or relied-upon commitments differ.

*** Structural closure and required closure

A structural DAG checker establishes that the supplied graph has valid nodes,
matching references, reachable roots, and no unsupported cycles. That result is
useful but does not establish that the supplied graph includes every dependency
required by the source claim.

The artifact-specific constructor therefore derives a required dependency
manifest from the original normalized subject and requested judgment. Admission
compares the supplied/resolved evidence with that required closure. For conditional
induction, the closure includes the catalog, shared initialization, every invariant,
every declared operation, each required preservation/base judgment, and their
actual premises. Omitting a difficult operation's edge must fail even when the
remaining graph is a perfectly valid DAG.

This completeness is source-relative. A proof about a declared catalog still
needs an explicit argument or premise that every relevant physical writer and
initializer is covered. Neither the dependency manifest nor a host-supplied route
inventory proves its own real-world completeness.

The constructor derives routine closure mechanically. Where an opaque implementation
can have dependencies that cannot be discovered, its declared effect/dependency
contract and implementation-conformance premise remain in the required closure.
The system does not pretend it statically enumerated those hidden branches.

*** Acyclic justification, cyclic execution

Proof justification edges are acyclic. Recursive definitions or invariant arguments
can live inside an admitted component using a specified induction, guardedness,
or fixed-point rule; two certificates cannot discharge each other's premises
through an unsupported cycle.

Runtime protocols, retry loops, subscriptions, and state-machine cycles are not
proof-DAG edges. Likewise, an “implements” edge or observed execution sequence
must not be treated as a proof dependency simply because all are displayed in
one report. The assurance graph records edge kinds and the projection that forms
the justification DAG.

| Identity | What it names |
|----------+---------------|
| Semantic interface identity | A normalized exported contract under its interpretation/encoding versions. |
| Claim identity | A proposition about an exact subject, scope, parameters, context, and premises. |
| Evidence identity | The particular derivation/check result and its checking profile/dependencies. |
| Implementation binding | The actual callable, generated executor, provider, or build realization about which correspondence is claimed. |
| Assembly/deployment identity | The coherent installed configuration and selected realization set. |
| Runtime instance identity | The particular invocation, execution, observation, transport, target, or resource lifetime. |

A source-file hash alone does not bind arbitrary Clojure behavior. Closed-over
values, Var resolution, libraries, provider configuration, and permitted effects
may matter. The implementation binding must include or explicitly assume the
relevant dependency stability. A name such as =request/claim= does not capture a
replacement function body.

Fingerprints are identity evidence under canonical encoding and digest assumptions.
They are not truth evidence. The profile states whether equality compares canonical
terms directly or relies on collision resistance. Diagnostic source spans and
comments are retained separately from semantic identity unless a claim explicitly
includes them.

*** Cache validity and controlled evolution

The following reuse distinctions are required:

| Change | Reuse consequence |
|--------+-------------------|
| Formatting or diagnostic prose changes without semantic change | Contract-level derivations may remain reusable; diagnostics must still point to current source. |
| Implementation changes while the public contract is unchanged | Contract proofs may remain valid; evidence about the old implementation cannot be copied to the new binding. |
| Required type, rule, outcome, guard, or assumption changes | Dependent claims are rechecked against the changed interface/premise. |
| Checker/rule semantics change or a profile is withdrawn | Old admission is not automatically accepted under the new policy. |
| Provider/configuration changes outside a qualification envelope | Dependent deployment applicability requires requalification or an explicit supported compatibility argument. |
| One runtime resource retires | That instance loses effect rights; a static semantic certificate does not revive it. |

A concrete HumanHelp example is a Claim outcome interface extended or altered
without updating its choreography. A plan covering only successful acceptance
and rejection cannot claim total settlement handling when the model interface
includes =:already-incorporated= and =:commit-unknown=. The operation may be
well formed; the old dependent mapping fails the required composition judgment.

Similarly, changing =:request/status= from a required closed enum to an optional
value can invalidate a rule's unguarded read. Changing only the Request renderer's
CSS need not invalidate that rule proof. Adding a variant to a closed enum can
invalidate exhaustive matching even when the old variants remain.

Compatibility beyond exact identity is permitted only through a checked bridge
for the consumer's actual required propositions. The bridge preserves closed-world
assumptions, variance where applicable, premise scopes, and protocol meaning.
It does not mean “the new interface has more fields, so it must be safe”.

A cache can miss and reconstruct a valid new artifact. That is different from
accepting a stale dependent. Tests exercise both direct stale composition and
the production cache path, identifying the first actual enforcing boundary.

*** Freshness comes from the trusted consumer

A “current source” argument is meaningful only when the consuming construction
or installation obtains it from the authoritative application source/configuration.
An attacker can always supply yesterday's source and yesterday's matching graph;
checking that pair establishes only yesterday's subject.

Admission binds one immutable closure or detects changes before use. Reload
constructs a new candidate and switches coherently under Part VII. A cached
certificate, new handler, old plan, and mutable route table cannot be mixed on
the strength of independent earlier checks.

** Assumption propagation and genuine discharge
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-certificate-assumption-propagation
:END:

An assumption is identified by its proposition and scope, not just its label.
Its record includes the subject, parameters, semantic context, dependencies,
origin, applicable implementation/enforcement boundary, and policy owner where
appropriate. Two assumptions named “current Membership” for different principals,
Requests, Organizations, or invocations cannot be merged.

For every exported claim, the checker computes the premises actually used by its
selected justification. A shared upstream artifact may carry other claims with
other premises; the report must not either erase required premises or inflate
one claim's dependency list with unrelated assumptions. Alternative evidence paths
can coexist, but the selected path and its premises remain reconstructible.

*** A discharge rule

To replace an open premise P with evidence E, composition must establish that:

1. E has crossed its required checking/admission boundary.
2. E establishes exactly P or a proposition that entails P through an admitted
   rule, under an explicit type-correct, capture-avoiding substitution.
3. Subject, parameters, authority domain, semantic context, scope, and temporal/
   runtime applicability match the required premise.
4. E's own premises and dependency edges are inherited by the dependent claim.
5. The substitution and resulting dependency graph remain acyclic and preserve
   the selected profile's mandatory obligations.

The original premise and discharge record remain visible in the ledger. It may
leave the open-premise set only because this checked replacement establishes it.
Copying the premise into another certificate, changing =:open= to =:discharged=,
accepting a matching assertion, or running a test does not perform that discharge.

The baseline propositional kernel does not implement arbitrary hypothetical
assumption discharge. Certificate-level substitution of accepted evidence is a
separate checked composition judgment. Any richer logical discharge rule needs
its own bound-variable/context semantics and cannot be inferred from the word
“certificate”.

*** Example: implementing an Organization policy

A Claim proof can depend on an opaque contract that the selected Organization
policy callable implements the declared predicate on specified inputs without
undeclared effects relevant to the claim. Admission binds that premise to the
actual callable/realization and its dependency context.

A successful unit test is supporting evidence, not discharge of universal
implementation conformance. A checked implementation argument may replace that
premise for the exact supported subset and binding; its own primitive/library
assumptions remain. Changing the callable invalidates that implementation evidence
even when the public policy interface stays unchanged.

Separately, a real Claim invocation needs current Request, Membership, association,
and policy facts protected at the atomic boundary. A static policy implementation
proof does not establish their values. A runtime check for one invocation can
establish an instance-specific premise, but cannot become a proof for all future
commands or another principal.

*** Premise acceptance and contradictions

Each profile defines which obligations must be checked, which premise classes
may remain, and which runtime boundaries must enforce concrete instances. It
also defines how unsupported/unclassified evidence affects admission. The author
must explicitly opt into a weaker accepted-premise scope where that is permitted;
there is no automatic “trust it because proving failed” path.

Detectable contradictions in the supported logic are reported. General consistency
of arbitrary opaque predicates or business policies is not decidable by this
system. A conditional result under inconsistent assumptions may be vacuous and
cannot establish a usable application. Positive scenarios and independently
specified product capabilities remain separate evidence.

Environmental progress premises retain their precise meaning: eventual service
availability, fair scheduling, sufficient retention, or a user's eventual Refresh
action. Declaring a timeout does not prove that clocks advance, callbacks run,
or the UI survives long enough to observe it. Safety and progress conclusions
must not exchange these assumptions silently.

*** Qualified external assumptions

An external premise such as XTDB atomic guarded incorporation names the exact
behavior relied upon, the provider/adapter/configuration applicability envelope,
and conformance checks capable of exposing relevant violations. It is not one
indivisible assumption that all of XTDB is correct.

A provider version change outside that envelope makes the deployment-specific
application of the premise unestablished until qualified. A discovered assumption
violation invalidates dependent applicability even if the abstract theorem remains
valid. Tests can detect relevant regressions and support continued qualification;
they do not prove the provider's universal implementation semantics.

The same discipline applies to HTMX lifecycle/effect behavior, browser primitives,
authentication, compiler/build behavior, and external services. Reports link a
failed qualification to the affected claims rather than announcing a generic
framework proof failure.

** Binding proof claims to production realization

Certification must meet the construction and installation boundaries of
[[#req-application-assurance-claims]]. A good proof about an uninstalled or
semantically different program does not make the real application correct.

*** Source-owned construction and issued callables

A useful strategy derives semantic subjects, operation catalogs, obligations,
plans, and compatible executors from one source-owned construction. Issued
callables retain their exact admitted plan and implementation binding. Installed
handlers delegate to those callables through the declared trusted input and
response mappings.

This constrains accidental drift and permits identity checks that metadata alone
cannot supply. It does not prove that an arbitrary input-conversion callback
selects the right principal, that a response callback preserves disclosure, or
that the running server installed the checked handler. Those remain distinct
contracts/evidence subjects.

Coverage follows effects. Non-HTTP writers, startup initialization, imports,
administration, migrations, and background work must be represented or retained
as explicit premises of relevant invariant claims. It is not a universal rule
that every writer must correspond to one HTTP route or that every route writes.

One global single-use controller is not a substitute for per-invocation authority
and effect ownership. Independent requests require independent bound contexts;
reentrant or overlapping requests must not borrow each other's transaction,
principal, or result evidence. Narrow one-request fixtures retain their narrow
claim.

*** Transaction and outcome correspondence

A physical incorporation claim binds the authoritative operation, canonical
arguments, stable command identity, principal, protected dependencies, generated
transaction/guard realization, and actual backend outcome. Receipt of a submitted
transaction is not necessarily evidence of successful incorporation; a generic
post-read cannot attribute a change to this command.

Runtime evidence must preserve the distinction between the actual prestate at
the atomic boundary and a candidate pre-read. A proof over the latter applies
to the transaction only through a qualified guard/recomputation correspondence.
A post hoc mismatch report can reveal a defect but cannot undo an already
performed unauthorized write; prevention needs the gate before the effect.

A guarded-transition observation over caller-supplied pre/input/post values is
an abstract relation check. It is not evidence that those values were read from
the actual transaction. Physical evidence capture needs its own source and
implementation binding, including what it does not observe.

A compatible command replay must use the recorded binding and current disclosure
check before applying guards intended only for new incorporation. Certificates
must not encode “Request is open” as a mandatory replay precondition after a
successful Claim. Publication and response failure do not erase a known commit.

*** Browser build, output, and installation

The generated browser bootstrap, manifest, executable plan registry, actual asset
bytes, and serving path are linked under Part VII's qualified build contract.
A receipt must be produced by that construction; arbitrary matching labels or
digests cannot prove that unknown JavaScript embeds or executes the declared plan.

Concrete render evidence binds the actual expanded output, assembly, prepared
affordances, and serialization boundary. Deferred, streamed, cached, and error
responses preserve that checking context. The report states which effect/output
forms are covered and which remain opaque or unsupported.

Browser adoption evidence follows Part VI: admitted observation, permission and
scope, current target/resource identities, final effect admission, and physical
completion. A pre-swap approval or HTTP completion is not completed adoption.
A local card observation cannot discharge an unrelated collection obligation
merely because its provider position is newer.

*** Correspondence has a direction and a scope

Evidence that all observed physical executions matched an abstract transition
is scoped to those observations. A universal refinement claim needs the relevant
simulation/correspondence argument and complete effect/writer coverage under its
premises. Matching a modeled successor from an arbitrary supplied predecessor
is weaker than an observed adjacent physical execution history.

Every correspondence claim states its abstraction map, actual source/target
semantics, observable behavior, treatment of hidden steps/failures, and trust
boundary. Where a source binding or physical observation cannot be established,
that edge stays conditional or unestablished. The report must not infer it from
nearby successful edges.

** HumanHelp compositional certification pilot
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-humanhelp-certification-pilot
:END:

HumanHelp Claim is the first required substantial pilot. It must connect the
production public model contracts to the actual Part VIII user journey. A toy
scalar transition is useful for checker conformance but is not the HumanHelp
Claim model: real ownership involves RequestAssignment, Membership, Organization,
command disposition, and guarded current state.

The pilot consumes the actual Request, assignment, User, Membership, Organization,
and Location interfaces where the chosen operation genuinely depends on them.
It does not invent a =:request/helper-id= field or an Organization policy Boolean
to make a simplified schema look like production. A derived projection can expose
such a fact only through a named mapping with its own binding/coverage contract.

*** Claim obligation manifest

The source-derived manifest must account for at least:

| Subject | Required obligation |
|---------+---------------------|
| Public input and identity | Correct domain Request target, canonical operation/interface and arguments, stable command ID, trusted principal, and separate transport correlation. |
| New-command guard | Current Request eligibility, actor/target-helper policy, Membership, Organization/Location association, assignment constraints, and all relevant policy dependencies. |
| Atomic incorporation | Required Request/assignment changes, command exclusion/result, and declared durable publication intent at the qualified atomic boundary. |
| Replay and disclosure | Compatible prior binding, current permission to disclose, historical outcome semantics, and no second incorporation. |
| Outcome interface | All four model outcomes with exact payload meaning and distinct malformed/unauthorized invocation handling. |
| Choreography and presentation | Complete accepted/rejected/unknown mapping, branch knowledge, correlation, ownership, and command-specific recovery. |
| Projection and lowering | Actual browser/server artifacts correspond to the admitted distributed behavior under the specified relations. |
| Application realization | Real control, generated runtime, installed route, issued executor, required providers, safe rendering, and permitted acquisition. |
| Adoption and recovery | Scoped card result, selective membership where applicable, completed installation, retained unrelated advisory debt, and surviving unknown-command recovery. |
| Assurance scope | Named opaque premises, initialization/writer coverage, backend qualification, and checker/correspondence limits. |

A missing requirement is a failed obligation or an explicitly permitted premise,
never an inferred fact from a namespace name. Presence of every manifest label
alone is not evidence; each resolves to a checked judgment or declared boundary.

*** Three separate pilot conclusions

First, semantic construction must establish the claims supported by its rule,
operation, choreography, projection, and plan judgments. Its proof DAG preserves
all unproved contracts and scope limits.

Second, production evidence must connect the actual implementation and installed
host/browser path to that construction. Source-relative joins and issued callable
identity are useful evidence here, but a supplied route snapshot does not prove
that every physical entrypoint is covered or that the server adopted it.

Third, independent real-browser acceptance must demonstrate Claim through the
real control and persisted Request/assignment truth, including two helpers,
compatible replay, changed Membership/policy, and lost outcome delivery. Tests
must not manufacture the command protocol the control failed to emit.

None of these conclusions substitutes for another. A green browser test does not
close a missing projection obligation. A complete abstract proof does not show
that the button works. A successful Claim pilot does not establish full HumanHelp
capability completion, including the separately unresolved response-flow contract
in Part VIII.

*** Illustrative root report

The report is generated from admitted subjects and observed evidence. This compact
inspection shape illustrates the distinctions, not an input accepted for issuance:

#+begin_src clojure
{:root {:application :humanhelp/example
        :operation :request/claim
        :assembly/ref assembly-ref
        :profile/ref profile-ref}
 :claims [{:claim/ref typed-guard-claim
           :evidence/ref typed-check-evidence
           :premises []}
          {:claim/ref invariant-preservation-claim
           :evidence/ref conditional-proof-evidence
           :premises [matching-machine-premise
                      implementation-contract-premise
                      backend-qualification-premise]}
          {:claim/ref installed-handler-claim
           :evidence/ref installation-evidence
           :scope/ref installed-host-scope}
          {:claim/ref real-browser-claim-scenario
           :evidence/class :tested
           :evidence/ref browser-run-evidence}]
 :obligation-ledger/ref source-obligations
 :dependency-closure/ref checked-closure
 :accepted-premises/ref policy-accepted-premises
 :unresolved-obligations/ref remaining-obligations
 :applicability/ref current-applicability
 :decision/ref profile-admission-decision}
#+end_src

Each reference resolves to a subject-bound record, including evidence class,
checker/implementation identity, scope, and actual result. An empty premises list
for a typing judgment does not eliminate its identified trusted checker semantics.
A root may be admitted conditionally under a profile while still displaying
accepted premises; it cannot hide a mandatory unresolved obligation in that list.

The report answers “what was checked?”, “under which assumptions?”, “what actual
implementation and deployment does this cover?”, and “which required claims are
still unavailable?”. It does not answer whether Alice is authorized now merely
because it names the Claim operation.

** Profiles, diagnostics, and policy review

A certification profile is a versioned executable policy over required claim
kinds, accepted evidence mechanisms, permitted premise classes, implementation
bindings, and applicability. It is evaluated by the actual construction/install
path. Omitting the profile cannot disable the ordinary structural requirements
from Part VII.

The baseline compositional profile requires checked source categories and types,
source-derived obligation/dependency closure, valid derivations for every claim
labelled kernel-checked, and exact binding to the operation/projection/plan
subjects it consumes. It permits explicitly registered opaque implementation and
external-provider premises only where its claim-specific policy says so. It
rejects missing mandatory judgments, unsupported proof forms presented as checked,
and unclassified premise categories.

A stronger conditional-induction requirement additionally requires the base and
all preservation judgments for its declared machine and invariant set. If an
operation's abstract effect is outside that judgment's supported fragment, the
constructor needs a compatible dedicated proof mechanism or a different explicitly
requested claim/profile. It cannot remove that operation from coverage or call an
asserted preservation condition a checked preservation proof. Arbitrary host code
can still implement an abstract operation under an explicit implementation premise.

Profiles can require a proof for some propositions and accept an assertion for
others. This granularity is central to full-language support. A profile is not
one global proof percentage or a switch between “all proved” and unmanaged code.

*** Rejection categories and repair context

A diagnostic carries the affected subject and obligation, source location,
required and supplied facts/types/bindings, first enforcing boundary, downstream
artifacts not admitted, and remaining premises where useful. It uses stable
structured categories; English wording can improve without breaking tests.

| Result category | Example and interpretation |
|-----------------+----------------------------|
| Syntax/type invalid | Ordering RequestStatus numerically, unknown reader, wrong nominal relationship endpoint. |
| Refinement unavailable | Reading an optional value before presence/non-nil conditions establish the needed use. |
| Knowledge/provenance unavailable | A browser branch depends on an undisclosed private observation. |
| Authority/resource obligation unmet | Required trusted placement or current ownership is missing; no proof token supplies it. |
| Derivation invalid | Unknown rule, missing premise, forward reference, wrong subject, or mismatched conclusion. |
| Required coverage missing | A retained branch, operation, invariant, or dependency was omitted from the proposed evidence. |
| Stale/incompatible evidence | Changed interface, callable, checker profile, deployment binding, or scoped premise. |
| Counterexample | A meaningful admitted model permits a behavior violating the declared property; report its scope and source correspondence. |
| Unsupported judgment | Valid-looking source asks for a feature not implemented by the selected checking profile. |
| Incomplete checking | Timeout, resource bound, unavailable dependency, or infrastructure failure prevented the claimed result. |
| Policy not admitted | A required premise/evidence class is disallowed by the selected profile. |

The system should identify the primary obstruction without treating every
unconstructed downstream artifact as another independent bug. Repair suggestions
must not silently change business policy, disclose private facts, weaken proof
requirements, or replace unknown command outcomes with rejection.

*** Malformedness and product intent

A rule accepting ages strictly greater than 18 can be well typed even if the
business intended 18 or older. Comparing an email address with the number 18 is
a type error under the stated nominal interface. Formal checking distinguishes
these cases; it does not infer the intended age policy from prose.

Rules retain human-facing meaning, rationale, correctness notes, and useful
examples alongside source identities. That material is review evidence, not an
implicit theorem. If prose says terminal Requests never reopen while the
transition catalog allows reopening, the proof reasons from the actual catalog.
The mismatch is a policy review finding until the intended restriction becomes
an explicit checked invariant or rule.

Human or heuristic review can identify suspicious asymmetry, permissive guards,
missing disclosure conditions, unreachable product features, or discrepancies
between policy and rationale. Such findings remain separate from proof judgments.
If the owner adopts the finding as a requirement, the semantic source changes
and normal checking runs again.

*** Semantic policy inventory and escapes

The audit surface accounts for correctness-relevant decisions within its declared
inspection scope. A decision depends on an admitted rule, certified interface,
runtime-enforced premise, or explicitly named opaque contract. Observed decision
logic outside those bindings is a detected semantic-policy escape and is reported
as such.

Generated or closed checked code can support a complete correspondence claim
for its specified subset. Silence from an analyzer of arbitrary Clojure cannot
establish that the body has no undiscovered policy branches. Registering the
whole body as an opaque implementation contract is legitimate when the profile
accepts it; it makes that trust boundary explicit without claiming discovery
of every internal condition.

An uninspected rule/writer inventory is unknown, not empty. Counts such as “zero
unresolved detected escapes” must state the inspected scope. A large number of
checked propositions does not imply that the important application invariants
are covered. One rule may simultaneously have a typing judgment, a conditional
proof, a runtime premise, and an opaque implementation dependency; counts are
not automatically a partition of rules or a meaningful percentage of correctness.

** Systematic mutation and conformance corpus
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-certification-mutation-corpus
:END:

The corpus is an executable acceptance specification for the supported boundaries.
It starts from several valid programs, including the real HumanHelp pilot, and
introduces isolated defects at declared injection points. Every case records its
expected classification before running; unexpected acceptance cannot be relabelled
as an application-policy exception afterward.

A case records the baseline/source identity, isolated change, affected claim,
expected checking or runtime boundary, structured diagnostic, and the prohibited
artifact/effect being observed. It also runs the valid control through that same
path. Rejecting every input is not a correct checker.

*** Required mutation families

| Family | Mutations and required distinction |
|--------+------------------------------------|
| Schema/type | Remove a field, change a nominal type, alter optional/nullable, add/remove a closed variant, or change relationship cardinality. A coherent new schema can invalidate an old dependent without being intrinsically malformed. |
| Rule interpretation | Reorder a guarded read, substitute truthiness for presence, coerce nil/absence, change short circuit, or normalize distinct enum types together. |
| Propositional proof | Remove a premise, change an antecedent/goal, use a forward step, inject a rule tag, select the wrong conjunction child, or hide an unused premise. |
| Transition proof | Change a guard/assignment, confuse prestate with poststate, omit a write, treat absent as nil, or claim a frame over an undeclared physical field. |
| Induction | Omit an operation/invariant/base case, give operations inconsistent state contexts, mix partial preservation results without a composition rule, or claim progress from safety alone. |
| Dependency closure | Supply a valid DAG missing a source-required edge, substitute a transitive dependency, create a cycle, or offer yesterday's source as the current installed subject. |
| Assumption discharge | Copy a premise as evidence, change its principal/context/scope, drop replacement-proof premises, or disguise an accepted assertion as discharge. |
| Distributed admissibility | Remove communication, branch on unavailable data, strengthen asserted provenance, overlap unsupported receive contracts, or omit an outcome/recovery branch. |
| Resource ownership | Reuse a retired execution/target/permit or duplicate a linear capability through ordinary propositional reuse. |
| Admission representation | Forge a map/status, construct an unregistered handle instance, mutate serialized evidence, or substitute a copied checker report for original source. |
| Implementation/artifact binding | Swap an issued executor, change callback dependencies, serve different bytes under the same stamp, or install a different handler. |
| Checking completeness | Stop exploration early, omit domain cases, relabel a bounded trace search as exhaustive, or return success after resource exhaustion. |
| Product and policy controls | Preserve acceptance of an intentionally coherent policy change and fail independent capability tests when a promised user action is removed. |

Additional outcome/disclosure cases cover forged browser principal or authority,
unauthorized command-result lookup, incompatible command reuse, and replay after
a successful commit or later lifecycle change. Some enforce at the trusted runtime
boundary, not construction; the case must name the correct boundary.

*** Injection points are part of the test contract

Source mutations operate on fresh unadmitted source. Certificate forgery mutates
serialized candidate data before load/admission. Installed-handler substitution
is tested at host adoption or the declared replacement boundary. Tests do not
assume opaque admitted handles are writable maps.

Direct checker tests may exercise a stale composition and expect dependency
mismatch. The production cache path may instead miss and reconstruct, or reject
the old rule at a different appropriate boundary. Both tests assert that the
prohibited stale artifact is never admitted; they do not force one incidental
error ordering onto every supported path.

Instrumentation observing prohibited construction/effects is independent of the
mutant's claimed result. Missing instrumentation is an incomplete test. Nil is
not evidence that no effect occurred. Post-effect checking cannot receive credit
for preventing an effect it merely detected afterward.

*** Positive and accepted controls

The corpus includes valid boundaries, ordinary source/formatting changes with
unchanged meaning, supported conditional opaque contracts, and coherent but
product-surprising policy changes. It also demonstrates rejected assumptions
under a profile that forbids them. These controls ensure the checker is selective
and the policy boundary is real.

A changed rule may legitimately remain well typed while violating a separately
declared invariant; that case should produce the corresponding counterexample
or failed preservation judgment. It is not automatically a malformed-rule case.

Each completed case is a correct classified rejection, a correct established
judgment, or an intentional accepted control. An unexpected result fails the
case. Infrastructure failure, timeout, and unsupported test mechanisms are
incomplete, not successful mutant kills. Corpus coverage and kill rates are
evidence about the corpus, not a soundness theorem.

*** Conformance must test meanings, not just report shapes

Required semantic cases include optional absent/present-nil values, nominal enum
separation, short-circuit refinement order, integer/codec boundaries, exact reader
footprints, frame presence preservation, simultaneous assignment, proof premise
propagation, and source-derived dependency completeness.

Differential tests compare genuinely independent interpretations where practical.
Both positive and negative mutations must change the intended semantic fact,
rather than merely invalidate a checksum before the interesting boundary is
exercised. A report-shape test remains useful but cannot stand in for these
semantic cases.

The browser/backend conformance suites connect abstract judgments to the qualified
physical boundaries defined in Parts IV, VI, VII, and VIII. They identify tested
versions/configurations and preserve expected injected faults without suppressing
unrelated errors.

** Developer workflow and completion criteria

A normal developer writes production schemas/contracts, ordinary application
logic, named rules, authoritative operations, choreography, views, and assembly
choices. Gesso generates internal proof terms and bookkeeping where mechanically
possible. Developers need not handwrite derivation trees, copy transitive hashes,
manage certificate registries, or learn an external proof assistant to use the
ordinary construction path.

An unsupported proof goal must explain the missing judgment and available honest
choices: provide supported evidence, use an explicit accepted contract, change
the claim/profile, or revise the program. It must not suggest asserting the exact
failed conclusion without showing the resulting trust boundary and policy impact.

The compositional milestone requires:

1. Executable, versioned syntax, interpretation, and judgment contracts for the
   supported rule/proof fragments, with meaningful conformance cases.
2. Source-derived obligation and dependency coverage, including exact declared
   operation/invariant accounting where induction is claimed.
3. Checked derivations and dedicated-checker results bound to their actual subjects,
   with no report-shaped path to certificate issuance.
4. Claim-specific premise propagation and evidence substitution that preserve
   scope, context, dependencies, and applicability.
5. Opaque full-language contracts integrated into the normal assembly path, with
   useful surrounding proofs and no hidden promotion from assertion to theorem.
6. Safe semantic reuse and separate invalidation of changed implementation,
   checker, provider, asset, and deployment bindings.
7. Production enforcement of the selected profile and applicable realization
   checks at their actual installation/invocation/effect boundaries.
8. A complete HumanHelp Claim pilot connecting semantic inheritance, production
   realization, and independent real-browser evidence in their proper scopes.
9. A mutation corpus with valid controls, stable diagnostics, and explicit
   unsupported/incomplete classifications.
10. A generated root report that exposes which claims are established, conditional,
    tested, inapplicable, or still unavailable, and why.

The milestone does not require a proof of all Clojure, XTDB, HTMX, the JVM, or the
browser. It requires that claims relying on those components state the exact
assumptions, qualification, and correspondence evidence they use. External formal
soundness and broader deployment results are separately named goals, not implied
by the word “certified”.

** Compositional certification invariant

A certified Gesso construction exports only the propositions its checked evidence
and explicit premises justify. Its source, normalized subject, semantic interfaces,
obligations, dependencies, implementation bindings, and applicability remain
connected. Shape, names, fingerprints, successful tests, and copyable reports
cannot silently replace the required checking boundary.

The full host language remains available. The closed reasoning fragments have
precise executable meanings; opaque logic enters through named contracts whose
assumptions remain visible. Intrinsic typing, distributed admissibility, conditional
induction, implementation correspondence, runtime authority, and product intent
are distinct judgments that compose without erasing those distinctions.

The proof kernel checks its small derivation language. Dedicated checkers establish
their stated judgments. Source coverage prevents omitted obligations; correspondence
binds checked subjects to actual plans and effects; runtime boundaries establish
current instance facts. None alone is the entire assurance argument.

HumanHelp demonstrates that this composition reaches a real application. Its
Claim certificate cannot manufacture current authorization, its browser success
cannot manufacture a proof, and its current card cannot manufacture command
attribution. The root report makes the valid connections and the remaining limits
explicit enough for both developers and downstream checking to rely on them.

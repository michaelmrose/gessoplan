# R5.1 Part I — 01.09 preservation audit: admission and recovery

Reference: canonical R4 on `main`. Candidate: R5.1, section 01.09.
Status: source-level preservation review. No claim of executable Gesso
enforcement or formal proof is established by this document revision.

## R4 commitments preserved

- Both original subsections remain: earliest legitimate rejection and
  runtime-only conditions with required recovery.
- The three Claim defects retain their distinct detection points:
  source-level missing plan, rendered Reassign with missing helper argument,
  and Membership withdrawal after a valid render.
- Current authorization remains a runtime/model responsibility, not a
  speculative fact that must be static forever.
- The seat-reservation example continues to distinguish advisory observation,
  atomic current guards, command identity/replay, authoritative rejection,
  uncertain outcome, and command-specific reconciliation.
- Participant disconnection does not itself prove permanent semantic loss;
  application policy owns leases, withdrawal and valid recovery successors.
- Environmental progress premises remain conditional, and local retirement
  does not fabricate a committed/rejected command result.

## Focused R5.1 improvements

1. **Effective render context.** A fragment whose action semantics depend on
   ancestor HTMX/form attributes, target selection or assembly generation
   cannot be certified from isolated markup. Admitted context is checked before
   effective managed output is exposed, or the claim stays unsupported and
   the required gate rejects. Deferred, cached and streamed output do not
   silently bypass the chosen boundary. This is consistent with Part VII.
2. **Source-bound construction versus toy fixtures.** The short
   `broken-assembly` map illustrates a controlled negative test, not a new
   application-authoring registry or self-authenticating artifact manifest.
   Real construction uses source-owned meanings, actual generated artifacts
   and separately established server installation.
3. **Unsupported is not contradicted or accepted.** A checker unable to
   establish a property cannot claim rejection *for that property* or claim
   admission by omission; policy determines explicitly required checking,
   named acceptable premises and their runtime enforcement.
4. **Boundary-sensitive negative evidence.** Conformance must hit the
   intended first enforcing boundary, prevent the forbidden effect, and
   retain a positive valid execution. Failure of late output validation
   cannot retroactively turn an already incorporated command into a
   rejection.
5. **Unknown is not a fresh retry.** Static operation coverage includes
   both typed rejection/reacquisition and the independent command-specific
   reconciliation path after uncertain incorporation.

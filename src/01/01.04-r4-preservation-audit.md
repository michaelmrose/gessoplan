# R5.1 Part I — section 01.04 R4 preservation audit

**Baseline:** canonical R4 on `main`. **Candidate:** R5.1 section 01.04.
**Scope:** assurance-class definitions and admission treatment of unresolved
proof obligations. This is a normative-document review, not a formal proof.

## R4 commitments preserved

- Mechanically checked semantic properties, runtime enforcement, trusted
  assumptions, assembly verification, conditional liveness, and tested evidence
  remain distinct. No class is reduced to a universal confidence score.
- The checkout example still exercises all six assurance families and rejects
  authentication by certificate, provider truth by browser test, unconditional
  progress by assembly closure, and browser proof by provider trust.
- Nominal semantic categories, validated representations, and checked
  propositions retain their different admission meanings.
- R4's acceptance scope and evidence-origin boundaries survive; R5 makes the
  logical subject, premises, coverage, applicability, and status more explicit.
- R5's stronger separation of attempted proof from completed liveness evidence
  is retained. Naming fairness or availability assumptions does not prove
  progress.

## Focused clarification in R5.1

1. Distinguish the **claim disposition**, **evidence mechanism**, **individual
   check result**, and **admission/release decision**. Part XI's gate result
   `:passed` does not turn a completed test into a theorem.
2. The sample checkout authentication row used to say `:enforcement-bound`
   and supply a `:runtime-enforced` evidence object merely by naming a handler.
   It now records an open physical enforcement obligation and requires actual
   installed-boundary correspondence.
3. The sample browser row now limits `:established` to its exercised scenarios,
   with separate `:tested` evidence. A named test suite without a completed,
   applicable run is not established evidence.
4. Unsupported reasoning or inconclusive exploration can be accepted only
   under an expressly scoped, separately identified premise contract, not by
   changing the failed check's status to trust. Mandatory gates remain mandatory.

## Continuation

Sections 01.05–01.10 require individual R4 review. Part VII owns actual
production-host adoption, Part IX owns claim dependency/discharge, and Part XI
owns required release-gate results; this revision does not duplicate them.
No R4 requirement has been intentionally deleted in 01.04. The twelve-part
semantic preservation audit is not yet complete.

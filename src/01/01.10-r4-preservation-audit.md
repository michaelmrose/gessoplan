# R5.1 Part I — 01.10 Fail Closed and Diagnostics: R4 preservation audit

Baseline: R4 `main`; candidate: R5.1 `01.10`.
Scope: failure containment, production checks, authoring, structured errors,
inspection, and the scope of justified proof investments.
This is a normative-document review, not evidence of executable compliance.

## R4 commitments preserved and strengthened

- All six original subjects survive: semantic fail-closed behavior; actual
  production checks; one canonical developer authoring surface; stable
  structured errors; inspectable proof/check state; and proof complexity
  justified by useful correctness requirements.
- A declared managed operation cannot silently become an ordinary HTMX
  mutation when basis, plan, artifact binding, or command envelope is absent.
  Intentionally ordinary HTMX remains allowed under its own contract.
- Actual production tests must check the intended enforcing boundary, with
  a positive valid fixture so reject-all implementations do not pass.
- Ordinary Clojure remains available behind admitted contracts. Authors do
  not manually construct wrappers, certificates, or transitive proof graphs.
- Error explanations retain owner/subject, required versus observed facts,
  assurance class, legal repair paths, and explicit uncertainty/disclosure.
- Inspection remains diagnostic, not an additional runtime authority source.
- R4's preference to defer formal machinery that provides only theoretical
  elegance is explicitly retained, without losing R5's stronger recognition
  that checker soundness is a concrete guarantee worth establishing.

## Focused R5.1 corrections

1. Fail-closed semantics are *effect-relative*: before an effect, block it;
   after possible submission or incorporation, do not invent rollback or
   rejection. Retain durable command obligations and applicable unknown
   reconciliation. A formerly serving assembly may remain only while its
   required premises remain valid.
2. Production-enforced claims require tests reaching the *specified first
   enforcing boundary* and confirming the forbidden effect is absent, where
   the gate promises prevention. Rejection by some unrelated wrapper cannot
   stand in for this proof of installed enforcement.
3. Errors must explain whether the protected effect is known absent, known
   incorporated, or uncertain, and name the applicable observed subject and
   time/assembly/profile scope. An after-the-fact read is not a causal
   attribution proof.
4. Inspection distinguishes source-derived closure, issued artifacts,
   installed/served coverage, and per-invocation observations. It must not
   reexecute effectful user code merely to generate diagnostics, or allow
   submitted/copyable reports to acquire authority.
5. Reinstated a default of deferring complexity with no identifiable claim,
   accepted-program, correspondence, or implementation-soundness benefit.

## Follow-up and limits

Part VII owns actual production host/render coverage and effectful inspection
constraints. Parts IV and VI own command outcome and physical presentation
recovery. Part IX owns evidence issuance and premises; Part XI owns lifecycle
activation and release failure. The final **whole-Part-I review** remains
necessary after this last individual section; the twelve-part R5.1 plan has
not yet been promoted to an accepted canonical R5 design.

## R4 preservation and R5 architectural gains

R4 Part IV's `Command disposition and participant knowledge are
distinct` and `Retry and local retirement after an unknown outcome`
provided an extensive Alice/C42 lost-HTTP-response example. Its core
distinction survives: (1) authoritative domain state, (2) bound
semantic command disposition, and (3) currently admissible
participant evidence/knowledge are separate coordinates.
A timeout cannot turn an incorporated command into rejection;
a current Request read can repair display but cannot by itself
attribute a command; retained command evidence allows a
permitted compatible replay without repeating effects.

R5 usefully consolidated that example into a concise
model-resolver/reconciliation policy and strengthened the explicit
recovery-owner, basis and browser resource ownership obligations.
This section retains those improvements while restoring specific
R4 counterexamples and correcting an obsolete model representation.

## Corrected C42 Claim and RequestAssignment semantics

The original R5 04.08 prose said the transition "assigns Request R"
and evaluated "finding Alice as helper" from a Request read. Part VIII
instead states that production RequestAssignment owns active primary
assignment, collaborator distinction and history; Request owns its
lifecycle state. The worked scenario now says C42 atomically
establishes Request =:claimed=, Alice's active primary
RequestAssignment, the command result and the durable work of its
explicitly selected profile. An authorized current projection
derives assignment from RequestAssignment, not a nullable Request
helper field. A later Reassign may yield Bob as primary without
invalidating C42's historical incorporation.

The illustration explicitly distinguishes C42 (semantic command),
R (domain Request), E1/E2 (browser execution), T1 (transport request),
the durable worker identity and actual current resource owners.
Its narrative speaks omnisciently about the commit, while E1
genuinely lacks evidence because the response disappeared.

## Missing causal/authorization join repaired

A record that proves C42 incorporated need not prove *which*
physical attempt did so; a query of current RequestAssignment
cannot prove C42 incorporated. Two qualified observations at
different bases do not automatically establish a common
snapshot or ordering. Stale/wrong-tenant/expired records,
unauthorized disclosure and temporarily unavailable reads
do not justify a definitive negative conclusion.

The schematic `request/resolve-claim!` illustrates an authorized
public model operation; it is not asserted to exist in current
Gesso. Its intended contract binds the full canonical command
intent and actual authenticated actor, performs appropriately
scoped authoritative acquisition, and applies current disclosure
policy before emitting any command-specific result. The
command's bytes or a public `:request-id` are not credentials.
A private command existence fact may have to remain
indistinguishable from unavailable evidence.

The example now distinguishes two recovery paths:
*command-specific historical reconciliation* and *current*
Request/RequestAssignment acquisition. Their outcomes may
be used together under qualified bases and adoption policy,
but are not interchangeable. A local author result may
settle admitted presentation; an unrelated remote change
requires its independently defined selective adoption.
Live notification, durable work recording, provider delivery
and actual browser installation are not synonyms.

## Recovery ownership and physical lifecycle

The R4 retirement rule is preserved and joined with the
stronger R5/Part VI owner-relative boundary: E1 retirement
revokes E1's timers, private callbacks, provisional owner,
and DOM installation rights. An admitted E2 or current
coordinator may receive surviving C42 knowledge through
its own mapping; merely knowing C42 does not transfer
E1's retired capability. Old T1 callbacks cannot roll
back a newer E2 installation or invent business rejection.

A read-only resolver is not an execution plan. For a promised
recovery beyond E1's lifecycle, the plan declares a continuing
authorized owner, retention of exact binding, scheduling/backoff,
evidence sources, permissible retry semantics, terminal
unavailable/unknown handling and optional manual path. Safety
holds even if recovery makes no progress. Liveness additionally
requires explicit eventual availability, retention, fair
scheduling, sufficient basis and continued authority premises.

## Positive and adversarial acceptance

Eleven documentary cases include:
- Lost T1 response after complete C42 incorporation.
- Historical C42 success followed by Bob's Reassign.
- Current primary ownership without command attribution.
- Two concurrent compatible physical attempts.
- Stale, expired or wrong-tenant negative command lookup.
- Successful E2 reconciliation without E1 revival.
- Stale E1 callback after a newer installation.
- Different current-state and historical disclosure rights.
- Outstanding durable notifier work after C42 resolution.
- Exhausted recovery scheduling with still-unknown outcome.
- Same logical authority with insufficient read progression
  on one web node.

Positive cases remain mandatory: a valid compatible C42 record
must be useful and a current adopted Request/RequestAssignment
projection must repair display. Reject-all gates are not an
acceptable substitute.

## Actual Gesso implementation evidence inspected

At `michaelmrose/gesso` `application-assurance` v1622:

- `gesso.model.command-resolution` adds one command-result
  create operation to the same normalized model transaction.
  `reconcile` makes exact closed-binding and trusted
  disclosure decisions; missing record returns
  `:unresolved`, not a permission to retry.
  It does not itself implement authoritative lookup,
  terminal rejection, a durable recovery scheduler,
  expiry fencing or full business authorization.
- `gesso.model.resolved-operation` builds an experimental
  trusted submit/reconcile wrapper. It checks for an
  existing record before construction, interprets a
  qualified positive submission result and rereads
  authoritatively on ambiguous outcomes; absent
  readback remains unresolved. It does not implement
  an automatic browser recovery owner.
- `gesso.model.resolution-observation` collects
  *controlled-database* physical diagnostic witnesses.
  Its test harness does not reconcile real production
  commands or prove unrestricted database isolation.
- Inspected `test/gesso/model/resolved_operation_test.clj`
  for lost acknowledgment and actual XTDB readback
  fixtures, as well as bounded command-resolution
  coverage. These tests were inspected, **not executed**.
- R5.1 Part VI `req-owner-relative-event-admission`
  and Part VIII RequestAssignment semantics were
  consulted as cross-part design requirements, not
  as claims that a particular current browser/production
  model integration has passed acceptance.

Current code's narrower `:incorporated`,
`:unresolved`, `:replay` etc. vocabulary does
not automatically implement the final public four
model outcomes or prove complete production enforcement.

## R4 material considered and preserved

The R4 Part IV `Relationship between core, FX, and choreography`
section (beginning near R4 line 336) made four central commitments:

1. A public model operation, an FX implementation, and its distributed
   choreography are related but not the same callable or type. A
   model's business semantics remains usable directly from a REPL,
   batch job, test or server endpoint under identical authority rules.
2. The stable operation name occurs across model core, UI capability,
   Choreo, server operation registry and transport, but *name
   equality does not establish installed interface correspondence*.
3. Higher coordination cannot reach into private FX staging or
   rewrite the domain's outcomes. Internal preparation and physical
   transaction steps are not new distributed semantic operations.
4. R4's illustrative Request Claim supplies the Request and stable
   command ID while obtaining the helper principal from a trusted
   runtime. A compatible replay must resolve before treating a
   now-claimed Request as a new-command rejection; lost publication
   cannot retroactively reject an already incorporated Claim.

The concise R5 candidate preserved the basic layered direction,
trust binding, closed input schema, explicit replay policy, and
nonbinding inspection-map notation. The review restored precision
about alternative caller contexts, actual callable/guard binding,
and the fact that several internal FX callbacks are not several
model commands. This does not reinstate obsolete R4 `biff/run-state-machine`
pseudocode as a universal FX requirement.

## Found mismatch: actual HumanHelp primary-assignment semantics

The R5 04.02 worked inspection contract asserted
`:request/helper-assigned`. That looked like a Request-local helper
field, whereas the Part VIII production-model contract explicitly
uses **RequestAssignment** to represent active primary ownership,
collaborators, lifecycle history and uniqueness. The R5.1 example
now declares `:request/claimed`, a protected active primary
RequestAssignment, and preservation of primary uniqueness.
Collaborator membership is not itself evidence of a second
primary owner. These are **schematic labels**, not a new competing
model schema or an assertion that they are implemented runtime
keywords.

The adjoining later Part IV 04.05 example still contains
`:request/helper` and will require a separate correction during
its own detailed section review. This focused 04.02 revision
deliberately does not silently edit 04.05.

## Found ambiguity: selected publication guarantee

The earlier example stated notification intent was recorded
only *if required* but unconditionally claimed
`:publication/intent-recorded`. This is reconciled by
explicitly choosing the **durable work-recorded** variant
for the worked Claim contract. The named contract must
actually record the authoritative command result, protected
Request/RequestAssignment transition and durable notification
intent together. An authored best-effort notification profile
remains legal, but its contract cannot inherit a stronger
durable-work or delivered-notification claim.

## Positive and adversarial acceptance

Six paired construction judgments now cover:

- eligible new Claim with proper RequestAssignment and retained work;
- valid collaborator presence without conflating primary ownership;
- forged client helper identity against trusted principal binding;
- compatible historical replay after later reassignment;
- an installed FX/handler with a matching name but missing
  assignment or publication enforcement; and
- a separately admitted best-effort protocol that does not
  falsely claim a durable publication result.

The operation name is not a proof of physical installation,
and an inspection map has no authority. Disambiguated command
history does not promise current state after later lifecycle
changes. None of these cases requires changing the application
semantics between single-node and multi-node deployments.

## Actual Gesso source inspected

At `michaelmrose/gesso`, branch `application-assurance` v1622:

- `gesso.model.core` derives ordinary descriptor-backed persistence,
  Graph and simple FX operations; complex domain workflows remain
  application-authored and share `gesso.model.command`/ `gesso.model.tx`.
- `gesso.model.command` describes persistence and optimistic version
  guards without independently supplying authorization semantics.
  `gesso.model.tx` composes actual commands, guards, assertions and
  Live change descriptions; it does not own domain policy.
- `gesso.model.resolved-operation` provides an experimental
  application-facing operation with trusted read, disclosure and
  construction callbacks and optional covered table/target/effect
  contracts; its own documented interfaces do not grant universal
  backend or writer certification by themselves.
- `gesso.model.certified-operation` ties a checked abstract
  derivation to a qualified operation and a trusted registered
  executor. Its registration is proof-linked but explicitly
  subject to additional physical/deployment premises.
- `gesso.assurance.admission` and `gesso.assurance.dependency`
  supply structural admission/closure, not business proof
  or physically installed endpoint assurance.
- `gesso.assurance.proof.operation-interface-provenance`
  emits source-relative diagnostic lineage, not a production
  execution credential. Its mutation tests reject copied or
  altered manifests but do not prove general physical
  correspondence.
- `test/gesso/model/core_test.clj`, `test/gesso/model/command_test.clj`,
  `test/gesso/model/tx_test.clj`, and
  `test/gesso/assurance/proof/operation_interface_provenance_mutation_test.clj`
  were inspected for pertinent fixtures and counterexample
  coverage. No tests were executed during this documentation revision.

These details inform target requirements; they are not a claim
that all intended public Claim/RequestAssignment enforcement,
proof correspondence, and physical notification conditions
have already been implemented.

## R4 obligations preserved

R4 Part IV establishes the source-owned path from named business rules,
operation requirements, and transition effects through commit-time protection
to concrete authoritative mechanisms. Earlier evaluation does not substitute
for atomic protection of dependencies that can change before incorporation,
and incidental full-language business-policy branches cannot silently obtain
checked status. R4's identity, command, progression and advisory examples
provide specific adversarial histories that must not collapse into a broad
green "atomic transaction" or "refresh succeeded" label.

R4 Part IX's `Mechanical rejection, formal counterexample, and intent
review` distinguishes rejected construction, an admitted-but-failing
modeled invariant, and an advisory observation about surprising policy.
Its `Assurance coverage is proposition coverage, not line coverage`
requires visible inspected-scope boundaries. A source inventory with no
detected unclassified writers is not necessarily a complete physical
writer inventory; a large count of checked propositions is not a measure
of undiscovered business policy.

R4 Part IX's mutation-corpus requirements also preserve:
- positive accepted controls at the same actual admission/runtime boundary;
- named injection points and independent observation of prohibited effects;
- exact finite search scope, exhaustion and counterexamples without
  promoting bounded success to universal proof;
- semantic ownership of missing rules, declared required capabilities,
  assumption discharge and invalidation after source/backend change;
- independent physical binding and end-to-end verification rather than
  accepting report-shaped maps as authority.

The accepted R5.1 Part VIII requires non-vacuous HumanHelp
Create/Claim/Unclaim/Reassign and board behaviors, concrete paired
backend and browser oracles, and visible creator S under excluding
filters while remote R remains advisory. Its selected one-Refresh
grant is retained here as a positive and adverse acceptance subject.

## Stronger R5 design retained

R5's preferred eight named invariant families remain intact. It
separates derivation, construction, runtime enforcement, testing,
assumption and actual physical observation. A trusted or opaque
conformance premise is not a theorem, and a framework proof of a
fragment is not the physical application or DOM claim. The original
Part IV conformance matrix is preserved; the new controls complement
it rather than replacing it with a second certification language.

Part IX owns general certificate and proof-kernel machinery. Part X
owns full physical/refinement correspondence. Part IV states the
operation-family properties that must be evidenced under those
mechanisms, without prescribing one proof backend or requiring
application authors to write Quint/TLA/Recife.

## Focused normative changes

1. Make every assurance status proposition- and source-scoped; no
   report-map promotion or unexamined writer/handler substitution.
2. Add explicit coverage and non-vacuity requirements for required
   operations and reachable positive traces.
3. Separate malformed construction, model counterexample, unsupported
   premise and advisory intent review.
4. Require each conformance case to identify baseline, mutation,
   enforcement boundary, expected disposition and independent oracle.
5. Add twelve paired accepted/adverse controls spanning Claim
   concurrency, replay/disclosure, actual submission, command
   uncertainty, external provider effects, scoped reads, subscription
   gaps, Refresh grants, DOM adoption, HumanHelp selection, opaque
   code and missing capabilities.
6. Distinguish finite representative enumeration from inductive
   preservation and external runtime correspondence.
7. Strengthen per-operation-family completion report to include
   source versions, required capabilities, installed physical scope,
   open premises and invalidation of dependent claims.

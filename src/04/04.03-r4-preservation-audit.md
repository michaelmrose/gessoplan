# R5.1 Part IV — section 04.03 R4 preservation and Gesso correspondence

**Section:** `r5-source/04/04.03-model-outcomes-and-invocation.org`.
**Disposition:** third substantively reviewed Part IV section;
Part IV and the twelve-part R5.1 plan remain drafts. Normative
Org text specifies finished-Gesso behavior, not current feature status.

## R4 preservation: outcomes, attempts, and participants

R4 Part IV's `Relationship between core, FX, and choreography`
worked Claim contract gave separate committed, rejected,
already-incorporated, and commit-unknown model results. R4's
`Authoritative transition contract` made a confirmed failed
physical attempt different from unknown submission and from
post-commit notification failure. Its command-identity material
kept durable command C42 distinct from E1/E2 and transport
attempts T1/T2, including possible replacement hosting.

R5's shorter section preserved the four model variants, closed
payloads, typed invocation errors, historical/current reads,
presentation mapping and five logical invocation phases. It did
not sufficiently qualify which evidence identifies a *specific
physical attempt* versus merely proving command incorporation
from an authoritative record. It also left too much room to
treat an unsuccessful attempt as definitively rejected.

The revision keeps the original architectural improvement,
without reinstating R4's older complete FX pipeline as a
mandatory concrete API. The four public outcome variants
remain exactly the same.

## Focused normative refinements

- `:committed` requires evidence correlated to the admitted
  invocation/submission, under the qualified physical adapter
  contract; a successful current-state lookup or copied result
  does not establish attribution.
- `:already-incorporated` can recover a compatible retained
  historical result after an ambiguous prior submission, without
  claiming which physical attempt actually won. "Already"
  describes the recovered incorporation/no-new-incorporation
  path, not a universal timestamp proof that the command
  necessarily predated a particular attempt.
- `:rejected` is scoped to the declared authoritative
  rejection/closure policy. Precheck denial, XTDB assertion
  abort and missing command record do not by themselves
  close other concurrent/pending attempts.
- `:commit-unknown` preserves indeterminacy, while
  typed invocation failures and unavailable disclosure
  remain separate failure categories.
- Command result, current RequestAssignment projection,
  physical publication, and current-browser installation
  are separately qualified and admitted.
- Cross-host retry has identical semantic command and
  invocation rules, even though different hosts can have
  different read visibility until qualified progression.

Ten acceptance cases exercise positive and adversarial
pairings: affirmative physical acknowledgment; lost
acknowledgment recovered through record read; conflicting
physical attempts; malformed input; unclosed rejection;
failed post-commit publication; newer RequestAssignment
state; nondisclosable record; retired browser execution;
and cross-host retry with unchanged immutable binding.

## Implementation correspondence inspected

At Gesso `application-assurance` v1622:

- `gesso.model.resolved-operation/physical-result`
  classifies an acknowledged transaction as physical
  `:committed`, recognized post-commit Live failure as
  physical `:committed`, explicit XTDB assertion failure
  as physical `:rejected`, and other cases as
  `:indeterminate`. These classify *physical attempts*,
  not a universal proof of terminal business rejection.
- The `build-operation` wrapper returns an invocation
  `:incorporated` result on positive acknowledgement.
  Otherwise it invokes a fresh authoritative
  command-record reconciliation. Its local status symbols
  are implementation vocabulary, not automatic equality
  with R5.1's proposed model outcomes or browser settlements.
- `gesso.model.command-resolution/reconcile` returns
  `:unresolved` for no record, `:unavailable` when
  disclosure is denied, `:incompatible` for a mismatched
  retained binding, and `:replay` only for a disclosed
  compatible record. It is a pure helper, not an authenticated
  lookup or an executable recovery scheduler.
- `gesso.model.resolution-evidence` and
  `gesso.model.resolution-observation` can compare bounded
  prepared-plan/physical document observations under
  explicitly named test and backend premises; they do
  not infer production authorization or universal
  linearizability.
- `gesso.model.resolution-event-observation` observes
  a controlled physical submission with correlation
  and a recognized outcome source, but its commentary
  correctly excludes general production attestation.
- Inspected tests in `test/gesso/model/resolved_operation_test.clj`,
  `test/gesso/model/command_resolution_test.clj`,
  and `test/gesso/model/resolution_event_observation_test.clj`
  cover ambiguity, replay, disclosure, physical assertion
  rejection, no-event/no-witness and post-commit delivery
  classifications. They were inspected, **not executed**.

These are feasibility and design lessons, not evidence
that every required physical correspondence or universal
semantic theorem is already established by current code.

## Cross-part requirements and R4 comparison disposition

- **04.04–04.05:** enforce trusted Request,
  RequestAssignment, Membership, Organization, command
  record and durable work at one qualified atomic boundary.
  The old `:request/helper` illustration still needs
  attention in 04.05.
- **04.06–04.08:** specify immutable canonical command
  binding, retention, concurrent resolve-or-incorporate,
  terminal rejection semantics and recovery ownership
  without substituting source-level names for actual evidence.
- **04.09–04.10:** separate external provider work,
  physical effects, publication debt and postcommit
  delivery from command incorporation.
- **04.11–04.16:** preserve true scoped XTDB read
  evidence, sufficient progression, current disclosure,
  authorization and browser adoption without pretending
  a shared database makes all hosts instantly current.
- **Parts V–VIII:** maintain presentation ownership
  and HumanHelp-specific explicit Refresh/RequestAssignment
  behavior; historical Claim success is not a current
  primary helper guarantee.
- **Parts IX–XI:** distinguish generated proof
  propositions, bounded execution witnesses, trusted
  physical implementation, actual release/production
  coverage and remaining environmental premises.
- **Parts X/XII:** multi-node is realization of the
  same model/Choreo semantics; routing strategy is
  deliberately left out of the public outcome contract.

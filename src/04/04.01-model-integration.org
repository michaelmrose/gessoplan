* Part IV — Authoritative operations and progression
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: part-04-authoritative-operations-and-progression
:PART_REVISION: 04-20261107
:END:

This part defines the public model boundary, authoritative construction and
execution, command disposition, durable propagation, and scoped acquisition of
authoritative state. It connects the assurance classifications of Part I, the
identity and evidence model of Part II, and the operation contracts of Part III.
Browser coordination follows in Part VI; whole-application closure follows in
Part VII.

The central requirement is that the operation which an application admits, the
transition which it submits, and the outcome which it reports describe the same
semantic act. A well-typed declaration alone cannot establish that relationship.
Neither can a successful HTTP response, a copied certificate, a local callback
return, or an independently observed database state.

Ordinary full-language Clojure remains available under the
[[#req-full-language-construction][checked construction contract]]. Gesso proves
what its admitted semantics and evidence support, identifies what remains an
assertion or environmental premise, and preserves those distinctions through
execution. An opaque implementation is not silently treated as proved, and a
restricted proof mechanism does not define the limits of the application
language.

The declarations and API names below are illustrative specifications. They do
not claim that the exact displayed functions are already implemented. Inspection
maps explain admitted objects; constructing a similar map does not create an
execution capability or authoritative evidence. Example identifiers denote
separately bound fixture values unless a literal is supplied.

** Model integration
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-model-public-boundary
:END:

Application models own domain truth. HumanHelp supplies the running examples,
but Membership, Organization policy, and Request lifecycle rules remain
HumanHelp semantics rather than special cases in Gesso.

A model may organize its implementation as follows:

| Namespace | Responsibility |
|-----------+----------------|
| =request.domain= | Business predicates and pure transitions. |
| =request.schema= | Public value schemas and private persistence representations. |
| =request.graph= | Relationships and declared graph/query semantics. |
| =request.fx= | Acquisition, preparation, and authoritative effect realization. |
| =request.core= | Narrow public semantic interface. |
| =request.choreo= | Distributed programs consuming public model interfaces. |

The layout is conventional; the dependency rule is normative. Public =core=
operations may use their model's internals. Choreography consumes public =core=
interfaces and does not reach through them into private domain, graph, schema,
or FX implementation. A model's =core= does not facade its choreography.

For example:

#+begin_src clojure
(ns net.humanhelp.site.model.request.choreo
  (:require
   [gesso.choreo.core :as choreo]
   [net.humanhelp.site.model.request.core :as request]))

;; The authoritative participant invokes a public model operation.
;; trusted-context is supplied by that participant's admitted runtime.
(choreo/at :request-authority
  (request/claim! trusted-context claim-command))
#+end_src

This is not permission for the browser to supply =trusted-context=, nor for the
choreography to call =request.fx= directly.

*** Placement and semantic ownership

The placement test is whether a rule still matters when the model operation is
called synchronously from one JVM, without a browser or distributed protocol.
Claim eligibility, current authorization, valid state transitions, command
deduplication promised by the model, and atomicity normally pass that test.
Browser optimism, role participation, transport retries, projected execution
lifetime, and presentation settlement normally belong above the model.

Some information crosses this boundary because it has an independent model
meaning. A stable command ID can be a public model input because durable replay
requires it. An expected domain version can be a public precondition. Neither
requires the model to know a tab's execution ID or HTMX swap policy.

Choreography may consume a public business judgment; it must not reimplement a
slightly different Claim policy. A view may suppress a button as a convenience,
but that suppression does not establish permission to commit. Conversely,
business policy must not exist only inside choreography while =core= remains a
pass-through to unconstrained writes.

Cross-model calls use public semantic interfaces. When Request depends on
Membership and Organization, their models expose the necessary facts,
predicates, and composable authoritative obligations through those interfaces.
They need not expose private storage paths. A coordinating operation may then
protect the combined obligations in one transaction.

A cross-model public interface must make its *semantic commitment*
inspectable without disclosing the consulted model's private storage
or implementation. For a condition needed by another model's
authoritative transition, this includes its logical subject and
parameters, relevant trusted principal/authority, declared read
dependencies, and the point at which the condition must hold.
An admitted implementation may supply a data-level obligation,
a qualified guard constructor, or a trusted callback bound to that
public contract. Merely naming =:membership/active= in an operation
manifest does not establish that its actual guard is installed.

A successful preliminary Membership or Organization read is evidence
of that observation at its qualified basis, not automatically proof
that its predicate still holds when a later Request Claim commits.
Where Claim promises atomic enforcement of combined eligibility,
the authoritative operation must compose the relevant model-owned
conditions into the *actual protected decision* using qualified
transaction-current guards, assertions, or an equivalently justified
backend mechanism. A stability premise may replace such a guard only
when separately admitted for the precise interval and scope; an
unchecked preflight Boolean or caller-supplied `authorized?` cannot.
If the chosen backend cannot protect the promised combined condition,
report the missing enforcement/correspondence obligation or weaken
the *declared* atomic guarantee explicitly; do not silently accept
a race.

| Cross-model construction | Required judgment |
|--------------------------+-------------------|
| Request Claim composes Request state, current Membership eligibility, and Organization policy at one admitted authoritative incorporation boundary | Permit the operation under its declared backend and trusted-guard premises; keep the originating public rule owners distinct |
| A prior Membership read returned =:active=, but Membership was revoked before the Claim commit | The stale read alone cannot authorize incorporation; enforce the current guarded condition or retain the explicitly qualified stability premise |
| A browser or choreography sends ={:claim-allowed? true}= and the transaction uses that value in place of current model guards | Reject the attempted authority substitution, regardless of input shape |
| A coordinating operation uses admitted public constraints from two models without importing either model's private FX or XTDB schema | Permit composition where the declared atomic backend contract and cross-model dependencies are actually satisfied |
| The backend cannot enforce the promised joint invariant, but an assembly report infers safety from two separately successful reads | Do not certify the joint transition; report an open or failed physical obligation at the correct boundary |

The rule and its source owner remain the same in a single application
process or across several web nodes sharing one qualified logical
authority. Multi-node routing and propagation can change which host
learns about a change or when it can observe a sufficient basis;
they cannot introduce a second Claim policy or substitute a stale
host-local authorization cache for transaction-current enforcement.


A workflow spanning peer models should live at the level that owns the workflow.
An onboarding flow coordinating Invitation, Membership, Organization, and
external provisioning need not belong to Invitation merely because it starts
there. Semantic dependency also does not dictate a literal namespace load order.
Contracts may be represented separately to avoid circular implementation loads.

*** Public interfaces expose commitments, not implementation privilege

A public operation interface must expose enough information for a caller and
the assembly checker to establish:

- the semantic operation and interface version;
- input and outcome schemas, including closed semantic discriminants;
- required authority and the source of the trusted principal binding;
- semantic preconditions and the point at which they must hold;
- promised atomic effects and explicitly separate later effects;
- command binding, replay, disclosure, and uncertainty policies;
- authoritative observation or progression evidence supplied with outcomes;
- named assumptions and the assurance status of each claimed guarantee.

It must not make callers depend on private XTDB forms, FX scratch values, or
helper Vars. A synchronous REPL invocation remains meaningful when supplied an
admitted context and authorized principal; bypassing HTTP does not bypass the
model's contract.

Ordinary application code should receive scoped semantic capabilities rather
than a raw database mutation handle. Clojure namespace privacy, records, and
nominal wrappers do not sandbox a determined author in the same trusted process.
The assurance claim therefore states its trusted-code and writer boundaries.

A bypass writer can invalidate assumptions used by later supported operations,
not merely forfeit assurance for its own invocation. Coverage must account for
jobs, migrations, imports, administrative tools, other services, and direct
database access wherever their writes can affect a protected invariant. An
admission policy may accept a named external-writer premise; it must not report
that premise as established by observing only Gesso-issued calls.

*** Well-formed rules and appropriate policy are separate judgments

Where semantic types are known, malformed rules must fail admission. Comparing
a closed status enum as though it were a number cannot become a certified rule.
For ordinary Clojure predicates outside the analyzable vocabulary, the interface
records their types, contracts, implementation binding, and asserted meaning.
Merely invoking such a predicate does not prove its contract.

Neither type checking nor invariant preservation decides whether a business
policy is desirable. Gesso can establish that the declared Claim policy is
consistently enforced under stated premises. HumanHelp still chooses that
policy. The executable semantic core and any later proof kernel/checker have
different responsibilities, as established in Part I.

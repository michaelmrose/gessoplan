## Method and limits

The eighteen individual section reviews and R4 preservation audits
are inputs, not a substitute for checking the resulting whole. R5
is preferred when its normative construction is clearer or sounder;
R4 remains the preservation baseline for significant requirements,
counterexamples and legitimate design freedom. The plan describes
the intended finished Gesso system, including unimplemented future
capabilities, without mistaking source inspection for execution.

The review checks complete R4 coverage, the original source and
product requirement denominators, explicit owner/consumer relations
between adjacent sections, actual installation and safety boundaries,
directional release/rollback history, separation of four release
decisions from four completion milestones, and publication integrity.

## Seven integrated adversarial histories checked editorially

1. A Claim rule changes but unrelated search meaning does not.
   The Claim proof/plan dependency cone is reconsidered, while
   unrelated valid evidence remains eligible; changed backend
   implementation needs physical requalification even with
   an unchanged semantic fingerprint (11.03–11.04, 11.18).
2. A valid new candidate fails during installation after old
   schema, credentials or workers change. Private initialization
   resources remain fenced; known-unsafe A cannot resume solely
   because B failed, and rollback cannot undo domain history
   (11.07, 11.17–11.18).
3. An A client submits C42, loses its response, and wakes under B.
   Directional decoder/route compatibility and retained actual
   command evidence control permitted recovery. It cannot invent
   a rejection or C43; Request state alone is not attribution
   (11.08–11.10).
4. A process dies after committing a Request but before
   publishing. Durable publication intent and dispatcher
   re-delivery must survive, while router interest registration
   and stale replacement filters need independent gap coverage.
   SSE write is not Bob's adopted board (11.16, Part X).
5. Bob reconnects and receives a remote lifecycle notice during
   an authorized Refresh. Only the covered authorized scope
   consumes the one-use grant; newer incomparable advisory
   work remains, and stale callbacks cannot regress newer
   authorized browser state (11.09–11.10, 11.13, 11.16).
6. S0/S1 independent kernel results exist, but production parser
   or issuer binding remains only trusted. The formal statement
   is conditional; no claim of universally proved production
   acceptance or real XTDB/browser trace follows (11.14–11.15).
7. All exposed Claim controls pass, but the original HumanHelp
   Response flow has not been selected or built. Operational
   completion remains partial, whereas a separately approved
   narrower profile can be reported without impersonating
   the full product milestone (11.01, 11.12–11.13, 11.17).

These are documentary stress histories and future test oracles,
not executed production, browser or theorem test results.

## Reconciliation correction and intentional nonchanges

The single normative edit changes 11.08's heading from
"four independent release identities" to "four distinct
release coordinates." Its paragraph now distinguishes three
identities/realizations from the fourth directional compatibility
relation. The A/A, A/B, B/A and B/B matrix, source identities,
allowed/refused behavior and original stable anchors are unchanged.

No other Part XI semantic contract has been rewritten in this
reconciliation. The original section-by-section R4 audits stay
independent, and a separate editorial gate may challenge the
whole-part conclusions. The README now explicitly distinguishes
its lossless-split history from the revised normative publication.

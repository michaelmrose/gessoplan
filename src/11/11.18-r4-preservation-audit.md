## Editorial judgment

Keep the R5 two-heading ending and the original list of mutation/rejection
scenarios; strengthen it into an independent acceptance contract for
the release mechanism itself. It must prove that the mechanism enforces the
selected candidate at the real host/issuer/effect boundary, refuses
invalid changes before the intended protected effect and still lets eligible
positive releases through. "Generated report says passed" and "test suite
has a named case" are not independent physical observations.

Preserve R4's key question for every change: exact original subject,
proposition, accepted premises, evidence mechanism, first trusted physical
enforcer, and entitlement of the next consumer to rely on it. Restore the
full source→operation→Choreo→projection→executable→proof→assembly→
physical host/backend/browser→release chain as a series of distinct,
falsifiable joins rather than treating one certification bit as authority.

Scope and source completeness must come from the original requirements.
A mandatory stronger profile cannot silently become a smaller passing
profile; an explicitly permitted operational preview may legitimately
release while unselected stronger milestones remain open. Evidence
invalidation must be accurate in both directions: no stale green physical
claim after an implementation change, and no unnecessary reproof of a sound
unrelated abstract theorem. A post-submit diagnostic may be a failure
without reversing a committed domain operation.

## Thirty-two R4/R5 preservation and strengthening decisions

1. Release-mechanism acceptance is its own declared validation subject,
   not an automatically correct harness for validating applications.
2. Original independent product/proof obligations determine the denominator.
3. Semantic identity and physical provider/artifact/installation identity
   have separate dependency and invalidation scopes.
4. Changing Claim meaning invalidates its complete source-derived cone,
   not unrelated search semantics.
5. An unchanged semantic proof does not qualify a changed XTDB adapter,
   actual writer, compiler, Biff handler or served browser artifact.
6. Genuine current proof issuer re-admission is required for serialized,
   forged or corrupt cached proof/evidence packages.
7. A copied passing report, status digest or self-authored map cannot
   become trusted issuance or authenticated test receipt.
8. Changing checker, normalization, source semantics or imported premises
   reconsiders the affected proof and certificate relations.
9. Accepted external premises remain conditional and subject-specific,
   not independently proved by report formatting.
10. All seven nonpassing mandatory result kinds remain blocking under the
    originally selected release claim.
11. A genuinely missing original capability does not disappear because
    the current UI does not render it.
12. An independently approved narrower preview preserves its own claim
    identity and missing full-milestone requirements.
13. Strict selected proof or physical gates cannot silently downgrade after
    failure, timeout, unavailability or unsupported source.
14. Legitimate conditional, operational and other allowed positives must
    still succeed; reject-all mechanisms do not pass the claim.
15. Candidate, installation, activation and completion retain distinct
    physical evidence subjects and real deciding boundaries.
16. The four independent completion milestones retain separate scope and
    denominators; no global successful status substitutes for them.
17. The exact generated, served and loaded browser bytes have independent
    correspondence checks under their compiler/serving assumptions.
18. Actual selected Ring/Biff listener, non-HTTP writers and guarded effects
    must adopt the checked implementation, not an unused system-map handler.
19. Concurrent activation preserves coherent assembly, predecessor and
    supported rolling-generation direction/asset contracts.
20. Partial installation and failed cleanup retain the original failure,
    owned-resource accounting and protection of valid shared work.
21. A known-invalid previous premise forbids unsafe fallback even when
    installation of the replacement fails.
22. Rollback is a fresh qualified activation against current migration,
    durable work, command compatibility and authorization.
23. Possibly committed command C42 remains unknown until historical
    evidence; lost response cannot justify rejection or creating C43.
24. Real historical command records and current state reads remain distinct
    evidence subjects, as do durable publication, SSE send and DOM adoption.
25. Registration/failover gaps and stale router filters require real
    detection, replay, conservative authorized delivery or recovery.
26. HumanHelp remote updates remain advisory until an authorized explicit
    Refresh; release changes do not mint grants or current disclosure.
27. Actual positive user/physical actions and negative effect-prevention
    oracles use the same installed gateway.
28. Real fault schedules include missing workers, no invoked gate, timeout,
    unrelated wrapper rejection, stale provider and unexecuted paths.
29. Performance/recovery/currentness claims specify environment, finite
    scope, capacity, liveness premises and observed limits.
30. Application business source, semantic core, proof checker, issuer,
    installation guard, product owner and report have separate authority.
31. Every dependent handoff states exact proposition, assumption,
    evidence source, checker/enforcer and transferable conclusion.
32. The final rule stops a required unsupported edge or exposes its
    permitted conditional premise instead of describing the whole
    framework as unconditionally formally verified.

R4's proposition/premise/checker working question and mixed assurance
classification remain fully explicit. R5's stronger change/physical
failure controls remain. No essential R4 or preferred R5 requirement
was intentionally dropped. The normative text describes a timeless
finished-system contract, not the state of a specific implementation.

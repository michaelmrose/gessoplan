## Governing editorial decision

Retain R5's preferred source-bound, checked, one-shot submission
architecture and restore the physical evidence and real Claim
requirements that R4 makes central. Clarify that a *logical
command* is not a *physical attempt*, that one-shot local
capabilities do not implement durable idempotency, and that
controlled process-local gateway observations cannot
certify actual production adoption, actor authority,
transaction-internal prestate, causality or complete
deployed writer coverage.

The expanded normative section retains all six steps
of the original production chain while making its
exact guarded lowering, physical attempt semantics,
parallel issuance, async retirement, durable
command outcomes and physical evidence classes
separately falsifiable. Section 10.06 still owns
the detailed lost-field/revision/ABA proofs;
there is no competing transaction calculus here.

## R4 and accepted-R5.1 preservation decisions

1. **Production is the object of the assurance claim.**
   A formal Claim transition, emitted transaction vector
   and actual XTDB guarded operation are distinct subjects;
   one checked abstract theorem does not authenticate the
   route or backend that executed them.

2. **Trusted invocation context.** Principal, tenant,
   selected operation, target and current model
   authority come from trusted server/service
   boundaries. Browser role, authority stamps,
   observed basis and claimed target are untrusted
   proposals until independently bound and checked.

3. **Source-current issuance.** A certificate/plan
   matches the original required operation and
   current trusted assembler, issued executor,
   installed route, callable/Var, asset and
   applicable provider contracts. Same-shaped
   maps, matching stale source and past
   audit reports confer no capability.

4. **Canonical whole-vector lowering.** The gateway
   derives or independently checks all supported
   physical transaction operations. User-injected
   SQL, swapped submit callbacks, extra writes,
   unsupported codecs, or changed expected
   operation/target cannot ride along after
   a partial check.

5. **Guard and write footprints differ.** Current
   Membership/Organization/Location, Request and
   primary-assignment absence/uniqueness can be
   required guards without all becoming writes.
   Whole-document replacement needs physical
   frame protection; detailed lost-field and
   ABA handling is owned by 10.06.

6. **A pinned pre-read is not the atomic prestate.**
   External observation establishes a recorded
   basis, not transaction-internal current values.
   Physical guard and backend isolation premises
   must connect the actual state that was
   atomically incorporated.

7. **A single submission owner is scoped to one
   attempt.** Process-local one-shot issuance
   prevents a second local use of that capability;
   it is not a process-global one-request lease,
   global mutex, anti-duplication protocol or
   cryptographic credential.

8. **Logical C42 and H1/H2 are different.**
   Several attempts may refer to one protected
   durable command. Its operation/target/principal/
   canonical-argument binding, duplicate replay,
   exclusion and historical result are durable
   model responsibilities, not a side effect
   of one-shot memory.

9. **Post-submit exception means uncertainty.**
   If a write may have reached XTDB, failed
   transport/readback cannot invent
   nonincorporation or a fresh-authorized
   retry. Command outcome remains typed
   unknown until allowed authoritative
   evidence resolves it.

10. **Domain plus command result must meet the
    declared atomic commit contract.** The
    selected protected path relates the
    domain write and canonical command result,
    and durable outbox/publication intent
    when promised. Asynchronous SSE delivery
    is not magically part of the same
    database transaction.

11. **Independent requests can be concurrent.**
    Request-local callbacks and distinct
    physical attempt capabilities permit
    legitimate parallel requests. A global
    submission lock or permanently occupied
    callback would fail meaningful product
    availability even if narrow tests
    looked serially safe.

12. **Capacity is not authority.** Semaphores,
    queue bounds and timeouts are operational
    controls. Saturation is not a model
    authorization rejection, proof of
    noncommit or a reason to silently
    change original command semantics.

13. **Deferred execution requires transferable
    explicit scope.** Thread-local bindings
    expire; workers, callbacks, outbox
    processors and async completion may
    not inherit a retired request
    principal or submission capability.

14. **Retirement is non-destructive.** A retired
    request's capability cannot issue
    again. Cleanup must not remove
    a newer registered writer, and
    cancellation/post-dispatch checks
    cannot undo already committed
    side effects.

15. **Declared route inventory is not actual
    deployment.** Literal route/source/writer
    joins may be verified locally;
    dynamic targets, later handler
    replacement, stale premediation
    handlers, external writers and the
    actual serving process require
    independent checks.

16. **Evidence is classified by origin.**
    Canonical lowering, local attempt
    entry, public transaction key,
    pinned ledger/domain observation,
    callback identity and backend
    qualified commit each establish
    different claims. Matching reports
    never make the stronger claims
    automatically true.

17. **A checked local readback does not prove
    physical causation.** A different
    writer may have changed a
    later Request document. No bounded
    single-basis observation alone
    authenticates the transaction's
    internal prestate or all writers.

18. **Physical prevention and post-effect
    diagnosis differ.** Source rejection
    and a pre-effect gateway guard can
    prevent specified effects in their
    scope; later audit mismatch
    is a diagnostic, not
    retroactive permission or
    cancellation of an unsafe write.

19. **Real protected Claim stays positive.**
    Eligible Claim with genuine
    concurrent contention must
    actually work; reject-all,
    artificially serialized
    single request, or a
    reduced catalog omitting
    Unclaim/Reassign does not
    satisfy product acceptance.

20. **Topology does not alter semantics.**
    Multi-node placement needs
    durable command exclusion,
    scoped routing, outbox
    registration and replay/
    failover evidence, but must
    preserve the same source-owned
    model authority and user
    Refresh permissions as
    single-node Gesso.

## Actual Gesso v1622 inspected source and nonclaims

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_restricted_submission_gateway.clj`
  defines an opt-in physical `xtdb.api/execute-tx` owner
  with one-shot submission and rechecked finite transaction
  lowering. It denies caller-substituted vectors
  and callbacks *within the gateway*, but its comments
  explicitly disclaim an installed Biff writer,
  caller-node or principal authentication,
  other-writer exclusion, transaction causality
  and general protected app semantics.
  Its registry and one-attempt journal are
  process-local, not durable credentials.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_complete_guard_execution.clj`
  strengthens the declared top-level whole-document
  update CAS and field-count assertion within a
  supported finite subset. Its v1600
  opt-in mode adds a canonical command-record
  CREATE in one submitted vector. It does
  not authenticate supplied prestate,
  automatically cover nested or other documents,
  prove ABA exclusion or physical writer closure.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_monotonic_revision_execution.clj`
  checks a selected exact natural revision
  successor and source-bound gateway, including
  optional plan-bound one-shot modes.
  Its own documentation makes all-writer
  monotonic discipline a separate
  deployment premise; direct XTDB
  writers can bypass the revision
  convention. Some earlier Biff
  constructor variants retain distinct
  weaker behavior.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_biff_writer_assembly.clj`
  validates a local static literal route-to-writer
  registry join and some observed invocations.
  Its writer enumerator is host supplied;
  route inclusion and actual dynamic
  serving adoption remain open.
  Post-dispatch rejection cannot undo
  a previously submitted write.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_biff_request_lifecycle.clj`
  installs and retires a fresh scoped
  callback for one synchronous Biff
  dispatch. Its early/limited lifecycle
  can reject concurrent controller
  invocations. Its own source notes
  registry drift cleanup,
  direct XTDB clients and
  alternative entrypoints as open.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_biff_request_mediation.clj`
  is an opt-in final Biff handler
  wrapper around the request lifecycle,
  with bounded/queued policy in
  some variants. It explicitly
  does not show that a deployed
  server consumed the final handler,
  and cannot establish parallel
  request execution in its
  legacy serialized form.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_biff_parallel_request_context.clj`
  adds distinct independent request-local
  callback registrations permitting
  simultaneous checked handler dispatch.
  Later opt-in versions construct one
  plan-bound monotonic two-write
  submission gateway and may obtain
  pinned postsubmit observations.
  The source explicitly disclaims
  client-target authenticity,
  protected model authorization,
  complete raw writer exclusion,
  final server adoption, transaction
  causality and global refinement.
  A parallel request-local registry
  is not a deployment writer census.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_operation_target_binding.clj`
  compares separately presented operation,
  expected table/document and candidate
  command before submission. It is
  only a local equality/shape check:
  the independently provided target
  may still be a false or untrusted
  application claim.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_unknown_command_reconciliation.clj`
  observes a canonical command record
  at one pinned XTDB basis without writes.
  It refuses to infer command
  incorporation from a matching
  domain document or a missing
  record at one read. Its report
  is redacted and potentially
  a command-existence oracle
  if shown to an untrusted caller.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_atomic_submitted_readback.clj`
  joins a process-local original
  two-write submission return
  with same-node pinned domain,
  command and ledger observations.
  It explicitly marks transaction
  causality and full protected
  model/actor authority unestablished;
  a failed readback after submission
  leaves outcome unknown.

- The v1622 source includes corresponding
  `xtdb_gateway_biff_parallel_*`,
  `xtdb_gateway_biff_unified_submission_test.clj`,
  `xtdb_gateway_biff_unified_race_test.clj`
  and `xtdb_gateway_unknown_command_reconciliation_test.clj`
  test fixtures. Their existence and
  assertions do not mean these
  tests were newly executed
  here or that their experimental
  opt-in modules constitute
  complete deployed production
  assurance.

**Implementation nonclaims:** No overall all-routes
Biff/XTDB mediation, automatic public model principal
validation, universal backend isolation theorem,
production-node authentication, general durable retry
recovery, all physical writer coverage, independently
checked transaction causality, browser-originated
settlement proof or completed HumanHelp Claim
product acceptance is established merely
by these local modules and fixtures.

## Positive and adversarial acceptance pairs

1. **Trusted principal:** valid helper and current
   Request/Membership bind Claim; browser
   helper flag cannot become principal.

2. **Source/target:** exact selected plan emits
   the appropriate canonical operations;
   malicious source builder changes
   Request target or adds a write.

3. **Lowering:** supported guarded update,
   typed values and command-result create
   pass; unsupported SQL, raw vector
   append or codec substitution rejects
   before execution.

4. **One-shot local:** one submitted attempt
   returns the real local API result;
   second use of the exact consumed
   gateway does not submit again.

5. **Logical replay:** compatible C42
   can be reconciled through durable
   command state; separately issued
   H2 cannot cause duplicate
   incorporation.

6. **Different command:** independent
   valid C43 may execute concurrently;
   C42's one-shot does not
   block the entire application.

7. **Parallel request:** two
   authorized unrelated requests
   use fresh independently issued
   request writers; a process-global
   lease is not necessary
   for correctness.

8. **Physical one-primary:** real
   two-helper concurrency under the
   qualified atomic writer keeps
   one active primary; stale
   pre-read/unconditional write fails.

9. **Lost result:** H1 submits
   and result disappears;
   response remains honest
   unknown until allowed durable
   reconciliation, not invented rejection.

10. **Atomic result:** domain
    incorporation and canonical
    command record share the
    declared commit; a variant
    with domain-only write
    cannot be called durable
    command incorporation.

11. **Complete field frame:** valid
    guarded replacement preserves
    unrelated current fields;
    an unguarded summary change
    is not silently overwritten.

12. **Call-site witness:** original
    captured submit and response
    support the bounded local
    observation; fabricated
    return/map or wrong node
    cannot gain issuer/physical
    authority.

13. **Causal limit:** pinned
    ledger and Request readback
    corroborate one basis;
    a separate writer changing
    Request prevents causal
    inference from the match alone.

14. **Installed route:** actual
    final current Biff route
    invokes the issued registered
    writer; an old handler or
    direct XTDB bypass remains
    uncovered or is rejected
    by a proven mediation policy.

15. **Retirement:** completed
    request retires its
    capability without deleting
    another request's active
    callback; deferred stale
    callback cannot submit
    through expired scope.

16. **Async service:** a valid
    worker with explicit scoped
    service identity and new
    execution lease may submit;
    retaining expired thread-local
    HTTP principal cannot.

17. **Capacity:** bounded
    scheduling preserves typed
    admitted/rejected/unknown
    distinctions; queue
    saturation is not
    silently labeled model
    authorization denial.

18. **Outbox:** published intent
    survives a lost signal
    with permitted replay;
    duplicate invalidations do
    not force extra domain
    incorporation or
    user-unapproved Refresh.

19. **Host proof:** current
    application source/handler/
    asset joins to selected
    transaction within its
    qualified scope;
    copied stamps or
    host-supplied incomplete
    writer manifests
    cannot establish
    global closure.

20. **Positive product:** real
    eligible Claim plus
    Unclaim/Reassign and
    separate HumanHelp
    product capabilities
    remain possible;
    reject-all gateway
    construction cannot
    substitute for product
    acceptance.

Each negative case names its exact
original source, mutation, expected
first enforcing boundary, and an
independent qualified observer
for forbidden physical effects.
A successful source check
does not imply an unobserved
outside writer was prevented;
post-effect discovery is
not pre-effect enforcement.

## Editorial outcome and next section

10.05 retains R5's restricted,
request-local single-submission
design, while restoring R4's
atomic Claim/writer requirement
and clarifying the gap between
logical command idempotency,
physical attempt limitation,
installed server adoption and
authentic outcome evidence.

*** Finite exploration and abstraction

Finite exploration can expose a genuine semantic counterexample, establish a
property *exactly for one complete finite model*, or supply a bounded
conformance check of an implementation. These are three different evidence
classes. To transfer a finite-model theorem beyond its enumerated domain, Gesso
must provide a separately justified abstraction/refinement relation from the
original admitted source to that model, and a further implementation relation
for the actual production execution if the claim concerns XTDB, transport or
the browser. A finite test does not become an unbounded proof because the
software is deterministic or its sampled observations match.

The finite subject is derived from the canonical Gesso model, Choreo or
browser-adapter semantics admitted under Parts I–IX. It retains exact
source-generation, operation/initialization and invariant obligations,
policy choices, source-derived reader/guard/frame semantics and open premises.
Developers should not have to construct a second handwritten formal app with
rules that merely resemble the runtime. An intentionally simplified
independent test model may be useful, but its results are explicitly
test-relative until the original-source correspondence is justified.

**** Exact finite claims: closure, totality and vacuity

An exact-finite result records the following as the *complete, closed
model under examination*:

| Required element | Exact claim boundary |
|------------------+----------------------|
| Initial states | Original admitted initial-condition/initializer relation, all selected starting states and their eligibility/inhabitation checks |
| State and input domains | Each field's type, absence/present-nil interpretation, enum values and representative or exhaustive status, including identity and collection bounds |
| Operations | Independently source-derived complete original operation IDs, guards, typed inputs, simultaneous writes, frames, outcome/failure/unknown transitions and environmental steps |
| Transition semantics | Admitted enabled steps and successor relation, stuttering, scheduling, terminal/deadlock treatment and whether rejected operations are transitions or errors |
| Property | Exact invariant, deadlock-freedom, eventuality or trace obligation, its quantifiers, scope and any fairness or environmental premises |
| Exploration | Reachable-state/edge coverage, generated-vs-pruned states, cutoff, visited-state equivalence, trace/history handling, bounds and result kind |
| Evidence | Checker/translator version, canonical input, actual outcome/counterexample, original source location, independent oracle and open abstraction/production bridges |

An exhaustive result requires the whole reachable graph of *that* finite
model under its declared transition relation, not merely all states up to a
search depth or all choices returned by an unverified event generator.
A BFS/DFS exploration may use state deduplication only when its equality
or quotient is sound for the specific state/trace property. In particular,
merging distinct histories because their current map happens to match
can discard command-history, fairness, previous assignment or in-flight
physical callback distinctions relevant to the theorem.

A depth limit, state cap, edge cap, missing choice, unexpected exception,
unsupported operation, unavailable dependency or timeout makes the selected
exhaustiveness claim *incomplete*. It does not become a proof of absence of
counterexamples. An actual reproduced counterexample to an adequately
specified finite model can still be reported even if exploration did not
finish; it must carry the actual trace and scope, not a fabricated
"complete" status. A potential deadlock is distinguished from a declared
terminal state, a blocked environmental step and a liveness violation.
An empty initialization set makes a universal invariant trivially true;
that is not sufficient positive evidence that the application works.

For source-relative induction, the finite oracle can enumerate selected
prestates and operation inputs and check base and step preservation without
exploring only reachable states. It still must name whether each domain
contains *all* values of the declared type or only representatives.
Enums and Booleans may have complete finite domains under the
given versioned interface; integers, strings, UUIDs, timestamps,
dependency depths and arbitrary sequences usually do not.
Checking all input combinations over a representative set is exact
for *that set*, not proof that no omitted value changes an operator's
semantics. A finite model with every guarded operation disabled
can look safe while missing required positive Claim, Unclaim or
manager Reassign behavior.

**** Safety transfer requires the right simulation relation

Let C denote the original/concrete semantic transition system, A
the finite abstract system, R a relation from relevant C states
to A states, and P_C/P_A the corresponding safety predicates.
The intended *concrete-to-abstract* safety transfer needs:

#+begin_example
Initial coverage:
  for every admitted concrete initial state c0,
    there exists abstract initial a0 with R(c0, a0).

Step simulation:
  whenever R(c, a) and a relevant concrete step c --e--> c',
    there is a permitted finite abstract trace a ==e==> a'
    (possibly with justified hidden/stuttering steps),
    with R(c', a') and preserved required observations.

Property reflection:
  for every related (c, a), P_A(a) implies P_C(c).

Then an A invariant over all its reachable states transfers to
related C reachable states, under the stated relation and premises.
#+end_example

The quantifiers and direction matter. Showing that each abstract step
can be mimicked by one convenient concrete execution does *not* establish
that every concrete execution is represented. Likewise, mapping each
concrete state to some abstract state without checking its successors
does not establish safety transfer. A proper abstraction may
*overapproximate* concrete behavior and therefore admit spurious
abstract counterexamples. A finite abstract violation is an actual
failure of the chosen abstract property, but need not be realizable in
the original semantics or in a deployed browser/XTDB execution;
concretization and relation inspection decide the stronger attribution.

For *production* safety, a second relation must qualify the actual
installed code, original source version, authorized backend prestate,
all relevant writers, transport/DOM effects and failure semantics.
An abstract source theorem does not authenticate the route, model
caller, physical transaction or browser mutation. A single
real-world event can correspond to multiple abstract steps, but
that stuttering/partial-order permission must not hide an
unauthorized write, stale DOM adoption or additional active
primary assignment.

**** A bounded slot is not a bounded history

Logical browser active/queued slots, request concurrency and
selected finite state projections do not bound command identifiers,
resource or effect instance IDs, history length, attempt count,
provider bases, user inputs, dependency depth, retired metadata,
in-flight network requests or physical DOM side effects.
Gesso must either prove a state/data bound *under actual scheduling
and retention premises* or choose a sound abstraction for each
unbounded dimension; it cannot infer finite-state completeness
from one bounded queue setting.

Equality-class or symmetry reduction of opaque IDs is often
preferable to arbitrarily choosing UUID 1–4. The abstraction
must retain every equality and inequality relation relevant to
guards, callback ownership, command replay, identity freshness,
reused DOM IDs and noninterference between owners. An old resource
A and active B may be represented by classes =:old= and =:current=,
but two outstanding callbacks to *different old identities* cannot
always be merged without proving the property ignores their
difference. Fresh allocation can change the class partition.
Recycling an old ID or aliasing two logical commands changes the
transition relation unless ruled out by a source/protocol premise.

An unbounded collection may admit a cutoff or data-independence
proof, but that theorem must justify the cutoff for the
particular property and all supported transitions. A finite
representative Request/RequestAssignment model with two helpers
can expose a one-primary race; it does not prove
arbitrary number of helpers or all admin/migration writers
without the required symmetry, cardinality, operation-catalog
and physical-refinement arguments.

**** Resource ownership: a local abstraction with an explicit limit

The following executable example isolates one ownership predicate. It uses
opaque resource and effect identities and an active phase. Identity equality
and phase are checked again at the point of mutation. It is not the complete
Part VI adapter: execution ownership, plan compatibility, authority scope,
observation coverage, and batch completion remain additional conditions there.

#+begin_src clojure
(defn owned-effect? [state callback]
  (and (= :active (:resource-phase state))
       (some? (:resource-instance state))
       (= (:resource-instance state) (:resource-instance callback))
       (some? (:effect-id state))
       (= (:effect-id state) (:effect-id callback))
       (= :awaiting-effect (:effect-phase state))))

(defn ownership-observation [state callback]
  {:active? (= :active (:resource-phase state))
   :same-resource? (and (some? (:resource-instance state))
                        (= (:resource-instance state)
                           (:resource-instance callback)))
   :same-effect? (and (some? (:effect-id state))
                      (= (:effect-id state) (:effect-id callback)))
   :awaiting? (= :awaiting-effect (:effect-phase state))})

(defn abstract-owned-effect? [observation]
  (and (:active? observation)
       (:same-resource? observation)
       (:same-effect? observation)
       (:awaiting? observation)))

(def resource-a
  {:resource-phase :active
   :resource-instance :fixture/resource-a
   :effect-id :fixture/effect-a
   :effect-phase :awaiting-effect})

(def callback-a
  {:resource-instance :fixture/resource-a
   :effect-id :fixture/effect-a})

(def resource-b
  (assoc resource-a
         :resource-instance :fixture/resource-b
         :effect-id :fixture/effect-b))

(assert (owned-effect? resource-a callback-a))
(assert (not (owned-effect? resource-b callback-a)))
(assert (not (owned-effect? (assoc resource-a :resource-phase :retired)
                           callback-a)))
(assert (not (owned-effect? (assoc resource-a :effect-phase :completed)
                           callback-a)))
(assert (= (owned-effect? resource-b callback-a)
           (abstract-owned-effect?
            (ownership-observation resource-b callback-a))))
#+end_src

The four Boolean observations support a local predicate comparison. They are
not by themselves a finite transition system. A finite abstraction for the
whole adapter must also specify allocation, identity freshness, replacement,
retirement, queues, and adversarial late events while preserving the necessary
equality relationships. It must account for two callbacks that alias the same
identity and callbacks that do not. Collapsing all old identities into one name
without preserving the relevant relations can change the modeled behavior.

The final-mutation requirement follows even before a universal abstraction
proof exists: callback A may pass its first check, be deferred, and arrive at
mutation after resource B has replaced A. The shell must evaluate ownership
against B at that moment. Retaining the earlier Boolean =true= is not permission.
The same distinction applies when a completed effect has consumed its phase but
a duplicate callback arrives.



**** From four Boolean observations to a complete browser machine

The preserved Clojure example above has four *local predicate*
observations: active resource, equal resource instance, equal effect ID
and awaiting-effect phase. They demonstrate the Boolean equivalence
of =owned-effect?= and =abstract-owned-effect?= for the specific
constructed inputs; they do not enumerate all reachable adapter
states or prove the concrete-to-abstract relation. In particular the
four Booleans do not encode how an execution acquires ownership,
what happens when targets are replaced, how effects are scheduled,
or whether the shell rechecks permission at the physical mutation.

A full finite adapter subject would also model, as needed by the
selected claim:

- creation/allocation and freshness of resource, execution and
  effect identities; permitted alias relationships and changes
  across target reuse, plan replacement and owner retirement;
- normalized callback delivery, duplicates, delay, loss,
  cancellation failure and physical requests remaining after
  semantic retirement;
- current plan and application generation, installed asset/route
  and target compatibility, active/queued logical slots, effect
  phases and bounded or abstracted retained metadata;
- authoritative observation frontiers, provisional/confirmed
  command ownership, permission to acquire, and the different
  physical events for transport completion versus DOM install;
- multi-target HTMX/OOB partial completion, repair, abort,
  continuity ownership and current effect-time revalidation;
- environment/adversary choices that must be included to
  warrant a universal claim over the declared scheduling scope.

The finite model should retain a stable *logical* command C42
independently of physical attempts H1/H2 and current resource A/B.
Conflating those identities could make a late response look like
a valid result of a new command. An optimistic current card is
also not an authenticated statement of C42's durable
incorporation. The transition relation must preserve these
distinctions rather than assuming every successful HTTP
response is a current authorized DOM adoption.

The physical browser requirement can be falsified by a precise
interleaving:

#+begin_example
A is the current resource; effect F from A is pending.
A callback for F arrives and passes callback-entry checks.
The callback is deferred before its managed DOM mutation.
A retires; new resource B adopts the same target DOM ID.
The deferred A callback resumes.
At the physical mutation boundary A no longer owns the target.
The shell must reject A's mutation even if its earlier check passed.
#+end_example

The originally preserved predicate rejects this case *only when
evaluated against the current B-owned state at the mutation point*.
A cached earlier =true= is not a permission lease. The
browser adapter can model that admission; a theorem about
the real shell additionally needs actual code/HTMX/DOM
effect-time correspondence, physical scheduling and
installed artifact currentness. This also applies to a
completed effect that receives a duplicate callback
after its awaiting phase is consumed. For a batch,
successful installation of one target does not prove
every other target adopted.

**** Liveness needs divergence, fairness and permission premises

Finite safety and finite liveness require different claims.
An abstract safety simulation can hide arbitrarily many
internal steps while preserving protected invariants. A
progress/termination theorem additionally needs to show
that the relation does not admit an infinite concrete
divergence that the abstract model silently skips.
Fairness assumptions must refer to the actual relevant
scheduler, participant, provider, delivery or
recovery mechanism; a bounded test depth does not
establish them.

A fairness-sensitive model must identify which actions
are enabled, which remain continuously or repeatedly
enabled, what strong/weak fairness applies and whether
environment or user actions are assumed. The declared
property is specific—for example, a retained qualified
command result becomes observable when authorized
recovery is repeatedly attempted under sufficient
provider availability. That is not the same as claiming
every network partition heals, every user presses Refresh
or every provisional UI state settles automatically.
Under HumanHelp's selected remote-advisory policy,
a remote board's actual acquisition is conditional
on *user-granted* scoped Refresh; SSE delivery does
not authorize background collection reading.

Finite cycle detection can identify a lasso violating
an explicitly encoded fairness/liveness obligation
in the selected model. If the model is cut at depth
N, a finite sequence with no terminal state is not
in itself proof of infinite divergence. Conversely,
a reachable terminal path does not prove all fair
runs eventually reach it. For a concrete liveness
transfer the abstraction must preserve applicable
fairness, enabledness and non-divergence,
not only a Boolean ownership invariant.

**** Evidence categories and reproducible adversarial acceptance

An assurance report should explicitly identify:
the original source and selected abstraction generation,
the exact finite domains and whether they are exhaustive,
initial and transition catalogs, property/temporal
meaning, complete-vs-bounded exploration status,
checker/tool options, evidence origin, reachability/
deadlock/fairness interpretation, original source
locations of violations and open simulation,
concretization and production-enforcement premises.
A copied finite result is not issuer-controlled proof
or a current deployment authorization. Section 10.12
owns the reusable evidence-package lifecycle.

The following paired checks distinguish the strength
of each conclusion:

1. A complete declared Boolean/enum domain is explored;
   one omitted enum variant invalidates exhaustive coverage.
2. Nonempty valid initial states and a genuinely enabled
   positive Claim exist; empty Init or reject-all
   operations cannot pass whole-product acceptance.
3. Every original operation and selected environment
   step appears; removing Unclaim, Reassign or a
   relevant competing writer invalidates the
   corresponding source/physical completeness claim.
4. All reachable states/edges in a finite model are
   exhausted; a depth/node/edge cap is labelled
   incomplete rather than counterexample-free.
5. Source base/step enumeration respects absent
   versus present nil and simultaneous writes;
   conflation or sequential assignment produces
   a discriminating independent-oracle mismatch.
6. A valid symmetry/equality abstraction
   preserves current-vs-old callback relationships;
   collapsing distinct alias classes or fresh
   allocation incorrectly changes an observable
   outcome.
7. A complete source/abstract initial and
   transition simulation implies the named
   concrete safety property; state mapping
   without step simulation is rejected.
8. A deliberate abstract overapproximation
   produces a scoped counterexample; it
   cannot be reported as a physical XTDB
   or Chromium incident without concretization.
9. Competing helper C42/C43 commands are
   represented distinctly and at most
   one primary can incorporate under
   the claimed atomic model; merged
   command IDs falsely erase the race.
10. A valid A callback mutates a current
    target when fully authorized; a
    deferred A callback after B replaces
    it must fail at *effect time*.
11. Duplicate callback and completed
    effect cannot reuse a consumed
    permission; a cached initial
    =true= cannot grant a second
    DOM mutation.
12. Real multi-target partial installation
    retains incomplete/repairable
    outcome; one target HTTP 200
    cannot qualify full-batch
    authoritative adoption.
13. A finite lasso under justified
    fairness can refute selected
    progress; a depth-limited
    prefix is not an infinite
    liveness counterexample.
14. A fair authorized recovery
    is conditional on retention,
    availability and appropriate
    user action; it cannot
    silently force remote Refresh
    or assume network healing.
15. A finite source-level transition
    result is reported as bounded;
    copied diagnostic or
    controlled physical observation
    cannot be promoted to
    original-source theorem
    or actual host authority.
16. A selected abstraction backed
    by a checked step/property bridge
    can justify the corresponding
    quantified safety domain;
    a finite sample without the
    bridge retains its finite-only
    evidence status.
17. Real browser execution agrees
    on the exercised supported
    resource/effect mutation case;
    a CLJC adapter-model pass
    does not itself prove that
    Chromium and HTMX executed
    the same physical schedule.
18. Positive HumanHelp creator-local
    and explicitly user-authorized
    remote adoption remain possible;
    a model that hides these
    required cases is not a
    complete application witness.

Each case specifies source identities, finite/abstract
universe, exact bounds and declared fairness,
positive execution, adversarial change, first
checking or physical boundary, independent
observable result and still-open assumptions.
Correct source rejection is not evidence that an
unmediated physical writer was prevented.
A finite checker timeout remains incomplete;
an unknown physical outcome remains unknown;
neither may be marked as an exhaustive safe run.

The final standard is *finite proof with honest
scope* and *generalization only through a
justified relation*. A small abstract state
machine is valuable when it covers the right
behaviors, not because its state count is small.

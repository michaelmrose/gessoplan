## Editorial decision

Keep R5's clearer and smaller executable resource-ownership witness over R4's larger UUID/retired-set pseudo-obligation data. The R5 example is strictly more appropriate *as a local executable predicate comparison*, but it necessarily leaves broader R4 identity freshness, retirement, pending metadata, physical browser and full-machine completeness requirements unresolved. Recover these requirements normatively in distinct, testable abstraction/proof sections rather than silently portraying the four-Boolean example as their implementation.

An exact finite result is proof only of the declared finite state machine and its *complete* explored transition relation. To transfer it to a larger original semantic universe, prove or explicitly qualify the relevant initial/step/property relation; to transfer it to deployed software, additionally prove or qualify the installed physical refinement. Failed exploration, incomplete event generators, missing positive operations and unsupported source fragments cannot become a universal theorem by report formatting.

## R4/R5 preservation and strengthened decisions

1. **Finite evidence has three meanings.** A reproduced counterexample in a selected finite model, a complete invariant over that model, and a bounded implementation conformance sample have separate conclusions. None is a full deployed theorem by itself.
2. **Original source is the subject.** The full initializer/operation/invariant catalog and canonical Gesso rule/Choreo/browser source determine the model in scope. A caller-selected smaller catalog, manually synchronized duplicate model or pruned difficult operation cannot establish original-source completeness.
3. **Exact domain disclosure.** Each field's finite values, optional absence, present nil, enum/Boolean coverage, scalar representatives, input domains, identity abstraction, collection bounds and outcome/failure classes are recorded. “Exhaustive” always states *of what*.
4. **Whole reachable graph, not only bounded depth.** Complete exploration requires closure of all reachable states and relevant transitions under the declared relation. A depth cap, choice bound or unverified event generator warrants only qualified partial/bounded evidence.
5. **Search failures are not safe outcomes.** Timeout, node/edge budget, unexpected checker error, unsupported operation and source/transition omission are explicit incomplete/unsupported statuses. A genuine discovered counterexample remains reportable with correct finite scope even when search later exhausts resources.
6. **Correct deadlock and terminal semantics.** A state with no enabled domain operation is not necessarily a declared successful terminal; an unresolved network wait, failed acquisition, blocked environmental action or fairness-dependent liveness state needs explicit treatment.
7. **Nonvacuous initialization.** An empty Init or universally disabled operation guard can make invariants vacuously true; that cannot substitute for independently required real eligible HumanHelp Create/Claim/Unclaim/Reassign and product acceptance.
8. **Concrete-to-abstract direction.** Every selected concrete initial state has a related abstract initial state; each concrete transition is simulated by permitted related abstract behavior; and abstract safety implies the concrete property over related states. Abstract-to-concrete example matching is not enough.
9. **Stuttering is property- and observation-specific.** Hidden or several abstract steps for one physical event are permissible only where the chosen refinement relation justifies them; a simulation cannot hide unauthorized physical writes or stale DOM adoption.
10. **Abstraction may overapproximate.** A finite abstract counterexample can refute that abstract property yet be spurious for concrete production. Its real realizability and the validity of the source-to-generated relation are separate investigation obligations.
11. **Closed enums differ from representative integers.** Full v1 enum/Boolean enumeration can be exact within a versioned type; a small integer, string, UUID, timestamp or sequence sample does not cover the larger domain without a theorem.
12. **Finite slots do not bound all metadata.** Browser queues may be bounded while effect IDs, commands, attempts, dependency histories, retirement sets, pending physical requests, provider bases and user inputs are unbounded. Coverage needs a real bound or separate sound abstraction for each relevant dimension.
13. **Equality partitions matter.** The abstraction must preserve same-current, distinct-old, same-effect, aliasing and freshness relations needed for permission, command replay and target reuse. Collapsing all old IDs into one token without proving data independence can change the modeled machine.
14. **The R5 example remains strictly local.** The four observed Booleans establish a predicate equivalence for chosen fixture inputs. They do not model allocation, installation, message scheduling, all callbacks, plan generation, authority or actual shell effects.
15. **Preserve R4's resource freshness obligations.** A current/old/other classification needs sound transition rules for replacing owner A by B, reusing a target DOM ID, retiring resources, creating new effects and leaving old physical requests in flight.
16. **Permission is checked at mutation.** A callback can pass entry admission under resource A, be deferred, and attempt a DOM mutation after B owns the same target. The current effect-time check must reject A rather than trust a cached Boolean.
17. **Duplicate and partial effects are distinct.** A consumed effect cannot be re-used by a duplicate callback. One successful HTMX/OOB target does not establish completion of an entire multi-target batch; repair remains source/effect-scoped.
18. **Logical command and resource IDs differ.** C42 remains stable across H1/H2 attempts; retiring/replacing browser A/B does not determine historical command incorporation. An identity abstraction merging those categories can manufacture false settlements.
19. **Fairness is not a safety assumption.** Safe simulations can stutter forever. Liveness transfer must specify scheduler and participant fairness, backend availability, qualified retention, progress-enabledness and necessary explicit user action.
20. **Finite prefix is not an infinite lasso.** A bounded depth trace with no completion is not automatically proof of divergence. An actual finite graph cycle with appropriate fairness semantics may demonstrate a liveness violation under the selected model.
21. **Single and multi-node preserve semantics.** Node placement, replay/gap recovery and asynchronous delivery add physical refinement/fairness obligations; they cannot strengthen user authorization or automatically trigger remote board acquisition.
22. **Finite evidence is not trusted issuance.** A copyable source/finite oracle result, process-local diagnostic or constructed trace does not confer an operation certificate, runtime principal, XTDB retry permission or browser effect lease.
23. **Independent product gates remain.** A reduced finite model with all difficult actions hidden, or a reject-all adapter, cannot be accepted as a completed application even if its selected safety invariant passes.
24. **Source-to-physical reporting stays honest.** The record retains model bounds, checker versions, original source, complete-vs-partial status, open abstraction and implementation links, independent positive/adversarial oracles and privacy-safe traces.

## Eighteen paired positive/adversarial acceptance obligations

1. **Exact enum:** A closed current enum domain enumerates all its values; omitting a new variant invalidates the advertised exhaustive result.
2. **Initial inhabitance:** At least one legitimate state and enabled positive Claim exists where required; empty Init and reject-all fail application acceptance.
3. **Catalog closure:** Every required original operation and initializer is included; deliberately pruned Unclaim/Reassign cannot make an artificially small graph “complete.”
4. **Graph closure:** All reachable finite states and edges are examined; a depth/node/edge cutoff is incomplete, not counterexample-free universal safety.
5. **Presence/frame:** Complete source types distinguish absent/present-nil and simultaneous writes; conflated presence or sequential RHS is detected.
6. **Identity symmetry:** Sound equality/freshness partition keeps current A, retired B, effect and aliases distinct; collapsing relevant old IDs changes the candidate model and cannot silently transfer safety.
7. **Initial-step-property bridge:** The checked initial/step relation and abstract safety imply the declared concrete safety; a state-only mapping with no transition simulation does not.
8. **Spurious abstract failure:** An intentionally overapproximated state produces a finite trace; it is not reported as an observed XTDB/Chromium incident without concrete realization.
9. **Competing commands:** Distinct C42/C43 can contend in the abstract guarded one-primary model; merged command IDs cannot stand in for that race.
10. **Effect-time retirement:** Callback A mutates while A is current; deferred callback A is prevented after B replaces A even if the DOM ID is reused.
11. **Duplicate effect:** First justified callback consumes its phase; a duplicate callback cannot mutate a second time using cached entry admission.
12. **OOB partial adoption:** Multi-target response reports exactly the physically installed subset; one completed target/HTTP 200 does not mark the whole batch authoritative.
13. **Fair lasso:** An actual fair cycle can refute the selected eventuality; a depth-limited nonterminal prefix is not proof of infinite divergence.
14. **Conditional recovery:** A retained command outcome may settle under named availability, fairness and authorized action premises; a user who never grants Refresh or a permanent partition prevents an unconditional convergence claim.
15. **Scoped result:** A finite source-level pass is reported as finite; a copied diagnostic or process-local gateway observation cannot issue proof/physical authority.
16. **Checked abstraction:** An independently justified concrete-to-finite initial/step/property relation supports its named extended domain; finite samples without that bridge remain samples.
17. **Real adapter/host distinction:** An exercised browser adapter transition matches a real permitted installed DOM effect when independently observed; pure CLJC simulation is not proof that Chromium/HTMX executed every possible schedule.
18. **Positive HumanHelp:** Creator-local adoption and one user-permitted remote Refresh remain exercisable along with required lifecycle actions; removing them from finite exploration is not independent product completion.

Each pair fixes the original source, selected model and checker, exact bound and property, positive scenario, adverse mutation, first checking/enforcement boundary, actual observation and remaining assumption *before execution*. Local semantic rejection cannot prove that an unobserved raw physical writer was stopped; an incomplete exploration is not complete safety, and unobserved physical outcomes remain unknown.

## Editorial outcome

10.11 retains R5's superior executable resource-ownership illustration while restoring R4's proof transfer and freshness/retirement obligations in precise form. It now distinguishes full finite graph exploration, independent base/step enumeration, original-source completeness, concrete-to-abstract simulation, identity/data abstraction, divergent liveness, current physical effect ownership and positive application behavior. This is a timeless Gesso design target, not a claim that today's experimental bounded oracles prove the deployed system.

**Nonclaims:** No new executable suite, independent mechanized abstraction theorem, physical browser/XTDB all-schedule proof, finished multi-node progress theorem, full HumanHelp acceptance or independent Part X editorial acceptance was established in this turn.

*** Writer coverage reaches beyond HTTP routes

The original operation catalog supplies the *abstract operation set*.
The deployed writer inventory supplies the claimed *physical coverage*.
Those are joined by checked operation identity, relevant guard/frame
footprints and actual effects, not inferred from matching source names.
A source-complete induction proof can be correct for its admitted machine
while a direct XTDB caller invalidates the corresponding physical invariant.
The missing fact is production correspondence or enforcement, not a flaw
that may be fixed by silently deleting the physical writer from the report.

For every protected claim, Gesso must identify the state, collection,
relation, tenant, authority and history it intends to cover and the
potential mutations that can invalidate that property. The inventory
includes ordinary routes, non-HTTP background jobs, imports, service
consumers, scheduled work, administrative and maintenance tools,
migrations, initializers, other processes and external services that can
write through independently held backend credentials. A handler need
not be an HTTP handler and every writer need not have a route. Requiring
a fabricated route for a scheduled operation would confuse placement
with semantic ownership.

A host-supplied enumerator establishes only what that enumerator
actually observed at the named process, registry and generation.
“No inventory available” is *unknown*, never an empty closed
inventory. A source-relative exact operation-ID join is useful but
does not establish that differently named or undiscovered physical
writers are absent, that a callable has its declared behavior, or
that the running service uses the checked registration.

**** A scoped physical writer universe

A strong whole-application claim declares its *protected physical
universe*, including the exact backend/provider and deployment
assumptions, not merely a list of approved Clojure Vars. The record
identifies at least:

- protected domain and property: real Request, RequestAssignment,
  command ledger and any other relevant persisted state, relationship,
  absence/uniqueness predicate, revision/history or effect;
- authority domain: tenant/Organization, allowed principals and service
  identities, current policy, and the intended set of admitted
  operations and explicitly permitted operational transitions;
- effect-capable entrypoint classes: HTTP, worker, scheduler,
  queue consumer, system bootstrap/initializer, migration,
  maintenance CLI, administrator, other service and direct provider
  credential holders;
- actual capability path: credentials, connection pools, clients,
  mutable dependency injection, route and callback/worker bindings,
  executable/handler generations and provider-side grants that permit
  mutation even when no Gesso operation is invoked;
- mutation and conflict footprints: PUT/patch/delete/erase/SQL and
  transaction/vector calls, entire replaced documents, protected
  predicates, expected revision and any domain-specific constraints;
- observation/coverage: the independent inventory source,
  installation scope, last successful reconciliation, open
  unclassified or inaccessible paths, and evidence that the
  enforcing boundary actually covers every relevant mutation;
- lifetime and change control: deployment/credential generation,
  startup and shutdown, dynamic worker creation, route replacement,
  job promotion, provider rotation, external operational access
  and the conditions that invalidate the coverage judgment.

The universe is relative to a stated threat and deployment model.
A capability restriction can exclude a path only when actual backend,
process or credential enforcement prevents that path from writing the
protected domain. A value in a host-declared capability table
=:restriction :host-declared= is not such enforcement. A user
who can import an unrestricted XTDB client, a service retaining
database write credentials, or an operator with direct update access
must be accounted for by real confinement, a separately controlled
exception contract or an explicit still-open premise.

An inventory may be *complete relative to the inspected host
registry* yet incomplete relative to all processes or privileged
writers. These are different claims. The consumer must not infer
universal closed-world coverage by taking the union of several
individually incomplete enumerators. Instead it must justify
the relevant universe and its partition into observed/mediated,
constrained external, and specifically qualified open paths.

**** Three independently established closure judgments

The framework keeps three closure questions distinct.

| Closure subject | What sufficient evidence establishes | Invalid shortcut |
|-----------------+-------------------------------------+------------------|
| Original abstract operation closure | Every original required initializer, operation, invariant and semantic dependency is represented in the checked machine | Accepting the caller's smaller operation list as the source |
| Observed deployed entrypoint and writer correspondence | Each inventoried current host route, worker or other entrypoint is matched to its original operation or explicitly scoped operational writer, callable and declared physical effects | Equating an operation name, stale route table or host-supplied list with exhaustive production discovery |
| Enforced physical writer closure | Every actual mutation capable of invalidating the selected property is mediated by a conforming protected boundary or covered by an independently qualified exception/provider assumption | Assuming “zero unclassified observed writers” means no hidden raw or external XTDB client exists |

An original semantic catalog may be complete while an installed
application has an additional writer. An observed inventory may contain
every registered route while an initializer or service principal writes
directly. A backend-enforced physical closure claim can use a
different implementation from the preferred Gesso gateway, but it
must demonstrate the same protected source-to-effect refinement
at the boundary it actually owns. The strongest claim cannot be
issued by concatenating a source-side proof and a copyable inventory
summary without that missing enforcement argument.

The source-derived catalog should independently account for
operation and initializer obligations; an installed-application
inspection should independently account for route, worker, dynamic
target, server startup and rendered affordance identities.
Source closure and product capability completeness also differ:
an internally closed application missing required HumanHelp
Unclaim or Reassign cannot pass the positive product gate.
A registered job that writes protected state must have its own
corresponding operation/initializer or explicit privileged
maintenance contract; it is not required to masquerade as a route.

**** Mediation, enforcement and operational exceptions

The preferred supported path constrains physical writers through
the admitted source-bound gateway and the qualified backend guards
from 10.05–10.06. A production-wide assurance claim additionally
needs evidence about *access to the backend*:

1. Only specified service principals and operator roles can acquire
   protected mutation authority; grants, network routes and backend
   capabilities are checked at the actual deployment boundary.
2. Normal application ingress paths receive only the appropriate
   source-bound executor/callback or least-privileged backend
   capability. The raw client used by a gateway must not be
   casually exposed through unrelated application closures.
3. A mutation through any class of privileged writer must satisfy
   the matching operation's authenticated authority, transaction-
   internal guard, protected frame, command/result and revision
   discipline, or its explicitly reviewed operational exception.
4. Known bypass-capable credentials or direct clients are either
   technically barred from the covered domain, included under
   an independently enforced and checked contract, or retained
   as open trusted premises that prevent the stronger claim.
5. Current deployed handler/worker/callback and credential
   generation must still match the inspected/issued assembly
   at the time of use. A previously complete startup
   snapshot is not proof of later runtime stability.

An operational exception is not a general “administrator bypass”
with automatically preserved invariants. It names who can invoke
it, its permitted domain and mutation, the required approval and
audit, the protected before/after or migration relation, whether
the invariant is maintained or temporarily suspended, and the
conditions under which stronger dependent certification resumes.
A break-glass write can be necessary, but its presence changes the
scope of any unqualified whole-domain proof.

A release migration can change schemas, rebuild assignments,
reset revisions or create initial records. If those effects are
outside the proved operation catalog, the deployment must either
prove that the migration refines an admitted transition, or
establish a new valid initial state and requalify the affected
invariant and physical realization afterward. Checking that an
abstract invariant held *before* migration does not prove it
holds afterward. A startup initializer has the same
physical responsibility even if it runs only once.

If an external provider enforces a relevant uniqueness,
transaction isolation or credential restriction, the
specific provider version, configuration, scope and failure
semantics enter the claim as checked evidence or a named
external trust premise. Merely using XTDB, Biff, a
container sandbox or a particular route framework
does not automatically provide those guarantees.

**** Dynamic entrypoints, multi-process identity and TOCTOU

Real deployments may change handlers, start workers, restart
nodes, rotate database credentials or replace callbacks while
the application continues serving. Inventory freshness,
currentness and the order of checking/activation matter.
A process-local private registry can strengthen one
in-process caller-origin check without authenticating
the actual serving process, a remote node, the physical
database client or a later deployment generation.

The selected assurance profile must specify how the
actual writer-enforcing boundary remains valid after
startup. A correctly checked route-to-writer table
whose handler is later replaced with a raw XTDB
client no longer establishes current coverage.
A positive request that passed an earlier registry
snapshot does not prove all concurrent or future
requests went through that registered writer.

A robust design may use generation-scoped
capability issuance, immutable checked routing,
runtime enforcement plus ongoing qualified
revalidation, provider-level permission separation,
or another demonstrably sufficient mechanism.
The chosen mechanism must close the relevant
check-to-use gap rather than simply run a
second enumerator after the write.
A post-effect discrepancy is useful
detection, never retroactive prevention.

Multiple nodes do not require a second model of
Claim or a new category of participant authority.
They do require qualification of *all* node,
service, scheduler, administrative and external
writer paths in the protected universe.
A failed host may lose process-local registry
state while its previously submitted transaction
can still incorporate; retry and command history
must remain durable and appropriately scoped.
Router interest propagation, outbox publication
and node-loss replay are different from
database write authority. An invalidation
re-delivered safely may be harmless, but it
cannot create an unmodeled domain write
or a forbidden automatic remote board read.

**** HumanHelp one-primary and command-history coverage

For the real Claim application, enumerate
actual Request and RequestAssignment mutation
entrypoints, plus relevant Membership,
Organization and Location policy dependencies.
An actor can be eligible under the abstract
Claim contract while a raw migration or
manager operation produces a second active
primary outside the constrained model.
The abstract theorem still describes its
declared transitions; the whole-domain
physical theorem does not follow.

A stronger end-to-end Claim invariant requires:
the source-derived original Claim/Unclaim/
Reassign and lifecycle catalog; protected
concurrent assignments including absence
and uniqueness; real public model guards;
all relevant normal and operational writers;
transaction-local current-state/frame
correspondence; durable command identity
and result; and actual installed call sites.
A changed Membership or Organization
may alter authorization before the Claim
commits even if that model is not
written by Claim itself. The physical
guard dependency must therefore be
protected under the qualified isolation
contract, not added spuriously to the
write footprint just to make a catalog
look complete.

A direct maintenance writer might
still be permitted under a
separately approved operation.
It cannot be used to claim
the original all-operations
one-primary theorem without
matching induction/transition
coverage or an explicit
reestablished initial
state. A copyable
=complete?= report
does not grant administrative
authority or turn an unknown
writer universe into
an empty one.

**** Adversarial coverage and honest reporting

Coverage tests require a valid
source/installed operation
and a deliberately selected
negative neighboring writer,
not a reject-all system.
At minimum, demonstrate:

- A registered HTTP Claim
  and separately registered
  authorized scheduled
  operation are both
  accounted for under
  their correct actual
  entrypoint classes;
  the job is not
  assigned a fake route.
- An original source-complete
  operation catalog joined
  with a current route/
  issued-writer table is
  reported as exactly that;
  inserting a raw writer
  the enumerator did not
  report cannot be
  considered disproved.
- A declared table-wide
  =:put-docs= or =:sql-dml=
  capability touching
  the protected domain
  cannot be reduced
  to only the currently
  listed documents.
- A genuine enforced
  read-only role cannot
  mutate the protected
  domain; a host map
  claiming =:read-only=
  while the same
  credential can write
  fails the strong
  closure claim.
- A live admin/migration
  writer that changes
  RequestAssignment
  must carry an
  appropriate source/
  operational transition
  contract; a hidden
  backdoor breaks
  unqualified one-primary
  physical safety.
- A startup initializer
  and later maintenance
  phase satisfy their
  declared initial-state
  and requalification
  obligations; a
  migration that silently
  resets revision/ABA
  history does not.
- A current checked
  Biff handler actually
  invokes its registered
  writer during one
  admitted request;
  source-matched
  registration alone
  cannot certify the
  real deployed
  server or other
  handler generations.
- An async callback
  or worker uses
  explicitly scoped
  service principal
  and execution
  capability; it
  cannot borrow a
  retired request's
  thread-local
  writer lease.
- A privileged
  direct XTDB client
  is excluded by
  real provider/
  capability enforcement
  or retained as
  a named premise;
  a missing enumerator
  entry cannot
  close the universe.
- A new deployment
  generation changes
  an actual handler,
  callable or
  credential; affected
  physical correspondence
  is invalidated,
  while unrelated
  abstract proofs
  can remain valid
  where qualified.
- Two real helpers
  race for a Request
  while another
  permitted writer
  mutates its
  assignment/revision;
  the protected
  one-primary
  invariant remains
  enforced or the
  additional writer
  is shown
  outside the claim.
- A failed transaction
  and dropped/duplicated
  outbox notice preserve
  actual command
  history and permitted
  advisory recovery;
  a router cannot
  turn a notification
  into a new Claim
  or unauthorized
  collection read.

Each test names the original source,
protected domain, capability path,
required source or operational
transition, selected host/credential
generation, first enforcing
boundary and an independent
effect observer appropriate to
its scope. A static mismatch
can reject a claim before
execution; a controlled
post-dispatch observer can
detect a difference *after*
an effect. Neither can
prove that all other physical
writers are absent without
the separate closure mechanism.

Root assurance reports separate
the following conclusions:

| Report result | Meaning |
|---------------+---------|
| =:source-catalog-closed= | Every original required abstract operation/initializer obligation in the inspected source is represented |
| =:registered-paths-classified= | Every writer actually returned by the scoped host enumerator is classified under the inspected generation |
| =:declared-capabilities-covered= | Supplied writer target/mutation declarations correspond to the protected-domain declaration in their stated finite scope |
| =:installed-mediation-observed= | One or more actual selected installed call sites/requests were observed with identified source and writer |
| =:physical-writer-closure-established= | An independently qualified enforcing boundary excludes or constrains *every* relevant writer within a precisely declared deployed universe |
| =:physical-writer-closure-unknown= | The actual universe or its enforcement has not been established; an empty inventory is not sufficient |

The labels above are *normative
report concepts*, not a claim
that today's Gesso exports
these keywords or that a
caller can assert
=:physical-writer-closure-established=
in an untrusted map.
The issuer applies the
selected profile and
the actual supporting
evidence. A checked
source theorem, matched
operation-ID lists
and a convenient
inventory do not
produce the last
result by arithmetic.

The final requirement
is deliberately
strong: every real
effect capable of
invalidating the
claimed invariant
must be included,
restricted, or
explicitly assumed
under a scope
that the claim
truthfully discloses.

** Completion gates

HumanHelp is the proving application for the finished Gesso design,
not a permanently privileged demonstration or a convenient subset
of the currently working UI. Completion requires a coherent
installed production-backed application and independent evidence
that its promised user capabilities work under the selected,
qualified substrate. The gates are normative acceptance
obligations; neither their presence in this document nor a green
source-derived assembly means that the current implementation
has already met them.

An acceptance claim names its exact application source and
admitted generation, code/build/artifact identity, deployed
handler and browser assets, authentication and disclosure
profile, relevant XTDB/Biff/HTMX/CLJS versions and configuration,
tested actor/Request/Organization scope, operational
assumptions, known opaque boundaries, and observed evidence.
It is invalidated or narrowed when any required binding,
source, runtime, writer coverage or provider contract changes
without applicable requalification. A partial result is
reported as partial, with the independent failed, missing,
conditional or untested gates still visible.

*** Non-vacuous gate set and independent acceptance

The fourteen gates below are distinct obligations, not
fourteen names for a single passing source check.
Each has at least one eligible positive and its applicable
negative/fault case; completed checks must exercise the
*real* enforcing or user-facing boundary rather than an
uninstalled fixture or a copied result.

| Gate | Acceptance obligation |
|------+-----------------------|
| 1. Production model authority | Request creation and lifecycle mutations use public production Request and related User, Membership, Organization/Location semantics; current guarded Request/RequestAssignment transactions and actor/target policy produce the model-defined outcomes, including actual duplicate and unknown-command handling. |
| 2. One admitted application source | Choreo, role-local plans, routes, server operations, trusted providers, Live/acquisition, concrete rendering and checking consume one source-derived ApplicationAssembly. Tests inspect that construction; they do not manufacture a second implementation whose internal consistency hides production drift. |
| 3. Installed host and writer adoption | The actual serving Biff/Aleph handler and protected mutation paths use the admitted construction and declared capabilities; the selected policy qualifies relevant outside writers and exposes unknown writer coverage rather than assuming registration is exhaustive. |
| 4. Current browser and asset correspondence | Generated plans, build manifest/receipt, actual served JavaScript and dependent assets, client bootstrap and deployment generation agree under the selected qualification. Incompatible, stale or forged bindings fail before they can authorize protected effects. |
| 5. Claim end to end | A genuine rendered enabled Claim control generates its own command in the actual browser, reaches the trusted route, commits the guarded Request/primary assignment and settles physically; fresh authorized read and reload confirm persisted truth. |
| 6. All lifecycle capabilities | Claim, Unclaim, Mark on the way, Complete, Cancel and Reassign each have an independently eligible browser-originated success; Reassign uses real authorized target selection, and each has meaningful current-policy denial, concurrency and applicable recovery evidence. |
| 7. Rich-board product behavior | Real Create, creator-local visibility, adopted-cohort search and sort, both priority toggles, terminal options, details, accessible status, focus/scroll continuity and the independently selected response interaction behave as the declared HumanHelp product contract requires. |
| 8. Selective multi-client adoption | Distinct authenticated users/contexts demonstrate that remote creation and remote lifecycle changes, including already-visible cards, remain advisory until explicit permitted Refresh or a separately admitted local-result/recovery acquisition; local incorporation cannot adopt unrelated remote members or erase their debt. |
| 9. Explicit Refresh correctness | A user action creates a bounded scoped one-use read/adoption grant; actual authoritative observation and installed coverage justify precisely the discharged requirements, with protected handling of later advisories, stale responses, partial swaps and retry. |
| 10. Fault and recovery behavior | Lost/unknown command outcomes, already-incorporated replay, failed acquisition/installation, postcommit publication crash, lost SSE continuity, deployment changes and retired callbacks take the declared safe and usable recovery paths, with truthful uncertainty and bounded resource ownership. |
| 11. Structural and runtime enforcement | Controlled malformed operation, assembly, plan, route, provider, render, native-form/HTMX action, protocol, asset, handler, and physical-effect cases reject at their first real applicable boundaries; paired valid production cases remain usable. |
| 12. Semantic and assurance evidence | Application/business invariants, finite/conditional proof scopes, initial states, complete declared operations, independent required capability coverage, source-to-issued-execution bindings, interference and opaque premises are described accurately; unqualified physical writers and missing refinements are not certified by a generated report. |
| 13. Qualified physical substrate | Selected Biff lifecycle, HTMX/SSE browser adapter, CLJS/compiler/artifact supply, XTDB guards/absence/progression, authentication, publication/recovery and actual resource behavior satisfy the assumptions needed for each claimed gate, or the claim remains explicitly conditional/open. |
| 14. Independent real-browser acceptance | Chromium/Playwright uses the installed service and generated runtime with genuine controls, sessions and requests, current model readback after reload, meaningful two-client scenarios, deterministic backend races and narrow fault expectations; deleting a required capability or suppressing unexpected failures makes this gate fail. |

R4's eleven original completion gates are preserved in
this more explicit partition: semantic-route exclusion,
runtime adoption, artifact currentness, real Claim, the
full lifecycle, rich board, multi-client behavior,
production-constructor tests, malformed fixtures,
Chromium tests and supported Biff/Gesso substrate.
Extra gates make recovery, explicit Refresh,
source/physical proof and dependency qualification
visible rather than assuming they follow from a
green browser test. The original gates do not
become optional simply because they now have
different numbering and clearer ownership.

*** Product contract decisions, profiles and exclusions

The required user capabilities are independent of
the current implementation's registered operation
list. Removing Reassign from Request Choreo and
from the derived finite machine does not make
the reduced application acceptable if HumanHelp
still promises Reassign. Permanently disabling
all Claim actions is likewise not an acceptable
way to satisfy negative tests. A particular
Request may legitimately deny a command under
current model policy while another eligible
fixture satisfies the positive capability.

The selected remote-lifecycle adoption decision
at [[#decision-humanhelp-remote-lifecycle-adoption]]
requires explicit Refresh for ordinary remote
creation *and* lifecycle changes, including a
Request that was previously visible. That
choice is normative. A locally correlated
settlement or permitted affected-Request
recovery read has its own narrower grant;
neither silently grants remote collection
adoption. Loss of authenticated authority is
a separate safety transition: protected
projection and future trusted access are
revoked according to the supported client
contract, rather than delayed to honor a
user's Refresh preference.

The independent response-flow requirement
at [[#decision-humanhelp-response-flow]]
must be selected by HumanHelp product authority,
with actor eligibility, intent, source/model
owner, input, disclosure, observable success,
persistence/external effects and adverse
recovery behavior. A working Claim is not
automatically proof of the intended response
interaction. If HumanHelp expressly chooses
"respond by claiming", its own versioned
product contract and real user acceptance
must establish that this fulfills the complete
promise; otherwise the response-flow gate
remains open. No new messaging model,
provider or contact-disclosure policy is
invented solely to close a checklist.

The primary strong publication/recovery
profile at 08.08 requires a qualified
transaction-coupled publication intent and
its durable at-least-once recovery worker.
A selected weaker periodic-reconciliation
or user-Refresh profile has different
liveness and crash guarantees and must be
accepted as an explicit product/deployment
choice with its own tested behavior and
disclosed limitations. It cannot silently
borrow the primary profile's durable
delivery or timely notification claim.
Exactly the same discipline applies to
command history retention, backend writer
coverage, Live continuity, application
generation pinning and external effects.

An unsupported extension may be excluded
only by a precise declared boundary that
does not undercut an independent required
HumanHelp capability. For example, a
profile excluding arbitrary JavaScript
requests still protects actual managed
HTML and trusted lifecycle ingress;
the exclusion does not justify bypassing
the real Claim button or its server route.
Unknown or unsupported is not "passed".

*** Whole-path safety, evidence and causality

A completed HumanHelp Claim has a real
actor and Request, source-owned semantic
operation and browser plan, eligible
rendered affordance, actual compatible
served runtime, browser-generated command,
route-selected trusted executor, qualified
guarded transaction, truthful outcome,
scoped current read and physically
settled adopted result. The evidence chain
identifies each of these as an actual
binding, observed effect or explicitly
accepted premise. A successful UI
animation alone cannot establish
authoritative mutation.

Create with selective adoption, a
legitimate target-helper Reassign and
the one-use Refresh journey likewise
need connected positive outcomes.
Multiple plausible snapshots do not
prove one adjacent execution history;
the report must connect command,
transaction, actor, read and physical
installation with adequate correlation
and trusted provenance. A matching
model abstraction does not establish
that every physical XTDB writer refines
it. An exactly matching browser
manifest cannot prove arbitrary
compiled JavaScript correct.

A meaningful negative acceptance
states whether the forbidden request
was never emitted, denied at trusted
ingress, rejected by current model
policy, excluded atomically by XTDB,
or prevented from physically
installing. These are distinct
claims. A valid commit followed by
failed HTML/OOB delivery remains
committed and requires authorized
recovery; postcommit output failure
cannot be credited as a precommit
domain rejection. A lost command
response may leave the historical
outcome unknown even if the current
Request state is freshly observed.

The desired final invariant includes
both safety and qualified progress.
A retired callback cannot reinstall
obsolete DOM or consume one-use
Refresh permission. Unknown C42
must have its declared bounded
recovery/disclosure path; if the
evidence horizon expires, honest
indeterminacy is permitted. An
already-connected viewer whose
remote board has changed may
remain on older adopted content
indefinitely when the chosen
policy requires an explicit
Refresh the user never performs.
The pending advisory, safe action
and scope of its coverage remain
visible as the user-facing
progress contract; automatic
remote adoption is not introduced
to manufacture convergence.

*** HumanHelp evidence and release decisions

For each gate the independent acceptance
record gives a state such as satisfied
under a named profile, conditional on
enumerated premises, open/untested or
failed; it does not collapse these
into one green "complete" label.
It records the source, code/asset
and host identity, test suite and
actual execution results, adversarial
coverage, responsible owner,
assumptions and expiration or
requalification triggers.
A passing test *definition* is
not proof it was executed, and a
caller-reported observation is not
authenticated by a report digest.

The final installed HumanHelp application
must have real independently authenticated
multi-client browser contexts, matching
source-to-host and source-to-asset
evidence, genuine production model
transactions, actual target/adoption
observations and fault outcomes at
their claimed boundaries. The real
host's ability to serve unrelated
ordinary routes does not excuse
missing managed-operation enforcement.
All proposed positive and adverse
capabilities must be tested with the
actual qualified backend/adapter when
the gate requires physical evidence.
The suite must not ignore unexpected
console errors, failed HTTP requests,
domain exceptions or missing assets
by an overbroad diagnostic waiver.

No earlier or later proof-certificate
claim is created merely by satisfying
these HumanHelp product gates.
Parts IX and X own compositional
certification and stronger
implementation correspondence;
Parts XI and XII own release and
roadmap coherence. HumanHelp's
function is to exercise those
reusable Gesso interfaces and
surface counterexamples, not to
introduce HumanHelp-specific
semantics into the proof kernel.
The source-derived assembly may
be called closed for its admitted
scope while wider writer coverage,
physical refinement or independent
business correctness remain open.
Such a result is useful but is
not full HumanHelp completion.

#+begin_example
Positive — eligible joined acceptance:
  Two distinct authenticated HumanHelp clients start
    with a qualified production Request cohort.
  A's real Claim reaches guarded Request/assignment commit;
    a fresh reload confirms actual model truth.
  A creates S, and B receives an authorized advisory
    without automatically adopting S.
  Manager M reassigns R17 to an eligible target helper,
    with exactly one active primary after commit.
  B explicitly Refreshes and installs the scoped authority;
    only covered advisory requirements are discharged.
  Independently, every required lifecycle and selected
    response-flow capability passes its positive case.
  The installed source, asset, host, model and physical
    evidence support each claim under the named profile.
#+end_example

#+begin_example
Adversarial — green reduced application is incomplete:
  Remove Reassign and its matching plan from the source.
  All remaining declared structural and finite checks pass.
  Independent HumanHelp capability acceptance fails:
    a promised eligible Reassign is unavailable.
  A copied assurance report cannot change that result.
#+end_example

#+begin_example
Adversarial — partial success and unqualified recovery:
  Genuine Claim commits with a correct RequestAssignment.
  Response delivery fails; C42 history is still unknown
    to the browser, although current Request truth can be read.
  Publication intent is only an in-memory callback.
  The application may report committed Request truth and
    bounded best-effort Live, but not durable crash recovery.
  If response flow is unselected, full product acceptance
    remains open independently of Claim's success.
#+end_example

** HumanHelp proving-application invariant

#+begin_quote
The finished HumanHelp proving application consumes the
authoritative =net.humanhelp.site.model.*= public production
semantics for User, Membership, Organization, Request and
RequestAssignment. One canonical, source-owned
ApplicationAssembly binds Request Choreo, its actual
browser plans/assets, concrete trusted routes and server
operations, required capabilities, Live/acquisition and
rendered affordances to the installed Biff/Aleph
application generation. It does not maintain a competing
example Request store, business-rule machine, revision
frontier or operation registry.

Every enabled lifecycle action retains its exact
source-owned semantic identity and is executable only
through an admitted generated browser and trusted
production model boundary. Missing optimism, observation,
route, plan or authorized recovery never silently
degrades to an anonymous lifecycle POST. Actual Claim,
Unclaim, Mark on the way, Complete, Cancel and Reassign
are available in independently eligible cases and
remain subject to current authenticated actor/target,
Organization/Membership, Request state and guarded
Request/RequestAssignment transaction semantics.
Historical incorporation, authoritative rejection,
uncertain delivery and currently displayed Request
truth are separate propositions.

One user's correlated confirmed Create or lifecycle
result may promptly update only the permitted local
Request/card and membership scope. Remote creation and
remote lifecycle changes, including those to an
already-visible Request, remain advisory until a
separate explicitly permitted Refresh or affected-
Request recovery acquisition. Search, sort, terminal
options, drafts, response interaction, details and
continuity operate on justified adopted content
without inventing authoritative collection membership.
A one-use Refresh establishes the actual scoped
observation, installation and coverage needed to
discharge its corresponding debt; a stale response,
failed swap, unrelated local result or reconnect
cannot erase uncovered or later obligations.

Each protected execution, target, transport,
subscription, grant and recovery owner has bounded
resources and current physical effect rights.
Retirement prevents obsolete callbacks from
installing or rolling back newer justified state.
Actual model commits are not undone by failed
notification or rendering, and unknown outcomes
and disconnected interest gaps follow their
declared qualified recovery rules.

Independent HumanHelp product intent and real
multi-client Chromium/browser, public model,
XTDB/host, negative-boundary and physical
observation evidence establish only their
properly scoped conclusions. Formal finite
checks, conditional abstract proofs, source
admission, operational qualification and
deployed correspondence never impersonate
one another. Full acceptance requires every
promised capability—including the separately
selected and tested response-flow experience—
and the applicable safety, recovery, evidence
and substrate gates to be satisfied for the
identified application and runtime profile.
#+end_quote

This is the Part VIII *target-system invariant*,
not an assertion that current HumanHelp or
Gesso source, existing test definitions or an
R5.1 editorial review has already certified
the physical application. Further whole-Part
VIII and whole-plan reconciliation may sharpen
cross-part terminology, assumptions and
proof obligations without changing the
independent product acceptance principle.

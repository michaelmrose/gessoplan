## Preservation decisions against R4

1. **Production Request truth remains in production models.** R4 requires the model layer to own business rules, transaction guards and outcomes independently of HTMX, browser and example UI. Retained, with the actual Request/RequestAssignment aggregate and Organization/Location/User/Membership boundaries explicit.
2. **Choreo does not duplicate domain policy.** R4's Request Choreo calls public Request operations and coordinates participants/settlement; it does not create an alternative authorization engine. This is preserved as a source-owned semantic/projection relationship.
3. **All six lifecycle capabilities remain independent products.** Claim, Unclaim, Mark on the way, Complete, Cancel and Reassign require separate eligible positives. Deriving a reduced formal subject from a reduced UI inventory cannot certify HumanHelp; deletion of Reassign from both the source and derived model remains a product failure.
4. **Claim is a real guarded atomic aggregate transition.** R4's X17 basis is observation context, not a global stale-command rejector. Current Request, assignment absence, actor/helper dependencies and relevant guard/write set must justify commit; unrelated progression must not invalidate a valid claim. The revised specification retains this model and adds qualified representation/trace evidence.
5. **Real browser/production acceptance cannot be replaced by tests of a fabricated protocol.** R4 model, Choreo, preflight, rendered-surface, server integration and Chromium layers retain distinct oracles; 08.12 owns the detailed test matrix. Section 08.11 sets their proof and evidence boundaries without reimplementing test harness requirements.
6. **Adversarial and recovery paths remain mandatory.** Concurrent Claim, changed Membership, inaccurate target, command replay, unknown outcome, stale browser installation and insufficient acquisition all remain challenge cases.
7. **The completed plan's claims remain conditional where necessary.** R4's connected positive evidence is required, but a report that one hand-authored model matches itself cannot establish physical correspondence or a correct human policy.

## Gesso source correspondence (inspection, not claimed test execution)

- `src/gesso/model/formal_obligation.clj` independently describes safety properties of a *finite serial prepared-transaction subject* and explicitly does not claim HumanHelp authorization, XTDB refinement, unbounded theorem or full writer coverage.
- `src/gesso/model/formal_check.clj` uses Recife 0.22.0/TLC to explore the selected finite prepared machine. Its green result is diagnostic for those reachable states only, not a durable independent certificate or proof that the modeled plans include every physical state-dependent writer.
- `src/gesso/model/formal_family.clj` expands finite application-owned principal/argument choices at one selected prestate; its own documentation says every selected choice must be enabled and precomputed, and that later state-dependent re-preparation needs a separate reachable checker.
- `src/gesso/model/relational_invariant.clj` implements small finite relation rules with stated complete-state and non-vacuity limitations; the rule constructor does not prove HumanHelp's full Request/assignment/Organization relation is represented correctly.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/induction.clj` checks source-derived initial/preservation derivation across a closed *declared* abstract operation catalog, but explicitly leaves proof-kernel soundness, physical writer completeness, actual backend correspondence and certificate admission open.
- `src/gesso/model/inductive_plan.clj` binds source-checked conditional abstract induction to an issued plan. It does not assert that the deployed handler invokes it or that its backend transition is refined.
- `src/gesso/assurance/application_induction.clj` joins qualified source-level catalog and issued-plan evidence and retains open physical prestate, actual write-path and source/XTDB refinement obligations.
- `src/gesso/assurance/observed_correspondence.clj` and `src/gesso/model/transition_conformance.clj` compare selected bounded abstract/physical observations but do not authenticate arbitrary caller-supplied histories, physical chronology or all writers.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/physical_writer_inventory.clj` and `src/gesso/assurance/proof/physical_writer_capability_coverage.clj` derive host-observed/declared writer coverage but explicitly do not prove enumerator completeness or actually restrict external processes and arbitrary XTDB writes.
- `src/gesso/model/tx_refinement_premises.clj` and `src/gesso/model/tx_refinement_evidence.clj` keep versioned backend/refinement obligations open when no qualified proof is available.
- The v1622 `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_command_outcome_bridge.clj` joins one specific submitted-command return, pinned readback and reported outcome, but disclaims causal attribution, general authorization, complete writer coverage or browser settlement.

These implementations are meaningful narrower precursors. Their explicit non-established scopes are a reason to keep the *finished* R5.1 requirements, not a reason to omit them or claim conformance already exists.

## HumanHelp implementation correspondence and limits

- `src/net/humanhelp/site/model/request/domain.clj` owns pure Request and RequestAssignment business/state semantics, including `:open`, `:claimed`, `:on-the-way`, `:done`, `:cancelled`. It explicitly disclaims cross-document assignment uniqueness and cross-model foreign existence without Request Graph/FX and real transaction coordination.
- `src/net/humanhelp/site/model/request/fx.clj` owns dependency acquisition and guarded transaction plans using public Membership/Organization APIs. Request is the concurrency root; assignment mutations also update Request revision. It requires explicit current time/seed capabilities where used. That is a concrete implementation strategy, not independent proof of every physical writer.
- `src/net/humanhelp/site/model/request/core.clj` is the public boundary exposing trusted model operations, assignment/aggregate reads and dependency-planning contracts. Tests must consume this boundary instead of authoring a parallel fixture mutation engine.
- `src/net/humanhelp/site/model/request/choreo.clj` builds browser plans and role projections for all six lifecycle operations; it delegates guarded Request authority to the public core instead of treating browser-supplied participant roles as authenticated principals.
- `test/net/humanhelp/site/model/request_claim_integration_test.clj` contains a real XTDB Claim/RequestAssignment atomicity and rejected second Claim integration test. Its foreign-model Membership/Organization dependencies are stubbed at public seams, so the test does not establish full physical cross-model dependency enforcement or all possible interleavings. This file was inspected, not run.
- No existing test or source inspection proves that all physical HumanHelp/XTDB writers, every available helper state, all backend snapshots, every lifecycle product positive and physical browser effects are covered. The normative target explicitly retains those independent requirements.

## Positive and adversarial review cases

1. Eligible Claim has one primary assignment after an actual guarded commit; fresh read and command outcome agree.
2. Two eligible competing helpers cannot both create a new active primary; controlled overlapping submissions exercise actual backend guards, not merely concurrent button clicks.
3. The configured state/assignment invariant distinguishes “at most one” from required existence in assigned state.
4. Reassign uses a separately authorized target helper, rejects stale/ineligible target and preserves model-defined status/assignment history.
5. A competing Membership revocation or Organization policy change is modeled as a writer, supported repair, or explicit premise.
6. A location/tenant-scoped read cannot produce positive or negative evidence outside its permitted query/disclosure domain.
7. Create, Unclaim, Mark on the way, Complete and Cancel retain independent positive and negative cases.
8. Unknown C42 from a lost outcome is not converted to a rejected transaction or a new C43 retry.
9. Compatible replay after Claim or later Reassign does not reincorporate a historical command; a current card read does not fabricate command attribution.
10. Source-derived finite exploration exposes exact bounded actor/state/operation domains, tested guards and impossible/unexercised branches.
11. Removing Reassign or disabling every Claim fails independent positive coverage despite a green closed model.
12. A simple prepared-plan permutation cannot be used as evidence for changing enablement/reprepared operations after a prior transition.
13. An uninspected raw XTDB, background or administrative writer remains an open application-interference obligation.
14. A copied command-result snapshot and unrelated predecessor/poststate cannot be stitched into one certified physical execution trace.
15. A version/configuration change to XTDB or a supported host invalidates dependent assumptions until independently requalified.
16. Local S selective adoption cannot consume unrelated remote R or erase one-use Refresh debt; real DOM/advisory behavior needs its own evidence.

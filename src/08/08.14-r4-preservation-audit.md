## R4 preservation decisions

1. **Connected evidence is a real cross-boundary chain.** R4 required an actual rendered operation, generated browser plan and artifact, trusted route/executor, authorized model outcome, Live publication, acquisition and physical installation, all from the production assembly and observed execution. This section preserves each link while forbidding false causality from matching names, report-shaped maps or a list of individually plausible state snapshots.
2. **A prior valid commit cannot be undone by a later output failure.** R4 explicitly separates malformed pre-submit rejection from committed Request truth, lost settlement delivery and late physical installation. The revised report distinguishes no-submission, committed-but-undelivered, and unknown-to-participant evidence.
3. **Publication is downstream of model authority.** R4's Live publication is an advisory about newer authority, not a second Request store or an automatically adopted remote card. R5 adds a stronger transaction-coupled durable publication-intent profile with at-least-once retry; it remains explicitly conditional on backend qualification.
4. **Creator-local and remote adoption remain separate.** R4's creator may learn its own correlated result without broadcast. Another viewer's new Request remains advisory until explicit Refresh. The selected R5.1 remote-lifecycle policy also keeps already-visible remote changes advisory until qualified user approval.
5. **Refresh debt survives races and partial output.** R4's one-use Refresh, scoped acquisition and physical success criteria survive. Covered obligations discharge only after actually permitted installation; a successful response or larger-looking token cannot clear unrelated or later debt.
6. **Reconnect, stale target and recovery remain mandatory.** R4 browser reconnect and late optimistic callback tests remain; durable command uncertainty is distinct from physical resource lifetime. Cleanup cannot erase the sole owner of an unresolved command.
7. **Deletion/removal discipline remains intact.** R4 demanded removal of dead demo store, obsolete visible revision, duplicate registries and misleading tests, with path/reason/replacement history. The revised specification preserves the semantic requirement and source traceability without embedding a transient migration checklist in the canonical architecture.
8. **HumanHelp failures remain evidence, not automatically generic features.** R4's four-way classification—generic Gesso semantic/closure gap, HumanHelp integration/assembly bug, HumanHelp business-rule defect, external/runtime premise failure—is preserved and given explicit ownership.
9. **Positive cases are independent of diagnostic reporting.** The creator, manager and remote viewer must actually succeed where eligible; no amount of detailed logs can rescue an application that cannot pass Claim, Reassign and selective Refresh.
10. **Safety and progress are different propositions.** Explicit Refresh means a user may validly keep old state by never refreshing. Availability, delivery fairness, qualification, user action and retention are premises for stronger convergence; correct stale-effect rejection alone does not establish liveness.

## Gesso implementation correspondence: bounded precursors, not total certification

- `src/gesso/assurance/report.clj` describes an original-source-bound conditional assurance *report*, expressly not an independent theorem prover, authorization, test-run authentication or deployment certification. Test observations remain caller-reported, fingerprints are change detectors and external changes may escape supplied boundaries.
- `src/gesso/assurance/subject.clj` provides structural identity for supported pure bounded source interfaces. The structural digest does not attest to Clojure implementation semantics or transfer any proof capability.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/installed_route_physical_correlation.clj` invokes an actual recorded Biff handler once and captures bounded in-process XTDB adapter call-site evidence. Its docstring explicitly refuses claims about deployed HTTP-server capture, honest callbacks, transaction-internal prestate, every writer or universal refinement.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_command_outcome_bridge.clj` (v1622) joins one specific pinned readback to application-reported outcome and disclosure predicate. It explicitly disclaims causal attribution, authenticated principal, retry permission and browser/Live settlement.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc` gives one in-flight Live refresh per logical fragment, queued/coalesced requirements, generation-gated callbacks and immediate semantic retirement without comparing opaque basis values as integers or timestamps. This is a portable semantic precursor, not evidence that an arbitrary real browser installed the intended DOM.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/shell.cljs` holds opaque physical timers/transports/continuity resources, exactly owned by adapter-issued executions and generations, and translates physical completion back into normalized events. Its current transport cancel is best effort; an obsolete late callback is still checked against semantic ownership.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/continuity.cljs` has idempotent resource release that does not reacquire later physical ownership. It does not establish that every possible DOM listener in an unrelated host script is registered.
- `src/gesso/live/dispatch.clj` has a bounded worker/queue `ArrayBlockingQueue`, explicit overflow modes and a coalescing slot. A coalesced/drop notice does not by itself prove that every semantically distinct HumanHelp advisory obligation survived.
- `src/gesso/live/flow.clj` constructs client-local Missionary flows, optional relief/coalescing, and indexed interest filtering. `src/gesso/live/transport/sse.clj` translates events into SSE/manifold transport with close/cancel hooks. Neither is a durable crash-recoverable publication outbox by itself.
- `src/gesso/live/optimistic/resolution_assurance.clj` creates an opt-in controlled process-local diagnostic for one actual registered executor, with sensitive inputs private to its evidence handle. It explicitly excludes universal XTDB causality, auth policy and full proof.
- The separate physical-writer inventory/coverage modules establish bounded enumerated/declared scope, not the completeness of all external XTDB writers.

## HumanHelp implementation correspondence and unclosed requirements

- `src/net/humanhelp/example/application_preflight.clj` derives one production Request Choreo → route → trusted server → publication → Live graph → acquisition backbone and explicitly rejects a second separate registry. It is an assembly source, not an observation of actual browser or installed Aleph server.
- `src/net/humanhelp/example/live.clj` routes production Request changes and provides real fragmentation/stream helpers. It also has the `send-new-request-advisory!` function whose commentary states that other connected browsers receive client-only advisory while the creator gets correlated content. This source does not by itself implement or establish transaction-coupled durable publication intent.
- `src/net/humanhelp/example/board.clj` reads from production Request and User models and uses Gesso Live progression rather than Request revision/wall clock as an observed basis. It does not itself enforce a one-use remote adoption permission or prove real client receipt.
- `src/net/humanhelp.clj` configures Gesso Live with finite coalescing/queue options and Aleph startup. This is meaningful operational configuration but not proof of semantically lossless debt compaction, eventual delivery or a durable queue after process death.
- `test/net/humanhelp/example/live_test.clj` checks Request change expansion, basis propagation and fragment routing with focused test seams. It is not a real two-browser advisory/Refresh/reconnect test, and it was inspected rather than executed in this revision.
- `test/net/humanhelp/example/chromium_acceptance.clj` contains a substantial real-browser lifecycle path including Claim/Unclaim/Mark/Complete/Cancel/Reassign and persisted readback. It does not alone establish publication-crash recovery, complete two-viewer selective-adoption debt, provider-wide writer coverage or the operational bounds required here. The test file was read; its execution and pass/fail status were not observed.
- Earlier 08.10/08.13 source review found a concrete mismatch between HumanHelp's current startup module/order call and Gesso's v1622 canonical Biff module guard. This remains a **source-level risk/compatibility finding**; no actual runtime startup test was performed in this revision.

## Connected positive and adversarial obligations

1. Real Claim from actual browser C42 through installed route and qualified XTDB guard/readback, plus settled card and reload.
2. Local creator S result independent of remote viewer B's advisory and absent automatic adoption.
3. Manager Reassign with current eligible target and one live primary; changed target eligibility fails current guard without partial ownership.
4. Genuine one-use Refresh discharges only actually covered, physically installed requirements and leaves later/advisory obligations.
5. Browser timeout after C42 submission preserves historical uncertainty rather than silently retrying a new C43 or rolling back committed authority.
6. Crash after Request commit and before publication is recovered by the selected durable intent/worker, or reported under the explicitly weaker Refresh/reconciliation contract; live callback existence alone fails the strong profile.
7. Bounded advisory overload compacts to authorized coarse unknown/debt with repair rather than pretending missing requirements never occurred.
8. Incomparable progression values are not merged as if wall-clock or maximal token ordering were authoritative.
9. Retired E1/T1 with failed physical cancellation cannot overwrite newer authorized T2 installation.
10. Removing a card/subscription cleans only its owned resources; other authorized shared connection owners survive.
11. Navigation, logout, policy change, deployment and crash transfer command uncertainty to permitted recovery without reusing obsolete execution/DOM resources.
12. Postcommit output failure preserves Request/assignment truth and separately tested recovery.
13. Caller-crafted chain, copied report or unrelated state snapshots cannot claim a complete adjacent physical execution.
14. Hidden XTDB writer or stale host generation leaves stronger physical-writer/installed-path claim open, not silently certified.
15. Pending resource, publication and recovery budgets have explicit normal/overflow/shutdown evidence and no leaked protected effect rights.
16. Removing a required HumanHelp product path or duplicating example-owned business authority fails independent acceptance despite a green internal registry.
17. Defect triage distinguishes reusable Gesso gap from example assembly, production domain and versioned external/runtime failures.

## R4 requirements retained and strengthened

- **Logical one-active/one-queued bound:** R4 limits each
  admitted coordinator scope to one active acquisition and
  one queued slot, while explicitly refusing to equate
  that invariant with one physical HTTP request in existence.
  The reviewed section extends the cycle through installation
  where necessary, and requires an independent bound on
  pending physical mutations if reads and swaps are separated.
- **Debt and acquisition permission are independent:** R4
  explicitly says the arrival of an advisory does not itself
  authorize a read. The revised contract preserves product
  selection between explicit one-Refresh, bounded automatic
  and declared deferred policies. A queue slot is not a grant.
- **No silent requirement loss:** an O2 advisory arriving during
  A1 remains outstanding unless a later *actual qualified*
  observation satisfies it at the required acquisition or
  adoption stage. Failure, timeout and retry exhaustion do
  not clear debt.
- **Current owner and stale physical resources:** late A1
  response cannot mutate an A2 owner even if its basis looks
  stronger; abort may fail and physical requests can outlive
  the logical cycle. Identity and permit checks are separate
  from backend progression.
- **Real provider comparison:** R4 does not assume X18/X20
  are integers or that a sample fixture proves progression.
  The revised text preserves Part IV's admitted
  satisfaction/combination relation and specifically treats
  incomparable requirements without lossy max-token logic.
- **Physical incorporation:** HTTP response, fetched data,
  installed card and whole-board adoption are separate.
  Advisory obligations with adoption-stage discharge persist
  until an actual covered installation completes.
- **Retention and resource bounds:** single queued *slot*
  is not constant-memory proof for arbitrary requirements.
  The coordinator must specify supported scope, sound
  combination/overflow, physical remnants, backpressure
  and resource/retention policy.

R5's single semantic owner, exact grant inspection map,
qualified actor/query scope, source-normalized events,
adopted evidence and subscription continuity remain the
preferred architecture. This review does not restore R4's
large illustrative helper functions as a competing runtime
implementation. The detailed O1/O2 physical history and
initial subscription fence remain part of 06.07's next
focused review, not deleted from the plan.

## Changes made in 06.06

1. Bound the coordinator to applied authoritative query,
   actor/tenant disclosure and current owner instance,
   rather than one global Request ID or reusable DOM selector.
2. Distinguished advisory debt from source admission,
   current read scheduling, trusted server authorization,
   actual acquired observation and physical adoption.
3. Clarified exact grants including captured obligations,
   read count, expiration, retries and successor budget.
   The permission is checked at actual physical issue;
   a queued intent cannot retain expired authority.
4. Kept incomparable requirements and token-free advisory
   debt through conservative provider-backed representation.
   Duplicate suppression requires actual current coverage,
   not coincidental label equality or callback ordering.
5. Separated one logical cycle, outstanding installation
   obligations, retired physical remnants and retained
   incomplete debt, including failure without a queue slot.
6. Made owner/query changes, page suspension, disconnected
   subscription, multi-node silence and future interest
   recovery explicit without prematurely selecting a
   particular distributed router implementation.
7. Named checker premises and liveness limits; finite
   scheduling tests do not imply convergence without
   grants, fairness, availability and recovery.
8. Added eight paired positive/adverse histories for
   one-Refresh, O2 during A1, incomparable coverage,
   partial failure, stale A1, full-cycle single-flight,
   applied query changes and subscription gaps.

## Inspected Gesso v1622 implementation (source only)

The supplied Gesso application-assurance archive corresponds
to commit 1b5e10e71c00e7784b019dee7dc1198a96fd03f6.

- src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc stores per-fragment
  :inflight, :queued-refresh? and :queued-requirements.
  :live/invalidated starts a logical :fragment/refresh
  when idle, or records coalesced queue work while active.
  The active request's exact canonical requirement can
  be recognized as already bound and need not trigger
  another identical request.
- The adapter binds physical :request-generation and
  :request-id to the current HTMX attempt. Stale before/
  after response and failed events are diagnosed without
  giving an obsolete generation new installation rights.
  It does not guess ordering of opaque requirements.
- On a failed bound attempt, the adapter carries that
  attempt's opaque requirements into an already warranted
  queued successor; it avoids an unconditional retry
  if there is no queued request. Its per-fragment record
  does not in this excerpt maintain an independent
  persistent obligation ledger for the failed attempt
  when no successor is queued.
- src/gesso/live/browser/core.cljs normalizes admitted
  invalidations, triggers HTMX-owned refresh through
  :fragment/refresh, and uses online/pageshow/
  visibilitychange as advisory convergence-repair
  boundaries. The source does **not** by itself
  establish explicit user-granted one-Refresh policy
  as the default implementation behavior.
- Source-inspected
  test/gesso/live/browser/adapter_test.cljc includes
  first-invalidation-starts-one-fragment-refresh-test,
  invalidations-during-one-request-coalesce-without-starting-another-request-test,
  advisory-invalidation-during-request-queues-headerless-successor-test,
  bound-request-ignores-duplicate-covered-canonical-requirement-test,
  failed-bound-request-preserves-unsatisfied-active-and-queued-canonical-requirements-test,
  and failed-bound-request-without-queued-work-does-not-manufacture-retry-test
  (test source names are representative of their specific fixtures).
  Tests were read/located, **not executed** for this review.

The current implementation supplies meaningful coalescing,
request-generation and some failure-preservation behavior,
but the finished design is stronger. In particular, directly
starting a refresh on invalidation is appropriate for an
explicitly auto-refresh profile, not for HumanHelp's selected
remote-advisory/one-user-Refresh policy. Neither a
:queued-refresh? boolean nor a successful HTTP callback
proves retention and justified discharge of all independent
adoption-stage debt. This is a potential *implementation
correspondence gap*, not a reason to weaken R5.1's target.

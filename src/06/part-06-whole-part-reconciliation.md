## Cross-section architectural consistency

1. **Command versus execution versus physical attempt.** C42,
   E42, H1/H2, effect instance, card target B1/B2 and P1
   installation permit have distinct admission, retry,
   authority and retirement lifetimes. Historic Claim
   incorporation cannot be inferred from a current card,
   nor can a network failure manufacture rejection.
2. **Example-label scope.** The Claim card observation was
   called O1 in 06.04, colliding with the O1/O2 advisory
   obligations of 06.06–06.07. It is now **CardObs1** in
   06.04, its 06.05 installation history and its sectional
   audit. The R4-preserved O1/O2 Refresh example is unchanged.
   P1 denotes an installation permit while P17/P18/P20
   are example provider positions; numeric-looking names
   never establish progression. R may be the Request
   entity in one example and a board-response label
   in another, with no shared identity implied.
3. **HTMX/FFI physical control.** 06.03–06.05,
   06.10–06.11 and 06.15 all require effective
   *pre-actuation* gates when protected sends or
   mutations can be vetoed. OOB, headers,
   extensions, scripts and reentrant effects
   are in the declared physical footprint or
   excluded from the stronger managed claim.
   AfterSwap by name is not universal completion.
4. **Source, acquisition, installation, adoption.**
   The trusted read/observation establishes
   source/scope/progression, a current permit
   licenses only its bound physical footprint,
   an actual completed postcondition supports
   scoped adoption, and only the obligations
   proved satisfied at their required stage
   may be discharged.
5. **Coordinator, advisory and one-Refresh.**
   The default one-logical-active/one-coalesced-
   successor policy bounds scheduling, not
   outstanding physical handles or arbitrary
   incomparable debt. Advisory-only explicit
   user Refresh and bounded auto-refresh are
   distinct declared profiles. One current
   user grant never silently licenses A2
   when only A1 was admitted.
6. **Initial subscription/reconnect/multi-node.**
   Registration, router propagation and
   baseline acquisition need a justified
   overlap/fence, replay or qualified
   reacquisition. Duplicate redelivery
   cannot reconstruct a change for which
   no invalidation was ever generated.
   A stream going silent is not evidence
   that every relevant change arrived.
7. **Incompatible plans and durable history.**
   Old E7/P7 machine, timer and target rights
   retire independently of retained C77
   command-result obligations; current
   P8 assets, actor/Organization authority
   and resolver contracts need separate
   admission. A received current Order
   cannot by itself prove historic C77
   attribution or completion of the
   subsequent payment workflow.
8. **Resource/event owner isolation.**
   Semantic retirement precedes best-effort
   physical cleanup. Late handles, duplicate
   callbacks, same-value CSS and shared
   subscription references require exact
   owner/resource instance relations.
   Legitimate current shared B3/S9 advisories
   and independent K8 historic resolution
   remain possible after E42 retires.
9. **Positive and negative knowledge.**
   Membership absence and entity existence
   are not equivalent; a filtered board
   is not a global set. Current authorized
   later presence may supersede prior
   negative membership under provider
   progression and a new physical permit.
10. **Safety, liveness and correspondence.**
    Portable semantics, exact finite
    checking, actual adapter-step observation,
    independent physical/backend witnesses
    and quantified global-Choreo refinement
    remain different. Trace matching
    cannot invent an actual sender,
    receive, commit, OOB effect or
    completed target. Liveness adds
    quiescence, grant, availability,
    fairness and bounded resource premises.
11. **Hostile-client security and application
    closure.** Browser permissions,
    diagnostics and plan digests do not
    confer trusted model authority.
    The assembled application must
    cover its *actual* server routes,
    handlers, emitted plans, renderer
    and browser/HTMX/asset variants
    while retaining independent
    server-side transaction/tenant guards.

## R4 preservation and supersession

- R4's concrete Claim walkthrough retains the useful strict
  separation of C42 semantic intent, E42 local execution, H1
  transport, provisional Claiming presentation, trusted
  settlement, protected card acquisition, and after-swap
  installation. The abbreviated R5 sequence is expanded with
  precise owner/source/effect qualifications without copying
  the old illustrative listener implementations as new APIs.
- R4's source-normalized event and effect-shell contract is
  retained. The listener may correlate and physically
  veto; it does not decide business eligibility, historical
  command disposition, authoritative observation or which
  execution "probably" owns a callback.
- R4's failed-send and lost-response cases remain uncertainty,
  not fabricated rejection. A historical command readback
  and current Request/assignment observation resolve different
  questions. A later manager Reassign can coexist with
  historical C42 success.
- R4's stale DOM effect example explicitly requires a current
  permit *before physical mutation*, not just rejection of
  its eventual stale callback. The revised FFI contract
  retains exact opaque resource registration, current owner
  gating, partial results and cleanup after semantic retirement.
- The selected HTMX profile remains external trusted
  infrastructure. Ordinary raw HTMX is not erased, while a
  missing mandatory managed projector, route, correlation
  or effect cannot silently become a naked request.
- Adversarial client-supplied identities, sidecars and
  guessed schema tags cannot satisfy current trusted server
  authorization or source-admission conditions.

The older R4 Claim sketch's simplified
=:request/helper-id= is superseded by the real production
Request and active primary RequestAssignment relationship.
A view's pending displayed helper is not the persisted
authorization or absence predicate. No obsolete single-field
Claim model is restored to the normative plan.

## Gesso v1622 code inspected, not executed

Inspected the provided application-assurance archive at
1b5e10e71c00e7784b019dee7dc1198a96fd03f6.

- src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc keeps a portable
  [next-state effects] transition, effect/transport generations,
  :optimistic/settlement-observed and :optimistic/timeout-fired
  event paths, and separate :transport/succeeded or
  :transport/failed outcomes. It distinguishes confirmed,
  reconciled, rejected, already-incorporated and failed
  optimistic resolutions but does *not* infer the real
  RequestAssignment transaction merely from those labels.
- src/gesso/live/browser/shell.cljs holds opaque resources,
  commits semantic state before interpreting effects,
  normalizes immediate or Promise transport completion, and
  requires synchronous provisional install/finish. Its
  HTMX boundary intentionally does not duplicate HTMX's
  actual request or native DOM swap.
- src/gesso/live/browser/core.cljs binds real managed HTMX
  fragment refresh request identity and generation,
  normalizes beforeRequest/beforeSwap/afterSwap, and
  performs physical cancel on exceptions in supported
  gates. A browser callback does not authenticate a
  server principal or prove all response/OOB effects.
- src/gesso/live/browser/runtime.cljs checks that
  attached Choreo, optimism, HTMX transport bridge and
  shell handlers still refer to expected implementations,
  supplying relevant implementation-closure evidence
  for the inspected runtime composition.
- Source-inspected tests include
  successful-optimistic-completion-awaits-authority-test,
  optimistic-network-failure-does-not-fabricate-trusted-settlement-test,
  authoritative-supersession-wins-and-retires-old-optimistic-generation-test,
  and missing-optimistic-install-handler-is-diagnostic-and-does-not-retire-command-test.
  These test names and sources were inspected, not run.

These are meaningful partial precedents. They do not establish
installed HumanHelp production RequestAssignment authorization,
all physical FFI host effects and deferred callbacks, qualified
XTDB causality or result readback, universal browser refinement,
every deployed route/asset, or real one-Refresh product acceptance.
The canonical target need not be limited by that status.

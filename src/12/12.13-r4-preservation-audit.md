## Cross-gate editorial decision

Keep the stronger R5 architecture and do not promote
A–H into one global "ready" Boolean. They recur
when the corresponding *actual* construction,
issuer, host, transaction, browser or
publication surface first exists. The
original scope and independent product
capability denominator are retained.
Each revised gate specifies a meaningful
positive, nearby adversarial failure,
earliest legitimate enforcement and
physical/evidence limitation.

The two groups A–H and I–P overlap by
design without becoming independent
copies of the same business rule:
A against managed-to-ordinary downgrade
combines with I's admission discipline;
B/C's original subject/source ownership
connects to J's requirement completeness
and N's explicit opaque policy limits;
D and F connect to K/L's current
authorization, command uncertainty and
scoped acquisition; E/G connect to M/O's
evidence and production obligations;
H protects first responsible failure,
while P reserves business-intent review
for its original authority.

The revised Gate G extends R4's
"real browser" to the actual physical
Biff/XTDB/browser/host scope already
required by R5 Stages 4, 6, 8, 10–12.
That is a *strengthening*, not a
replacement of real Chromium as a
nonoptional product gate. Gate H
requires prevention at the first
legitimately enforcing boundary;
a later checker detecting a committed
invalid write is diagnostic, not proof
that the write was blocked.

## Concrete Gesso v1622 source evidence and limits

The bounded implementation comparison inspected:

- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight.clj`.
  The canonical whole-application construction
  checks operation/route/browser/settlement/
  acquisition and generated artifact source
  relations. It explicitly refuses to
  infer that a deployed server adopted its
  checked `:biff.ring/handler` or that
  arbitrary pre-serialized Ring responses
  were examined. This informs A, E, F,
  G and H; it is not universal enforcement.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/rendered_surface.clj`.
  Scans supplied Hiccup for semantic affordances
  and physical request conflicts. It expressly
  does not prove all dynamic Clojure render
  states were enumerated or authenticate
  the full assembly; this informs A/H.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/build.clj`.
  Uses exact browser manifest/stamp and
  emitted-byte artifact receipt, while
  noting that arbitrary emitters may
  ignore a supplied manifest and a
  receipt does not prove a server served
  those bytes to a browser. This informs
  E without claiming full release.
- `src/gesso/model/tx.clj`.
  Owns generic `:commands`, `:guards`,
  `:assertions`, `:changes` transaction
  mechanics and Biff FX/Live seam, expressly
  *not* domain authorization or application
  RequestAssignment truth. This informs
  C/D/F/G.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/certificate.clj`.
  Rechecks source/graph for a narrow set of
  JVM-local proof handles; explicitly
  no current actor authorization,
  arbitrary code protection or general
  XTDB/CLJS refinement. This informs B/D/E.
- `src/gesso/live/application_preflight/semantic_audit.clj`.
  Derives a scope-limited read-only operation
  view, marks original model predicates
  *not inspected*, and refuses to treat
  a copied report as full authority. This
  informs C/B and later I/N.
- `src/gesso/live/browser/adapter.cljc`.
  Contains the pure normalized-event/
  abstract-effect policy, rejects
  authoritative actions in untrusted
  browser execution, preserves opaque
  progression and retires old effects.
  It does not by itself prove physical
  DOM/HTMX behavior, Bob's selected
  one-use Refresh or backend truth.
  This informs D/F/G/H.

No fresh Gesso tests were run. In
particular, source-level names,
report fields and fixture examples
are not proof of complete installed
Biff adoption, physical XTDB guards,
authorization, real Chromium browser
acceptance, external kernel soundness
or total writer coverage. The
original full HumanHelp product
inventory (including Response)
remains separate.

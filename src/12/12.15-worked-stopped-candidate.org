** Worked stopped candidate: a green-looking release is not admission

This is a *controlled acceptance counterexample* spanning all Gates A–P.
It shows why passing local semantic checks, building JavaScript, and
observing several browser successes cannot make a defective candidate
an admitted production release. The candidate is schematic and
deliberately inconsistent. It is not executable Gesso API, a real
HumanHelp defect report, or evidence that the corresponding checks
have run. The literal names below identify narrative subjects,
not supported constructor functions or wire encodings.

Assume the original approved release intent requests a real
HumanHelp operational application, source-derived compositional
Claim assurance, and a selected external-formal/checker
correspondence profile. The required gates and product capabilities
are fixed *before* candidate generation. Some deliberately
narrower release could be independently requested, but this
candidate cannot change the original intent when its stronger
requirements fail.

The candidate reports success for an abstract Claim derivation,
a compiled browser bundle, an earlier Chromium journey and a
source-derived application graph. Those observations can all
be true within their own scopes while the current candidate
remains inadmissible.

The following is *illustrative EDN data only*, not a Gesso
constructor, accepted schema, current API or test result:

The `:response` entry means only that the *independent*
product requirement is still owed. Its precise Response
interaction semantics remain a HumanHelp product decision
at [[#decision-humanhelp-response-flow]]; this example
does not select a workflow. The synthetic provider list
is likewise an example of required/supplied mismatch,
not a complete real Biff FX contract.

The operation's actual RequestAssignment history,
current Membership, original command record,
transaction write footprint, installation/activation
identity, subscription registration, and trusted
progression are intentionally missing from this
green-looking report. Their absence is *not* proof
that they were safely implemented elsewhere.

*** Classify each defect at its earliest legitimate owner

Do not feed the entire broken candidate to one late
release check and treat the first random exception as
coverage of every gate. Record individual mutations
of a previously eligible baseline, the expected
first enforcing boundary, the required result and
the independently observed absence of forbidden
effects. A defect rejected during source assembly
prevents later candidate installation; a separate
controlled eligible instance is needed to challenge
a runtime-only or physical fault.

The defect matrix is a *falsification plan*, not
a claim that one running candidate visits all
these boundaries. Every chosen mutant starts
with its corresponding real eligible baseline,
then changes one owned condition while holding
other known prerequisites fixed enough to reach
the declared check. If an earlier unrelated
contradiction masks it, the test is inconclusive
for that target boundary rather than a
successful kill. A post-effect observer can
diagnose harm but cannot prove prevention.

A physical check reports the *actual* submitted
transaction, executed handler or browser
effect and its independent observation window.
Silence in logs, absence of a rendered card,
an EDN =:passed= status, expected test-runner
exception or a disconnected fixture does
not independently demonstrate that a
forbidden write or disclosure never happened.

*** Stop the candidate without changing model truth

The above release intent has *four distinct
decisions* under Part XI:

| Decision subject | This defective candidate's disposition | What it must not imply |
|------------------+----------------------------------------+------------------------|
| Candidate construction/admission | Block the selected strong request on known incompatible artifact, missing FX/recovery, original capability and proof coverage | A generated green subgraph cannot redefine the requirements |
| Host installation | No permission to present this candidate as a valid installed generation; a separately admitted repaired candidate needs actual handler/writer/assets observed | A local system map is not an installed listener |
| Activation/public exposure | No permission for this candidate to take over traffic or continue incompatible old callbacks | Earlier deployment or old theorem is not successor permission |
| Requested completion | Operational HumanHelp, compositional Claim and selected external formal qualifications remain independently unestablished | Failing one profile does not retroactively invalidate any genuine narrower theorem or committed domain history |

The full original requirements cannot be silently
relabeled =:inapplicable= after a blocker. For a
mandatory result, distinguish Part XI's
=:failed=, =:incomplete=, =:unavailable=,
=:unsupported=, =:skipped=, =:stale= and
=:missing= instead of replacing all with
one =:false=. A genuinely =:passed= local
gate still retains its precise subject
and applicability; it does not make
the combined strong release eligible.

A candidate whose static assembly is known
invalid is stopped before newly exposing its
managed effects. That does *not* assert that
every possible dynamic race was exercised.
If an earlier separately admitted generation
already incorporated C42 before a later
response, render, notice or host failure,
the authoritative commit is *not rolled back*,
and C42's client outcome remains uncertain
until valid command-specific evidence
establishes it. Retire this candidate's
unissued or obsolete effects and keep
separately owned command/disclosure,
publication, and recovery obligations
according to their true histories.

An old generation may remain active only
while its own qualified policy, writer,
principal and release assumptions still
hold. Changing the planned artifact does
not resurrect a stale browser callback;
rollback of code does not undo a durable
RequestAssignment or authorize replaying
an unknown command as a new C43.

*** Reconstruct at the original owner, not by editing success flags

The repaired candidate is a *new qualified
construction*, not the old candidate with
renamed file digests or hand-edited
`:passed=` fields. Re-establish the
following owned sequence; independently
satisfy its original selected profile
rather than allowing the release report
to choose a smaller denominator.

1. **Source and requirements.** Reacquire
   current public HumanHelp model and
   original product decisions, including
   RequestAssignment, Membership,
   Organization, actual Reassign target
   selection, all six lifecycle actions,
   Create, search/options, scoped
   Refresh, pending Response decision,
   original operation/invariant catalog
   and protected writer obligations.
   Select the Response contract through
   the product owner before claiming
   full operational completion.
2. **Construction and type/Choreo
   admission.** Regenerate the supported
   typed rules, source-owned operations,
   global/local Choreo, executable plans,
   route/FX requirements and real declared
   recovery branches. Reject new
   contradictions *before* installation;
   do not invent a browser-specific
   Claim policy or silent legacy route.
3. **Trusted providers and backend.**
   Bind actual clock, actor, Request,
   Membership, Organization and selected
   protected models to the real serving
   handler. Establish current principal,
   immutable logical command binding,
   transaction guard/frame/cardinality,
   exact submitted writer and required
   durable command/publication storage.
   The physical provider's internal
   prestate and all-writer coverage
   require their actual qualified
   evidence or disclosed premise.
4. **Browser and publication.**
   Rebuild from the current semantic
   manifest; check emitted/staged bytes,
   actually served/loaded bootstrap,
   current Biff handler, real HTMX
   request/swap/OOB completion and
   resource permissions. Preserve
   Bob's remote advisory-only policy,
   one-use scoped Refresh, independent
   manual acquisition if no notice
   arrived and honest uncovered debt.
5. **Proof and release evidence.**
   Regenerate original-source obligation
   closure and certificate candidates;
   recheck issued handles, used premises,
   changed implementation dependencies,
   mandatory external checker S0–S4
   edges and real observed browser/
   backend cases as the *selected*
   profile requires. A bounded
   Claim proof is useful but not
   full product or S4 transfer.
6. **Reevaluation.** Re-run each applicable
   independent positive and targeted
   negative test at its legitimate
   first boundary with actual physical
   observation, then independently
   evaluate candidate, installation,
   activation and named completion
   decisions. Do not call a repaired
   subgraph passed merely because
   it now avoids an earlier
   obvious failure.

The sequence above describes
*dependencies and evidence subjects*,
not functions named =prepare!=,
=compose-application!=, =admit-generation!=
or =release/admit!=. R4 used
API-shaped examples to make
the intent readable; it
explicitly did not assert
those names were installed
Gesso entrypoints. Production
implementations may combine
phases while retaining the
separate subject and
check/observation obligations.

*** Demonstrate nonvacuous recovery and bounded claims

A fixed source is not enough:
the same accepted generation
must permit real legitimate
Claim/Unclaim and Reassign
operations and, when full
HumanHelp completion is
requested, the independently
selected Response behavior.
The relevant actual
Chromium/Biff/XTDB paths
must be tested through
rendered controls and
current authenticated
actors. Include an
eligible Creator-local
Create path; remote Create
or lifecycle change
remains merely advisory
to Bob until his
authorized Refresh.

For the original C42 failure
window, use at least one
controlled legitimate
incorporation followed
by a lost HTTP response,
and an independently
qualified historical
resolution. Challenge
another helper competing
for the primary assignment,
revoked Membership after
pre-read, C42 incompatible
binding, missing durable
notice, late subscriber,
router failover, no advisory
before manual Refresh,
partial OOB installation,
resource retirement and
incompatible old assets.
Each outcome retains
known committed, known
rejected, already
incorporated or unknown
*business* meaning,
separate from release
gate =:passed=/=:failed=.

A narrow explicitly declared
operational demonstration,
a conditional abstract
theorem, an independent
external kernel theorem,
a source-linked diagnosis
and a tested physical
fault scenario are
valuable *different*
achievements. Their
results may be retained
as valid for their actual
subjects even while
this strong candidate
is stopped. The
prohibited move is
promoting one into a
stronger completion
claim without its
correspondence.

The acceptance result
for this example is
therefore not that a
single root status says
"rejected"; it is that
each contradiction is
attributed to its
actual first owner,
forbidden effects are
blocked where claimed,
permitted positive
paths remain usable,
postcommit truth is
not rewritten, and
the original release
profile's unmet
obligations remain
visible until genuine
independent evidence
discharges them.

## Question and independently controlled sources

Could a generated application be internally closed, pass all of its own generated proof cases, and still fail the application Gesso promised to deliver? **Yes.** A missing real Reassign control, omitted Unclaim proof case, undiscovered raw XTDB writer, uninspected Clojure policy branch or stale server handler may escape any check whose *denominator* comes only from generated successes.

The final specification must keep six distinct roots of responsibility:

1. **Original product intent:** independently approved user capabilities and operational outcomes, including eligible positive journeys.
2. **Semantic source:** public model rules, typed operation definitions, named opaque contracts, authorized effects and invariants.
3. **Application construction:** assembled route, browser, model, acquisition, recovery, provider, writer and installation edges.
4. **Proof requirements:** obligations independently regenerated from the original semantic source and selected assurance profile, not from the candidate proof graph.
5. **Physical writer universe:** current actual backend permissions, services, routes, workers, initializers, migrations and other relevant writer effects.
6. **Release decision:** exact candidate, installation, activation and milestone-specific completion subjects with their distinct evidence.

The semantic source is singular *for business-rule meaning*; this does not abolish an independent product oracle or prove that all physical paths respect the same rule. The checker is not a second model policy author. An original capability requirement does not duplicate Claim's current Membership guard: it says an eligible real user must be able to execute Claim and see its legitimate result.

Historical baselines consulted: original R4 \`main\` Parts VII and IX and preferred R5 versions, plus the accepted R5.1 source and R4-preservation records. Original R4 requires real application closure and compositional evidence; preferred R5 improves declarative source-first architecture; R5.1 gives their missing identity, inventory, proof-scope and real-writer qualifications. Neither historical design requires a magical general verifier for arbitrary Clojure.

## Claim boundaries that must never collapse

| Claimed scope | Requirement owner and applicable evidence | Invalid substitution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product completeness | Independently chosen user-capability oracle, real eligible browser histories and model outcomes | Generator's implemented-operation list becomes the product's required list |
| Semantic inventory | Public model and chosen original application source, with exact opaque, effect and interpretation contracts | Operation names or absent scanner findings prove every Clojure policy branch is represented |
| Assembly closure | Required source-derived graph *and* classified active realization edges | Connected nodes alone prove installed transport and all real writers are known |
| Certified judgment | Original subject/profile determines every proof case; checked issuer and premises justify it | Caller-selected proof DAG, digest or report defines its own required obligations |
| Physical protected writes | Observed and actually mediated/qualified write capabilities, including non-route and external paths | No extra writer in a host-supplied snapshot implies no hidden backend writer |
| Release eligibility and completion | Independently predeclared gates for candidate, host installation, activation and named milestone | A build, local abstract theorem, or former successful test transfers to actual deployment or another milestone |

**A closed declared catalog is not automatically a closed physical writer world.** Physical exhaustiveness demands a qualified boundary that truly denies unregistered writes, or independent full-world evidence and reviewed exceptions; an observational inventory alone does not supply either.

**A closed proof-justification DAG is not automatically a complete proof obligation DAG.** The original source and profile independently determine which preservation, initialization, dependency, knowledge and correspondence cases must exist. A DAG omitting one required case fails even if every included edge is valid.

**Source-level, installation-level and user-level coverage are different dimensions.** A browser may be usable while no general theorem exists. A source may be fully checked while a physical writer bypasses its guard. A deployment may be correctly mediated while required Response functionality has not yet been selected and tested.

## Required checking and installation order

**A. Freeze exact requested requirements.** Bind the original product and assurance promise, source generation, selected profile, supported/opaque rule classes and independent capability denominator before construction. A later approved scope revision is a *new claim* with an explicit history, not a reinterpretation of the failed original.

**B. Construct from source-owned semantic declarations.** Derive operation interfaces, guards, target/read/write/frame, recoverable outcomes, Choreo projections and declared dependencies. A supported fragment may be checked by the relevant typed rule semantics; arbitrary opaque Clojure remains a named, scoped premise until justified.

**C. Reconcile both directions of application closure.** Every required source edge needs an admitted route, executor, provider, asset, observation, settlement and recovery path *when applicable*. Every installed physical effect/active route/worker that claims to realize a semantic role needs a source contract. A legitimate read does not acquire a fabricated mutation just to satisfy a universal graph shape.

**D. Derive proof obligations separately from proof candidates.** The proof-justification projection must be well founded, but the runtime graph may have legitimate feedback through Live, commands and recovery. Confirm all original operations and obligations, not just all *offered* DAG nodes, and keep undecidable or opaque premises explicit.

**E. Require physical enforcement or disclose its absence.** Source and surveyed writer coverage are useful, but a true all-writer claim additionally requires current deployed authority, capability restriction, all relevant bypass paths, actual host/credential identities and independent exception contracts. Where the backend cannot exclude or independently qualify a writer, refuse that stronger guarantee.

**F. Test real positive and adverse acceptance.** Source check success does not establish an enabled, working user control, current trusted authorization, protected RequestAssignment effect and physically completed browser update. Mutant tests must fail at the first legitimate enforcing boundary, paired with a genuine eligible control proving the system does not just reject everything.

**G. Issue only what the chosen profile actually earned.** Gate \`:passed\` is valid only with subject-specific evidence and issuer. Required \`:missing\`, \`:unsupported\`, \`:unavailable\`, \`:stale\`, \`:skipped\`, \`:failed\` or \`:incomplete\` never count as pass. Candidate admission is not actual host installation; installation is not activation; activation is not product or external-formal completion.

## Positive and adversarial paired histories

1. **Working, real Claim.** An eligible helper's actual rendered Claim invokes the admitted browser plan, trusted Ring route and protected Request/RequestAssignment transition; installed result matches the independently required product outcome. Source checking, installed guard observation and real browser acceptance each support only their qualified subjects. **Reject:** manually posting a command makes a broken or disabled Claim button count as product success.

2. **Missing Reassign.** Generated assembly and abstract catalog omit real manager-target Reassign, although the original product contract requires it. The smaller abstract set may remain valid *as a smaller set*. **Reject:** count all *generated* operations and claim full HumanHelp coverage.

3. **Missing proof case.** Unclaim is present in the original operation catalog but absent from a caller-supplied, otherwise well-formed proof DAG. **Require:** source-generated missing-obligation diagnostic. **Reject:** proof-graph acyclicity and a valid hash of included nodes as evidence of full certification.

4. **Uninspected policy.** A Clojure helper performs additional authorization checks that the supported typed rule frontend cannot inspect. **Require:** an explicit opaque contract, trust/effect assumptions and selected profile policy. **Reject:** a syntactically valid public rule name or zero scanner findings is proof of the helper's meaning.

5. **Background writer.** A scheduled maintenance job alters protected RequestAssignment state but has no HTMX control or Choreo role. It must be an admitted operation, initializer or explicitly qualified operational exception. **Reject:** the browser operation catalog is complete, therefore every protected state writer is covered.

6. **Hidden raw XTDB client.** Every *observed* registered writer has the correct issued callback, but a second process holds a raw write credential. **Require:** physical credential enforcement/mediated capability or honest undischarged premise. **Reject:** zero observed extra entries proves exhaustive production writer coverage.

7. **Migration or startup initializer.** A new release's migration can rewrite fields outside the proved transition set. **Require:** admitted refinement or a fresh valid initial state and requalification after the migration. **Reject:** a pre-migration induction automatically covers post-migration state.

8. **Handler/asset substitution.** Checked candidate sources and browser build are accurate, but the serving host installs a different Ring handler or older JavaScript bundle. **Require:** current actual installation evidence. **Reject:** a matching declaration or build fingerprint proves the production listener uses it.

9. **Forged success report.** A caller constructs a plausible \`:passed\` proof graph or copies a real old report. **Require:** actual original-source obligation regeneration and trusted current checker/issuer verification. **Reject:** report data becomes runtime authority or alters the required premise set.

10. **Same semantics, different implementation.** A trusted writer adapter changes while its abstract interface stays stable. Original semantic proof may remain reusable *if its dependencies permit*; installed physical correspondence must be requalified. **Reject:** same operation ID or interface digest automatically qualifies a different provider/host.

11. **Unselected Response interaction.** Claim and other lifecycle actions work, but the separately required HumanHelp Response-flow product contract is not yet selected or proved through a real positive interaction. **Require:** retain that unresolved product-owner decision and fail stronger product completeness. **Reject:** deleting the requirement or substituting a dummy unrelated action to make generated coverage complete.

12. **Post-failure profile shrinkage.** A selected release requires a proof or all-writer guarantee that current tools mark \`:unsupported\`. A different, independently authorized preview could make a narrower claim. **Reject:** changing \`:strict?\` or silently shrinking the original denominator after failure to relabel the same release \`:passed\`.

13. **Reject-all false green.** Every action fails before the effect boundary, so negative tests pass, but no real eligible operation can succeed. **Require:** nonvacuous positive business/UX and valid current negative-control pair. **Reject:** “no forbidden writes” alone earns product acceptance or complete certification.

14. **Rolling node/credential replacement.** An old in-process source/route/writer report predates new worker registrations or backend permissions. **Require:** rechecking or enforcing current installation/capability applicability before the protected effect, not a stale snapshot. **Reject:** old registry coverage proves all future hosts and concurrent entrypoints remain covered.

These scenarios are *specification challenges*, not a claim that they were exercised as runtime tests during this review. In particular, no test asserted physical absence of a hidden XTDB client.

## Preservation against R4, R5 and application usability

The original R4 whole-app and certification duties require not merely individual correct components but the actual application path and its relevant effects. R5 strengthens the unified source-derived semantic authoring model. R5.1 makes explicit the important *negative* facts: a closed declared source may still miss actual effects, a connected proof DAG may lack original required nodes, and a green abstract report may lack production applicability.

This does not force app authors to handwrite Quint, TLA+ or duplicate Claim in a second rule DSL. It allows ordinary Clojure through named opaque contracts while denying unqualified theorem claims. Positive HumanHelp capabilities remain an independent acceptance oracle, not another source of business guards. Partial proof or a consciously narrower release may be honestly reported under a new approved claim without rewriting the original requirement history.

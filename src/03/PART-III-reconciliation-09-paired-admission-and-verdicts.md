## Source-derived issue

Part III already contains extensive adverse cases and a separate
positive construction table. Its diagnostics in 03.05, 03.10
and 03.21 also distinguish invalid syntax/knowledge,
unresolved external evidence, unsupported capacity, runtime
rejection, and command uncertainty. But 03.25 did not yet
require *paired* positive and adverse controls for the
same meaningful assurance question. A rejected fixture
could pass for an unrelated parser defect; an always-reject
checker could accumulate negative successes; or a valid
bounded witness could be mislabeled universal refinement.

## Normative correction

Section 03.25 now requires a positive admitted control and a
minimal semantically targeted adverse case for eleven
representative property families. The pairs cover:

1. Role-local knowledge and source-authenticated receipt.
2. Behavior-sensitive projection without gratuitous disclosure.
3. Legitimate session-only pre-command retirement and closed tags.
4. Ordinary opaque Clojure with explicit retained premises.
5. Compatible authoritative replay versus incompatible reuse.
6. Durable work-recording and independently valid best-effort policies.
7. Worker/effect provenance and truthful provider uncertainty.
8. Physical installation versus mere response completion.
9. Concrete correspondence witnesses versus unsupported global claims.
10. Finite checking/capacity and conditional progress.
11. Type-correct business policy versus independently declared intent.

The section now also specifies fixture attribution requirements:
same contract/profile and premises except the intended mutation;
exact changed subjects when identity legitimately differs;
positive admission evidence; negative *intended* rejection;
no use of copied reports as authority; no silent weakening
of a mandatory assurance or release claim.

## R4 and R5 preservation

R4 required early errors for knowable contradictions,
knowledge-safe projection, a positive usable Clojure authoring
path, explicit dynamic boundaries, and truthful runtime
recovery. The revised matrix preserves those requirements
without copying obsolete single-process example code.

R5's stronger separation of static invalidity, unsupported
formal capability, open opaque premises, dynamic operation
rejection, indeterminate command disposition and application
policy mismatch is retained. The paired checks require
nonvacuous accepted constructions and precisely scoped
claims, not a new user-facing Quint/TLA+/Recife requirement.

A valid typed rule can violate intended product policy
without being ill-typed; the application owns its rule and
independent acceptance claim. A complete finite model result
is still confined to its quantified finite domain.

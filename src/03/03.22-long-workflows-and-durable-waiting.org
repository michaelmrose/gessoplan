** Long logical workflows and short runtime executions
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: req-durable-workflow-boundary
:END:

A choreography may describe a logical workflow that spans human-scale time.
Its business meaning can remain continuous while its participating runtime
executions start and finish independently. Neither the global description nor
the presence of an =:await= operation implies that one Aleph node, browser, or
other process must retain a suspended machine in memory for hours or days.

An in-execution wait and a durable business wait have different contracts.
An active role-local =:await= suspends that execution until an admitted event,
retirement or recovery decision. A durable business wait instead records a
model-owned pending condition or obligation from which later authorized actors
can establish new entry facts. That distinction is explicit in the assembly;
neither form silently acquires the guarantees of the other.

For example, a business flow may conceptually involve:

An approval workflow requests approval, waits under its domain policy, and
continues when an admitted decision establishes the next condition.

A robust HumanHelp realization may instead be:

| Period | Short execution and durable connection |
|--------+----------------------------------------|
| Request submission | E1 incorporates pending approval, binds its command result, and records required durable follow-up under the authoritative operation contract |
| Human-scale delay | Pending domain state and any deadline/work obligation remain discoverable and recoverable under their declared authority and retention contract |
| Approver action | E2 starts with its own authenticated principal, admitted plan, permitted current observation and explicit selection, then incorporates a guarded decision |
| Later requester action | E3 separately acquires its authorized entry facts, if further work is needed, without inheriting E1's or E2's execution-private resources |

The durable wait lives in model state, along with whatever command/outcome,
pending-work, deadline or notification obligations the business contract
requires. Durable existence alone does not guarantee that a later actor will
discover or act on it. Where progress is required, the application declares
the discoverable query, permitted recipients, wakeup or scheduling strategy,
missed-notification repair, and assumptions about authorization, actor
action, availability and fairness. A pending state without any permitted
continuation is not a completed workflow.

Subject to the authoritative store's declared durability, recovery,
retention and compatibility assumptions, the durable workflow fact can survive:

- web-node failure;
- deployment;
- load-balancer rerouting;
- client disconnect;
- browser suspension;
- long offline periods.

The ordinary Gesso choreography contract does not promise durable persistence,
transparent relocation or automatic migration of arbitrary suspended
server-local executions. A durable model record, an admitted command receipt,
and a recoverable task are not snapshots of all in-flight runtime state.

Applications that require persisted workflow-machine continuations use a
separately admitted, opt-in capability. Its contract covers stable checkpoint
identity, atomicity relative to authoritative decisions and pending effects,
recovery discovery, owner/fencing transfer, plan and data compatibility,
replay and idempotency, uncertain effect disposition, resource reconstruction,
expiry, cancellation, and eventual retirement. Restarting from a serialized
machine map without these guarantees is not durable-continuation admission.
The capability may be realized by a dedicated workflow engine or a
Gesso-managed facility, provided the same semantic obligations hold.
No such facility is imposed on ordinary short choreographies.

*** Durable waiting belongs in authoritative state by default

Human-scale waiting is normally represented as durable domain state rather than
a suspended in-memory choreography execution.

This has several consequences.

First, subject to those persistence, retention and compatibility assumptions,
the durable workflow meaning can survive:

- deployment;
- process loss;
- browser suspension;
- load-balancer rerouting;
- reconnect;
- long periods without any participant online.

Second, later actors can acquire the relevant facts by authorized
authoritative observation rather than requiring the original process to resume.
The observation establishes current, scoped eligibility; the actor's fresh
execution still checks its own authorization and operation guards at
incorporation. A queue entry or notification is not a grant to approve every
visible item, and the later execution does not automatically inherit the
historical outcome or transient authority of a prior command.

Third, a deadline belonging to business policy is durable authoritative
meaning, not merely a browser or process-local timer. If the contract requires
action after a deadline, a qualified durable scheduler, recovery scan or
equivalent admitted mechanism owns that obligation. The scheduled attempt
must recheck current eligibility and distinguish deadline occurrence,
effect dispatch, and successful incorporation. A delayed callback cannot
retroactively turn an expired authorization or retired resource into a valid
operation.

Fourth, a durable operation with unfinished publication, external-provider
work or command resolution retains that work's stable identity and recovery
owner after its initiating execution retires. A process failure or timeout
does not prove the corresponding effect never occurred. Reconciliation and
retries follow the public operation's idempotency and disclosure rules;
a new execution identity does not silently create a new command intent.

Finally, the framework avoids making one runtime's memory an accidental
source of business truth. The later-execution and expense-history examples
in the following section elaborate those identity and command boundaries.

*** Durable-wait acceptance counterexamples

| Apparent shortcut | Required semantic outcome |
|-------------------+---------------------------|
| E1 records =:pending= only in an in-memory Choreo machine and its web node crashes | Do not claim durable workflow survival; persist the required authoritative state and recovery obligations |
| A durable pending record exists but no actor, queue, schedule or discovery path can reach it | Do not claim required eventual completion; supply a qualified continuation strategy or state the missing progress premise |
| E2 starts from an authorized pending queue but directly approves every row visible in it | Require explicit admitted selection where the application needs it and recheck current operation guards at incorporation |
| A user was authorized when E1 started, so E2 inherits that principal and capability days later | Reject borrowed transient authority; E2 establishes a fresh principal, knowledge, plan and current permission |
| An E1 notification fails after commit, and the application reports E1's authoritative command rejected | Keep the committed outcome and separately recover required publication debt |
| A process-local deadline timer fires after the workflow's authority, owner or policy changed | Recheck durable deadline and current business eligibility; local timer arrival is not authorization |
| A worker times out on an external effect and starts a new command to perform the same business intent | Preserve stable command/effect binding and reconcile uncertainty before any retry or newly admitted intent |
| A persisted continuation is loaded with an incompatible plan and reused effect capabilities from its old process | Require checked migration or retirement, owner fencing, effect recovery, and reconstructed resources |
| Two approvers concurrently observe pending state and both submit approval commands | Authoritative incorporation must guard the current state and command binding; earlier read eligibility is not a shared commit permit |
| The workflow has a graph path to an approved terminal but every eligible actor may remain offline forever | State the environmental fairness and actor-action premises; graph reachability is not a liveness proof |

These requirements describe completed Gesso independently of one particular
process manager, workflow engine, database, transport, or scheduler.

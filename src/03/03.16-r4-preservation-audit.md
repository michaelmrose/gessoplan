# R5.1 Part III — 03.16 Behavior-Sensitive Branch Choice

**R4 baseline:** `main` Part III: “Behavior-sensitive choice is the
central branch rule,” “Supported equivalence and conservative rejection,”
and “Trace preservation and progress are separate obligations.”
**R5.1 candidate:** section 03.16.
**Disposition:** focused preservation and improvement audit, not whole-Part-III
acceptance or deployment certification.

## R4 requirements preserved

- Role-local knowledge is required when projected behavior actually
  diverges, before the first different action, not for every syntactic
  occurrence of a role in a global branch.
- The projector must not disclose the manager's private branch inputs
  to Finance, and cannot synthesize unowned messages or local facts.
- Identical notifier behavior may make the choice irrelevant to the
  notifier, provided meaning, causal prerequisites and observable
  behavior truly agree.
- Finance needs a communication or authorized observation path before
  acting; it must recheck current model eligibility at incorporation.
- Unknown equivalence or missing knowledge is rejected or carried as
  a named, applicable unresolved premise, never silently accepted.
- Trace-safety exclusion alone is not progress, total participation
  or liveness; deployment/environment assumptions remain explicit.

## R5 superior design retained

- Keep the neutral notifier message and separate Finance disposition
  cases for both successful manager paths, rather than R4's illustrative
  Finance path that could wait indefinitely on an absent message.
- Preserve `:review-unavailable` as a session-level disposition that
  does not assert a possibly committed command was rejected.
- Keep the later durable Finance execution and explicit user selection,
  rather than the R4 schematic `for-each` that could misleadingly
  suggest automatic approval of an entire queue.
- Preserve the concrete conservative graph-equivalence baseline,
  explicit receive-alternative disjointness, cycle treatment, and
  refusal to compare opaque host functions by their names.
- Preserve separate trace, correspondence, terminal, progress and
  application-capability obligations.

## Focused improvements in 03.16

1. State that branch equivalence is relative to semantic observations and
   confidentiality/disclosure, not just equal payload bytes or final values.
2. Identify differing notification presence, destination, timing and
   resources as potentially meaningful observable differences.
3. Label the illustrative disposition map as schematic while requiring
   an admitted closed variant schema, sender knowledge and
   session/command/source binding for its intended semantics.
4. Forbid silent branch selection from overlapping optional fields or
   the absence of a message.
5. Distinguish successful concrete witness replay from all-schedules
   projection/refinement, and safety from deadlock freedom, liveness
   and confidentiality.
6. Add nine counterexamples covering neutral-message occurrence,
   private branch values, silent Finance waiting, copied correlation,
   destination leaks, overlapping receive alternatives, unknown
   outcomes, vacuous liveness and witness overclaim.

## Current Gesso correspondence (audit only)

- `gesso.choreo.verify` establishes definite global and role-local
  knowledge checks, but documentation expressly does not claim
  arbitrary liveness or universal projection refinement.
- `gesso.choreo.project` handles foreign control flow, authoritative
  barriers, and disjoint receive contracts. It rejects statically
  overlapping alternatives for the same sender/event/channel.
- `gesso.choreo.proof` checks several finite structural projection
  properties; structural origin checks alone are not dynamic refinement.
- `gesso.choreo.correspondence` checks concrete lockstep and weak
  schedules, retaining terminal-outcome compatibility relationally.
  A successful witness does not quantify over all schedules.
- Current code's contract syntax is not used as a ceiling on the
  canonical intended message variant and typed-discriminator design.

# R5.1 Part III — 03.17 Communication, Causality, and Payload Closure

**R4 baseline:** Part III “Message payload closure protects knowledge
boundaries” and its related knowledge and projection obligations.
**R5 candidate:** “Communication, causality, and completion” and the
preserved message payload closure requirement.
**Disposition:** focused preservation audit. The canonical Org section is
the finished-system specification, not implementation-status documentation.

## R4 obligations retained

- Closed messages establish only declared communicated facts, never
  arbitrary sender context or extra fields merged into machine state.
- Domain `:request-id` is distinct from transport attempt
  `:transport-request-id`.
- Open transport payloads remain subject to declared extension
  contracts; openness alone grants no new knowledge or branch authority.
- Knowledge-path justification and communication causality remain
  independent of value shape, sender claims and projector convenience.

## Superior R5 treatment retained

- Global semantic communication is distinct from physical send and
  the role-local machine's admitted receive.
- Ordering, loss, duplication, replay, timeout and channel behavior
  have declared semantics and explicit liveness premises.
- Command, execution, transport and message occurrence identities
  are distinct, with scoped correlation and admission.
- Replaying a received semantic occurrence must not repeat effects,
  while separately valid outcome evidence may still be reconciled.
- A foreign authoritative predecessor requires exact, suitable
  command or observation evidence, not traversal of the global graph.
- Await alternative knowledge is edge-specific, and independent
  work may commute only with a justified relation.
- Coarse observation traces do not certify privacy or application
  presentation.

## Focused changes

1. Distinguish enqueue, transport receipt, admitted machine consumption
   and business/browser adoption; contract-specific acknowledgments
   do not imply a stronger event.
2. Specify timeout and retry without inventing rejection, nonreceipt
   or new command incorporation.
3. Require trusted first-contact routing and origin binding before
   learning a correlation identifier from a message.
4. Separate semantic occurrence deduplication from accidental
   payload-equality suppression of independent occurrences.
5. Require command-specific authoritative result evidence before
   discharging a foreign predecessor.
6. Preserve sender knowledge and disclosure checks for transmitted
   optional semantic facts and communicated-only receiver provenance.
7. Clarify Request domain identity versus per-command Claim settlement.
8. Add ten adversarial acceptance cases for these distinctions.

## Current Gesso evidence (audit only)

- `gesso.choreo.semantics` models global `:communicate` as one atomic
  semantic occurrence and explicitly distinguishes global value
  existence from role-local knowledge.
- `gesso.choreo.project` projects separate send and receive gates,
  retaining required/optional/correlation/open fields.
- `gesso.choreo.machine` enforces declared payloads and sender
  knowledge for transmitted semantic fields, omitting undeclared
  open extras from semantic history and role knowledge.
- `gesso.choreo.realization` models a transport queue with drop,
  duplication and independently admitted receiver consumption;
  already consumed root duplicates cannot become second semantic
  communication occurrences.
- `gesso.choreo.knowledge` establishes only declared fields from
  communication, without authenticating arbitrary sender claims.
- None of these observations certifies every real transport, deployed
  handler, fairness assumption or browser installation path.

## Remaining cross-part joins

- 03.15–03.16: role-local knowledge and behavior-sensitive branching.
- 03.18: alternative knowledge paths cannot silently change authored
  communication or the causal proposition required by a continuation.
- Parts II, IV and VII: trusted command identity, disposition,
  actor authorization, installed operations and assembly.
- Parts V–VI: browser effect ownership, adoption and recovery.
- Parts IX–X: all-schedules projection correspondence, trace scope,
  physical origin and explicit progress/environment assumptions.

The R4 payload-closure obligation remains intact. This revision does
not constrain Gesso's future design to its present implementation.

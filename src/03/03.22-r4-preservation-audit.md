# R5.1 Part III — 03.22 Long Workflows and Durable Waiting

**R4 baseline:** `main` Part III, “Long logical workflows and short runtime
executions” and “Durable waiting belongs in authoritative state by default.”
**R5.1 candidate:** section 03.22 with preserved
`req-durable-workflow-boundary` anchor.
**Disposition:** focused R4 preservation review, not implementation status or
a certification of the full Part III design.

## R4 normative requirements preserved

- A logical business workflow may span human-scale time without keeping a
  suspended Aleph/server process or browser execution alive.
- Authoritative domain state, rather than ephemeral process memory, normally
  represents durable business waiting.
- Short separately entered E1, E2 and E3 protocols may be connected by
  authoritative state and later observations.
- Business state survival across process loss, deployment, load-balancing,
  suspension, disconnect and offline periods is conditional on the actual
  persistence and recovery contract.
- Ordinary Choreo does not transparently persist arbitrary suspended
  runtime machines; durable machine continuations are a distinct opt-in
  facility, not mandatory for every application.
- Later actors can acquire domain facts through trusted observation; a
  process's memory must not become the accidental source of business truth.

## Superior R5 structure retained

- Preserve R5's concise four-period HumanHelp approval table instead
  of replacing it with R4's longer ASCII event sketch.
- Continue to separate business workflow continuity from process/runtime
  continuity, without making particular Aleph placement a semantic guarantee.
- Keep the subsequent 03.23 section as owner of fresh-execution identity,
  detailed Expense E1/E2/E3 command binding and historical interpretation.

## Focused completed-design improvements

1. Make an in-execution =:await= versus a durable domain wait an explicit
   construction distinction, without forbidding the opt-in durable
   continuation capability.
2. Clarify durable pending state, command results and follow-up work as
   separate model-owned facts or obligations under their respective
   atomicity and retention contracts.
3. Define what a required wakeup/discovery path must cover, including
   reader authorization, missed-notification repair, qualified progress
   assumptions and the possibility of long offline periods.
4. Require later approval to establish a new principal, current model
   guards, authorized knowledge and an applicable plan, not borrow an
   earlier execution's permissions.
5. Separate durable business deadlines from disposable local timers;
   deadline-triggered work must be recoverable and revalidate authority.
6. Keep unresolved external effects, command incorporation and required
   post-commit publication debt under stable recovery ownership after E1.
7. Define the opt-in checkpoint/persisted-continuation boundary precisely:
   effect atomicity, fencing, plan migration, uncertain effects, resource
   reconstruction, replay, cleanup and retirement.
8. Add ten negative acceptance cases for crash survival, absent wakeup,
   implicit bulk approval, borrowed authority, lost notification,
   timer staleness, uncertain retries, incompatible checkpoints,
   conflicting approvers, and unjustified liveness.

## Current Gesso correspondence (audit only)

- `gesso.choreo.machine` owns portable role-local protocol state and
  distinguishes `:await` from `:return`; its declared responsibilities
  do not include durable server process placement or external persistence.
- `gesso.choreo.verify` checks definite role knowledge and terminal
  graph reachability; it expressly does not claim arbitrary liveness.
- `gesso.choreo.project` emits local executable plans and has bounded
  plan compatibility/recovery checks, not a universal durable
  checkpoint/migration engine.
- `gesso.live.browser.adapter` owns execution/effect generations and
  resource retirement, supporting the distinction between disposable
  execution ownership and durable authoritative business meaning.
- Part IV's existing authoritative operation contracts already own
  persistent command outcome, external intermediate states and required
  publication debt; this revision makes the Part III architecture
  consistent with those requirements.
- These observations are not limits on the finished design and cannot
  substantiate a claim that physical recovery is already complete.

## Cross-part obligations

- 03.23: fresh E1/E2/E3 identities, command-specific outcomes, stable
  retries and the difference between durable domain history and a
  suspended execution.
- Parts II and IV: role knowledge, precise time semantics, command

- Parts VI–VII: physical resource lifetime, work-owner transfer, worker

- Parts IX–XI: the property-specific proof of persistence/continuation,

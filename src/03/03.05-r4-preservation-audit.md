# R5.1 Part III — 03.05 Partiality, Realizability, and Early Errors

**Baseline:** R4 `main`, Part III “Choreography may be partial with respect
to the whole application,” “Choreography validity and application
realizability are distinct questions,” and “Construction must fail when the
contradiction is already knowable.”
**Candidate:** `r5.1` source unit 03.05.
**Status:** focused preservation review, not whole-Part-III acceptance,
complete installed-application proof, or a general liveness theorem.

## R4 normative obligations retained

- A choreography may describe a single operation or bounded protocol rather
  than the lifetime of the application. Durable model state can relate
  separate executions across people, hosts, and days.
- Correctness of the choreography and completeness/realization of an
  application are distinct. A valid semantic graph does not imply that
  its browser plan, route, server capability, acquisition, or recovery
  are installed.
- Intentionally unexposed operations are not necessarily assembly defects;
  required use and product capability completeness have different scopes.
- Statically knowable malformed graphs, undeclared roles/authorities,
  impossible continuations and role-local knowledge violations fail at
  the earliest competent construction/checker boundary.
- Runtime status changes and authorization races are not predicted by
  static construction; supported operations must retain dynamic result
  and recovery branches.
- An accepted opaque full-language premise does not erase a concrete
  contradiction or justify fabricating proof of a missing edge.

## Superior R5 semantics retained

- Short Claim and approval workflows explicitly preserve committed, rejected,
  already incorporated, or indeterminate command outcomes and allowed
  reconciliation; they do not assume a universal durable choreography runner.
- Application assurance distinguishes source construction, required
  realization edges, physical/deployed enforcement, and behavioral
  capability acceptance under Part I's three claims.
- Missing required edges, uninspected physical premises, and unsupported
  static proof obligations receive distinct classifications rather than
  false successful semantics.

## Changes in this revision

1. Explain cross-execution continuity: E2 re-acquires principal, authority,
   subject and current observation; E1's resource/command rights are not
   inherited merely because durable business state relates E1 and E2.
2. Make the *declared required-use scope* explicit and distinguish graph,
   artifact, route, authority, settlement and acquisition/recovery edges
   from actual deployed handler adoption or physical causality.
3. Require declared recovery paths to have inspectable usable owner,
   knowledge, binding and physical capabilities where they are necessary
   and the relevant checker can inspect those facts.
4. Add seven negative/positive classification cases covering optional
   operations, missing plans/routes, role-local knowledge, concurrent
   Claim changes, unavailable recovery, unverified handler deployment,
   and E1/E2 independent admission.
5. Keep static contradiction, missing assembly, environmental premises,
   and dynamic uncertainty distinct; none is silently promoted to a
   general semantic theorem.

## Current Gesso implementation correspondence

- `gesso.choreo.verify` implements structural and role-local must-analysis,
  including reachable/terminal paths, but declares no physical deployment,
  runtime authorization or liveness proof.
- `gesso.choreo.preflight` checks a limited PlanRegistry boundary: canonical
  ExecutablePlans, required logical keys, role binding and optional digest
  agreement. It does not certify complete app realization.
- `gesso.live.application-preflight.assurance` distinguishes bounded
  application backbone/supplied surface preflight from installed Biff/HTML
  adoption, includes explicit startup/raw response escape hatches, and
  acknowledges incomplete dynamic render enumeration.
- `gesso.live.application-preflight.semantic-audit` reports that trusted
  model predicates, source rule dependencies and atomic transaction guards
  remain uninspected by the current operation summary.
- `gesso.assurance.proof.application-assurance-report` expressly records
  missing installed writer/route completeness, universal physical
  refinement, browser execution correspondence and independent kernel
  soundness.
- These checks provide a real, narrower implementation substrate and do
  not establish every design-level required edge for arbitrary apps.

## Deferred cross-part obligations

- 03.06–03.11: one semantic declaration and derived application bindings,
  operation interfaces, admission and typed failure diagnostics.
- 03.15–03.25: role-local knowledge, communication/causality,
  independently executable plans, and durable handoff.
- Parts IV, VI–VII: guarded incorporation, settlement/reconciliation, real
  browser/HTMX/SSE and installed application/route closure.
- Parts VIII–X: HumanHelp end-to-end acceptance, proof correspondence and
  explicit treatment of uninspected physical/environment premises.

No original R4 partiality, realizability, or early-error requirement was
intentionally discarded. All Part III anchors/second-level headings remain
unchanged. This audit is documentation, not runtime certification.

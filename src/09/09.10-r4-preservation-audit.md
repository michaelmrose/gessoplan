## Governing decision

R5 provides the preferred concise structure: checked and
consumer-specific composition with exact identity as the
default. Restore the valuable R4 operational stale-dependency
and repair narrative without copying its illustrative APIs as
if they were already implemented. Strengthen distinct graph
edges, versioned semantic identity, implementation binding,
checked directed compatibility and the dependent-cache
path. Treat the whole design as the timeless specification
of a finished framework, not a v1622 implementation list.

## R4 preservation and design decisions

1. **Composition is about relied-on propositions.** An artifact
   class, identifier string or filename does not state which
   upstream guarantee, model scope, premise and authority the
   dependent proof actually uses. Interfaces export exactly
   those commitments, not private checker/compiler internals.

2. **Check two different graph properties.** Generic structural
   reachability, acyclicity, exact edge fingerprints and roots
   are necessary. Independent source-derived required nodes/
   edges and full obligation-case coverage are separately
   necessary for a complete source-relative claim.

3. **Induction coverage is first-class.** The original catalog,
   initial condition, invariants, every operation, base/step
   case and open premise belong in the complete required
   closure. Omitting a difficult Unclaim case cannot be
   justified by a valid smaller DAG.

4. **Source completeness differs from physical coverage.**
   A complete declared catalog is still not an authenticated
   inventory of real Request writers, routes, dynamic handlers,
   generated browser assets, startup hooks and workers.
   This unresolved correspondence must remain visible.

5. **Proof edges and runtime loops differ.** Reciprocal proof
   justification is invalid without an admitted internal
   induction/fixed-point rule. Runtime retries, state cycles,
   subscriptions and provider-correlation edges may legitimately
   cycle but do not discharge proof premises.

6. **Distributed provenance applies at composition sites.**
   The dependency must be available to the particular role
   and branch with sufficient actual issuer, disclosure,
   observation scope and currentness, not just globally
   well typed or declared at the same field name.

7. **Keep six distinct identities.** Interface, proposition,
   checking evidence, implementation, installation and
   runtime instance answer different questions. They cannot
   be substituted merely by sharing one hash or class.

8. **Hashing follows checked normalization.** Semantic ID
   equality depends on versioned encoding and preservation
   assumptions. A digest is not a proof of semantic truth,
   and no copied field or successful structural ID grants
   checking or runtime authority.

9. **Implementation identity includes actual behavior-bearing
   dependencies.** A Clojure Var name or source-file digest
   is insufficient when closures, bindings, libraries, host
   configuration or generated assets affect the contract.
   Undiscoverable dependencies create scoped premises.

10. **Changed upstream meaning invalidates relevant
    dependents.** Organization O14 Boolean policy replaced
    by O15 closed enum is a legitimate source evolution
    and a stale Claim C21 dependency. Its downstream
    operation, Choreo, projection and executable plan cannot
    be reused on an old justification without checking again.

11. **Preserve R4's repaired Claim case.** New C22 uses
    explicit authenticated principal and helper-role
    evidence for `:helpers-only`, along with current
    Membership/Organization association. A typecheck is
    not a proof of complete production authorization
    or atomic model guard implementation.

12. **Outcome-set evolution must not invent totality.**
    Adding `:already-incorporated` and
    `:commit-unknown` cannot preserve a downstream
    accepted/rejected-only terminal handling contract.
    Closed-enum exhaustive cases and optional-field
    refinements require renewed judgments.

13. **Compatibility is directional and context-dependent.**
    One consumer's required propositions might survive a
    source change while another's do not. A checking
    bridge must preserve type variance, negative/closed
    facts, premise applicability, trust and role-local
    disclosure; no speculative “more fields means safe”
    subtyping or blanket transitivity is admitted.

14. **Assumptions cannot be lost in composition.** The
    bridge must propagate relied-upon premises and
    cannot relabel trusted policy or current-runtime
    obligations as checked. Section 09.11 separately
    governs actual verified discharge.

15. **Cache reuse is assurance-class-specific.** Pure
    contract theorem evidence may survive unrelated
    CSS changes, but browser artifact/install evidence
    must be checked against its own changed binding.
    Impact is a typed reverse closure of required
    dependency edges rather than full namespace
    invalidation by default.

16. **Old-current consistency is not currentness.**
    The trusted consumer obtains source, profile and
    installed generation. A stale source and stale
    self-consistent DAG from an attacker may still
    describe an old subject correctly, but it cannot
    certify the current installed application.

17. **Reload is coherent, not a partial patch.**
    The replacement application/plan/source is checked
    as one candidate before cutover. Runtime grants and
    retained durable command histories have their
    own validity and cannot be revived by a new
    static semantic fingerprint.

18. **Product acceptance remains independent.** Real
    authorized Claim, creator-local Create, remote
    advisory and one user-granted Refresh require
    positive installed behavior and nearby adversarial
    rejection; no abstract certificate is a product
    acceptance substitute.

## Source correspondence: actual Gesso v1622

- `gesso.assurance.subject/describe-subject` (subject.clj)
  validates pure, bounded declarative interfaces and
  calculates SHA-256 from a versioned ASCII canonical
  representation. It ignores map/set entry order and
  source metadata, preserves vector order and nominal
  keys, and treats integer numerals as mathematical
  integers for this *structural identity only*. The
  namespace expressly says identity is unadmitted and
  not implementation/semantic equivalence proof.

- `gesso.assurance.admission` registers narrow structural
  subject/premise handles using object identity with
  a private WeakHashMap. Copying a map/report does not
  recreate checking status. It explicitly does not
  prove business truth, implementation conformance
  or durable runtime authority.

- `gesso.assurance.dependency/check-graph!` checks
  exact named nodes, live subject handles, expected
  edge fingerprints, no duplicate/self/cyclic
  references, roots and reachability. Its in-process
  checked graph is a *snapshot*, not an issued proof.
  `require-current-graph!` reconstructs against a
  consumer-provided fresh graph; it does not source
  that trusted graph itself.
  `affected-nodes` returns reverse transitive
  impact; it explicitly does not revoke or
  recertify deployed certificates automatically.

- `gesso.assurance.proof.induction-dependency`
  and `proof.application-dependency` reconstruct
  required graph content from the original
  abstract induction catalog and source-derived
  ledger. Their exact match rejects internally
  coherent but pruned graphs. This establishes
  source-relative *declared* catalog closure,
  not actual installation/writer coverage.

- `gesso.assurance.proof.certificate` admits
  source-checked abstract propositional,
  guarded-transition and conditional-induction
  kinds only, with exact bound graph, subject
  and open-premise checking; its issuer does not
  grant production effect authority. Its
  freshness check requires original proof
  source and current graph supplied by the
  responsible consumer, not copied report
  metadata.

- `test/gesso/assurance/dependency_test.clj`
  includes `stale-dependency-detected-before-stale-
  graph-comparison`, changed root/added/removed
  node rejection, and exact reverse-dependency
  closure. `test/gesso/assurance/proof/
  application_dependency_mutation_test.clj`
  contains a decisive adversarial control:
  a *generic* structurally checked DAG with
  pruned Unclaim can pass structural checking
  while application source-derived checking
  rejects with
  `:source-dependency-coverage-mismatch`.
  These are inspected tests, not newly
  executed passing tests.

Current Gesso does **not** implement a general
certified semantic subtyping/compatibility bridge,
a universal namespace-to-physical writer inventory,
an independently proven implementation-binding
completeness theorem, or deployed
browser/XTDB all-schedules correspondence from
those source identities. These are requirements
or openly scoped premises for the finished
framework, not capabilities inferred from v1622.

## Positive and adversarial conformance cases

1. **Exact source composition:** Real, recognized
   source-derived checked dependencies pass.
   **Adverse:** valid DAG missing Unclaim source
   evidence fails required closure.
2. **Fresh graph:** Stable subject/edge/root passes
   current graph check. **Adverse:** changed
   dependency fingerprint without rebinding fails.
3. **Source-required edge:** Original catalog includes
   every obligation and role. **Adverse:** forged
   checked DAG with a removed original invariant
   fails at required-closure checker.
4. **R4 stale Claim:** O14/C21/O31/H12/P7/E5
   produce valid old scoped result. **Adverse:**
   O15 policy enum cannot be paired with cached
   C21, despite a valid new O15.
5. **Repaired policy:** New C22 binds explicit helper
   principal and closed policy branches.
   **Adverse:** `:helpers-only` accidentally
   admits a non-helper.
6. **Outcome evolution:** Choreo covers all current
   Claim outcome variants. **Adverse:** new
   `:commit-unknown` silently falls through an
   old total settlement branch.
7. **Closed enum:** Same variant set supports
   exhaustive match. **Adverse:** newly possible
   enum variant passes old exhaustiveness proof.
8. **Type presence:** Checked optional-value guard
   justifies field use. **Adverse:** required→optional
   change is treated as a harmless extension.
9. **Semantic identity:** Reordered map entries
   maintain exact canonical identity.
   **Adverse:** ordered vector/nominal type change
   reuses the old fingerprint as if equivalent.
10. **Unchanged abstract theorem:** Renderer-only
    CSS edit does not necessarily invalidate
    model-rule theorem. **Adverse:** browser
    artifact/install evidence is reused for
    changed served bytes.
11. **Source binding:** Current Clojure callable
    and relevant provider dependency are qualified.
    **Adverse:** same Var symbol but different
    closure/writer body inherits old proof.
12. **Bridge accepted:** Separate checked
    directional bridge preserves one consumer's
    actual guarantees and premises. **Adverse:**
    bridge is reused across a different consumer
    requiring an excluded closed-world fact.
13. **Cyclic protocols:** Retry/state loops remain
    legitimate under admitted semantics.
    **Adverse:** reciprocal proof certificate
    cycle is treated as fixed-point proof.
14. **Known premises:** Upstream open premise
    stays visible under dependent claim.
    **Adverse:** unverified statement is renamed
    to `:discharged` by a caller.
15. **Trust domain:** Role sees authorized fact
    from actual source. **Adverse:** pasted field
    or old observation is counted as role-local
    authoritative knowledge.
16. **Consumer freshness:** Actual live assembler
    obtains current source and rejects a stale
    cache. **Adverse:** attacker-supplied
    old source and old matching graph gets
    current-app certification.
17. **Hot reload:** Fully checked replacement
    changes generation coherently.
    **Adverse:** old plan, new route and changed
    operation writer are combined via a
    copied version stamp.
18. **Runtime identity:** Old durable C42
    historical outcome can be separately recovered.
    **Adverse:** retired browser lease or old
    principal authorization revived by a
    static semantic certificate.
19. **Minimal invalidation:** Unrelated source
    formatting doesn't force semantic proof
    rebuild. **Adverse:** required dependency
    edit is missed by production cache lookup.
20. **Positive product:** Eligible Claim and
    allowed selective Create/Refresh work under
    selected assembly. **Adverse:** reject-all
    construction or extra remote Refresh
    is misreported as passing assurance.

The case oracle must state its desired result and
the first enforcement boundary before running the
mutant. A proof or structural-check outcome
cannot be credited with detection of an actual
physical defect without qualified observations.

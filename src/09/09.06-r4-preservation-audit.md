## Governing design and nonclaims

R5 improves on R4 by committing to one *closed abstract guarded-transition
relation*, a small and checkable consequence calculus, one source-owned
operation catalog, and source-derived initial/preservation obligations.
Retain those improvements and the distinction between logical and
resource-sensitive facts. The proposed finished Gesso system remains
forward-looking: its users should declare business rules and build
applications in ordinary Clojure/Gesso, with routine checker artifacts
generated automatically. It should not require an independently
hand-maintained TLA+/Quint/Recife application model.

A checked conditional transition fact or induction candidate **does not**
establish its own rule/checker meta-soundness, original Clojure-to-abstract
translation, current authorized actor, actual XTDB write, completeness
of all database writers, physical browser installation, or HumanHelp
product correctness. The normative text names these as genuinely separate
bridges, rather than weakening the goal of eventual reusable assurance.

## R4 obligations retained and sharpened

1. **Logical reusable facts versus physical one-use rights.** The R4 proof-DAG
   dependency contract and resource ownership principles coexist: proofs
   can reuse ordinary logical premises; issued Refresh/read grants, client
   execution/target leases, current actor capabilities and repair obligations
   cannot be duplicated by propositional conjunction or certificate replay.
   Affine versus linear resources require distinct permitted consumption,
   discard and retirement behavior. Neither checking resource *claims*
   nor writing =:verified?= into a map issues a resource.

2. **Source-owned exact transition semantics.** The model relates one typed
   prestate, disjoint typed input, guard, exact read/write sets, simultaneous
   assigned poststate fields and complement frame. No sequencing between
   assignments, ambient Clojure callback, implicit mutation or arbitrary
   database query is hidden in this initial calculus. Checked *construction*
   is not a universal transition consequence, and one sampled witness
   is not a theorem.

3. **The frame preserves presence as well as value.** R4's source-level
   invariants and no-silent-downgrade rule demand an accurate state
   interpretation. Optional absent is different from present nil;
   a copied/unchanged optional field cannot be replaced by a total
   `nil` value without an applicable rule. Written optional poststate
   can be total if the assignment makes it present. Optional
   prestate/input read terms remain partial unless guarded.

4. **A guarded consequence depends on T matching.** The abstract
   conclusion is `T(s,x,s') => C(s,x,s')`, universally quantified over
   *compatible* triples. A checked `:guard` proof says the guard
   follows from the matching relation; it does not assert that a real
   Request currently satisfies the guard or give the browser authority
   to Claim.

5. **Closed inference rules and nominal phase identity.** Guard,
   assignment, frame, conjunction introduction/elimination and
   whole-operand equality substitution retain exact types,
   source and pre/input/post phases. Unsupported arithmetic,
   predicate rewriting, inferred absence, field deletion
   and relationship updates do not magically enter the calculus.
   Extensions need new typed constructors and checked bridges,
   not a caller-registered "trusted proof rule."

6. **Original proof source rechecking.** R4's faulty-elaborator
   containment applies to transition derivations too: each
   admitted proof is checked from its original declaration,
   exact source identity, rule-specific fields, prior steps,
   final goal and still-open abstract relation premise.
   A copied report/fingerprint cannot authorize a transaction.

7. **One shared operation-set and invariant source.** R4's
   proof/DAG obligations and R5's stronger design require
   a single original catalog sharing initial condition,
   nominal state types, invariant set and transition
   projection. Per-operation proof inputs must not
   secretly disagree about the invariant. Missing,
   duplicated or substituted catalog entries are not
   just a conveniently smaller proof target when an
   independent application capability still requires them.

8. **Simultaneous invariant strengthening.** For each
   invariant `I_j` the preservation proof may assume
   *all* invariants `Inv(s)` hold in the common prestate:
   `Inv(s) ∧ T_i(s,x,s') ⇒ I_j(s')`.
   This is stronger and more useful than forcing
   each clause to preserve itself under only its
   own assumption. All base cases and the
   operation-by-invariant cross-product must be
   checked from the admitted catalog; partial
   success cannot become a whole-catalog theorem.

9. **Valid mixed-family proof needs an actual inference.** Frame
   extensionality, literal postcondition, copy equality and
   a sound mixed/conjunction constructor are valuable.
   R4's compositional principle does not license
   unioning partial success-ID sets. Conversely
   a correct *new* checked composition rule may
   combine valid children from different families
   with identical subject, state, clauses and
   open premises. The current narrower family
   limitation is not a timeless prohibition.

10. **Original obligation ledgers are not retrospectively
    discharged.** Source-derived base/preservation
    obligations, catalog coverage and meta-assumptions
    remain visible after a conditional induction
    candidate is generated. A source report or
    opaque certificate handle cannot quietly rewrite
    their status or upgrade an abstract result to
    an unqualified production proof.

11. **Initial satisfiability and physical coverage are
    independent.** The mathematical base case
    may be vacuously true when the initial class
    is empty. An application acceptance gate
    requires an eligible witness or checked
    existence proof, plus actual initializer
    coverage. A restricted checker that catches
    explicit false does not establish the
    completeness of its satisfiability analysis.

12. **Model proof versus production refinement.** R4's proof
    artifacts cannot affect runtime semantics.
    Transfer to XTDB requires a qualified
    abstraction of real state and effects,
    actual transaction prestate/absence,
    guard and atomicity evidence, complete
    relevant writer inventory and trusted
    current actor checks. A change to
    implementation, external writer or
    source dependency can invalidate this
    correspondence while leaving the
    abstract theorem intact.

13. **Correct HumanHelp invariant meaning.** At most one
    active primary assignment per Request
    is a safety property. Exactly one when
    claimed adds conditional existence and
    Request/assignment status consistency.
    A scalar RequestStatus proof cannot
    certify RequestAssignment uniqueness,
    concurrent dual Claim, or manager
    Reassign under current Membership
    authorization. These independent
    obligations remain in the application/
    production model proof subject.

14. **Safety and progress have different semantics.**
    A safety induction is not a fair-scheduling,
    eventual notification, crash recovery,
    command-outcome convergence, resource
    reclamation or guaranteed user Refresh
    theorem. R5.1 Part VIII *selects*
    advisory-only remote lifecycle adoption,
    so lack of user Refresh is not itself
    proof of broken safety. Strong
    liveness depends on named fairness,
    worker availability and retained
    recovery evidence.

15. **Non-vacuous acceptance, not reject-all.** Genuine
    positive frame, assignment, same-origin
    copy, supported mixed conjunction,
    initial consequence and full declared
    induction are required alongside
    negative cases. Unsupported proof
    constructor remains open, not "proved
    false" or silently admitted as stronger
    theorem. Exact first-boundary diagnostics
    distinguish malformed source, unsupported
    theory, missing operation, physical
    writer escape and incorrect product
    truth.

## Actual Gesso v1622 correspondence — source-level evidence

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/transition.clj` implements the
  restricted v1233 abstract guarded assignment/frame
  relation. It validates nominal typed source and
  checks *specific* pre/input/post witnesses;
  source comments expressly exclude real XTDB
  execution, arithmetic assignments, side effects,
  deletion, or relation proof.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/derivation.clj` contains the
  v1235 small closed conditional consequence
  checker. Its source rechecks the v1233
  transition, exact finite proof DAG,
  =:guard=, assignment, frame, conjunction
  and equality substitution. Optional
  pre/input equality terms cannot be
  treated as total without proof.
  Report data remain copyable/unadmitted
  with matching abstract relation open.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/obligation.clj` generates
  initial and preservation obligations
  for each declared invariant from
  guarded-replacement *source*. It
  explicitly retains incomplete
  invariant-set and real initializer
  coverage, outside writers, checker
  soundness and physical effects as
  open. The generated ledger
  is not an admission credential.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/operation_set.clj`
  rechecks a nonempty exact *declared*
  catalog with common initial/invariant
  and nominal state projection. It
  rejects duplicate/extra/missing
  relative to **declared IDs** but
  does not know that the declared
  set includes every production
  operation or external writer.
  Its results are source-relative
  and copied descriptions.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/initial.clj`
  uses deliberately restricted
  source-derived base-case rules:
  exact same predicate, mandatory
  =:and= conjunct, or independent
  literal true. It leaves a
  manifestly false initial class
  open instead of manufacturing
  a useful induction theorem,
  and does not solve general
  satisfiability or initializer
  correspondence.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/preservation.clj`
  checks frame extensionality for
  reader footprints wholly framed
  in both presence and value.
  `src/gesso/assurance/proof/unified_preservation.clj`
  combines four *restricted*
  families—frame, literal, mixed
  frame/literal conjunction,
  same-origin copy—only when one
  family independently supports
  the whole source invariant.
  This prevents unsound union
  of unrelated fragment results;
  a separately proved
  composition rule could
  legitimately extend it later.

- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/induction.clj`
  demands original-source
  base and per-operation
  preservation for every
  declared invariant. It
  reports conditional abstract
  candidates only, with
  checker meta-soundness,
  physical writer closure,
  initialization and
  implementation correspondence
  open. Captured sibling-checker
  recomputation catches some
  substitution/tampering,
  not shared checker bugs
  or independent meta-soundness.

- `test/gesso/assurance/proof/derivation_test.clj`
  and `induction_test.clj`
  include genuine positive source
  examples and mutations for
  forged transition facts,
  optional-field frames,
  bad proof dependencies,
  partial induction, omitted
  catalog member, false initial,
  changed shared invariant
  and physical nonclaims.
  They are **test definitions**
  inspected via GitHub—not
  tests executed in this
  editorial revision.

This is substantial existing groundwork, but not evidence that
current Gesso supports every relation, general invariant
composition, independent kernel meta-soundness, complete
HumanHelp physical writer confinement or all
XTDB/browser refinement obligations.

## Positive and adversarial acceptance challenges

1. A genuine guarded abstract transition witnesses the
   specified guard, assignments and full frame
   on matching typed pre/input/post values.
2. A false guard, wrong assigned value or
   changed nominal state type does not
   admit the same transition/proof.
3. A required framed field remains equal,
   and an optional absent field remains
   absent—not present nil.
4. A written optional field becomes
   total only under an explicit
   assignment establishing presence.
5. Whole-operand equality substitution
   works for a compatible checked
   equality; forged arithmetic or
   cross-phase substitution is rejected.
6. A valid frame-preserving invariant
   is supported while a rule that
   actually writes its reader
   remains open for that family.
7. A literal postcondition and
   same-origin copy case pass
   their selected restricted
   proof constructors.
8. A valid supported mixed frame/literal
   conjunction passes while
   an unsupported cross-family
   proof-ID union is refused.
9. A valid initial-state consequence
   passes; initial false and an
   insufficient base condition
   cannot yield a useful
   unconditional application claim.
10. A correct source catalog with
    common state schemas/invariants
    passes its declared-set gate;
    missing/duplicated/altered IDs
    fail relative to that set.
11. Omission of promised HumanHelp
    Reassign does not pass the
    independent operation-capability
    completeness gate even if a
    smaller formal catalog is closed.
12. An invariant with a missing
    initial proof or one
    unsupported operation remains
    open; good results for other
    invariants remain usable
    under their limited scope.
13. Shrinking the invariant set,
    changing a guard or swapping
    proof sources invalidates
    old conditional candidates.
14. A forged proof status or
    modified source report does
    not become an admitted
    certificate or authorize
    a production mutation.
15. Claim/Unclaim/Reassign abstract
    examples do not imply actual
    RequestAssignment uniqueness
    without a relational model
    and writer/XTDB correspondence.
16. Two simultaneous real Claim
    requests challenge a protected
    atomic guard; a successful
    serial demonstration alone
    is not the physical race.
17. An external raw writer,
    import or administrative
    bypass remains open writer
    coverage or fails the
    selected stronger profile.
18. A valid finite safety
    induction makes no claim
    of fair scheduling, eventual
    advisory consumption or
    unknown-command convergence.
19. A genuine eligible initial
    and product operation
    provides positive non-vacuity;
    an unsatisfiable initial
    class cannot qualify a
    full product-completion claim.
20. An unsupported richer
    relation is not falsely
    represented as scalar
    assignment semantics
    or theorem disproof.

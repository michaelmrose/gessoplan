## Judgment

Keep R5's precise and substantially superior baseline: two separate
presence/non-nil refinements, left-to-right short-circuit typing,
conservative normalization and explicit opaque-policy contracts.
R4 contains important requirements that cannot be lost in a short
summary: refinement **scope and lifetime**, proof of syntax-to-normalized
meaning, detection of **correctness-relevant semantic-policy escapes**
within an actually inspected surface, no completeness inference about
uninspected Clojure, business-intent review as non-theorem evidence, and
correct model/transaction placement for mutable opaque authorization
inputs. The revised normative section makes these obligations concrete.

The finished framework's source language remains normal Clojure,
with dedicated finite/closed checkers for supported fragments and
explicit typed opaque boundaries elsewhere. This is not a proposal
for HumanHelp authors to maintain separate handwritten TLA+/Quint
sources or a parallel operation registry. An implementation-specific
constant or internal namespace is evidence, not a timeless contract.

## R4 preservation decisions

1. **Path-sensitive rule typing is not a free Boolean solver.**
   R4 requires explicit evaluation/refinement semantics. Facts
   available after an =:and= true prefix, an =:or= false prefix,
   true implication antecedent, or negation are valid only on
   those evaluated paths. False conjunction/true disjunction
   do not imply every child has the same result. Joins preserve
   only facts established on every incoming path.
2. **Presence and non-nil remain independent.** Optional absent,
   optional present nil and present false have different meaning.
   An optional nullable integer needs dominating presence and
   non-nil evidence before ordering. Neither Clojure truthiness
   nor checking a different reader supplies that evidence.
3. **The observation identity matters.** R4 expressly binds
   refinements to source, subject and observation context.
   An immutable snapshot's reader fact cannot be reused with
   a different Request, next XTDB basis, new session,
   protected transaction, or later Live message.
   Applicability needs a separate checked link.
4. **Intrinsic safety differs from choreography and commit rights.**
   A well-typed rule can be invalid at a browser point with
   insufficient knowledge/provenance, or obsolete after a
   Membership/Organization update. Current authorization
   remains a production model/XTDB guarded fact, not a
   language-type outcome.
5. **Normalization preserves actual observable semantics.**
   Map/set insertion order, integer representation and
   omitted default-false flags may be canonicalized in the
   baseline. Operand/order, reader identities, nominal
   enums/domains, optionality/nullability and failure
   paths cannot be commuted or folded based on mere
   classical truth-table equivalence.
6. **Original-source elaboration and checker independence.**
   R4 states that author syntax to normalized rule meaning
   needs checked preservation or a named premise. A cached
   type report, an old fingerprint or the normalizer
   claiming success does not itself supply that proof.
   Hashes are structural change detectors, not
   logical equivalence or implementation credentials.
7. **Compatibility is checked, not guessed.** Exact semantic
   identity is the safe default; an evolved interface may
   satisfy a dependent only through a checked compatibility
   relation preserving required guarantees and closed-world
   assumptions. Changed host implementation or served
   asset needs its own correspondence even when the
   abstract normalized rule does not change.
8. **Opaque contracts carry typed provenance and obligation.**
   R4 allowed named trusted predicates/derivations without
   pretending they were pure. Include input/output, model
   owner, implementation/issuer, effects, termination,
   disclosure, dependencies and trust/refinement scope.
   A valid output Boolean or a descriptive =:assumed-pure=
   field is not proof of the underlying function.
9. **Opaque current authorization cannot be cached without
   preservation.** If Organization/Membership/Request facts
   change between checking and Claim commit, the current
   transaction must re-establish the relevant guard or
   verify a separately checked equivalence and protected
   dependency/linearization protocol. A browser-supplied
   eligible flag never grants model authority.
10. **Effects and termination are independently qualified.**
    Declared external effects need actual isolation or a
    named trusted completeness premise. A requested timeout
    does not guarantee cancellation of arbitrary JVM or
    external I/O. Output checking alone does not show
    the external service was invoked, confined, correct
    or that no mutation occurred.
11. **Detected policy escape differs from unknown code.**
    A correctness-relevant branch *found inside the
    inspected source subset* without an admitted rule,
    verified public dependency, runtime premise or
    opaque assumption is a typed semantic-policy escape.
    Uninspected arbitrary Clojure is an explicit open
    conformance boundary, not proof the whole program
    is free of hidden policies.
12. **Heuristic product review is useful but not a theorem.**
    R4's human, model-assisted or heuristic audit may
    identify a suspicious missing authorization or
    contradictory rationale. Such findings must be
    confirmed as business intent and promoted to
    named declarations before normal mechanical
    checking. A heuristic cannot issue or rewrite
    a proof certificate by itself.
13. **Relational extensions have explicit semantics.**
    Typed relationships, finite-family/quantified
    constraints, closed variants and guarded multi-record
    transitions may use separate dedicated checkers.
    Path type/cardinality does not establish current
    endpoint existence, actual query completeness,
    authorized access or absence of interfering XTDB
    writers.
14. **Source and independent product coverage survive.**
    Closed rule syntactic footprints cannot certify
    complete intended business requirements. A green
    Claim-only proof that omits required Reassign or
    outside policy writers fails the relevant stronger
    application/HumanHelp certification.
15. **Non-vacuity and exact diagnostics are required.**
    A good score rule, legitimate opaque Organization
    function, and actual guarded Claim must succeed
    under their respective profiles while malformed,
    stale, forged or unobserved cases reject at the
    first responsible boundary. A reject-everything
    checker does not meet this contract.

## Current Gesso v1622 source correspondence and limits

- `src/gesso/assurance/rule_type.clj` contains the conservative
  `refine` transfer for `:present`, `:not`, nil equality/
  inequality, `:and`, `:or` and `:implies`; its checker
  tracks present/non-nil sets under exact reader type
  footprints. It rejects optional unsafe reads and
  nonnullable ordering without adequate refinement.
  These are bounded closed-language checks, not
  global current Request knowledge.
- `src/gesso/assurance/rule_eval.clj` implements strict
  Boolean left-to-right short circuit over a validated
  immutable binding map and exact JVM integer comparison.
  Its source documentation says that provided bindings
  are assumed to be a trustworthy snapshot; it cannot
  prove their authentic provenance or transaction
  consistency.
- `src/gesso/assurance/rule_normalize.clj`
  revalidates original syntax/types and equates only
  named integer/map/set/default-false representation
  choices. It retains Boolean/argument order and
  nominal enum/reader/flag distinctions. Its return
  value is explicitly `unadmitted`, with
  `:proof-status :not-proved`, and is not a
  trusted runtime capability.
- `src/gesso/assurance/subject.clj` provides
  structural source-relative fingerprints, not
  theorem certificates or arbitrary Clojure
  executable equivalence checking.
- `src/gesso/assurance/opaque.clj` validates
  descriptive typed input/output contracts,
  effects modes `:unknown`, `:assumed-pure`,
  `:declared-external`, and termination modes
  `:unknown`, `:assumed-terminates`,
  `:requested-timeout`. It explicitly does
  *not* execute the callback or service; it
  reports open obligations for provenance,
  business correctness, effects/confinement,
  termination, authoritative enforcement,
  runtime correspondence and, where applicable,
  complete external effects/timeout enforcement.
- `test/gesso/assurance/rule_type_test.clj`
  includes optional/nullable and wrong-order
  refinements; `rule_eval_test.clj` includes
  guarded =:or= and implication, false/nil/absent
  distinctions and unsupported hostile values.
  `rule_normalize_test.clj` has explicit
  reordering, enum identity, flag, operand,
  changed-source and forged-report tests.
  These test definitions were *inspected only*,
  not run in this revision.
- Actual production Claim model dependencies and
  XTDB guards remain outside this tiny pure
  rule interpreter. A source-bound descriptive
  opaque contract is insufficient to establish
  issuer identity, current actor authority,
  actual atomicity, or complete physical writer
  coverage. Supported browser/CLJS lowering
  and more general relational theorem bridges
  require additional applicable evidence.

This implementation already provides meaningful, concrete
precursors to R5's improved design, especially for strict
flow-sensitive checking and conservative identity. It
does not by itself satisfy all future-system composition,
source completeness, runtime conformance or proof-kernel
soundness guarantees.

## Positive and adversarial acceptance cases

1. Optional/nullable score guarded by presence then non-nil
   then comparison: accepted with correct true/false
   and no invalid absent/nil dereference.
2. Reordered score comparison before either needed
   guard: rejected by intrinsic typing.
3. A disjunction `(not present) or (nil equality) or
   comparison` with exactly the correct false-path
   refinements: admitted; test absent, nil, zero
   and boundary integer values.
4. Do not treat false conjunction as all-false or true
   disjunction as all-true; a malicious branch-join
   attempt cannot infer unsupported reader facts.
5. A presence fact for R17 at basis A cannot type/
   authorize a dereference of R18 or a fresh
   R17 observation at basis B.
6. Equal map/set insertion order and 7/7N integer
   representations obtain equivalent normalized
   identity under the same semantic profile.
7. Swapping guarded operands, changing nominal
   enum domains, reader identity, type flags,
   operator/threshold or rule version does
   not acquire the earlier proof's identity.
8. Logic-equivalent-looking host algebra
   rewrites are not accepted as baseline
   canonicalization without side-condition
   and evaluation-preservation evidence.
9. A copied normalized/fingerprint report
   cannot replace rechecked source or a
   separately admitted certificate.
10. Same abstract normalized rule with a
    changed callback, output provider or
    served JS requires renewed concrete
    correspondence where relied upon.
11. A real named opaque Organization contract
    allows a qualified explicit assumption
    under a narrower profile; a fake
    `:effects :assumed-pure` field does
    not prove no side effect or purity.
12. A typed Boolean output attributed to
    the wrong issuer, actor, Organization,
    Request or stale Membership cannot
    authorize current guarded Claim.
13. A requested timeout is not claimed
    to cancel a noncooperative blocked
    JVM callback or external network call.
14. A branch detected in an actually
    inspected protected writer that changes
    authorization without matching rule/
    runtime/opaque contract is diagnosed
    as a semantic-policy escape.
15. An arbitrary uninspected Clojure body
    remains an open conformance boundary,
    not falsely certified free of hidden
    business-policy conditions.
16. A valid typed relationship path cannot
    establish missing target data, a
    disallowed read, an incomplete query
    or absence of another primary writer.
17. A human reviewer may flag a
    suspicious terminal lifecycle rule;
    its commentary does not become a
    proof until source declarations
    change and are rechecked.
18. A valid Claim and Reassign product
    case remains available despite
    negative acceptance mutations;
    removing required Reassign from a
    checked rule catalog fails the
    independent HumanHelp capability oracle.

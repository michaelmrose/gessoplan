## R4 requirements recovered and preserved

1. **Authoritative settlement is more than an HTTP success code.**
   R4 required command and execution correlation, a typed model
   outcome, progression and provisional resolution. The R5
   precursor improved this to the four Part IV authoritative
   outcomes and explicit participant `:accepted`, `:rejected`
   and `:unknown`. Section 07.08 now gives distinct conditions
   for `:committed`, `:already-incorporated`, `:rejected`
   and `:commit-unknown` and preserves current disclosure
   authorization for durable replays.
2. **A committed write survives response loss.** A timeout, HTTP
   error, absence of a command record in an insufficient read or
   a card showing a later claimant is not proof that the original
   command was rejected. Retain the original binding and
   resolver ownership across retries, rendering failure and
   deployment generations; do not mint a new logical command.
3. **Current projection and historical command are distinct.**
   R4's post-commit reread can supersede provisional UI without
   resolving which command incorporated. The C42 Claim case
   remains incorporated even when a later Reassign changes
   the visible card. No model outcome is inferred from a
   convenient present-state read.
4. **Scoped progression propagates into every required reread.**
   R4 required model, publication, required basis, fragment
   query, renderer and browser to agree. 07.08 explicitly
   avoids arbitrary maxima across incomparable scopes and
   distinguishes an actual satisfying observation from an
   echoed requested basis. Browser adoption needs current
   target/owner and physical completion.
5. **Managed fragments require initial authoritative knowledge
   or a permitted acquisition.** An empty fragment with
   neither is a structural defect. An SSE advisory alone
   contains no authoritative projection and grants no
   automatic read or DOM installation.
6. **Stable semantic owner versus replaceable DOM and stream.**
   Subscription, logical fragment, physical target, client
   generation and per-principal disclosure have separate
   ownership and retirement rules. Advisory and adopted
   frontiers can differ under the HumanHelp one-Refresh policy.
7. **Real publication is part of closure.** An operation that
   commits but never produces required publication can be
   domain-correct and application-incomplete. An in-memory
   SSE send after the transaction cannot by itself cover
   a crash interval. Durable intent, replay, polling or
   user-authorized reacquisition are distinct strategies
   with explicit coverage and liveness premises.
8. **Initial/subscription and reconnection gaps must be
   justified.** A change between initial render and new
   subscription registration can be lost. The revised
   specification requires a real fence, replay/buffer
   argument or compensating authoritative acquisition,
   including after failover and permission changes; it
   does not presume an atomic transaction is the only
   permissible design.
9. **Distributed routing preserves single-node semantics.**
   Hierarchical/coarse interest filters and replicated
   routers are allowed optimizations, but a node's
   locally unknown interest is not evidence of no remote
   need. Failed routers, late registrations, retention
   expiry and duplicate advisory re-delivery require a
   bounded repair/consistency contract. The specification
   does not demand exactly-once SSE or per-client records
   of every successfully broadcast invalidation.
10. **Positive and adverse cases matter.** Eighteen
    histories cover successful Claim, remote advisory
    and one-Refresh adoption, unknown response,
    authorized durable replay, nondisclosable/missing
    record, non-atomic command record, later Reassign,
    insufficient basis, continuation failure after DOM
    install, uninitialized fragment, inception gap,
    crash-before-publish, duplicate advisory, router
    failover, principal change, undeployed publisher
    and stale physical target.

R4's older illustrative server outcome labels
(`confirmed`, `reconciled`, `superseded`) are not
promoted to authoritative Part IV outcomes. R5's
stronger canonical vocabulary and explicit progression
obligation remain preferred. The longer R4 examples
are preserved as operational obligations, not as
current shipped public API signatures.

## Important current Gesso v1622 correspondence

- `src/gesso/model/command_resolution.clj` provides a
  typed command binding, transactional plan extension
  that can place a canonical `:gesso_commands` result
  in the **same** admitted model plan, and
  `reconcile` with a mandatory trusted disclosure
  predicate. A nil record yields `:unresolved`, not
  rejection or retry authority; a nondisclosable
  record yields `:unavailable`, an incompatible
  binding `:incompatible` and an admissible exact
  match `:replay`. Its header and assurance scope
  explicitly do not establish application authorization,
  retention, universal XTDB physical correspondence,
  unbounded linearizability or notification durability.
- `src/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_unknown_command_reconciliation.clj`
  performs an opt-in read-only pinned XTDB command-record
  observation after uncertain request execution.
  It explicitly states that the separate single-UPDATE
  gateway does **not** atomically write the canonical
  `gesso_commands` row, and a later matching row
  does not identify which attempt created it.
  Its result is a bounded/redacted diagnostic, not a
  safe-to-retry, settlement or transaction permit.
- `src/gesso/live/operation_acquisition_preflight.clj`
  derives route-exposed operation publication topics
  through a compiled Live invalidation graph to
  already-closed fragment acquisition realizations.
  An absent (nil) declaration is a structural
  error; an explicit empty topic set is supported.
  An unconsumed topic may be a warning, not an
  automatic failure, because it may serve elsewhere.
  This is relative to trusted declarations: actual
  model emission, durable delivery and global
  subscription coverage are not established.
- `src/gesso/live/acquisition_preflight.clj`
  checks a progression-safe fragment GET and managed
  SSE route relation for a compiled fragment. Its
  physical route facts are supplied by the host, not
  independently authenticated running Biff/Aleph
  routing or guaranteed authorization and delivery.
- `src/gesso/live/progression.cljc` defines typed
  opaque basis requirements and declared advancement/
  coverage relations, not a total order over arbitrary
  tokens. `src/gesso/live/consistency/xtdb.clj`
  supplies the selected XTDB-specific progression
  and acquisition consistency mechanisms; real
  served/client adoption remains separate.
- `src/gesso/live/core.clj` documents that its
  guarded `live-swap!` does not retry a successful
  mutation when subsequent publication fails, and
  publication failure is classified post-commit.
  It does **not** establish a durable outbox or
  multi-node interest replay guarantee by itself.
  `src/gesso/live/transport/sse.clj` explicitly
  separates framing and stream closure from
  interest filtering, fragment rendering, XTDB
  state and general authorization.
- Relevant source tests include
  `test/gesso/model/command_resolution_test.clj`,
  `test/gesso/live/operation_acquisition_preflight_test.clj`,
  `test/gesso/live/acquisition_preflight_test.clj`,

  `test/gesso/assurance/proof/xtdb_gateway_unknown_command_reconciliation_test.clj`

## R5.1 improvements over the strong R5 starting point

- An explicit outcome/participant evidence table prevents
  present-state, HTTP and replay results from collapsing
  into generic "confirmed" labels.
- The physical *atomicity* of command records is a qualified
  assertion, not a property inferred from an independent
  command-record lookup.
- Recovery's owner, disclosure and versioned binding survive
  process/request/plan retirement or terminate in an honest
  blocked/manual state, not invented command rejection.
- Scoped required progression, actual acquired observation
  and physical DOM completion are all separate evidence.
- Baseline/subscription inception and reconnect continuity
  are first-class interfaces, not merely "has SSE route"
  and "has GET route" checkboxes.
- Durable publication and advisory duplication have an
  explicit failure/replay/idempotency contract.
- Single-node and multi-node invalidation share identical
  semantic requirements; coarse/hierarchical router filters
  and replay-on-failure are permitted without assuming
  complete local interest knowledge or exactly-once sends.
- The HumanHelp remote advisory with an explicitly granted
  *one* Refresh is preserved, including expiry, duplicate
  delivery and later advisory debt.
- Eighteen positive/adversarial histories make both normal
  and failure-path claims falsifiable without collapsing
  structural closure into production correctness.

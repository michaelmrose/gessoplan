** Acceptance, qualification, and change impact

*** The application is usable as well as closed

The three claims in 07.01 are assessed at different boundaries.
This section owns *independent acceptance and qualification*, not
a second Choreo or Request business model. A closed application may
still lack eligible Claim, manager Reassign, initial board knowledge
or one-Refresh adoption. Disabled controls or negative tests
cannot satisfy the positive product profile.

The oracle describes intended observable behavior, not duplicate
executable rules. Disagreements require review rather than
silently rewriting either specification or implementation.

 
*** Three independent gates and a stable product oracle

Acceptance reports separately: (1) structural assembly closure,
(2) enforcement through actual deployed request/effect/output paths,
and (3) the application's promised *positive* behavioral completeness.
Success at one gate cannot mask failure or unsupported scope at another.

The product acceptance profile describes user-visible capabilities and
authoritative outcomes independently of the *current* registry. It is not
a second implementation of the business model. HumanHelp's required
profile includes creator Request creation with immediate visibility;
helper Claim and Unclaim; permitted completion and cancellation;
manager Reassign; relevant board search/sort/filter behavior; initial
authoritative acquisition; and another participant's advisory followed
by explicitly authorized one-use Refresh. Each promised capability
has a positive reachable scenario, real rendered control, actual
model result, and required physical observation, alongside rejection
and unknown-outcome recovery cases.

Deleting Reassign can leave a smaller internally closed application;
it must fail the independently selected product-completeness gate.
A permanently disabled Claim, hidden manager action, synthetic
protocol envelope or reject-all server does not substitute for
usable positive behavior. A disagreement between the independent
product oracle and source-owned execution creates a specification/
implementation review obligation, never automatic rewriting of
the oracle to match the current route table or vice versa.

Tests name exactly which providers, authority, browser, Live,
backend or deployment relationships their fixtures replaced.
A direct trusted-route fixture is useful but cannot satisfy the
real-browser end-to-end requirement by manufacturing the missing
rendered-control-to-command edge.

*** Conformance is bound to actual subjects

Evidence records the source/implementation identity, assembly/configuration,
provider and browser profiles, test/checker version, exercised paths, bounds, and
known limitations. A passing fixture with mocked providers is not evidence that
an unexamined production transaction or subscription implementation conforms.

Each backend profile names the assumptions required by dependent claims, including
atomic dependency protection, command-record incorporation, outcome interpretation,
read progression, retention, and failure/retry semantics where used. A current or
future backend version is qualified against those same assumptions. Detecting a
violation invalidates or blocks the dependent claim; it must not be hidden by a
version update or a stale successful receipt.

 
*** Backend qualification checks actual behavior, not labels

The selected backend profile identifies its exact query, basis,
write, transaction and retry interfaces and expected behavior:
guarded dependency evaluation, transaction-internal prestate,
atomic commit and conflict handling, canonical command-record
co-incorporation where promised, outcome interpretation,
authorized read progression, retention, and failure uncertainty.
Loaded backend/adapter identity, deployment and option surfaces,
tested schedules, checker version, assumptions and independently
unobserved facts are separately recorded.

For XTDB, successfully checking a reviewed 2.2.0-beta1 API,
query/write option keys and one-shot gateway fixture is a
meaningful **interface prerequisite**, but is not proof of
actual internal transaction state, authenticated physical
causality, all production writers or universal abstract-to-XTDB
refinement. A compatibility test may pass while a stronger
atomicity or progression premise remains unproved or is
observed to fail. The corresponding propositions must retain
different results.

Qualification can layer API/interface checks, bounded real
transaction tests with disposable state, guarded source-to-
physical correspondence checks, independently established
host/deployment facts, and stronger future proofs. An
unsupported or changed backend feature does not acquire the
older proof's applicability merely by returning the same
operation IDs or a familiar version label. A strict policy
rejects use of required unqualified behavior before the
protected effect; weaker scopes must be separately selected
and accurately disclosed.

Conformance tests can expose an assumption violation without proving the entire
backend. That is a useful and intended contract. Unknown qualification after a
relevant upgrade remains unknown until the selected admission policy establishes
sufficient applicability; a changed version number is neither proof of failure
nor proof of continued correctness.

 
*** Dependency-specific change impact and evidence reuse

An admitted assembly retains typed dependencies from semantic
rule, initializers, operation families, principal/authorization
contracts, executor and provider closure, routes and actual
writers, browser source and emitted assets, Live acquisition,
backend interface/behavior, deployment and checker/proof policy
to the particular proposition each supports.

| Change | Dependent evidence or behavior to revisit |
|--------+-------------------------------------------|
| Business rule, guard, operation semantics or arguments | Source-model checks, nonvacuity, executor correspondence, role/browser projection and independent product scenarios. |
| Handler, callback, provider or mutable Var | Exact issued implementation, typed input/effect, installation and physical enforcement, even if operation IDs remain unchanged. |
| ClojureScript/compiler, plans, assets or cache/serving policy | Exact built and served bytes, runtime conformance, compatible old-client operation and retained recovery. |
| XTDB backend, adapter, query/write options or semantics | Selected interface, atomicity, physical observation, command history, progression and proof applicability. |
| Biff routes, middleware, server, worker or raw writer | Actual handler adoption, protected writer inventory, effect containment, production checks and cleanup. |
| Live interests, routing, retention or access rules | Initial/subscription gap, reconnect recovery, authorized acquisition and one-Refresh debt. |
| Checker, proof kernel, policy or product profile | Validity of corresponding proofs and assumptions, required checks, and independently authored user capabilities. |

Reuse is permitted only where *all* original source, checker,
implementation, resource, environment and deployment assumptions
relevant to the particular evidence remain applicable. A
stable semantic digest does not freeze an opaque handler's
implementation, an unchanged JS filename does not prove the
emitted bytes, and a passing previous XTDB compatibility test
does not certify a changed backend.

Reverification follows known dependency edges and marks opaque
impact as open rather than pretending a partial dependency
catalog is exhaustive. Incremental checks may reuse untouched
subgraphs but must recheck their joins into the new admitted
generation. A copied green report, process-local handle or
old module/asset receipt cannot promote a newly changed
deployment into the prior generation's authority.

*** Required verification scenarios

The verification strategy covers semantic construction, production enforcement,
and product behavior with appropriately scoped evidence. At minimum, relevant
application profiles exercise:

| Scenario | Evidence sought |
|----------+-----------------|
| Same canonical definition feeds build, startup, render, and tests | Removing or changing an owned declaration affects the actual consuming paths. |
| An opaque builder returns an invalid candidate | The actual candidate is rejected before submission without claiming all builder behavior was proved. |
| Finite exploration reaches its declared bound or exhausts its resource budget | Scope-complete finite results remain distinct from inconclusive computation and unbounded claims. |
| Rule/operation/outcome exercise is vacuous | Required exercise gates reject; the report does not inflate attempted or disabled operations into commits. |
| A copied report or metadata-equivalent function replaces an issued product | Admission rejects the substitution or explicitly establishes a new binding. |
| The server captures an earlier handler while the system map later changes | Installation evidence detects or limits the server-adoption claim rather than trusting the later map. |
| A worker writes state outside the HTTP catalog | Its path is classified and covered, or the dependent closed-world claim fails/remains explicitly conditional. |
| Two valid requests overlap and complete in reverse order | Their scoped capabilities, outcomes, and rendering contexts remain separate and reusable. |
| A deferred/streamed/cached response bypasses the synchronous guard | The selected response contract prevents unchecked managed output or reports the unsupported path. |
| A fragment's effective form/action depends on outer context | Checking uses admitted context or rejects the unestablished interpretation. |
| Old HTML requests assets after a new assembly activates | It receives the bound compatible asset set or follows the declared compatibility failure/recovery path. |
| Artifact, handler, policy, provider, or backend premise changes | Dependent evidence is invalidated/rechecked without mutating an old admission in place. |
| Startup or activation fails after acquiring resources | No unchecked application exposure remains; cleanup and surviving work are accounted for. |
| The response fails after authoritative incorporation | Server knowledge and participant uncertainty remain distinct and recoverable. |
| A disconnected subscriber misses relevant changes | Initial/reconnect acquisition closes the required gap without needing a new advisory to arrive. |
| A required user capability is removed while the remaining graph closes | Independent product acceptance fails. |

Formal checks, controlled-defect tests, observed execution correspondence, and
real-browser acceptance are complementary. A unit test of a report shape cannot
replace evidence that the actual handler or browser edge is used. A successful
request cannot establish every route or every possible schedule.

 
*** Qualification matrix and controlled positive/negative tests

| Evidence level | Required positive control and adversarial limitation |
|----------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| Source and finite model | Original rule/operation/outcome coverage with nonvacuous positive and rejected branches, plus source-mutant and bound-exhaustion cases. |
| Inductive and formal | Exact abstract invariant, transition catalog, proof/checker version and retained premises; unmodeled writers or undecided proof remain open. |
| Source-to-implementation | Real issued executor, route, typed provider, browser plan and generated asset joined to admitted sources; equal-name substitutions fail. |
| Physical backend | Actual guarded XTDB write, command record, current observation and progression under selected profile; changed backend/atomicity premises invalidate. |
| Production serving | Biff/module/handler, network traffic, emitted HTML and served JS actually use checked paths; removing the real guard fails. |
| Browser and Live | Real rendered control and compiled JS/HTMX submit, authorized settlement/reread, Live advisory/Refresh and physical DOM adoption; uncertain delivery retains debt. |
| Product completeness | Explicit required Create, Claim, Unclaim, completion, Cancel and Reassign capability success; deleting one fails regardless of smaller assembly closure. |

Each test states its scope, original sources, concrete checker and
backend/host, selected actor/argument/schedule, applied fault,
observed results, bounds and unestablished premises. It checks
a corresponding *valid* behavior. An intercepted fake committed
map cannot prove physical XTDB incorporation; a direct injected
protocol command cannot prove a button worked; a local Biff
system map does not attest a separately deployed listener.

*** Additional positive and adversarial histories

| History | Required classification |
|---------+-------------------------|
| Real eligible Claim and manager Reassign controls trigger admitted guarded operations and authorized card adoption. | Positive product cases, separate from structural or deployment proof. |
| The operation registry deletes Reassign and all remaining preflight checks pass. | The independent product profile fails despite closed structural subset. |
| A disabled Claim is accompanied by a manually injected ideal protocol command. | Route fixture may pass; rendered control/browser acceptance fails. |
| Two clients receive remote advisory after Claim; the second uses one explicit Refresh. | Test initial knowledge, advisory debt, one-grant acquisition and installed physical observation. |
| Reviewed XTDB API/option checks pass but a transaction atomicity premise fails. | Preserve interface success and fail/open the stronger behavioral proposition. |
| A new backend version changes query/write options after preparation. | Refuse affected operation until requalification; no inherited stale receipt. |
| A source report is edited to assert verified, or a private issuance handle is copied. | Neither issues proof, admission or physical transaction authority. |
| A rule changes but retains operation ID =:request/claim=. | Recheck dependent models, physical executor, browser and product evidence. |
| New JavaScript bytes are served under an unchanged filename or a CDN returns stale bytes. | Fail exact serving-currentness claim until bound to admitted assets. |
| A scheduled raw XTDB writer is missing from the registered operation catalog. | Complete writer coverage remains unestablished or prohibited by actual capability boundary. |
| Static Hiccup passes while dynamic rendering emits a malformed semantic POST. | Actual output guard must block or production enforcement remains open. |
| Claim commits, then its response fails and the client retries. | Preserve historical command ID, incorporation and authorized recovery without fictitious rejection. |
| A subscriber misses a change between initial read and interest registration. | Require fence/replay/permitted reacquisition or leave no-gap claim open. |
| Two independent requests overlap and finish in reverse order. | Preserve separate invocation, owner, target and command scopes. |
| A later module replaces the checked Biff handler after an earlier success receipt. | Invalidate deployed-adoption evidence and re-admit or restrict service. |
| An abstract model passes safety, but manager Reassign never becomes enabled. | Record abstract safety and failed nonvacuous/product coverage separately. |
| A model checker times out or an external physical witness is unauthenticated. | Report inconclusive or conditional evidence, not a universal certificate. |
| A feature passes negative and structural checks but the real browser cannot activate it. | Fail real-browser/product completeness despite green isolated tests. |

Application qualification grants no new business authority.
Current principal and Membership, RequestAssignment state,
guarded transactions and disclosure of recorded commands
remain with their trusted model/runtime owners.

The suite includes positive controls for each rejection gate. Rejecting all
requests, never exposing a control, or disabling concurrency universally is not
an acceptable way to satisfy the intended application's behavioral requirements.

** Application assurance policy and operation coverage

The application definition contains one explicit assurance policy used by
construction, installation, and activation. Stronger finite, inductive, or
correspondence checks may be optional under one policy and mandatory under
another. Their selection is visible in the admitted subject and explanation.

A low-level compatibility API may preserve an unassured legacy path, but that
path cannot inherit the managed application's enforcement or theorem claims.
The ordinary managed startup path does not silently treat an omitted policy as
permission to skip required checks. It applies the declared default policy or
rejects a missing required selection.

 
*** Policy decisions are bound to subjects and effect boundaries

The policy is a source-owned selection of required *propositions*, evidence
mechanisms, qualification profiles, and failure dispositions. It is not an
unstructured =:strict?= boolean or a list of checker names that might run.
Each mandatory proposition identifies the assembly generation, operation and
effect scope, relevant implementation/source identity, proof assumptions,
and the lifecycle boundary where its evidence must be current.

| Boundary | Policy responsibility |
|----------+-----------------------|
| Source/assembly admission | Reject unavailable mandatory semantics, missing declared owners or an incomplete required catalog; retain explicit external premises where permitted. |
| Model checking | Require the selected finite, inductive or other proof obligations with stated bounds, soundness premises, rule/operation exercise and original semantic sources. |
| Installation and activation | Verify that the required checked dispatcher, assets, handlers, providers and writer restrictions are actually bound before exposing protected operations. |
| Invocation and protected effect | Revalidate dynamic authority, current provider/command binding, relevant physical target and commit-time guard through the trusted capability boundary. |
| Post-execution observation | Attribute results, record physical/model correspondence or contradiction, and trigger declared repair without pretending to veto completed effects. |
| Positive application acceptance | Verify independently required real capabilities and recovery histories; a closed reject-all application does not satisfy them. |

A selected strict profile cannot request a guarantee for which its implementation
has no supporting mechanism and then quietly lower that requirement. It
rejects admission or makes the limitation explicit under a separately selected
weaker profile. Unsupported, inconclusive and contradicted mandatory obligations
remain distinct. A compatibility/unmanaged path may be explicitly supported,
but must advertise its narrower assurance and must not inherit managed operation
credentials, protected effects or certification labels by resemblance.

*** One construction owns the semantic catalog and its checking products

A useful construction binds:

- the admitted application assembly;
- the exact relevant operation catalog and semantic definitions;
- rule/invariant declarations with their source ownership;
- actual executor/handler implementations;
- finite model families or inductive evidence where requested;
- the comparator and observation contracts used for implementation evidence;
- required exercise/coverage gates and their budgets;
- the installation and startup path consuming that construction.

Later comparison cannot accept replacement model callbacks or an unrelated
catalog while retaining the old admission handle. Diagnostic inspection reports
the already checked subject and results; it does not rerun user callbacks to
invent a fresh explanation.

This unified construction is an ownership discipline, not a requirement for one
large function or public record type. Layered internal constructors can provide
separate products while preserving their common sources and dependency bindings.

 
The admitted catalog is an *identified closed subject*, not whatever a test
chooses to enumerate. Each rule records its domain and source owner; every
operation's abstract meaning and required authoritative writer correspondence
are distinct from the executable handler body and its physical write capability.
An independent reference model can check a mistake in the production rule, but
a pair of identically wrong rules does not establish the intended business
policy. Unproved equivalence between a modeled callback and an issued executor
is retained as an obligation, not silently inferred from matching IDs.

Construction retains a dependency argument joining admitted initializers,
operations, effects, authorizations, rule/invariant subjects, checker
implementation and deployment qualifications. Later tests use that subject.
Substituting different families, predicates or host registrations after a
certificate/report has been produced cannot reuse its admission. Reports
are not capability-bearing replacements for the checked construction.

*** Finite operation families and non-vacuous coverage

A finite exploration may expand registered operations over declared principals,
arguments, states, and choices. The expansion must preserve the mapping from
each modeled occurrence to its actual operation contract. Synthetic model IDs
are diagnostic coordinates, not production command or execution identities.

The report records the declared bounds, resource budget, visited states or state
occurrences as appropriate, attempted transitions, remaining frontier, and whether
the requested exploration completed. A depth bound can define the intended finite
problem; exhausting a computation budget before exploring that problem is
inconclusive. Completing a depth-limited exploration does not establish unbounded
invariant preservation.

Coverage dimensions remain separate:

| Coverage question | What satisfying it establishes |
|-------------------+---------------------------------|
| Every relevant registered operation has a family. | No catalog member was omitted from that family mapping. |
| A rule matches a source in an explored state. | The rule's modeled domain was exercised at least there. |
| An operation has an enabled attempt. | The model's enablement relation selected an attempt; its incorporation guard may still reject it. |
| A required committed/rejected branch occurs. | That modeled outcome was exercised within the declared exploration. |
| A modeled path preserves an invariant. | The checked path/model satisfies the stated proposition at its scope. |
| A real observed invocation matches a modeled step. | The admitted comparison establishes its stated local correspondence, with retained premises. |

A policy can require particular rules, operations, and outcomes to be exercised.
These gates prevent vacuous green reports, but do not establish product usability,
all predicate branches, representative domain completeness, or production
execution. A rule with no matching modeled source is reported as unexercised;
it cannot be counted as a substantively tested business policy.

 
Nonvacuity is a vector of independent checks, not one coverage percentage.
For every named operation, the explanation can retain counts of disablement,
enabled attempts and committed/rejected modeled outcomes, with each observation
joined to the *original* registered operation and its finite principal/argument
choice. A disabled action is not a rejected transaction. A rejected attempt is
an attempted operation, but not a committed transition. A finite model that
visits only the empty initial state may be exhaustively explored within its
stated bound and still fail required Claim-positive exercise.

The complete report also identifies which chosen principals, arguments, guards,
branches and initialized states were *not* covered, when that is knowable. It
must never infer that the chosen fixture domain represents all possible
users, Requests, Organizations or membership races. A bounded model tree is
not an observed production execution trace, even where its terminal state
resembles a physical document. If an exercise gate is required and its
operation or outcome was not reached, an independent successful invariant
check cannot waive the failed gate.

Application authors should not write separate theorem-prover programs simply to
use these checks. Gesso derives supported model/checking inputs from its semantic
construction. Explicit reference callbacks or external models remain possible,
with their translation and correspondence premises visible.

*** Induction is scoped to a catalog and its realization

A conditional application invariant requires an admitted initial-state argument
and preservation by every relevant transition in the scoped catalog. Adding a
new operation or writer can change that obligation even if no existing operation
body changed. The evidence dependency graph records the actual catalog, rule,
backend, and implementation subjects.

An abstract catalog proof is not automatically a physical database proof. Applying
it to production also requires the stated initialization, writer coverage,
input/state mapping, atomicity, frame, and transition-correspondence premises.
Part IV defines the authoritative realization obligations; Parts IX and X define
how stronger evidence composes.

 
The target inductive safety argument has the form: every admitted initial
state satisfies =I=, and for each relevant operation, =I(s)= together with
its admitted transition premises implies =I(s')=. Its quantification is
over the *complete claimed transition catalog*, not over only the operations
that happened to be enabled in one finite exploration. A guard that is
always false makes preservation vacuously true; such a result does not establish
the positive behavior required by HumanHelp. Fairness, liveness, progress,
resource boundedness and eventual user Refresh/adoption require separate
named assumptions and arguments.

Applying the abstract invariant to actual XTDB state demands a justified
mapping for initial state, principal/arguments, transaction-internal
prestate, guarded commit, all relevant physical writers and observed
poststate. Concurrent transactions and multi-step external effects do
not become one serial transition merely because an abstract step is atomic.
A counterexample, unclassified operation or invalidated catalog requires
repair or a separately justified narrower claim, not deletion from the
proof subject to recover a green report.

A selected strict assurance policy must be invoked at the real candidate admission
and installation boundaries. Calling a checker in a test or exposing an optional
function named require-policy does not enforce that policy in production. The
installation result identifies which gates actually ran and which checks remain
required at invocation time.

*** Evidence must retain original subjects and provenance

An issued executor can be bound to a checked semantic source and its actual
implementation. An issued handler can then be constructed to call exactly that
executor after trusted request conversion. This provides stronger structural
correspondence than attaching an operation name to an arbitrary function.

The binding does not prove the correctness of opaque request conversion, output
rendering, backend behavior, or arbitrary extra effects. Those contracts remain
explicit. A wrapper around an issued executor does not automatically inherit its
issuance identity or all its guarantees; supported composition records the new
wrapper's role and effects.

A report copied from a genuine check is still a report. Admission consumes the
required original source/issued product or independently verifies a durable
certificate under its declared checker. It does not accept a caller's success
map as a substitute. Evidence about one callable cannot be transferred to another
because their names or displayed metadata agree.

*** Writer coverage includes routes and non-route work

The coverage graph joins the semantic catalog, admitted executors, installed
ingress, and actual effect providers. It distinguishes at least:

1. declared operations and their permitted effects;
2. registered/issued executor implementations;
3. installed HTTP and non-HTTP entrypoints;
4. observed invocation paths at the stated scope;
5. physical submission capabilities and any independent writers.

Matching operation ID sets is useful but does not prove semantic equivalence.
A host-provided inventory is evidence about what it enumerated, not proof that
it omitted nothing. Independent observations and effect containment can strengthen
coverage; the remaining exhaustiveness premise stays explicit.

The graph must not require every writer to be used by an HTTP route. Queue
consumers, scheduled jobs, migrations, and initializers have legitimate entrypoints
and different lifetimes. Each relevant writer has an admitted owner/path or an
explicitly classified external role. Conversely, a route can invoke several
admitted operations if its choreography/effect contract permits that composition.
The operation graph is not a universal one-route/one-writer bijection.

An unclassified writer blocks a claim requiring closed writer coverage. Under a
policy that permits a limited claim with external writers, their permitted
interference and its effect on dependent invariants are named premises. A generic
label saying external must not conceal a known contradictory mutation.

 
*** Protected-state closure covers physical write capability

The protected write domain is expressed in terms of the actual entities,
tables, fields and invariants the application claims to preserve. An accurate
operation-ID registry is still insufficient if a Biff handler, queue worker,
migration, initializer, raw XTDB call, external service or retry can reach the
same protected state through a different writer. An installed capability
inventory describes which paths can *potentially* mutate protected targets,
including broad full-document replacement, erasure or SQL effects; a claimed
write footprint does not itself enforce a restriction on the physical sink.

For a stronger all-writer claim, the system must either restrict the real
write capability at a boundary that every relevant path actually uses,
or establish correspondingly strong independent evidence of exhaustive
writer inventory and permitted interference. An instrumentation callback
that observes only the operations developers registered cannot certify
absence of an unregistered writer. Source-relative host enumeration,
path/handler identity, target-scope declarations and operation-ID equality
are useful intermediate evidence, but their scope and all unverified
completeness premises remain visible.

A selected invariant can survive *some* additional writers if their actual
effects are shown to preserve it under the declared interference model.
Neither a global raw-writer prohibition nor an assumption that every route
is managed is universal. The application instead declares its supported
writer policy and refuses to attribute the stronger invariant to effects
outside that policy. No trusted report map can grant itself physical
submission rights or prove a real XTDB gateway has excluded bypasses.

*** Request-scoped and work-scoped submission

The normal managed path gives candidate code the resources needed for preparation
and gives the admitted submission boundary the scoped effect capability. A raw
XTDB client need not be available to every handler merely because the application
uses XTDB.

A request has its own invocation identity, current principal binding, selected
assembly instance, issued effect resources, and completion/retirement protocol.
Fresh grants are issued for subsequent requests. A one-use observation or writer
handle must not become a production route that works once and then rejects every
later valid invocation.

A process-wide mutable slot that installs one request's callback and rejects
unrelated concurrent requests is not the general concurrency architecture. The
normal design uses request/work-local contexts or an equivalent isolated registry
with explicit ownership. A qualified serial controller can remain a supported
limited profile where the limitation is intentional and visible.

 
Two simultaneous valid Claim attempts must be allowed to reach independently
owned admitted request contexts without stealing each other's callable,
principal, source observation or one-use writer right. Their eventual guarded
transaction results may differ because only one can incorporate a conflicting
assignment; that is model concurrency, not a reason for the second request to
fail with an unrelated stale global-resource error. An application policy may
choose an explicit serialized deployment but may not present it as unrestricted
parallel progress.

An effect capability is issued to the correct owner and checked at its
actual use, not as a process-wide permanent flag. Reentrant callbacks,
cross-thread continuation, provider retries and cancellation must not
amplify that capability. The source of time, current principal, guarded
prestate and command attribution remain separately bound; completion of an
HTTP response does not by itself mean a durable submitted command has
committed or failed.

Asynchronous handlers, worker tasks, and durable workflows need explicit context
propagation or ownership transfer. Merely retaining a thread-local binding does
not establish their lifetime. Work that survives an HTTP response acquires its
own admitted owner; it cannot borrow the retired request's capability indefinitely.

Cancellation and cleanup first revoke further local use. They do not undo a
transaction already submitted or prove its rejection. Cleanup must not overwrite
an intervening registry change, and failure after dispatch retains command/effect
uncertainty and the required recovery owner.

One-use physical grants and semantic command deduplication remain separate.
Compatible retries preserve =:gesso/command-id= and its binding while receiving
new invocation/transport resources. Backend retries or repeated transaction
function evaluation obey the provider's declared contract and cannot silently
multiply external effects.

*** Observation after execution is not a pre-effect guard

A correspondence observer can detect that an installed route invoked the wrong
writer or that an actual result differs from its model. If that check runs after
dispatch, it is evidence and diagnosis; throwing from it cannot prevent or undo
writes that already happened.

Mandatory safety checks run before the protected effect, with commit-time
conditions enforced by the authoritative boundary. Post-execution observation
may add evidence, flag a broken premise, or trigger containment and recovery.
The explanation states which role each check plays.

A sequence of observed results matching a model path does not by itself establish
physical chronology or uninterrupted state continuity. Occurrence identities,
ordering evidence, captured state scope, and unobserved interference remain part
of the correspondence argument. Equal before/after documents do not prove that
no intervening writer ran.

 
A protected operation therefore has two deliberately separate questions:
*may this effect occur now?* and *what can later be concluded from observing
its execution?* A post-commit comparator cannot be offered as the former.
An observed mismatch may invalidate subsequent admission, demand
operator intervention or create a recovery obligation. It may not
retroactively convert a committed Claim into a rejected command, erase
an already visible physical mutation, or authorize a compensating write
without a separately admitted operation.

*** Policy acceptance and mutation histories

A compliant completed application must demonstrate both meaningful positive
execution and targeted rejection/failure, under the *same real selected
assembly* and evidence profile. The following histories are independent
acceptance requirements, not prefilled mock reports:

| History | Required disposition and evidence boundary |
|---------+--------------------------------------------|
| A current eligible helper clicks actual Claim and the issued command incorporates, then authoritative acquisition and installation complete. | The operation, guarded physical transaction, command attribution and physical presentation have separate positive evidence; a finite checker pass alone is insufficient. |
| Two eligible helpers concurrently attempt Claim on one open Request. | Independent request-local capabilities reach atomic guard evaluation; at most the admitted winner incorporates, and the other has a truthful rejection or recoverable unknown under its contract. |
| A finite model reaches a Claim guard but every configured outcome is rejected. | Report attempts but no committed-branch exercise; fail a policy requiring a positive Claim outcome. |
| Every abstract operation preserves =I=, but the finite model never enables required manager Reassign. | Keep conditional safety distinct; fail Reassign nonvacuity and independent product capability acceptance. |
| A registered operation has no matching finite family or its family uses a different abstract preparation callback than the physical executor. | Reject missing catalog coverage, or retain the source-to-executor correspondence obligation; equal IDs do not prove callback equivalence. |
| A rule exists and has been checked over some states, but no explored state matches its declared source predicate. | Record unexercised rule and reject a required exercise gate; do not count the rule as tested. |
| A scheduler or migration can write protected RequestAssignment through an undeclared XTDB path. | Leave all-writer coverage open or block the policy; a matching route/operation ID set is not complete physical enforcement. |
| A writer declares a narrow field footprint but can physically replace or erase the complete RequestAssignment document. | Use the real possible physical effect in interference/coverage obligations; declaration alone cannot narrow the sink. |
| A copied green assurance report is supplied at startup instead of a live checked construction and bound installation. | Refuse to promote a report to admission or transaction capability; recompute/check original admitted sources. |
| Two parallel requests share one one-use process slot and the second is rejected before its own valid execution. | Fail the normal concurrency contract; a deliberately serialized profile must disclose that limitation. |
| An unauthorized Claim is detected only by a comparator after the write has already committed. | The observation cannot discharge a pre-effect guard; require trusted invocation and atomic model guards, then record the contradiction and recovery. |
| An HTTP response is lost after submission and the client retries with the original command identity. | Permit compatible new invocation/transport resources while retaining command identity and uncertain outcome; no fabricated fresh logical command. |
| A check exhausts its time/memory budget or a required theorem lies outside the supported checker language. | Classify inconclusive or unsupported, applying the selected strict policy rather than silently accepting the obligation. |
| A strict policy requires complete physical writer coverage but the selected installation can only enumerate host-supplied registrations. | Refuse the strong guarantee; retain an explicitly weaker profile only when separately chosen and truthfully explained. |

The expected diagnostics identify original declaration, physical source,
required proposition, applicable policy, checker/host boundary, remaining
premises and missing or contradictory evidence. A negative-only test fixture
cannot establish the product's real positive capabilities. Nor does a
successful modeled or observed transition establish checker soundness,
deployed correspondence or future execution. Sections 07.05–07.15 own the
specific route, physical asset, acquisition, startup, output and acceptance
enforcement that makes these requirements operational.
